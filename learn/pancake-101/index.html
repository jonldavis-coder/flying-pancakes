Pancake 101

A short course in the techniques behind the recipes: mother pancakes, emulsions, reductions, and thickeners.

What this journal means by pancake

A pancake, in this journal, is a batter or a soft dough cooked on a griddle, in a pan, or in a shallow mold. Crepes, dosas, injera, johnnycakes, okonomiyaki, and cachapas all qualify. So do a few close relatives that are baked, such as a Dutch baby, or fried, such as poffertjes and calas, when the kinship is real and the page says so.

A loaf of sandwich bread is not a pancake. A stew is not a pancake. When a food is a cousin rather than a poured batter, the page says so in the first screen.

Batters

A wheat batter lifts with baking powder, baking soda, or yeast. Buttermilk or another acid is why soda is in the bowl. Stir until the flour disappears. A few lumps are kinder than a tough cake.

A crepe batter is thin and usually rested, so the gluten relaxes and the cake bends. A thick drop-scone batter holds a shape when you drop it.

Rice and dal batters for dosa and injera ferment. The sour smell is the point. Mold is not. Discard a batter that smells rotten.

The griddle

Medium heat cooks the center before the face burns. A smoking pan is too hot for buckwheat, cornmeal, and honey batters. A very hot tawa is right for a paper dosa and wrong for a set dosa. The page says which.

Oil, butter, ghee, and a dry pan are different tools. A dry comal for tortillas is not a buttered pancake griddle. Follow the fat the recipe names.

Flip once for most cakes. Injera, baghrir, ployes, and qatayef are cooked on one side. The page will say do not flip.

Potato, corn, and cassava

Potato cakes need the shreds squeezed dry. Wet potato steams. The starch that settles in the squeezed liquid can go back into the bowl.

Masa harina, masarepa, fresh corn, and plain cornmeal are four different ingredients. Substituting one for another makes a different bread.

Cassava pages use commercial cassava flour, tapioca starch, or yuca sold ready to cook. They do not teach a home process for bitter cassava.

Holidays

Shrove Tuesday, Maslenitsa, Hanukkah, Ramadan, and Chandeleur are different calendars. A pancake can belong to one of them without belonging to the others. The pages keep the dates straight.

Oil at Hanukkah, butter before Lent, and qatayef after the Ramadan fast are religious foodways. They are described as practice, not as a costume.
