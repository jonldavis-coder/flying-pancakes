Pancake 101

A short course in the batters behind the journal: wheat and buttermilk, crepes, fermented cakes, and the griddle.

What counts as a pancake here

A pancake, in this journal, is a batter or a soft dough cooked on a griddle, a skillet, a stone, or a molded pan. Crepes, galettes, dosa, injera, blini, hoecakes, and arepas all belong. A deep-fried dough ball is included only when people already treat it as a pancake relative, and the page says it is fried.

A waffle, a steamed bun, and a loaf of bread are left out. The British oat flapjack, which is a baked oat bar, is not the American flapjack. The glossary keeps those names apart.

Wheat batters and chemical leaveners

A diner pancake is wheat flour, a liquid, an egg, fat, and a leavener. Baking powder and baking soda are nineteenth-century tools. Earlier American slapjacks were often unleavened or raised with eggs and beaten air.

Buttermilk and soda make a tangy, tender crumb because the acid reacts with the soda. Do not overmix. Gluten tightens the crumb and the cake turns tough. The griddle is ready when a drop of water skitters.

Crepes and other thin batters

A crepe batter is thin, usually flour, egg, and milk, and it spreads across the pan. Resting the batter lets the flour hydrate. The first crepe is a test.

Buckwheat galettes use a different grain and usually take a savory filling. They are not a sweet crepe with the sugar left out.

Fermentation

Injera, dosa, baghrir, canjeero, and kisra get their sourness and their holes from time, not from a spoon of baking powder. Warmth speeds the ferment. A cold kitchen slows it. Salt is added after the batter is sour, because salt can slow the microbes.

The journal gives a home method and a time range. It does not pretend every household ferments for the same number of hours.

Corn, masa, rice, and potatoes

Cornmeal cakes, masa cakes, and rice batters do not behave like wheat. Masa for tortillas and pupusas is nixtamalized corn, not a bowl of plain cornmeal. The page says which one the recipe uses.

Potato cakes run from shredded latkes to mashed boxty and rösti. The starch that leaks from a shredded potato is part of what holds the cake together. Rinsing it all away can make the cake fall apart.

Feast days, named carefully

English pancakes belong to Shrove Tuesday, the day before Lent, when eggs and fat were used up. French crepes belong first to Chandeleur, Candlemas, on 2 February, not to Mardi Gras. Russian blini belong to Maslenitsa, butter week, which is longer than one Tuesday.

Cheese pancakes at Hanukkah are an older Ashkenazi custom than potato latkes. Potatoes became common in Eastern Europe much later. The pages say which custom is which.
