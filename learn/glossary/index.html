Glossary

Arepa
A corn cake from Colombia and Venezuela. Modern ones are often split and filled. The corn cake itself is older than the stuffed restaurant version.
Blini
Russian pancakes, often yeasted, eaten through Maslenitsa. Buckwheat blini and wheat blini are both traditional. The plural in English is often blini.
Cachapa
A Venezuelan sweet-corn pancake, coarse with fresh corn, not the same dough as an arepa.
Chandeleur
Candlemas, 2 February, the French day most associated with crepes. It is not Shrove Tuesday.
Crepe
A thin wheat pancake. In Brittany the buckwheat version is usually called a galette.
Dosa
A South Indian fermented pancake of rice and urad dal, cooked on a griddle. Plain, masala, rava, and neer dosa are different batters, not just different fillings.
Dutch baby
An American oven pancake. The name is usually explained as a twist on Deutsch, meaning German, not as a pancake from the Netherlands or from the Pennsylvania Dutch.
Flapjack
In the United States, a pancake. In Britain, a baked oat bar. This journal uses the American sense and says so.
Galette
In Brittany, a buckwheat crepe, usually savory. The same word in other French kitchens can mean a flat cake. The page says which one.
Hoe cake
A Southern cornmeal cake cooked on a griddle. The story that it was baked on a hoe blade is widely repeated and not settled. The cake itself is real.
Injera
The fermented teff flatbread of Ethiopia, cooked on one side on a mitad. Eyes on the surface are called ayen.
Johnnycake
A cornmeal cake, famous in Rhode Island in a thin, crisp form made with white flint corn. Journey cake and Shawnee cake are both proposed sources of the name. The origin of the word is not settled.
Latke
An Ashkenazi potato pancake associated with Hanukkah. Cheese latkes are the older dairy custom. Potato latkes spread after potatoes did.
Maslenitsa
The Russian and Slavic butter week before Lent, when blini are eaten. It is a week, not a single Tuesday.
Masa
Dough made from corn treated with an alkali, used for tortillas, pupusas, gorditas, and many other cakes. It is not the same as a cornmeal batter.
Okonomiyaki
A savory Japanese pancake. Osaka mixes cabbage into the batter. Hiroshima layers batter, cabbage, noodles, and other ingredients. They are two dishes.
Pajeon
A Korean scallion pancake. Haemul pajeon adds seafood. It is a batter cake, not a dressed salad of scallions.
Piki
A paper-thin Hopi corn sheet, often blue corn, cooked on a hot stone. This journal describes the food and does not treat ceremony as a recipe.
Poffertjes
Small Dutch yeast pancakes, cooked in a dimpled pan and usually dusted with powdered sugar.
Pupusa
A Salvadoran stuffed masa cake, cooked on a griddle and typically served with curtido and tomato salsa.
Shrove Tuesday
The day before Ash Wednesday in the Western Christian calendar. In England and Ireland it is the traditional day for pancakes.
Teff
Eragrostis tef, a tiny grain domesticated in the Horn of Africa and the usual flour for injera. One hundred percent teff injera is gluten-free.
