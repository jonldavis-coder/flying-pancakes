Shrove Tuesday Around the World

Pancakes tied to the days before Lent, and the Orthodox butter week beside them.

Shrove Tuesday, Mardi Gras, Ireland’s Pancake Tuesday, and Orthodox Maslenitsa are not the same day. This book groups the pancakes that belong to that season and says which calendar each one uses.

English and Irish pancakes are Shrove. French crêpes are more famously Chandeleur, February 2, and are here because they are also the rich pancakes of the late winter. Russian blini are Maslenitsa. Galician filloas are carnival. The book does not drag in a cake that only happens to be round.

English Shrove Tuesday Pancakes

Thin and eggy, with lemon and sugar.

10 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · about 8 pancakes

History

English Shrove Tuesday pancakes are thin pancakes cooked to use up fats and eggs before Lent. The day is also called Pancake Day. A pancake race at Olney in Buckinghamshire is a real annual event. A local tradition dates it to 1445. That medieval date is a town story, not a record this guide can verify, so it is not stated here as fact. Lemon and sugar are the usual English finish. The cake is a crepe, not a Scotch pancake.

Cultural background

Schools and families still toss pancakes on the day. The toss is a game. The recipe does not require it.

Why it tastes this way

Eggs and milk were the perishable riches of a late winter kitchen, and the Church calendar said to finish them. The batter is thin so it fries quickly in a little butter and stays flexible for lemon and sugar. The sharpness of lemon is the English taste of the day, rather than a sweet filling.

How it is made

Flour, egg, and milk are cooked as thin pancakes in a small pan.

Authentic touch. Rest the batter so the gluten relaxes, or the pancake fights you when you swirl it. A nonstick or well-seasoned pan and a thin pour are the practical version of a race-day toss.

How it is used

Lemon and caster sugar. Golden syrup is a later pour some families prefer.

Field notes

  • The Olney pancake race is real. The 1445 origin date is a local tradition, not a verified record.
  • Lemon and sugar, not maple, are the classic English finish.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup all-purpose flour
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 1/4 cups milk
  • 1 tablespoon melted butter
  • Pinch of salt
  • Lemon and sugar, to serve

Steps

  1. Whisk into a thin batter and rest 20 minutes.
  2. Cook thin pancakes in a buttered skillet, flipping once.
  3. Serve with lemon and sugar.
  4. Stack them on a warm plate.
  5. A splash of milk loosens the batter if the second half thickens.
  6. Eat them the day they are made.

Home cooking, not a professional food-safety instruction.

Variations

  • A savory cheese filling is a supper use, not the Shrove classic.
  • Scotch pancakes are thicker and are not this recipe.

Pairings and uses

  • Dessert

  • golden syrup

Irish Shrove Tuesday Pancakes

Thin, lemony pancakes fried in butter for Shrove Tuesday.

10 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · about 8 pancakes

History

In Ireland, Shrove Tuesday is Pancake Tuesday, the day before Lent. Households used up eggs, milk, and butter in thin pancakes before the fast. The custom is the same Christian calendar as England’s Shrove pancakes, and the Irish cake is typically thin, closer to a crepe than to a thick American stack. Lemon and sugar are the usual finish.

Cultural background

Families still cook a stack and argue over who flips. It is a one-day ritual that many people also cook on ordinary nights because the batter is simple.

Why it tastes this way

The taste is egg, butter, and lemon because those were the rich foods to finish before Lent, not because the pancake needed a special spice. A thin batter cooks fast and takes a sharp squeeze of lemon, which cuts the butter. The religious calendar, more than the climate, is why the cake has a date attached.

How it is made

A thin batter of flour, egg, and milk is cooked in a small skillet and finished with lemon and sugar.

Authentic touch. The pan should be hot and only filmed with butter. A thick pool of batter becomes a different, heavier cake and will not toss.

How it is used

Lemon juice and sugar. A few cooks use jam or a chocolate spread. Lemon and sugar are the Shrove plate.

Field notes

  • Pancake Tuesday in Ireland is Shrove Tuesday, the day before Ash Wednesday.
  • The cake is thin. A fluffy stack is a different breakfast.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1 large egg
  • 1 1/4 cups milk
  • 1 tablespoon melted butter, plus more for the pan
  • Pinch of salt
  • Lemon and sugar, to serve

Steps

  1. Whisk the batter until smooth and rest it 15 minutes.
  2. Heat a small skillet and film it with butter.
  3. Pour a thin layer and swirl.
  4. Cook until the edge lifts, then flip for half a minute.
  5. Stack with lemon and sugar.
  6. The first pancake is the cook’s treat. It tells you the heat.

Home cooking, not a professional food-safety instruction.

Variations

  • A thicker buttermilk drop scone is a different Irish cake, on its own page.
  • Orange instead of lemon is a fair home swap and tastes less sharp.

Pairings and uses

  • Holiday table
  • Dessert
  • Breakfast
  • lemon
  • sugar
  • Vegetarian
  • Nut-free

Sweet Crêpes

A thin wheat pancake with a tender, lightly sweet crumb.

40 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · about 10 crêpes

History

Crêpes are thin pancakes cooked across France. In Brittany they are a regional pride, and February 2, Chandeleur, is the candle-day associated with eating them. That date is Candlemas, not Shrove Tuesday, though both belong to the late-winter Christian calendar. Sweet crêpes are usually wheat. Savory buckwheat galettes are the other Breton cake and have their own page.

Cultural background

Street stands and home kitchens both cook them. Sugar, jam, chocolate, or a squeeze of lemon are ordinary finishes. Crêpes Suzette is a restaurant dish, not the everyday cake.

Why it tastes this way

A rested batter of flour, egg, milk, and butter spreads paper-thin and tastes of browned butter rather than of baking powder. Wheat, not buckwheat, is what makes a sweet crêpe pale and mild enough for sugar. Chandeleur explains a spike in home cooking. The rest of the year, the cake is simply a fast dessert.

How it is made

A thin wheat batter is swirled in a hot, lightly buttered pan and cooked briefly on both sides.

Authentic touch. Rest the batter at least 30 minutes, or the gluten snaps back and the crêpe tears. The first one seasons the pan and is rarely the prettiest.

How it is used

Sugar, jam, lemon, or a chocolate spread. Fold or roll.

Field notes

  • Chandeleur, February 2, is the French crêpe day most often named. It is not the same day as Shrove Tuesday.
  • Sweet crêpes are wheat. Galettes are buckwheat.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup all-purpose flour
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 1/4 cups milk
  • 2 tablespoons melted butter, plus more for the pan
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • Pinch of salt

Steps

  1. Blend or whisk into a smooth batter. Rest 30 minutes.
  2. Heat a crêpe pan and film it with butter.
  3. Swirl a thin layer and cook until the edge browns.
  4. Flip for a few seconds.
  5. Fill or sugar, and fold.
  6. Keep finished crêpes warm under a plate.

Home cooking, not a professional food-safety instruction.

Variations

  • Buckwheat galettes are the savory Breton cousin.
  • A splash of beer or cider in the batter is a Breton home habit and changes the flavor.

Pairings and uses

  • Dessert
  • Holiday table
  • Street food

  • jam
  • lemon
  • chocolate

Galette Bretonne

Earthly buckwheat, lacy and flexible, with a toasted edge.

40 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · about 8 galettes

History

The Breton galette is a buckwheat crêpe. Buckwheat reached Brittany and became a staple on poor soils where wheat was less dependable. Galettes are the savory cake. Wheat crêpes are the sweet one. A galetterie serves them as a meal, often with cider.

Cultural background

The plain galette is the bread. The complète, with ham, cheese, and egg, is a separate named serving on its own page. This recipe is the buckwheat cake itself, which is naturally free of wheat, egg, and dairy if you cook it in oil.

Why it tastes this way

Buckwheat has a mineral, slightly bitter taste that stands up to cheese and egg and does not suit sugar the way wheat does. Brittany’s damp climate and acid soils favored the crop. A watery batter cooked very thin gets lacy holes. Water, not milk, is the traditional liquid in many Breton recipes, which keeps the flavor on the grain.

How it is made

Buckwheat flour and water, with salt, are rested and cooked as thin savory crêpes.

Authentic touch. Use a hot, barely greased pan, ideally a flat billig if you have one. A heavy skillet works. Do not add sugar. If the galette tears, the batter needs a spoon more water and a hotter pan.

How it is used

Butter, cheese, egg, or simply folded beside a salad. Cider is the Breton drink with them.

Field notes

  • A galette in Brittany usually means buckwheat. In other parts of France the word can mean a different cake.
  • Water is the classic liquid. Milk makes a milder, less traditional batter.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup buckwheat flour
  • 1 1/4 cups water
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 tablespoon oil, for the pan

Steps

  1. Whisk flour, water, and salt until smooth. Rest 30 minutes or overnight in the fridge.
  2. Heat a skillet until a drop of water dances. Oil it lightly.
  3. Swirl a thin layer.
  4. Cook until the surface looks dry and the edge lifts.
  5. Flip briefly.
  6. Stack and cover so they stay flexible.

Home cooking, not a professional food-safety instruction.

Variations

  • A little wheat flour is sometimes added to make flipping easier. That version is not gluten-free.
  • Galette complète is this cake with ham, cheese, and an egg.

Pairings and uses

  • Street food
  • Supper

  • cheese
  • cider
  • egg
  • Vegetarian
  • Vegan
  • Gluten-free
  • Dairy-free
  • Nut-free

Russian Blini

Yeasted wheat pancakes, lightly sour, full of small holes.

1 hr 40 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 6 · about 16 blini

History

Blini are yeasted Russian pancakes. During Maslenitsa, the week before the Orthodox Great Lent, they are the festival food, tied in folk explanation to the sun and to using up butter and eggs before the fast. Maslenitsa is a real holiday on the Orthodox calendar. The sun symbolism is folk interpretation, so it is named here as that, not as doctrine. Blini are also eaten the rest of the year with butter, sour cream, herring, or caviar.

Cultural background

A risen batter makes them different from a baking-powder pancake. Toppings are rich because the week before Lent was for finishing dairy. Caviar is a famous topping and is not required.

Why it tastes this way

Yeast and a warm rest give a slight tang and a holey crumb that holds melted butter. Maslenitsa explains the butter: the fast ahead limited dairy, so the week used it. Wheat blini are milder than buckwheat ones. Sour cream and salt fish are the savory logic of a rich, mild cake. The holiday is religious and seasonal, which is why the same batter tastes like a feast in that week and like breakfast later.

How it is made

A yeasted batter of flour, milk, egg, and butter is risen twice and cooked as small pancakes.

Authentic touch. Let the batter rise until puffy. Cook the blini smaller than a dinner plate so they stay tender. Brush the cooked side with butter if you want the classic sheen.

How it is used

Butter, sour cream, smoked fish, or jam. Herring and caviar are traditional and add fish.

Field notes

  • Maslenitsa is the Orthodox pre-Lenten week, and blini are its pancake.
  • The sun story is folk symbolism, not a verified ancient rite this page can date.

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 cups warm milk
  • 1 teaspoon instant yeast
  • 1 teaspoon sugar
  • 1 1/2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 large egg
  • 2 tablespoons melted butter, plus more for the pan

Steps

  1. Stir milk, yeast, sugar, and flour. Rest 45 minutes until bubbly.
  2. Beat in salt, egg, and melted butter. Rest 30 minutes more.
  3. Cook small pancakes on a buttered medium griddle.
  4. Flip when the surface is full of holes.
  5. Brush with butter.
  6. Serve warm with sour cream.

Home cooking, not a professional food-safety instruction.

Variations

  • Buckwheat blini are the earthier Maslenitsa cousin.
  • A spoon of batter saved as a starter makes the next batch sourer.

Pairings and uses

  • Holiday table
  • Savory meal
  • Breakfast
  • butter
  • sour cream
  • jam
  • Vegetarian
  • Nut-free

Buckwheat Blini

Darker, earthier yeasted blini with a buckwheat bite.

1 hr 40 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 6 · about 16 blini

History

Buckwheat blini, grechnevye blini, are the older-tasting Russian pancake, made with buckwheat flour and yeast. Buckwheat was a staple grain. These blini are especially associated with Maslenitsa and with savory toppings. A portion of wheat flour in many home recipes makes them less fragile. This version uses both and says so.

Cultural background

They are smaller and darker than white blini. Sour cream, mushrooms, or fish are the partners. The batter should taste faintly sour.

Why it tastes this way

Buckwheat grows in a short season and tastes mineral, which is why these blini do not need sugar. Yeast sourness plus the grain tastes deeper than a white pancake. Butter and sour cream are the Maslenitsa fats. A little wheat keeps the cake from crumbling on the spatula, a practical compromise cooks actually use.

How it is made

Buckwheat and wheat flours are mixed with yeast, milk, and egg, risen, and cooked as small cakes.

Authentic touch. Do not expect them to taste like a diner pancake. If they taste bitter, the buckwheat was scorched on too hot a pan. Keep the heat moderate.

How it is used

Sour cream and butter, or mushrooms. Caviar is optional and is fish.

Field notes

  • Buckwheat blini are a Maslenitsa food and an everyday savory pancake.
  • This recipe includes some wheat so the cakes hold.

Ingredients

  • 3/4 cup buckwheat flour
  • 3/4 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1 teaspoon instant yeast
  • 1 teaspoon sugar
  • 1 1/2 cups warm milk
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 large egg
  • 2 tablespoons melted butter

Steps

  1. Mix flours, yeast, sugar, and milk. Rest 45 minutes.
  2. Add salt, egg, and butter. Rest 30 minutes.
  3. Cook small cakes on a buttered griddle over medium.
  4. Flip when set.
  5. Serve with sour cream.
  6. They should be tender, not crisp crackers.

Home cooking, not a professional food-safety instruction.

Variations

  • All-buckwheat batter is more traditional and harder to flip.
  • White blini are the milder page.

Pairings and uses

  • Holiday table
  • Savory meal
  • sour cream
  • butter
  • mushrooms
  • Vegetarian
  • Nut-free

Nalysnyky

Thin pancakes rolled around sweet cheese and baked.

30 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 rolls

History

Nalysnyky are Ukrainian filled crepes. A sweet cheese filling is a common home and feast version, and they also appear in the buttery foods of Masnytsia, the Ukrainian pre-Lenten week related to Maslenitsa. Savory fillings exist too. This page is the cheese nalysnyky, baked so the rolls set.

Cultural background

They are a dish for guests and for the pre-Lenten table, not only a Tuesday night. Farmer cheese and a little sugar are the filling. Butter on top browns in the oven.

Why it tastes this way

A thin egg pancake is a way to turn tvorog into a feast dish that can be made ahead and baked. Butter on the pre-Lenten table is the same logic as Russian blini: use the dairy before the fast. The cheese tastes gently sour. The pancake tastes of butter. Together they are richer than either one.

How it is made

Thin pancakes are filled with sweetened farmer cheese, rolled, and baked with butter.

Authentic touch. Spread a thin layer of filling. A fat roll bursts. Bake covered for part of the time so the pancakes do not dry into chips.

How it is used

Warm, with sour cream.

Field notes

  • Nalysnyky are filled. Deruny are potato cakes. They are not the same dish.
  • They belong to Masnytsia foods as well as to ordinary entertaining.

Ingredients

  • 3/4 cup flour
  • 1 large egg
  • 1 cup milk
  • Pinch of salt
  • 1 tablespoon oil
  • 1 cup farmer cheese
  • 1 egg yolk
  • 2 tablespoons sugar
  • 2 tablespoons butter

Steps

  1. Make thin pancakes from flour, egg, milk, salt, and oil.
  2. Mix cheese, yolk, and sugar.
  3. Fill and roll. Place in a buttered dish and dot with butter.
  4. Cover and bake at 375°F for 15 minutes, then uncover for 5.
  5. Serve with sour cream.
  6. Do not overfill.

Home cooking, not a professional food-safety instruction.

Variations

  • A savory meat filling is another nalysnyky and adds meat.
  • Blintzes are the related Jewish rolled pancake, often fried rather than baked.

Pairings and uses

  • Supper
  • Holiday table
  • Stuffed or filled
  • sour cream
  • Vegetarian
  • Nut-free

Finnish Blini

Small yeasted buckwheat cakes for the Shrovetide table.

1 hr 30 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · about 16 small cakes

History

Finland’s Laskiainen is the Shrovetide festival, with pea soup and cream buns as the foods most often named. Blini are also served in Finland, especially in communities with Russian influence, as small yeasted buckwheat cakes with toppings such as sour cream or fish roe. This page is those blini, not a claim that they replaced pea soup. The Orthodox and eastern connection explains the cake better than a generic “Finnish pancake” label.

Cultural background

They are small, risen, and eaten with savory or rich toppings. Roe adds fish. This recipe stops at the cake and sour cream.

Why it tastes this way

Buckwheat and yeast taste deeper and tangier than a baking-powder pancake, which is why blini suit sour cream and salt rather than maple. The eastern European method moved into Finnish tables through shared borders and Orthodox tradition. Shrovetide is a late-winter feast before the fast, which is why rich toppings show up then.

How it is made

A yeasted batter of buckwheat and wheat is risen and cooked as small cakes.

Authentic touch. Let the batter rise until it is bubbly. Flat blini are just buckwheat pancakes. The rise is the dish. Cook them small.

How it is used

Sour cream, melted butter, or fish roe if you eat fish. The roe is not in the batter.

Field notes

  • Laskiainen foods most famously include pea soup and buns. Blini are also served and are not a substitute story.
  • The yeast rise is what makes them blini rather than lettu.

Ingredients

  • 1/2 cup buckwheat flour
  • 1/2 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1 teaspoon instant yeast
  • 1 teaspoon sugar
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 cup warm milk
  • 1 large egg
  • 2 tablespoons melted butter

Steps

  1. Stir the batter and rest it 1 hour, until bubbly.
  2. Cook small cakes on a buttered medium griddle.
  3. Flip when the tops are full of holes and set.
  4. Serve warm with sour cream.
  5. They should taste faintly tangy.
  6. Do not make them large or the center stays heavy.

Home cooking, not a professional food-safety instruction.

Variations

  • Russian blini are the larger tradition this cake belongs to.
  • A purely wheat batter is milder and less typical.

Pairings and uses

  • Holiday table
  • Savory meal
  • Breakfast
  • sour cream
  • melted butter
  • Vegetarian
  • Nut-free

Galician Filloas

Thin Galician pancakes, often with sugar or a meat filling at carnival.

15 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · about 10 filloas

History

Filloas are Galician thin pancakes, cooked at carnival and at other gatherings. A sweet milk filloa is the usual home cake. Some carnival filloas traditionally used pig’s blood. This recipe is the milk version and does not give a blood recipe. Sugar or honey finishes them. They are cousins of Asturian frixuelos.

Cultural background

They are stacked with sugar. A savory meat filling appears at carnival in some valleys. The written cake is sweet and simple.

Why it tastes this way

Milk and egg make a mild crepe that tastes of the dairy country of green Galicia. Carnival is the season of rich pancakes before Lent, the same calendar logic as Shrove Tuesday farther north. Sugar on a hot filloa melts into the surface. The blood version is a separate old practice and is named only so it is not erased or romanticized here.

How it is made

A thin milk batter is cooked as crepes and sugared.

Authentic touch. Cook them thin in a lightly buttered pan. Stack with sugar while warm. The first one will look worse than the rest.

How it is used

Warm, with sugar.

Field notes

  • The written filloa is the milk pancake, not a blood recipe.
  • Frixuelos are the Asturian cousins.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup flour
  • 2 eggs
  • 1 1/4 cups milk
  • Pinch of salt
  • 1 tablespoon melted butter
  • Sugar, to serve

Steps

  1. Whisk a thin batter and rest 15 minutes.
  2. Cook thin crepes in a buttered pan.
  3. Sprinkle with sugar while warm.
  4. Stack and serve.
  5. Keep the pour thin.
  6. The first one seasons the pan.

Home cooking, not a professional food-safety instruction.

Variations

  • Frixuelos are the Asturian cousin.
  • A carnival blood filloa is a different historical recipe and is not written here.

Pairings and uses

  • Dessert
  • Holiday table
  • sugar
  • honey
  • Vegetarian

Asturian Frixuelos

Thin carnival pancakes from Asturias, finished with sugar or honey.

15 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · about 10 frixuelos

History

Frixuelos are the thin pancakes of Asturias, especially at carnival. The batter is flour, egg, and milk, close to Galician filloas and to other Spanish carnival crepes. Honey or sugar is the finish. They are a regional name worth keeping beside filloas rather than folded into one generic Spanish pancake.

Cultural background

Families cook a stack. The cake is thin. Anís appears in some batters and is left out here so the recipe is alcohol-free.

Why it tastes this way

Egg and milk are the richness before Lent, the carnival pattern. Asturias’s dairy is why the batter tastes of milk rather than of water. Sugar or honey is enough. The mountain and coastal kitchens of the region both claim them. The method is a crepe. The name is local.

How it is made

A thin batter is cooked as crepes and finished with sugar or honey.

Authentic touch. A small pan and a thin pour. Rest the batter. Butter, not a deep fry, is the fat.

How it is used

Stacked, warm.

Field notes

  • Frixuelos are Asturias’s carnival crepes.
  • This batter has no alcohol.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup flour
  • 2 eggs
  • 1 1/4 cups milk
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • Pinch of salt
  • Butter for the pan
  • Honey, to serve

Steps

  1. Whisk and rest the batter 15 minutes.
  2. Cook thin pancakes in butter.
  3. Serve stacked with honey or sugar.
  4. Do not make them thick.
  5. Eat them the day they are made.
  6. A small pan is easier.

Home cooking, not a professional food-safety instruction.

Variations

  • A splash of anise liqueur is a flavored variation and is alcoholic.
  • Filloas are the Galician kin.

Pairings and uses

  • Dessert
  • Holiday table
  • sugar
  • honey
  • Vegetarian
  • Nut-free
