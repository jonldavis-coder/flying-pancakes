Kenyan chapati

You come in by Train. Kenya · East Africa.

Pronounced chah-PAH-tee

Soft, flaky wheat flatbread with a lightly oiled, toasted surface.

30 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 flatbreads

History

Kenyan chapati is a griddled flatbread, not a batter pancake. It came through the Indian Ocean with South Asian sailors, traders, and families, and it became a daily bread in Kenya, Uganda, and Tanzania. The East African loaf is usually brushed with oil and rolled so the layers flake, then cooked on a dry pan. It is closer to a soft paratha than to a puffed dry phulka, and it has its own place at this table.

Cultural background

Chapati is weekend food and ordinary supper food. A stack disappears with beans, sukuma wiki, or a stew. The dough is mixed, rested, rolled, oiled, and rolled again. None of that is a bowl of batter. Homes in Kampala and Dar es Salaam cook the same bread.

Why it tastes this way

Wheat and salt are the flavor, with oil toasted between thin layers. The griddle adds brown spots that taste slightly smoky. Because fat is rolled in, the bread stays soft and flaky instead of crisp like a cracker. It does not taste sour. There is no ferment and no egg.

How it is made

Mix flour, salt, water, and a little oil into a soft dough and rest it. Roll each piece out, brush it with oil, coil it, and roll it again. Cook on a hot dry griddle, turning, until both sides blister and spot. Keep the stack covered so it steams soft.

Authentic touch

Treat it as a griddled flatbread. Roll, oil, and coil the dough so it flakes. Pouring a batter will not make Kenyan chapati. Cook it dry, with only the oil already in the dough.

How it is used

Chapati is an accompaniment to beans, sukuma wiki, and stews, and it is also breakfast with tea. It is a savory bread. It is not a dessert and not a pancake rolled around sugar.

Field notes

  • Kenyan chapati is a griddled flatbread, not a batter pancake.
  • The same bread is everyday food in Uganda and Tanzania.
  • Oil rolled into the dough is what makes the layers flake.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups wheat flour (about 250 g)
  • 3/4 teaspoon fine salt
  • 3/4 cup warm water
  • 3 tablespoons neutral oil, plus more for brushing

Steps

  1. Mix the flour, salt, warm water, and a spoon of oil into a soft dough. Knead until smooth. Cover and rest for 20 minutes.
  2. Divide the dough into 8 pieces. Roll one piece into a thin round.
  3. Brush the round with oil, roll it into a rope, and coil the rope into a disk. Roll the disk out again. This is a flatbread, not a batter.
  4. Cook on a hot dry griddle, turning several times, until both sides are blistered and spotted. Press the edges with a spatula so they cook through.
  5. Stack the chapatis under a cloth while you cook the rest.

The dough contains wheat. The griddle is dry but very hot. Oil in the layers can smoke if the pan is past medium-high.

Wrap leftover chapati so the surface does not dry. Refrigerate and reheat on a dry skillet. They stiffen in the refrigerator. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • More oil and an extra coil make a flakier bread.
  • Whole-wheat flour makes a denser chapati and still contains wheat.
  • A stuffed chapati is a different, thicker bread and not this plain one.

Pairings

  • Stewed beans
  • Sukuma wiki, braised greens
  • Tea
