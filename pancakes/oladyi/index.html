Arriving in Russia

Oladyi

You come in by Train. Russia · Eastern Europe.

Pronounced ah-LAH-dee

Small thick Russian pancakes on kefir, quicker than yeasted blini.

10 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · 12 small pancakes

History

Oladyi are small thick Russian pancakes, usually raised with kefir or sour milk and a little soda rather than a long yeast sponge. They are breakfast and snack food, not the ceremonial yeast blini of the Maslenitsa page, though both are pancakes. Apple oladyi are a common sweet variation. This recipe is the plain kefir cake.

Cultural background

They are home cooking, dropped from a spoon into a buttered pan. Sour cream is the topping. They do not need a butter-week setting to make sense.

Why it tastes this way

Kefir makes them tangy and tender. The crumb is thicker than a blin, so the inside stays soft while the outside browns. Sugar is light. Sour cream echoes the fermented milk in the batter. They taste fresher and less yeasty than a risen blin.

How it is made

Flour, egg, kefir, soda, sugar, and salt are mixed and cooked at once as small thick cakes in butter.

Authentic touch

Use kefir or a real sour milk, not sweet drinking yogurt if you can help it. Cook them small. A yeast blin is the other page.

How it is used

Warm, with sour cream, at breakfast or as a snack. Jam makes them a dessert.

Field notes

  • Oladyi are the small kefir pancakes, not the yeasted Maslenitsa blini.
  • They are cooked as soon as the batter is mixed.
  • Sour cream is the usual topping.

Ingredients

  • 200 g plain wheat flour
  • 1 tbsp sugar
  • 1/2 tsp baking soda
  • 1/4 tsp fine salt
  • 1 large egg
  • 300 ml kefir
  • 30 g unsalted butter, for the pan
  • Sour cream, for serving

Steps

  1. Whisk the flour, sugar, baking soda, and salt. Beat in the egg and kefir until just smooth.
  2. Cook spoonfuls in butter over medium heat until the tops set, then turn.
  3. Cook until the centers spring back.
  4. Serve with sour cream.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Diced apple folded into the batter
  • Yeasted blini, which are the butter-week page

Pairings

  • Sour cream
  • Jam
  • A spoon of honey, which adds a honey allergen
