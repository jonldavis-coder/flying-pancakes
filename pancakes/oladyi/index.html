Pronounced ah-LAH-dee

Small, thick, kefir pancakes, lightly sour.

10 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · about 12 cakes

History

Oladyi are small thick Russian pancakes, usually raised with kefir or sour milk and a chemical leavener rather than a long yeast sponge. They are homier and faster than blini, served with sour cream, honey, or jam. They are not the Maslenitsa centerpiece, though a family might fry them in that week too.

Cultural background

Kefir is the cultured milk that gives the tang. Yogurt thinned with milk is the workable substitute and should be called that.

Why it tastes this way

Kefir’s acid and baking soda make a quick lift and a sour taste that wants sour cream or a sweet jam, not both at once unless you like the contrast. They are thicker than blini because the batter is, so the inside stays soft. The speed is the cultural difference: oladyi are a morning cake, blini are a project.

How it is made

Kefir, egg, flour, and soda make a thick batter that is fried as small cakes.

Authentic touch

Fry them in a little oil, small and thick, and do not press them flat. They are supposed to puff.

How it is used

Sour cream, honey, or jam.

Field notes

  • Oladyi are the quick thick pancakes. Blini are yeasted.
  • Kefir is traditional. Thin yogurt is a substitute.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup kefir or plain yogurt thinned with a splash of milk
  • 1 large egg
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1 cup flour
  • Oil for frying

Steps

  1. Stir kefir, egg, sugar, soda, and salt. Add flour to a thick batter.
  2. Heat a film of oil over medium.
  3. Fry small cakes until puffed and browned on both sides.
  4. Drain.
  5. Serve with sour cream or jam.
  6. Stir the soda in just before cooking.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Apple grated into the batter is a common home oladyi.
  • Yeast blini are the different, holey cake.

Pairings

  • sour cream
  • jam
  • honey
