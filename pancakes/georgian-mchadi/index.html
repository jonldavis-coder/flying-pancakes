Arriving in Kutaisi

Georgian mchadi

You come in by Train. Georgia · Caucasus.

Pronounced m-CHAH-dee

Dense cornmeal cakes with a toasted crust and a mildly sweet grain center.

15 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 6 · 6 thick cakes

History

Mchadi are Georgian cornmeal cakes, a staple of western Georgia and a bread you also meet in Tbilisi. Maize is an American crop, so mchadi belongs to the centuries after corn reached the Caucasus, not to a pre-Columbian Georgian pantry. The cakes are often cooked in a pan, and some kitchens bake them. This recipe uses a skillet. They are served with sulguni cheese and with lobio, a bean stew. Neither of those is in the dough.

Cultural background

Cornmeal and salt are enough. The dough is stiff, shaped into thick rounds, and fried in a little oil until a crust forms. In Imereti, around Kutaisi, mchadi is ordinary food beside beans and cheese. It is not a sweet cornbread with sugar.

Why it tastes this way

Cornmeal tastes sweet and toasted when the crust browns, and plain when you hit the center. There is no wheat gluten to make it bready, so the crumb breaks rather than stretches. Oil in the pan adds richness. Salt is the seasoning. Sulguni on the side is salty and milky, which is why the plain cake works.

How it is made

Stir cornmeal, salt, and hot water into a dough you can press. Form thick cakes and pan-fry them in a little oil, covered, turning once, until the crust is brown and the center is no longer raw. Rest them a minute before you cut them.

Authentic touch

Shape a stiff corn dough and cook it in a pan, which is the method given here. Do not pour a wheat pancake batter. Cheese and beans are the company, not ingredients inside this gluten-free cake.

How it is used

Mchadi is an accompaniment to lobio and to sulguni, and it is a savory breakfast or supper. Sulguni is dairy and is not in the dough. The cake can also be eaten with nothing but salt.

Field notes

  • Mchadi are cornmeal cakes. This recipe cooks them in a skillet.
  • Some kitchens bake mchadi instead. That is a different pan.
  • One hundred percent cornmeal is gluten-free if the meal was not milled with wheat.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups cornmeal (about 280 g)
  • 3/4 teaspoon fine salt
  • 1 1/4 cups hot water
  • 2 tablespoons oil for the pan

Steps

  1. Stir the cornmeal and salt together. Pour in the hot water and mix to a stiff dough. It should not pour. There is no wheat in this recipe.
  2. Rest the dough for 10 minutes. Divide it into 6 pieces and press each into a cake about a finger thick.
  3. Heat the oil in a skillet over medium heat. Add the cakes. This method is the pan, not the oven.
  4. Cover and cook, turning once, until both sides have a brown crust and the center is cooked through, not chalky.
  5. Drain on a rack for a minute. Serve with beans or cheese if you want the usual plate. The cheese is dairy and is not in the dough.

Gluten-free only if the cornmeal is free of wheat. The pan oil is hot. Sulguni on the side is dairy.

Mchadi dry out quickly. Wrap them once cool and reheat in a covered pan. Refrigerate leftovers after a day in a cool kitchen, sooner if the room is warm. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • An oven batch, baked as thicker cakes, is the other common method and is not the one written here.
  • A spoon of oil in the dough makes a richer crumb.
  • Yellow or white cornmeal both work. A coarse grind makes a grainier cake.

Pairings

  • Lobio, a bean stew
  • Sulguni cheese, which adds dairy
  • Pickles
