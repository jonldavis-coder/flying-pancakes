Pronounced m-CHAH-dee

A dense corn cake, pan-cooked, meant to go with cheese and stew.

10 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 4 · 2 small rounds

History

Mchadi is a Georgian cornbread, often cooked in a skillet, made from cornmeal and water with little else. Western Georgia, where corn took hold, is especially associated with it. It is a bread for cheese and for stews such as lobio, not a sweet breakfast cake. It is included here as a griddled corn bread.

Cultural background

The texture is dense and slightly crumbly, closer to a hoe cake than to a fluffy pancake. Suluguni cheese alongside is a classic bite and is a separate dairy food.

Why it tastes this way

Cornmeal and water, cooked dry, taste toasted and plain because there is nowhere for sugar or wheat to hide. Corn became a western Georgian staple, which is why a corn cake, not a wheat flatbread, is the local bread in that kitchen. Cheese supplies salt and fat. The mchadi supplies substance.

How it is made

Cornmeal, salt, and water are shaped into a round and cooked in a covered skillet.

Authentic touch

Use a fine or medium meal and enough water to make a dough that holds, not a pourable batter. Press it into a cake and cook it covered, slowly, so the center loses its raw taste.

How it is used

Warm, with cheese or beside a bean stew.

Field notes

  • Mchadi is a Georgian corn cake, especially linked to the west of the country.
  • It is a bread, not a dessert pancake.

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 cups cornmeal
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 3/4 cup hot water
  • 1 tablespoon oil

Steps

  1. Stir cornmeal, salt, oil, and hot water into a dough. Rest 10 minutes.
  2. Shape two thick rounds.
  3. Cook in a lightly oiled covered skillet over medium-low, turning once, until both sides are browned and the center is cooked.
  4. Rest a few minutes.
  5. Serve warm with cheese.
  6. The center should not taste of raw meal.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A little oil in the dough makes them less crumbly.
  • They are not sweet. Syrup is the wrong finish.

Pairings

  • suluguni
  • bean stew
