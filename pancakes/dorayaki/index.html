Dorayaki

You come in by Plane. Japan · East Asia.

Pronounced doh-rah-YAH-kee

Two small golden-brown pancakes sandwiched around sweet red bean paste.

15 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 6 · 6 sandwiches

History

Dorayaki is a Japanese sweet of two small pancakes with anko, sweet red bean paste, between them. Sweet shops sell it as a snack, and it is a dessert rather than a meal. A popular story links the name to a gong, because of the round shape. That story is a story. This page does not treat it as a documented origin, and it does not pin the sandwiched form to a year. The cake is wheat, egg, and milk, with beans as the filling.

Cultural background

The pancakes are closer to a small castella crumb than to a thin crepe: sweet, cakey, and cooked only to a deep gold. Anko can be smooth koshian or chunky tsubuan. Either is correct. The sandwich is pressed lightly so the paste reaches the edge.

Why it tastes this way

Red beans cooked with sugar taste chestnut-like and not at all like a chocolate spread. The pancake is sweet from sugar and rich from egg and milk, with a slight bitterness where it browns. Honey is traditional in some batters. This one uses sugar so the allergen list stays free of honey. The two cakes and the paste are meant to be eaten together, not apart.

How it is made

Cook small sweet pancakes in a lightly oiled pan and sandwich pairs with sweet red bean paste.

Authentic touch

Cook small rounds and match them in pairs while warm. The tops should be dry enough to hold paste and soft enough to bend a little. High heat scorches the sugar.

How it is used

Eat them the day they are made, at room temperature or slightly warm, with tea. They are dessert.

Field notes

  • Dorayaki is two pancakes with red bean paste, not a single thick hotcake.
  • The gong story about the name is a popular tale, not a date this page will treat as fact.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup all-purpose wheat flour
  • 2 eggs
  • 1/3 cup sugar
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking soda
  • 2 tablespoons water
  • 1 cup sweet red bean paste (anko)
  • Neutral oil for the pan

Steps

  1. Whisk eggs and sugar until slightly thick. Stir in water, then flour and baking soda.
  2. Rest the batter 10 minutes. It should be thick but spreadable.
  3. Oil a pan lightly over medium-low heat. Pour small rounds, about 8 centimeters across.
  4. Cook until bubbles set and the underside is brown, then flip and finish. Cool the pancakes until they can be handled.
  5. Spread anko on half of them, cap with the rest, and press gently.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Chunky tsubuan instead of smooth anko
  • A chestnut piece in the center in autumn

Pairings

  • Green tea
  • Barley tea
  • A second pair, which is the usual mistake
