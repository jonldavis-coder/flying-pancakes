History

Belizean johnnycakes are often cooked in a skillet or baked, a Creole bread served with stew beans or at breakfast. They sit in the same broad Caribbean johnnycake family as the Bahamas loaf and are frequently a round cooked on the stove. This recipe is the skillet round.

Cultural background

They are split and buttered, or eaten with beans. Coconut milk appears in some recipes. This one uses water and oil so it is dairy-free.

Why it tastes this way

A simple flour dough cooked in a covered pan tastes toasted outside and soft inside, a bread for beans. The method is practical where an oven was not the tool. Coconut, when used, is the coast. This plain version tastes of wheat and salt so the beans can be the flavor. The Caribbean name is the link to the other johnny cakes.

How it is made

A soft dough is patted into a round and cooked in a covered skillet.

Authentic touch

Cook covered on low heat and turn once. High heat burns the crust and leaves a raw middle.

How it is used

With stew beans or butter.

Field notes

  • Belizean johnnycake is a Creole bread, often skillet-cooked.
  • Low heat is what cooks the center.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups flour
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 2 tablespoons oil
  • 3/4 cup water

Steps

  1. Mix a soft dough and rest 10 minutes.
  2. Pat a round about 3/4 inch thick.
  3. Cook in a lightly oiled covered skillet over low heat, about 10 minutes a side.
  4. The center should be set.
  5. Cool slightly and split.
  6. Serve with beans or butter.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Coconut milk in the dough is a richer coastal version.
  • The Bahamian page is the baked style.

Pairings

  • stew beans
  • butter
