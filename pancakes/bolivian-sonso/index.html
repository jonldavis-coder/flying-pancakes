History

Sonso is a yuca and cheese cake of eastern Bolivia, often cooked on a grill or griddle. It is a regional snack, related in spirit to Paraguayan mbeju and to other cassava-and-cheese breads of the lowlands. Use yuca sold ready to cook or commercial cassava flour. Do not process bitter cassava at home.

Cultural background

The cheese is salty. The yuca is mild. Together they brown on a hot surface. It is a snack, not a dessert.

Why it tastes this way

Yuca is a lowland staple, mild and starchy, and cheese salts it into a cake that tastes richer than plain casabe. Cooking it on a griddle toasts the outside. The combination shows up in several South American lowlands because both ingredients are there. Cheese is why this version is not vegan.

How it is made

Cooked yuca is mashed with cheese and salt and cooked as cakes on a griddle.

Authentic touch

Mash the cooked yuca until smooth and knead in the cheese. Cook the cakes until they have a crust. A raw center tastes starchy.

How it is used

Warm, as a snack.

Field notes

  • Sonso is yuca and cheese from eastern Bolivia.
  • Use yuca sold ready to cook, not bitter cassava you process yourself.

Ingredients

  • 1 pound yuca, peeled, boiled until soft, or 2 cups mashed ready-to-cook yuca
  • 1 cup grated dry cheese
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • Oil

Steps

  1. Mash the yuca smooth. Knead in the cheese and salt.
  2. Shape cakes.
  3. Cook on an oiled griddle until both sides have a crust and the center is hot.
  4. Serve warm.
  5. Use yuca sold ready to cook.
  6. Do not process bitter cassava at home.

Use commercial cassava flour or yuca sold ready to cook. Do not process bitter cassava at home from these pages.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Mbeju uses starch and cheese and is crispier.
  • A grill instead of a skillet is the more traditional heat.
