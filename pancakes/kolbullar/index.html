Arriving in Dalarna

Kolbullar

You come in by Train. Sweden · Northern Europe.

Pronounced KOLE-bool-ar

A simple pork and flour pancake from the charcoal woods of central Sweden.

10 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 4 pancakes

History

Kolbullar are the portable pancakes of charcoal makers and forest workers in central Sweden, including Dalarna and the old mining districts. Kol refers to charcoal. Pork, flour, water, and salt were cooked on a flat iron by the charcoal stack. This is a skillet version of that food. It is a meat dish. It is not a sweet pancake and not raggmunk.

Cultural background

The cake was working food: cheap grain, salt pork, and a fire that was already burning. At home it is still a plain, salty pancake. The romance of the woods is optional. The pork and the flour are not.

Why it tastes this way

Pork fat is the fat and the salt. The flour batter fries crisp at the rim and stays chewy. There is no egg and no sugar, so it tastes blunt and savory. Pepper helps. If you expect a dessert pancake, this will seem austere, which is accurate.

How it is made

Salt pork is diced and rendered. A batter of flour, water, and salt is poured into the fat, with the pork left in, and fried until set.

Authentic touch

Render the pork in the pan and fry the batter in that fat. The home pan stands in for the iron sheet by the charcoal stack. Do not add sugar.

How it is used

Hot, as a savory snack or a simple meal. It is not dessert.

Field notes

  • The name is tied to charcoal makers, kolare, who cooked them in the woods.
  • The batter is flour, water, salt, and pork. There is no sugar in the traditional cake.
  • This skillet version stands in for a flat iron over a charcoal fire.

Ingredients

  • 200 g salt pork, diced small
  • 150 g plain wheat flour
  • 300 ml cold water
  • 1/2 tsp fine salt
  • 1/4 tsp black pepper

Steps

  1. Whisk the flour, water, salt, and pepper into a smooth batter.
  2. Fry the salt pork in a pan until the fat renders and the pieces brown.
  3. Pour in enough batter to make a thick pancake around the pork. Cook until the bottom is brown and the top is set.
  4. Turn carefully and finish the other side. Repeat with the remaining batter. Serve hot.

Salt pork is already salty. Taste the batter before you add the full amount of salt.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Bacon instead of salt pork
  • An egg in the batter, which changes the texture and adds an egg allergen

Pairings

  • Lingonberry jam, a later table addition
  • Black coffee
  • A crisp pickle
