Turnip cake

You come in by Train. China · East Asia.

Pronounced loh bahk GOH

A savory daikon cake of rice flour, steamed until set, then pan-fried so the slices crust.

25 min prep · 50 min cook · Serves 4 · 1 loaf, about 8 slices

History

Turnip cake, lo bak go, is a Cantonese dim sum cake of rice flour and daikon. The English word turnip is the market name. The vegetable is daikon, a white radish. The batter is steamed in a tin until firm, cooled, sliced, and then pan-fried. That steam-then-sear method is the dish. Guangzhou and Hong Kong dim sum carts both serve it, so Hong Kong is listed beside China. Many carts add Chinese sausage and dried shrimp. Those add meat and shellfish. This written recipe uses daikon and shiitake so the tags stay vegetarian and free of shellfish.

Cultural background

The cake is a Lunar New Year dish as well as a dim sum standard. It should slice cleanly when cool and crust when it hits the pan. A wheat-flour batter would be a different cake. Rice flour is the structure, which is why this version is gluten-free.

Why it tastes this way

Daikon turns sweet and juicy when it is grated and cooked, and it lightens the rice flour. Shiitake adds a deep savory note that sausage would have brought in another kitchen. Sesame oil perfumes the batter. Steaming sets a soft, sliceable loaf. The later fry browns the surface so you get a crust against a moist interior. White pepper is the quiet heat, not chile.

How it is made

Cook grated daikon, stir in rice flour, steam the tin until firm, cool, slice, and pan-fry the slices.

Authentic touch

Steam until a skewer comes out clean, then cool the tin fully before you slice. Frying a hot, unset cake makes it crumble. The sear is a second step, not the only cooking.

How it is used

Serve the browned slices hot with chile sauce if you like. It is dim sum and New Year food, a savory snack or a feast plate.

Field notes

  • Lo bak go is steamed first and pan-fried second. Both steps belong to the method.
  • The vegetable is daikon. Rice flour, not wheat, sets the cake.

Ingredients

  • 500 grams daikon, grated
  • 1 cup rice flour, with no wheat added
  • 1/2 cup water
  • 4 dried shiitake mushrooms, soaked and chopped
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1/4 teaspoon white pepper
  • 1 tablespoon sesame oil
  • Neutral oil for frying

Steps

  1. Simmer the grated daikon in a pan with a splash of water until it is translucent and sweet, about 10 minutes. Stir in the mushrooms, salt, pepper, and sesame oil.
  2. Sprinkle in the rice flour and the 1/2 cup water. Stir until a thick batter forms.
  3. Pack the batter into a greased tin. Steam over boiling water for 40 minutes, until a skewer comes out clean.
  4. Cool the cake completely so it firms. Slice it.
  5. Pan-fry the slices in a little oil until both sides are brown. Serve hot.

Steam the cake until it is fully set before you cool and fry it.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Lap cheong and dried shrimp, which add meat and shellfish
  • A little chopped cilantro on the fried slices

Pairings

  • Chile sauce
  • Hot tea
  • A vinegar dip
