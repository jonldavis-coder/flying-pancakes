A soft besan pancake with turmeric, sesame, and green chile.

15 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 3 · about 6 pancakes

History

Pudla is the Gujarati besan pancake, close to cheela and seasoned in a Gujarati way: turmeric, sesame, sometimes a pinch of sugar, and green chile. It is breakfast or a tea snack. Sesame is an allergen in this recipe because it is in the batter.

Cultural background

It is eaten with pickle or chutney. The crumb is a little softer and more yellow than a plain cheela because of turmeric and a spoon of oil in the batter.

Why it tastes this way

Turmeric colors it and tastes earthy. A pinch of sugar is a Gujarati balance against chile, the sweet-hot habit of the region’s cooking. Sesame adds a toasted note. Besan is the protein of a vegetarian pantry. The pancake is fast, which suits a breakfast that is not waiting on a ferment.

How it is made

Besan, turmeric, sesame, chile, and a pinch of sugar are cooked as pancakes.

Authentic touch

Stir sesame in at the end so it stays whole. Cook on medium and oil the pan. Pudla should be supple, not a cracker.

How it is used

With pickle or cilantro chutney.

Field notes

  • Pudla is the Gujarati name to know for this besan pancake.
  • Sesame is in the batter.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup besan
  • 3/4 cup water
  • 1/4 teaspoon turmeric
  • 1 teaspoon sesame seeds
  • 1 green chile, minced
  • 1/2 teaspoon sugar
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 tablespoon oil, plus more for the pan
  • 2 tablespoons chopped cilantro

Steps

  1. Whisk everything except the pan oil into a batter. Rest 10 minutes.
  2. Cook pancakes on a medium oiled tawa.
  3. Flip when set.
  4. Cook until both sides are golden and the center is done.
  5. Serve with pickle.
  6. The turmeric will stain the pan. That is normal.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Besan cheela is the plainer North Indian cousin.
  • A handful of grated bottle gourd is a common vegetable pudla.

Pairings

  • pickle
  • cilantro chutney
