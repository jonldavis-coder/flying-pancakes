Silver Dollar Pancakes

The same buttermilk cake as a large pancake, cooked small so the edges crisp.

10 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · about 24 small pancakes

History

Silver-dollar pancakes are a size, not a separate batter. American diners, especially along the East Coast, used the name for pancakes about the diameter of the old silver dollar coin. The small round cooks faster and has more browned edge per bite. The style is twentieth-century diner cooking built on the standard buttermilk pancake.

Cultural background

Children’s plates and diner menus still use the name. The coin is the measure. A modern dollar coin is not the traditional reference.

Why it tastes this way

A small cake has more crust in proportion to crumb, so it tastes more browned and less cakey. That is the whole technical difference. The batter is the East Coast diner’s buttermilk batter: tangy, lightly sweet, cooked on a flat top that is already hot from eggs and bacon.

How it is made

Buttermilk pancake batter is dropped in small rounds on a hot griddle.

Authentic touch

Use a scant tablespoon of batter and do not spread it. If you spread it, you get a crepe, not a silver dollar.

How it is used

A pile of them with butter and syrup. They cool fast, so serve the plate as soon as the last cake flips.

Field notes

  • The name refers to the old silver dollar, a size guide, not to a different recipe.
  • Small cakes show whether the griddle is too hot. The centers should still set.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1 cup buttermilk
  • 1 large egg
  • 2 tablespoons melted butter

Steps

  1. Stir the dry ingredients together, then stir in the wet ingredients until the batter is combined.
  2. Rest 5 minutes.
  3. Heat a griddle or heavy skillet over medium heat and film it with butter.
  4. Spoon on the batter. Cook until the underside is browned and the top looks set.
  5. Flip once and cook the second side through.
  6. Drop the batter by the tablespoon. Flip as soon as the bubbles set. They cook faster than a large cake.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Any of the fruit pancakes on this site can be cooked this size.
  • A large cake from the same batter is just a pancake. The name is the size.
