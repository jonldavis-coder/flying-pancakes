Pronounced AH-pam BAH-lek

A thick turnover pancake filled with crushed peanuts and sugar.

15 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 4 turnovers

History

Apam balik is a Malaysian and Singaporean turnover pancake, cooked thick, filled with peanuts, sugar, and sometimes sweet corn or creamed corn, then folded. It is a night-market snack. Peanuts are in the classic filling, so this recipe includes them and is not nut-free. A peanut-free sugar-and-corn filling is the variation for anyone who cannot eat peanuts.

Cultural background

The crumb is soft and cakey. The filling is sweet and crunchy. Vendors fold it while hot.

Why it tastes this way

A thick batter on a covered griddle tastes like a soft cake, and the peanut-sugar filling melts into it. Peanuts are the crunch of a market snack. Sweet corn, when used, adds moisture. The fold is why it is called balik, turned over. It is a street pancake, not a breakfast crepe.

How it is made

A thick sweet batter is cooked, filled with peanut and sugar, and folded.

Authentic touch

Cook covered so the thick batter sets, add the filling, and fold before the top dries. Chop the peanuts fine so the fold seals.

How it is used

Warm, in wedges.

Field notes

  • Peanuts are the classic filling and a serious allergen.
  • Apam balik is folded, which is the balik.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup flour
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 2 tablespoons sugar
  • 1 egg
  • 3/4 cup milk
  • 1/2 cup finely chopped roasted peanuts
  • 2 tablespoons brown sugar
  • Butter

Steps

  1. Mix a thick batter from flour, baking powder, sugar, egg, and milk.
  2. Cook a thick round covered on medium-low until the center sets.
  3. Sprinkle peanuts and brown sugar on half and fold.
  4. Cook 1 minute more.
  5. Cut and serve warm.
  6. Peanuts are part of this classic filling.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Skip the peanuts and use sugar and a spoon of creamed corn if you need a peanut-free cake. It will not be the classic.
  • A banana inside is a home addition.
