Arriving in George Town, Penang

Apam balik

You come in by Boat. Malaysia · Southeast Asia.

Pronounced AH-pam BAH-leek

A turnover pancake folded over crushed peanuts, sugar, and a little butter.

15 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 4 turnovers

History

Apam balik is a Malaysian turnover pancake. Batter is cooked on a griddle, covered with peanuts and sugar, and folded. George Town stalls sell both a crisp thin kind and a thicker sweet kind. Singapore eats the same family of pancake, so it is listed here too. Peanuts are the usual filling, which is why this page carries a peanut allergen. It is street food, sweet, and distinct from Indonesian martabak manis even though the two are often mentioned together.

Cultural background

The name means a turnover. The fold is the point. Corn and extra butter show up in some batters. This recipe keeps the filling as peanuts and sugar so the cake matches its reputation. A gluten-free claim would be wrong: the batter is wheat.

Why it tastes this way

Peanuts and sugar melt against a hot pancake and taste toasted and sandy. Butter or margarine adds richness. The batter, with egg and condensed milk, browns and tastes like a sweet griddle cake rather than a crepe. Because the peanuts are crushed, they stick to the crumb instead of rolling out. The flavor is a street sweet built on sugar and roasted peanut, not on a fruit filling.

How it is made

Cook a sweet wheat batter, cover it with crushed peanuts and sugar, and fold it in half.

Authentic touch

Fold while the top is still moist enough to glue shut, and crush the peanuts fine. Whole nuts tear the pancake and fall out of the turnover.

How it is used

Cut the turnover into strips and eat it warm. It is a snack and a dessert, in Malaysia and in Singapore.

Field notes

  • Apam balik is folded over peanuts and sugar. The peanuts are not optional in this recipe.
  • Singapore sells the same pancake, so it is listed with Malaysia.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup all-purpose wheat flour
  • 1 egg
  • 3/4 cup water
  • 2 tablespoons sweetened condensed milk
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking powder
  • A pinch of salt
  • 1/2 cup roasted peanuts, crushed
  • 3 tablespoons sugar
  • 2 tablespoons butter
  • Neutral oil

Steps

  1. Whisk flour, baking powder, salt, egg, water, and condensed milk into a smooth batter.
  2. Mix the crushed peanuts with the sugar.
  3. Oil a pan over medium heat. Pour a ladle and cook until the top is bubbly and the underside is brown.
  4. Scatter peanut sugar and a few dots of butter over the pancake. Fold it in half and press.
  5. Cook another minute, slide it out, and cut. Repeat.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Canned corn and extra butter, a common stall addition
  • A thinner, crisper batter if you add more water

Pairings

  • Hot tea
  • A few extra crushed peanuts
  • Sweet corn stirred into the batter
