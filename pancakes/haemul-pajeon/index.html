Haemul pajeon

You come in by Plane. South Korea · East Asia.

Pronounced HEH-mul PAH-juhn

A crisp scallion pancake threaded with shrimp and squid, mild chile in the dip.

25 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 2 seafood pancakes

History

Haemul pajeon is the seafood scallion pancake. Haemul means seafood. Busan and other coastal tables pile shrimp, squid, and sometimes oyster or mussel into a wheat batter with scallion. This recipe uses shrimp and squid and tags shellfish. Squid is a mollusk. The allergen list here uses shellfish for it, and the ingredients name the squid plainly. It is a different pancake from the vegetable pajeon, even though the dip and the griddle are similar.

Cultural background

Coastal markets sell the seafood already cleaned. At home, pat it dry or the pancake steams. The heat of this version stays mild: gochugaru is in the dip, not a thick paste in the batter. Egg and wheat bind the cake.

Why it tastes this way

Shrimp and squid taste sweet and briny against the green scallion. Wheat and oil make the crust. A little rice flour keeps that crust from going limp under the moisture of the seafood. The dip's vinegar cuts the richness. Because the seafood is folded in, not laid on after, every wedge has some of it.

How it is made

Fold dried shrimp, squid, and scallion into a wheat batter and pan-fry until the center is cooked and the crust is brown.

Authentic touch

Dry the shrimp and squid well and cook the pancake longer than a vegetable one. Wet seafood in a thick batter leaves a raw middle.

How it is used

Cut into pieces and serve hot with the dip. It is a shared plate, not a dessert.

Field notes

  • Haemul pajeon is the seafood pancake. The vegetable scallion pancake is a separate page.
  • Squid is listed with shellfish in this allergen tag, and the recipe names it.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup all-purpose wheat flour
  • 2 tablespoons rice flour
  • 1 egg
  • 3/4 cup cold water
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 bunch scallions, cut in lengths
  • 150 grams shrimp, peeled and patted dry
  • 100 grams squid, cleaned, sliced, and patted dry
  • Neutral oil
  • Soy sauce, rice vinegar, and gochugaru for the dip

Steps

  1. Whisk both flours, egg, water, and salt. Fold in scallions, shrimp, and squid.
  2. Mix a dip of 2 tablespoons soy sauce, 1 tablespoon vinegar, and 1/2 teaspoon gochugaru.
  3. Heat a generous film of oil in a wide pan. Spread half the batter and nestle any protruding seafood into it.
  4. Cook on medium until the underside is brown and the shrimp and squid are opaque. Flip and cook through.
  5. Repeat with the second half. Rest a minute, then cut and serve.

Cook shrimp and squid until they are opaque and firm, not translucent.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A few oysters added with the shrimp, still shellfish
  • More gochugaru if you want medium heat

Pairings

  • Soy-vinegar dip
  • Sliced raw garlic
  • Steamed rice
