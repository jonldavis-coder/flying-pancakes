History

Tortas fritas are fried dough rounds of Uruguay and Argentina, famously associated with rainy days and with mate. A simple dough is rolled, slit or pricked, and fried. They are a snack, not a breakfast stack. Rain and mate are the cultural setting people actually name.

Cultural background

They can be salty or finished with sugar. This recipe salts the dough and offers sugar as a finish. They are eaten hot.

Why it tastes this way

Hot fat puffs a thin dough so it tastes toasted and rich, a comfort snack when the weather is bad. Mate, the bitter shared drink, is the partner in the Río de la Plata, which is why a salty fritter makes sense beside it. Sugar is the other habit. The rainy-day custom is real enough to report as custom, not as meteorology.

How it is made

A simple dough is rolled, slit, and fried.

Authentic touch

Roll thin, cut a slit so they do not balloon into a hollow ball, and fry hot. Drain well.

How it is used

Hot, with sugar or salt, beside mate if you drink it.

Field notes

  • Tortas fritas are a rainy-day fry bread of the Río de la Plata.
  • Mate is the drink people name beside them.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups flour
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 2 tablespoons oil
  • 3/4 cup water
  • Oil for frying
  • Sugar, optional

Steps

  1. Mix a soft dough and rest 10 minutes.
  2. Roll thin, cut rounds, and slit each one.
  3. Fry in hot oil until golden.
  4. Drain.
  5. Salt or sugar them.
  6. Serve hot.

Fry over steady heat.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Argentine torta frita is the same family.
  • Sopaipillas in Chile use squash and are a different dough.

Pairings
