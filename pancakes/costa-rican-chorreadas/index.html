Sweet fresh-corn pancakes, cousins of cachapas.

History

Chorreadas are Costa Rican fresh-corn pancakes, cooked on a griddle and served with sour cream or a crumbly cheese. They are the local sister of Venezuelan cachapas and Nicaraguan güirilas. The batter is corn. This written cake is vegan. Sour cream is the usual partner and adds dairy at the table.

Cultural background

They are a bit irregular. Sweet corn is the requirement. They are breakfast or a snack, not a stuffed dinner.

Why it tastes this way

Fresh corn’s sugar browns on a griddle and tastes like the season. A Costa Rican table often meets that sweetness with sour cream, the same sweet-salt idea as a cheese cachapa. No wheat is needed. The cake is a Central American corn pancake with its own name.

How it is made

Fresh corn is blended with a pinch of salt and cooked as small pancakes.

Authentic touch

Blend to a coarse batter and cook on medium. Cover for a minute if the center is slow. They should be spotted, not burnt.

How it is used

With sour cream or cheese if you eat dairy, or plain.

Field notes

  • Chorreadas are Costa Rica’s fresh-corn pancakes.
  • Sour cream is a table partner, not required in the batter.

Ingredients

  • 4 ears sweet corn
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 tablespoon masa harina, if needed
  • Oil

Steps

  1. Blend corn and salt to a coarse batter.
  2. Cook small cakes on a medium oiled griddle until spotted and set.
  3. Serve plain or with sour cream.
  4. They should taste of corn.
  5. Cover briefly if the center is slow.
  6. Eat them the same day.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A spoon of milk in the blend adds dairy and richness.
  • Cachapas and güirilas are the neighboring cakes.

Pairings

  • sour cream
