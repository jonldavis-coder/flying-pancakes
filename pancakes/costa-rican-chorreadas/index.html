Costa Rican chorreadas

You come in by Plane. Costa Rica · Mexico & Central America.

Pronounced choh-rreh-AH-dahs

Sweet fresh-corn pancakes, lightly eggy, served with sour cream.

15 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 pancakes

History

Chorreadas are Costa Rican pancakes made from fresh sweet corn, eggs, and milk, cooked for breakfast in the Central Valley and in rural kitchens. The kernels are ground while they are fresh. That batter is not masa, and it is not the dried-corn dough of a tortilla. Corn cakes in Mexico and Central America are older than the wheat hotcakes that arrived later. A chorreada is the fresh-corn branch of that older family, finished with natilla, the local sour cream.

Cultural background

When sweet corn is in season, home cooks grind it for breakfast instead of opening a bag of flour. Alajuela and the farms around the Central Valley both claim the habit. The cake is a weekday breakfast, not a stuffed street antojito.

Why it tastes this way

Fresh corn brings its own sugar and a juicy, green-corn aroma that dried cornmeal cannot copy. Egg and milk set the pancake and add a custard note. A spoon of fine cornmeal, if you need it, only thickens the batter. It is untreated cornmeal, not nixtamal. Sour cream on top is cool, tart, and salty against the sweet corn. There is no chile.

How it is made

Blend fresh corn with egg, milk, sugar, and salt until coarse. Cook thick pancakes on a buttered skillet and serve them with sour cream.

Authentic touch

Pulse the kernels until the batter is coarse, with visible pieces of corn. A smooth puree cooks into a flat custard. If the batter spreads too fast, stir in the spoon of fine cornmeal. Do not reach for masa harina.

How it is used

Serve them warm at breakfast, with sour cream and, if you want, a little extra salt or a drizzle of honey. They are a plate by themselves.

Field notes

  • Chorreadas use fresh sweet corn. They are not tortillas and not nixtamalized masa.
  • Natilla, sour cream, is the usual finish in a Costa Rican kitchen.
  • Corn cakes were here before wheat hotcakes. This one simply keeps the corn fresh.

Ingredients

  • 4 cups fresh sweet-corn kernels, cut from the cob
  • 1 egg
  • 60 ml milk
  • 1 teaspoon sugar
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1 tablespoon fine cornmeal, not masa harina
  • Butter for the skillet
  • Sour cream, for serving

Steps

  1. Pulse the corn, egg, milk, sugar, and salt until the batter is thick and still coarse. Stir in the cornmeal if it looks watery.
  2. Heat a skillet over medium heat and film it with butter.
  3. Spoon the batter into thick rounds. Cook until the underside is browned and the top looks set, then flip.
  4. Cook the second side until the center is no longer wet.
  5. Serve warm with sour cream.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A Nicaraguan güirila is a different fresh-corn cake, usually with cuajada in the batter and less sugar.
  • Leave out the sour cream and you still have dairy from the milk, unless you rebuild the recipe.
  • Masa harina does not belong in this bowl. The corn has to be fresh.

Pairings

  • Natilla or other sour cream
  • A mild white cheese on the side
  • Black coffee
