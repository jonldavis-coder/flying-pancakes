Bulgarian palachinki

You come in by Train. Bulgaria · Eastern Europe.

Pronounced pah-lah-CHEEN-kee

Thin Bulgarian pancakes with yogurt in the batter, sweet or with white cheese.

15 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 pancakes

History

Palachinki are Bulgaria's thin pancakes. A spoon of yogurt in the batter is a home habit and is how this page is written. They are eaten with jam or powdered sugar, and also in a savory way with sirene, the brined white cheese. Both servings are real. This recipe writes the sweet jam plate and names sirene as a variation so the ingredients stay a dessert batter. They are not Serbian palačinke and not the same filling as the Croatian page.

Cultural background

They are breakfast, snack, and dessert. Yogurt is a staple in Bulgarian cooking, so it is a natural liquid in a batter. The cakes are still thin, not a risen stack.

Why it tastes this way

Yogurt adds a gentle tang that milk alone does not. The crepe browns in butter and tastes slightly sour under the jam. Sirene, if you choose the savory plate, is salty and briny against that tang. The sweet version lets the jam stay bright.

How it is made

Flour, egg, milk, and yogurt make a thin batter. The crepes are cooked in butter and served with jam.

Authentic touch

Use plain yogurt, not a sweetened one. Keep the cakes thin. If you fill them with sirene, call the plate savory and expect a salty cheese, not a dessert.

How it is used

Rolled with jam, as breakfast or dessert. A sirene filling turns them savory.

Field notes

  • Yogurt in the batter is the Bulgarian home touch on this page.
  • Jam is the sweet serving. Sirene, a brined white cheese, is the savory one.
  • The cakes are thin palachinki, not small yeast pancakes.

Ingredients

  • 150 g plain wheat flour
  • 1 tbsp sugar
  • 1 pinch fine salt
  • 2 large eggs
  • 200 ml whole milk
  • 100 ml plain yogurt
  • 30 g unsalted butter, for the pan
  • Jam, for serving

Steps

  1. Whisk the flour, sugar, salt, eggs, milk, and yogurt into a thin batter. Rest 10 minutes.
  2. Cook thin pancakes in butter until both sides are spotted.
  3. Spread with jam, or leave them plain for a savory cheese filling.
  4. Roll or fold and serve.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Crumbled sirene and dill, a savory plate
  • A stack dusted only with sugar

Pairings

  • Jam
  • Powdered sugar
  • Sirene, for the savory variation
