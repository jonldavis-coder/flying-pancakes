Thin crepes filled with dulce de leche.

15 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 crepes

History

Panqueques in Argentina are thin crepes, a standard dessert when filled with dulce de leche. Dulce de leche is milk cooked with sugar until it is caramel, a Río de la Plata sweet shared with Uruguay. The crepe is the wrapper. The filling is the national flavor.

Cultural background

They are rolled and sometimes flambéed in restaurants. This recipe does not flame them. It warms the dulce de leche and rolls the crepes.

Why it tastes this way

Dulce de leche tastes of cooked milk sugar, richer and less tangy than goat’s-milk cajeta. A thin egg crepe is mild enough to carry it. The dessert is a café and home sweet of the twentieth century, French technique and a local caramel. Lemon and sugar would be a different country’s crepe.

How it is made

Thin crepes are spread with dulce de leche and rolled.

Authentic touch

Warm the dulce de leche so it spreads. Cold caramel tears the crepe. Do not boil it.

How it is used

Warm, as dessert.

Field notes

  • Dulce de leche is the filling that makes the crepe Argentine.
  • This recipe warms the caramel and does not flame it.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup flour
  • 2 eggs
  • 1 1/4 cups milk
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • Pinch of salt
  • 1 tablespoon melted butter
  • 3/4 cup dulce de leche

Steps

  1. Cook thin crepes.
  2. Warm the dulce de leche until it spreads.
  3. Fill and roll.
  4. Serve warm.
  5. Do not boil the caramel.
  6. A thin ribbon of filling is enough.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A sprinkle of coconut is a home finish.
  • Chilean panqueques are a close relative.

Pairings

  • dulce de leche
