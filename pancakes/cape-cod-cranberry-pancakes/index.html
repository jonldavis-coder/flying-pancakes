Cape Cod Cranberry Pancakes

Sharp cranberry against a mild buttermilk cake.

15 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · about 8 pancakes

History

Cranberries are native to the wetlands of southeastern Massachusetts. Wampanoag and other Native people used them long before commercial bogs. A wheat pancake full of chopped cranberries is a modern regional breakfast, common in the fall when the bogs are harvested. It is not a menu from 1621, and it should not be told as one.

Cultural background

The berry is too sharp to eat like a blueberry, so it is chopped and balanced with a little sugar in the batter. The cake shows up from September through the winter holidays.

Why it tastes this way

A cranberry’s acid and pectin come from a bog plant adapted to sandy, wet ground. That acid cuts a rich batter and can taste harsh if the pieces are large. A little sugar in the cake, plus maple at the table, is how New England makes the fruit breakfast-sweet without turning it into cranberry sauce. The geography is the bog, not a generic berry patch.

How it is made

Chopped fresh cranberries are folded into a lightly sweetened buttermilk batter.

Authentic touch

Chop the berries. Whole cranberries pop, roll, and stay sour in one bite. A dust of sugar on the chopped fruit before it goes in helps.

How it is used

Butter and maple syrup. A spoon of cranberry sauce on top is redundant but very much of the region in November.

Field notes

  • This is a modern breakfast. It is not presented as a 1621 dish.
  • Chopping the berries is what makes the cake pleasant rather than a mouthful of acid.

Ingredients

  • 3/4 cup fresh cranberries, chopped
  • 1 tablespoon sugar, for the berries
  • 1 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1 cup buttermilk
  • 1 large egg
  • 2 tablespoons melted butter

Steps

  1. Toss the chopped cranberries with 1 tablespoon sugar.
  2. Mix the batter and fold in the berries.
  3. Rest 5 minutes.
  4. Cook on a buttered medium griddle until set through.
  5. Flip once. The berries can stain the pan. That is normal.
  6. Serve with maple syrup.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Dried cranberries, soaked 10 minutes, work when fresh bogs are out of season.
  • Orange zest is a common holiday addition and is optional.

Pairings

  • maple syrup
  • butter
  • cranberry sauce
