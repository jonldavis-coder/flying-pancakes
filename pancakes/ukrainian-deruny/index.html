Crisp shredded-potato pancakes with onion.

20 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · about 10 cakes

History

Deruny are Ukrainian potato pancakes, grated potato and onion fried until crisp. Belarusian draniki and Jewish latkes are relatives, and the dishes are not identical in seasoning, holiday, or community. Deruny are everyday food, served with sour cream. They are also called terchuny in some places.

Cultural background

They are a meal with sour cream or mushrooms, not a Hanukkah symbol on this page. The potato itself is the shared eastern European fact.

Why it tastes this way

Grated potato fried in oil tastes sweet and browned because the starch contacts the pan. Onion adds savor. Ukraine’s reliance on the potato as a staple crop is why a pancake, not a wheat loaf, was the fast meal. Sour cream is the dairy of the region, cooling the crust. This version uses no wheat, only egg and the potato’s own starch.

How it is made

Grated potato and onion are squeezed, mixed with egg and salt, and fried.

Authentic touch

Squeeze the potato dry. Fry thin enough to crisp. Salt after they leave the pan so the crust stays dry.

How it is used

Sour cream. Mushroom sauce is a fair partner and is extra cooking.

Field notes

  • Deruny, draniki, and latkes are related potato cakes with different tables.
  • Sour cream is the usual Ukrainian topping.

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 pounds potatoes
  • 1 small onion
  • 1 large egg
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • Oil for frying

Steps

  1. Grate potato and onion and squeeze dry.
  2. Mix with egg and salt.
  3. Fry flattened spoonfuls in hot oil until crisp on both sides.
  4. Drain and salt.
  5. Serve with sour cream.
  6. Do not crowd the pan.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A spoon of flour is common and means the cakes are not gluten-free.
  • Draniki are the Belarusian cousin.

Pairings
