Buttermilk cake studded with toasted pecan.

15 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · about 8 pancakes

History

Pecans are native to the south-central United States and have long been gathered and, later, orchard-grown across the South. Folding toasted pecans into a buttermilk pancake is a modern Southern breakfast habit built on that nut, not an old plantation receipt with a documented date. The nut is the point of the cake.

Cultural background

Georgia, Texas, and neighboring pecan states all claim the breakfast. What they share is the nut, toasted so it stays fragrant in a soft batter.

Why it tastes this way

A pecan tastes buttery because of its oil. Toasting drives off raw tannin and makes that oil smell sweet. If the nuts go in raw, the cake tastes flat and the pieces steam. The South’s native pecan, rather than imported almond, is why this breakfast cake tastes the way it does.

How it is made

A buttermilk batter takes a handful of toasted chopped pecans and is cooked like any wheat pancake.

Authentic touch

Toast the pecans and salt them lightly before they meet the batter. Stir them in at the end so the batter does not turn gray from the nut skins.

How it is used

Butter and sorghum or cane syrup. More pecans on top if you like.

Field notes

  • Pecans are a tree nut. This recipe is not nut-free.
  • Toasting is the difference between a fragrant cake and a raw one.

Ingredients

  • 3/4 cup chopped pecans
  • 1 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1 cup buttermilk
  • 1 large egg
  • 2 tablespoons melted butter

Steps

  1. Toast the pecans in a dry skillet until fragrant. Salt them lightly and cool.
  2. Mix the batter as for buttermilk pancakes and fold in the pecans.
  3. Rest 5 minutes.
  4. Cook on a buttered medium griddle until both sides are browned and the centers are set.
  5. Serve hot.
  6. Keep leftover nuts aside. They soften if they sit in batter overnight.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Leave the nuts out of a few cakes for anyone who cannot eat tree nuts.
  • Black walnuts are a different, sharper nut used in Appalachian baking. They are not a silent swap.

Pairings

  • sorghum
  • butter
  • coffee
