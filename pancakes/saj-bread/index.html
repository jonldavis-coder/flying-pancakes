Arriving in Zahle

Markook saj bread

You come in by Train. Lebanon · Middle East.

Pronounced mar-KOOK

Paper-thin wheat flatbread, soft and lightly smoky from a few seconds on a dome.

35 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 6 · 6 paper-thin sheets

History

Markook, also called saj bread, is the paper-thin flatbread cooked on a domed griddle. In Lebanon and in Syria the dough is stretched until it is almost transparent, then laid for a short time on the hot dome. It is a flatbread, not a batter pancake. Mountain and Beqaa kitchens, including Zahle, still use the saj for this bread. It is torn and used to scoop or to wrap, rather than sliced like a loaf.

Cultural background

The saj is a metal dome set over heat. The cook stretches the dough over a cushion or by hand and flips it onto the dome. The bread is done in well under a minute. It is everyday bread where the griddle is part of the kitchen, and it is also a bread people buy in stacks.

Why it tastes this way

There is almost no crumb, so the flavor is toasted wheat and a trace of olive oil. The dome gives a light smoky spot where the dough touched the metal. Because it is thin, it tastes fresh only when it is soft. Dried out, it becomes a cracker, which is a different use. Salt is the only seasoning.

How it is made

Mix a soft dough and rest it well. Divide it, stretch each piece paper thin, and lay it on a hot saj for a few seconds a side until it pales and freckles. Stack the sheets in a cloth so they stay flexible.

Authentic touch

Stretch it until you can see light through a torn edge, and cook it on a dome if you have one. A thick disk in a frying pan is not markook. An inverted wok is an imperfect home stand-in for the saj, and the bread should still be paper thin.

How it is used

Markook is an accompaniment. It wraps grilled meats and vegetables, scoops labneh, or sits beside a stew. Labneh and meat are not in the dough. The same thin bread is cooked in Syria.

Field notes

  • Markook is cooked paper thin on a domed saj griddle.
  • The same bread is made in Syria as well as Lebanon.
  • It is a flatbread. The dough is stretched, not poured as a batter.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups wheat flour (about 250 g)
  • 3/4 teaspoon fine salt
  • 3/4 cup water
  • 1 tablespoon olive oil

Steps

  1. Mix the flour, salt, water, and olive oil into a soft dough. Knead until smooth. Rest it, covered, for 20 minutes.
  2. Divide the dough into 6 pieces and keep them covered. Roll, then stretch one piece until it is paper thin.
  3. Heat a saj, a metal dome, over the burner. An overturned wok can stand in if it is stable and clean.
  4. Lay the dough on the dome for a few seconds, until it pales and freckles, then turn it briefly. It should be thin enough that a torn edge shows light.
  5. Stack the sheets in a cloth so they steam and stay soft. Repeat with the rest.

The dough contains wheat. The dome is extremely hot. If you use an overturned wok, make sure it cannot tip.

Keep markook wrapped in cloth, then in a bag, so it does not dry into crackers. Refrigerate leftovers and sprinkle them with a few drops of water before reheating on the dome. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A slightly thicker sheet is easier for a beginner and less like market markook.
  • A dusting of za'atar turns it toward manakish and adds sesame if the blend contains it.
  • Syrian kitchens cook the same dome bread under local names, including khubz saj.

Pairings

  • Olives and olive oil
  • Labneh, which adds dairy
  • Grilled vegetables
