Arriving in Tunja

Arepa boyacense

You come in by Train. Colombia · South America.

Pronounced ah-REH-pah boh-yah-SEN-seh

A lightly sweet highland cake of corn and wheat, studded with cuajada and cooked on a griddle.

20 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 6 · 6 arepas

History

The arepa boyacense is a highland cake from Boyacá, built around cuajada, the fresh curd of the region. It is often a mix of precooked corn and wheat flour, enriched with egg, butter, and sugar, then griddled or baked. That makes it a different dough from a Medellín white-corn arepa and from a coastal fried arepa de huevo. Wheat in the dough means it is not gluten-free. Corn is still the main grain, and corn cakes in the region are older than wheat hotcakes, even when a later recipe folds wheat flour into the ball.

Cultural background

In Tunja and the towns around it, these arepas are breakfast and a traveler's snack, slightly sweet, with curd cheese you can see. They are thicker and richer than the thin arepa that comes with a bandeja. This recipe cooks them on a griddle.

Why it tastes this way

Sugar and butter push the cake toward a mild sweetness, while cuajada stays salty and cools that sweetness down. Wheat flour softens the crumb compared with a cake of only precooked corn. Egg helps it brown. You taste corn first, then butter, then the milky curd. There is no hogao on this plate and no fried egg inside.

How it is made

Mix precooked cornmeal, wheat flour, sugar, egg, melted butter, milk, and salt. Pat rounds, press in cuajada, and griddle until set.

Authentic touch

Press cubes of cuajada into the rounds so they show on the surface, instead of shredding the cheese until it vanishes. Cook on moderate heat. The wheat and egg brown fast, and the center still needs time.

How it is used

Serve them warm at breakfast or with hot chocolate. They are a snack cake, not a stuffed sandwich and not a fried coastal arepa.

Field notes

  • Cuajada, fresh curd, is the cheese people expect in an arepa boyacense.
  • Wheat flour in this dough means the cake is not gluten-free.
  • It is a highland griddled cake, not the fried egg arepa of the Caribbean coast.

Ingredients

  • 120 g precooked cornmeal
  • 60 g wheat flour
  • 2 tablespoons sugar
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1 egg
  • 30 g melted butter, plus more for the griddle
  • 80 ml milk
  • 100 g cuajada, in small cubes

Steps

  1. Stir the cornmeal, wheat flour, sugar, and salt.
  2. Beat the egg, melted butter, and milk, and mix them in until a soft dough forms. Add a spoon of milk if it is too stiff to pat.
  3. Divide into 6 rounds. Press cubes of cuajada into the tops.
  4. Cook on a buttered griddle over medium-low heat until both sides are browned and the center is set.
  5. Serve warm.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Some Boyacá cooks bake the rounds in a moderate oven until the cheese browns. The dough is the same.
  • Leave out the wheat flour and use only cornmeal if you need the cake free of gluten. The texture will be denser.
  • A savory version with less sugar exists. This one keeps the highland sweetness.

Pairings

  • Hot chocolate
  • A second piece of cuajada
  • Small black coffee
