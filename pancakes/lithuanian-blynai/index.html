Pronounced BLIH-nai

Yeasted pancakes, soft and slightly sour, served with jam or sour cream.

1 hr 10 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · about 12 pancakes

History

Blynai are Lithuanian pancakes, often yeasted, served sweet or with savory toppings. They belong to the same Baltic and Slavic pancake world as blini. Potato blynai are a separate cake. These are the wheat yeast pancakes.

Cultural background

They are a home dessert with jam, or a savory bite with herring. The batter rises. That is the difference from a quick crepe.

Why it tastes this way

Yeast and milk make a soft, faintly tangy cake that holds butter. Jam is the sweet path. The method is the eastern European yeasted pancake, adapted in a Baltic kitchen. A short rise is enough. A long sourdough is a different project.

How it is made

A yeasted milk batter is risen and cooked as small pancakes.

Authentic touch

Wait until the batter is bubbly. Cooking it flat and unleavened makes a crepe and wastes the yeast.

How it is used

Jam, sour cream, or butter.

Field notes

  • Wheat blynai and potato blynai are both Lithuanian and are different batters.
  • The yeast rise is the texture.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup warm milk
  • 1 teaspoon instant yeast
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • 1 cup flour
  • 1 egg
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1 tablespoon melted butter

Steps

  1. Mix all ingredients and rest 45 minutes.
  2. Cook small pancakes on a buttered griddle.
  3. Flip when the tops are set.
  4. Serve with jam or sour cream.
  5. They should look slightly puffed.
  6. Butter the pan lightly between batches.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Bulviniai blynai are the potato ones.
  • Russian blini are the close technique.

Pairings

  • jam
  • sour cream
