Arriving in Lower East Side, New York

Cheese blintzes

You come in by Train. United States · North America.

Pronounced BLINT-suhz

A thin, lightly sweet pancake wrapped around tangy soft cheese and fried in butter.

35 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 blintzes

History

Cheese blintzes are thin pancakes wrapped around a sweet cheese filling, an Ashkenazi dish from Eastern Europe that became Lower East Side and wider New York home cooking. Dairy blintzes are a Shavuot food and also an ordinary breakfast. The wrapper is a thin egg batter, and the cake is fried again after it is filled.

Cultural background

Farmer cheese or dry cottage cheese is the usual filling. Wet ricotta needs to be drained or the blintz leaks. The Eastern European origin and the New York life of the dish are both part of the story.

Why it tastes this way

The wrapper tastes of egg and butter, and the second frying makes it crisp at the seam. Farmer cheese is tangy and slightly dry, so the filling stays in a soft curd instead of running. A little sugar and lemon zest keep it from tasting like a plain cheese omelet.

How it is made

Thin pancakes filled with sweet cheese, folded, and fried in butter.

Authentic touch

Cook the wrappers on one side, fill the pale side, and fry the filled packets in butter until both faces are golden. Boiling them is not the dish.

How it is used

Hot, with sour cream or a little jam. Applesauce is a common side.

Field notes

  • Blintzes traveled from Eastern European Ashkenazi cooking to New York, including the Lower East Side.
  • Cheese blintzes are a dairy dish associated with Shavuot and also eaten as breakfast.

Ingredients

  • 3/4 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1 cup whole milk
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 tablespoon unsalted butter, melted, plus 2 tablespoons for frying
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1 cup farmer cheese
  • 1 large egg yolk
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • 1 teaspoon finely grated lemon zest

Steps

  1. Whisk the flour, milk, whole eggs, melted tablespoon of butter, and salt into a thin batter. Rest 15 minutes.
  2. Stir the farmer cheese, egg yolk, sugar, and lemon zest.
  3. Butter an 8-inch skillet lightly and heat it over medium. Pour about 1/4 cup batter and swirl it into a thin round.
  4. Cook until the top is set and the underside is spotted. Slide it out cooked side up. Repeat, stacking the wrappers.
  5. Spoon cheese onto the pale side of each wrapper. Fold the sides in and roll the wrapper closed.
  6. Fry the rolls in the remaining butter over medium heat until golden on both sides. Serve hot.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A potato filling makes a savory blintz and is a different recipe.
  • Dry cottage cheese can stand in for farmer cheese if it is drained well.

Pairings

  • Sour cream
  • Fruit jam
  • Applesauce
