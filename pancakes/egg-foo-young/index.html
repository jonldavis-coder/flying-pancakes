History

Egg foo young is a Chinese American egg pancake, a restaurant dish of the diaspora more than a single village recipe from Guangdong, though egg omelettes exist in southern Chinese cooking. Bean sprouts, onion, and a brown gravy became the plate in American Chinese restaurants. This page calls it what it is: a diaspora restaurant cake, with a home recipe.

Cultural background

The egg mixture is fried as small omelette-pancakes. A simple soy gravy is poured on. It is supper, not a sweet breakfast.

Why it tastes this way

Egg is the structure, and bean sprouts add water and crunch so it does not taste like a plain omelette. The gravy, soy and stock thickened slightly, is the American restaurant habit that makes the plate familiar. It is a story of adaptation: a Chinese egg dish reshaped by immigrant restaurants for a gravy-eating public. The flavor is savory, salty, and soft.

How it is made

Beaten eggs with sprouts and onion are fried as small cakes and served with a thin soy gravy.

Authentic touch

Use enough oil to fry the edges. Do not stir it like scrambled eggs. Let it set, then flip. The gravy should be pourable, not a paste.

How it is used

Hot, with rice and the gravy.

Field notes

  • Egg foo young is a Chinese American restaurant dish. That is the honest label.
  • Bean sprouts are the usual vegetable.

Ingredients

  • 4 eggs
  • 1 cup bean sprouts
  • 2 scallions, sliced
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • Oil
  • 1 cup stock
  • 1 tablespoon soy sauce
  • 1 teaspoon cornstarch
  • 1 teaspoon sugar

Steps

  1. Beat the eggs with salt and stir in sprouts and scallions.
  2. Fry four small cakes in a filmed skillet, flipping once, until set.
  3. Stir stock, soy, cornstarch, and sugar. Simmer until slightly thick.
  4. Pour the gravy over the cakes.
  5. Serve with rice.
  6. The egg should be fully cooked.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Shrimp or roast pork are restaurant additions and change the allergens.
  • Without gravy it is closer to a vegetable omelette.

Pairings
