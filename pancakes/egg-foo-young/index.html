Egg foo young

You come in by Plane. United States · North America.

Pronounced eg foo YUNG

A thick egg pancake with bean sprouts and onion, covered in a brown gravy.

15 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 2 · 4 egg cakes

History

Egg foo young is a Chinese-American restaurant dish. It took shape in diaspora dining rooms in the United States, including Chinatown kitchens such as those in San Francisco, as a thick egg pancake under a brown gravy. Cantonese cooking has egg dishes with related names. The gravy-covered pancake on American menus is not a single mainland classic with one agreed recipe. This page says that plainly. The written cake uses egg, bean sprouts, and mushrooms. Shrimp or roast pork versions are common on menus and would add shellfish or meat.

Cultural background

The pancake is deeper than a crepe and firmer than a French omelet. Gravy is part of the plate, ladled on when the cake is browned. It is dinner-menu food and also a late-night order. The recipe here is home cooking in that restaurant style, not a claim about one village.

Why it tastes this way

Egg is the main flavor, fried at the edge so it tastes browned and rich. Bean sprouts add water and a fresh crunch that keeps the cake from being dense. Onion and mushroom make it savory. Soy sauce in the gravy is salty and dark, thickened so it clings. The combination reads as a Chinese-American restaurant plate: soft egg, a little vegetable, and enough sauce to eat with rice.

How it is made

Pan-fry thick egg cakes with bean sprouts and onion, and serve them under a soy and mushroom gravy.

Authentic touch

Fry the cakes in a shallow film of oil until the outside is truly brown. A pale, steamed-looking round will not hold the gravy. Keep the gravy moving so the cornstarch does not lump.

How it is used

Serve two cakes to a person with rice, gravy spooned over. It is a savory restaurant-style plate.

Field notes

  • Egg foo young is a Chinese-American restaurant pancake, not one mainland classic.
  • The brown gravy is part of the plate people order in the United States.

Ingredients

  • 4 eggs
  • 1 cup bean sprouts
  • 2 scallions, chopped
  • 1/4 onion, sliced
  • 4 mushrooms, sliced
  • 1 tablespoon soy sauce
  • 1 teaspoon cornstarch
  • 3/4 cup water or unsalted vegetable stock
  • Neutral oil and a pinch of sugar

Steps

  1. Beat the eggs with a pinch of salt. Stir in bean sprouts, scallions, onion, and half the mushrooms.
  2. Oil a pan over medium heat. Pour a quarter of the egg mixture and cook it as a thick pancake until the underside browns. Flip and brown the other side. Repeat.
  3. In a small pot, fry the remaining mushrooms in a teaspoon of oil. Add soy sauce, sugar, and water.
  4. Stir cornstarch with a spoon of water, pour it in, and simmer until the gravy thickens.
  5. Serve the cakes with the gravy over them and rice beside.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A few shrimp in the egg, which adds shellfish
  • Roast pork in the egg, which adds meat

Pairings

  • Steamed rice
  • A vinegary cabbage slaw
  • Hot sauce on the side
