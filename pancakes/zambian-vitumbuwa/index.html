Zambian vitumbuwa

You come in by Plane. Zambia · Southern Africa.

Pronounced vee-toom-BOO-wah

Sweet coconut-rice fritters with a crisp fried crust and a soft grain center.

20 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · About 14 fritters

History

Vitumbuwa in Zambia are coconut rice fritters, dropped into oil and sold as snacks. The name sits close to Kenyan vitumbua, and the foods are not the same. The Kenyan cake is cooked in a rounded pan on the Swahili coast. This Zambian one is fried. Rice flour and coconut milk make the batter. It is a Southern African street and home snack, not a coastal molded pancake.

Cultural background

Lusaka stalls and home pans both turn out vitumbuwa for the afternoon. They are eaten hot, sometimes with tea. The batter is sweetened. It does not carry stew. Frying is the method that defines this page.

Why it tastes this way

Hot oil browns the outside fast, so the fritter tastes toasted and rich. Coconut milk perfumes the rice and keeps the center moist. Sugar is in the batter, not poured on later. Because the dough is fried rather than pressed into a mold, the crust is irregular and crisper than a vitumbua shell.

How it is made

Stir rice flour, coconut milk, sugar, yeast, and salt into a thick batter and give it a short rise. Drop spoonfuls into hot oil and fry until deep golden and cooked through. Drain and eat the same day.

Authentic touch

Fry spoonfuls in oil. Do not cook this batter in a rounded Swahili pan and call it the Zambian snack. The two names are neighbors, not one recipe.

How it is used

Vitumbuwa are a street snack and a home snack with tea. They are not a breakfast platter under savory stew. They are sweeter and oilier than a griddled rice pancake.

Field notes

  • Zambian vitumbuwa are fried. That is the method on this page.
  • They are not Kenyan vitumbua, which are cooked in a rounded pan.
  • Rice and coconut make the batter. There is no wheat in this version.

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 cups rice flour (about 200 g)
  • 1 cup coconut milk
  • 2 tablespoons sugar
  • 1/2 teaspoon instant yeast
  • 1/4 teaspoon fine salt
  • Oil for deep frying

Steps

  1. Stir the rice flour, coconut milk, sugar, yeast, and salt into a thick batter that falls from a spoon in a slow ribbon.
  2. Cover it for 30 to 40 minutes, until slightly puffy.
  3. Heat several centimeters of oil until a drop of batter browns in about a minute.
  4. Fry spoonfuls, turning once, until they are deep golden and cooked through. This is a fritter, not a molded pancake.
  5. Drain on a rack. Serve warm, and do not confuse them with Kenyan vitumbua.

Hot oil can boil over if the pan is crowded. The batter contains coconut. It is gluten-free only if the rice flour is free of wheat.

Eat them the day they are fried. Refrigerated fritters lose their crust. Reheat in a hot oven if you must keep them. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A mashed ripe banana in the batter is a softer, fruitier fritter.
  • Baking powder instead of yeast makes a faster batch with less ferment flavor.
  • Adding wheat flour is unnecessary and would remove the gluten-free tag.

Pairings

  • Tea
  • A dusting of sugar
  • Sliced banana on the side
