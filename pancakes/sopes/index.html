Arriving in Toluca

Sopes

You come in by Plane. Mexico · Mexico & Central America.

Pronounced SOH-pehs

A small thick masa base with a raised rim, topped with beans, cheese, onion, and salsa.

30 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 sopes

History

Sopes are small, thick masa cakes. While the dough is still soft from the first side on the comal, the cook pinches a rim so the toppings stay on. They are not gorditas. A gordita is split into a pocket. A sope is an open boat. They are not tlacoyos either, which are oval and filled before cooking. The dough is nixtamalized masa, part of the corn cooking that is older in Mexico than wheat hotcakes.

Cultural background

Sopes are market and home antojitos. Toluca and Mexico City both sell them with beans, salsa, onion, and cheese. The rim is the defining move, and it has to be pinched before the dough dries out.

Why it tastes this way

The base is thick enough to taste of steamed corn in the center and toasted masa at the bottom. Refried beans add salt and body. Fresh cheese is milky and a little sour. Raw onion and a mild tomato salsa cut the starch. Because the toppings stay on the surface, you taste them separately from the corn instead of as a hidden filling.

How it is made

Pat small thick disks of masa, griddle one side, pinch a rim, cook through, and top with warm beans, cheese, onion, and salsa.

Authentic touch

After the first side sets, flip the sope and pinch the rim quickly while the masa is flexible. If you wait until it is crisp, the edge cracks instead of rising into a wall.

How it is used

Serve two or three sopes as a snack or a light meal. Eat them with your hands once the cheese softens from the heat of the masa.

Field notes

  • The pinched rim is made with your fingers, not with a mold, while the dough is half cooked.
  • A sope is open on top. A gordita is split. They start from similar masa and become different cakes.
  • Plain cornmeal will not hold a rim the way nixtamalized masa does.

Ingredients

  • 250 g masa harina
  • 300 ml warm water
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 250 g refried beans, warmed
  • 80 g crumbled fresh white cheese
  • 1 small onion, chopped
  • 1 cup mild tomato and jalapeño salsa

Steps

  1. Mix masa harina, salt, and warm water. Knead, cover, and rest 20 minutes.
  2. Divide into 8 pieces. Pat each into a thick disk about 8 centimeters across.
  3. Cook on a dry comal over medium heat for 2 minutes. Flip. While the top is soft, pinch up a rim all the way around.
  4. Cook another 3 to 4 minutes, turning once, until the base is spotted and the center is set.
  5. Spread on warm refried beans. Add cheese, onion, and salsa. Serve hot.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Potatoes and chorizo are a common topping. Chorizo adds meat and is not vegetarian.
  • Leave the cheese off and the sopes can be vegan and dairy-free.
  • A wider sope is sometimes called a picadita or a huarache when the shape changes. This recipe stays small and round.

Pairings

  • Shredded lettuce for a colder crunch
  • Radishes and lime
  • Agua fresca
