Arriving in Singapore

Singaporean roti prata

You come in by Boat. Singapore · Southeast Asia.

Pronounced ROH-tee PRAH-tah

A flaky laminated flatbread from a Singapore coffee shop, crisp at the edge and chewy inside, served with curry.

30 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 6 pratas

History

Roti prata is Singapore's coffee-shop flatbread, cooked to order on a griddle and flipped until the layers separate. The dough is laminated with ghee or oil, the same family as Malaysian roti canai, and this page keeps the Singapore plate: a morning or late supper in a coffee shop, torn into curry gravy. Indian-Muslim cooks built that trade in Singapore as well as across the causeway. The pages stay separate so the coffee-shop habit is not collapsed into the Malaysian one. Tissue prata, stretched paper-thin and dusted with sugar, is a sweet variation.

Cultural background

In a Singapore coffee shop the prata arrives with a small bowl of curry and, often, a drink. The savory bread is the breakfast. A tissue prata is ordered when someone wants sugar instead of gravy. Home cooks can coil an oiled dough and griddle it. The flake still depends on fat between the layers.

Why it tastes this way

Ghee and wheat, fried in thin layers, taste toasted and rich. The interior stretches. Salt in the dough is plain, so the curry provides spice, coconut, and salt. A tissue prata tastes of sugar and crisp dough instead. The coffee-shop setting does not change the chemistry. It changes the meal around the bread: curry at breakfast, and the option of a sweet paper-thin cousin from the same ball of dough.

How it is made

Laminate a wheat dough with ghee, griddle it until flaky, and serve it with a curry gravy in the coffee-shop way.

Authentic touch

Stretch the dough thin and do not be afraid of an uneven edge. Those thin flaps are what crisp. Rest the oiled dough long enough that it relaxes, or it will snap back into a bun.

How it is used

Tear it and dip it in curry for a savory breakfast. For a sweet order, skip the curry and dust a thinner bread with sugar.

Field notes

  • Singapore roti prata is the same laminated-dough family as Malaysian roti canai, eaten here as coffee-shop food.
  • Tissue prata is the sweet, very thin version from the same dough.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups all-purpose wheat flour
  • 3/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1 teaspoon sugar
  • 2/3 cup water
  • 3 tablespoons ghee, plus more for the griddle

Steps

  1. Mix flour, salt, sugar, water, and a spoon of ghee into a soft dough. Knead until smooth.
  2. Divide into six pieces, coat them in ghee, and rest at least 1 hour. The rest is not active prep.
  3. Stretch one piece thin, spread a little ghee, and fold or coil it into layers.
  4. Griddle with ghee over medium heat, pressing and flipping, until both sides are spotted and flaky.
  5. Repeat and serve with curry, or stretch one piece thinner, cook it crisp, and dust it with sugar.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Tissue prata dusted with sugar
  • An egg fried onto the prata, which adds egg

Pairings

  • A mild potato and lentil curry
  • Sugar for a tissue-style piece
  • Coffee or tea
