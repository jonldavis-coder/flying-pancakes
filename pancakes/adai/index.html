Arriving in Madurai, Tamil Nadu

Adai

You come in by Train. India · South Asia.

Pronounced ah-DAI

A sturdy mixed-lentil pancake, peppery and hot from dried chile, with curry leaf in the batter.

30 min prep · 30 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 adai

History

Adai is a mixed-lentil pancake of Tamil Nadu and the wider South. Toor, chana, urad, and moong are soaked with rice, then ground with dried red chile and curry leaf. Cooks often grind and cook it the same day. A long dosa-style ferment is optional, not the definition. The result is thicker than a hotel dosa and hot enough to stand with a knob of jaggery or a spoon of avial on the side.

Cultural background

Madurai and other Tamil kitchens keep adai as a meal when a fermented batter was not planned. The several dals make it more filling than a thin rice crepe. Dried chile is the usual heat, so this version is medium.

Why it tastes this way

Each dal brings a different note: toor is earthy, chana is sweet, urad is creamy, moong is grassy. Rice keeps the cake from turning dense. Dried red chile and black pepper supply heat that green chile alone would not. Curry leaf, ground in rather than fried on top, perfumes the crumb all the way through.

How it is made

Soak a mix of lentils and rice, grind them coarsely with dried chile, and cook thick savory rounds.

Authentic touch

Leave the grind a little coarse. A silky batter is dosa territory. Adai should show flecks of dal and chile.

How it is used

Serve hot with jaggery, coconut chutney, or avial. The sweet jaggery against the chile is a Tamil habit, not a dessert course.

Field notes

  • Adai uses several lentils at once. It is not a single-dal cheela.
  • Same-day grinding is traditional. An overnight ferment is a choice, not a requirement.

Ingredients

  • 1/4 cup toor dal
  • 1/4 cup chana dal
  • 2 tablespoons skinned urad dal
  • 2 tablespoons moong dal
  • 1/3 cup raw rice
  • 3 dried red chiles
  • 10 curry leaves
  • 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
  • 3/4 teaspoon salt
  • Water and neutral oil

Steps

  1. Rinse the dals and rice together and soak them with the dried chiles for 3 to 4 hours.
  2. Drain and grind coarsely with curry leaves, pepper, salt, and a little water. The batter should be thick and speckled, not a smooth cream.
  3. Cook it promptly, or rest it for 30 minutes. No long ferment is required.
  4. Oil a hot griddle. Spread a ladle into a small, thick round. Make a tiny hole in the center if you like, a common adai habit that helps the middle cook.
  5. Drizzle oil, cook until the underside is deep gold, flip, and cook the other side. Serve at once.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A fistful of chopped onion folded into the batter
  • Less dried chile for a milder cake

Pairings

  • Jaggery
  • Coconut chutney
  • Avial
