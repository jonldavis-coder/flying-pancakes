Slovak palačinky

You come in by Train. Slovakia · Eastern Europe.

Pronounced PAH-lah-chin-kee

Thin Slovak crepes rolled with cocoa and sugar, a home filling without a brand of spread.

15 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 crepes

History

Slovak palačinky are the thin crepes of Slovakia, in the same name family as the Czech cakes and not the same recipe on this journal. Czech palačinky here are filled with quark. These are filled with unsweetened cocoa and sugar, a home pantry filling that is not a trademark spread. Jam and poppy seed are also real Slovak fillings. Poppy seed is a variation. Walnut would add a tree-nut allergen and is not in this recipe.

Cultural background

They are afternoon food and dessert, rolled while warm. A cocoa filling should taste of cocoa, not of a confectioner's brand. Families also use apricot jam.

Why it tastes this way

Cocoa is bitter and toasty against a mild, buttery crepe. Sugar dissolves in the heat of the cake and makes a simple fudge layer. Because there is no added fat in the filling except what is on the crepe, the roll is less heavy than a chocolate spread. The crepe itself is thin and eggy.

How it is made

Thin crepes are cooked, brushed with butter, sprinkled with cocoa and sugar, and rolled.

Authentic touch

Mix cocoa and sugar dry and sprinkle it on the hot buttered crepe so it melts. Do not use a branded spread and call it traditional. The Czech quark filling is the neighboring page.

How it is used

Warm, as a snack or dessert, with coffee or milk.

Field notes

  • This Slovak recipe uses cocoa and sugar, not a trademark spread.
  • The Czech page in this journal uses a quark filling.
  • Jam and poppy seed are other real fillings.

Ingredients

  • 150 g plain wheat flour
  • 1 tbsp sugar
  • 1 pinch fine salt
  • 2 large eggs
  • 350 ml whole milk
  • 30 g unsalted butter, plus more for the pan
  • 2 tbsp unsweetened cocoa powder
  • 3 tbsp sugar, for the filling

Steps

  1. Whisk the flour, 1 tbsp sugar, salt, eggs, and milk. Rest 10 minutes.
  2. Cook thin crepes in a buttered pan.
  3. Mix the cocoa and the filling sugar. Brush each hot crepe with butter and sprinkle with the cocoa mixture.
  4. Roll and serve warm.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A poppy-seed and honey filling, which adds a honey allergen
  • Ground walnuts, which add a tree-nut allergen

Pairings

  • The cocoa-sugar filling
  • Apricot jam, as an alternative
  • Milk or coffee
