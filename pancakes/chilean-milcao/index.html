Arriving in Castro

Chilote milcao

You come in by Boat. Chile · South America.

Pronounced mil-KOW

Pan-fried cakes of raw and cooked potato, salty, crisp at the edge, and dense inside.

30 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 cakes

History

Milcao is a potato cake from Chiloé, where potato diversity is old and specific. The dough mixes grated raw potato with mashed cooked potato. The starch from the raw half is what holds the cake together. Some Chilote cooks fold in pork cracklings. This recipe leaves the pork out, so the cakes stay vegetarian and vegan. A curanto, the pit cook of the archipelago, can include milcaos. The method here is the home skillet, not a pit.

Cultural background

On Chiloé, milcaos are a staple next to fish stews and in the curanto, and they are also simply pan-fried for a meal. Castro is a fair place-name for a food that belongs to the whole archipelago. The cake is potato and salt. Wheat never enters the bowl.

Why it tastes this way

Cooked potato brings a soft, familiar mash. Raw potato brings sticky starch and a fresher, sharper taste that mellows as it fries. Salt is the seasoning. Oil browns the outside. Because there is no egg and no wheat, the cake tastes only of potato, with a crisp face and a dense center. Pork cracklings would add salt and fat. They are a variation, not this batch.

How it is made

Grate raw potatoes and save the settled starch. Mash an equal weight of cooked potatoes. Mix them with salt, form cakes, and pan-fry until both sides are crisp and the raw potato is cooked through.

Authentic touch

Grate the raw potatoes, squeeze the liquid into a bowl, and let the starch settle. Pour off the water and put that starch back into the potatoes. Losing it is how the cake falls apart. Use a starchy potato, and mix half raw and half cooked by weight.

How it is used

Serve them hot beside a stew, or with butter if you are willing to add dairy. They are a bread as much as a side dish on a Chilote table.

Field notes

  • Milcao needs both raw and cooked potato. The raw starch is the binder.
  • Let the potato liquid settle and return the starch. Pouring it all away weakens the cake.
  • This batch has no pork. Cracklings are a separate, not-vegetarian variation.

Ingredients

  • 500 g starchy potatoes, peeled and left raw
  • 500 g starchy potatoes, cooked and mashed
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 2 tablespoons neutral oil for the skillet

Steps

  1. Grate the raw potatoes. Squeeze the liquid into a bowl and let it sit 10 minutes. Pour off the water and keep the starch that settled.
  2. Mix the grated potato, the reserved starch, the mashed potato, and the salt into a firm dough.
  3. Form 8 flat cakes.
  4. Pan-fry in oil over medium heat, turning once, until both sides are crisp and the center is cooked through, about 5 minutes a side. Break one open to check that the raw potato has turned tender.
  5. Serve hot.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Chicharrón folded into the dough is traditional in many houses. It adds meat and is not vegetarian.
  • Milcaos can be cooked in a curanto. This recipe stays in a skillet.
  • A little cooked pumpkin in the mash is a home addition on some islands.

Pairings

  • A fish stew, which adds fish
  • Pebre
  • Butter, which adds dairy
