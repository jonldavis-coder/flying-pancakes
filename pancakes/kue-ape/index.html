Kue ape

You come in by Plane. Indonesia · Southeast Asia.

Pronounced kweh AH-peh

A small coconut-rice cake with a crisp rim and a soft center that sinks into a crater.

20 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 4 · 16 small cakes

History

Kue ape is a Betawi snack of Jakarta, cooked in a dimpled pan. Rice flour, coconut milk, and sugar make the batter, and a little yeast helps the center rise and then fall, leaving the crater that identifies the cake. The rim turns crisp. The middle stays soft and pale. It is a mold cake. Serabi, the thicker Javanese coconut pancake served with syrup, is a different sweet and should not be substituted under this name.

Cultural background

Street vendors in Jakarta sell kue ape from a specialized pan, often in the afternoon. The crater is not a mistake. If the center stays domed and dry, the yeast or the heat was off. This recipe uses no wheat and no egg.

Why it tastes this way

Coconut milk sweetens the rice and smells rich when it browns at the rim. Sugar in the batter is enough. The soft center tastes almost like a warm coconut custard without egg, because the starch sets around moisture. The crisp rim tastes toasted. Yeast adds a faint fermented note, not a sour dosa tang. The contrast of textures is the reason to use the pan.

How it is made

Ferment a rice and coconut batter briefly with yeast, then cook it in a dimpled pan until the rims crisp and the centers crater.

Authentic touch

Do not fill the molds to the top, and do not panic when the centers sink. That sink is the kue ape. Oil the pan lightly so the rims fry rather than steam.

How it is used

Eat them warm, rims first or center first, as a street snack. They are best the day they are cooked.

Field notes

  • Kue ape is a Betawi mold cake. The soft crater in the middle is part of the dish.
  • It is not serabi, which is a plumper pancake served with palm-sugar syrup.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup rice flour, with no wheat added
  • 1 cup coconut milk
  • 3 tablespoons sugar
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1/4 teaspoon dry yeast
  • Neutral oil for the molds

Steps

  1. Whisk rice flour, coconut milk, sugar, salt, and yeast into a smooth batter.
  2. Rest it until slightly bubbly, about 30 to 45 minutes. That rest is not counted as active prep.
  3. Oil a hot dimpled pan. Fill each cup just over halfway.
  4. Cook on medium until the rims are brown and crisp and the centers have set and sunk.
  5. Unmold and repeat with the remaining batter.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A drop of pandan extract for a green crumb
  • A pinch more sugar if your coconut milk is unsweetened

Pairings

  • Hot tea
  • Grated coconut
  • A second cake, because they are small
