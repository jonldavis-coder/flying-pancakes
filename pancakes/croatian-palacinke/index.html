Thin crepes with jam or a sweet walnut filling.

10 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 crepes

History

Croatian palačinke are the same Balkan crepe family, with a coastal and inland life as dessert. Walnut fillings and marmalade are both traditional. This written cake uses marmalade so it stays nut-free, and the walnut filling is the variation. They are not a savory main in the usual order.

Cultural background

They are rolled and sometimes dusted with sugar. A lemony marmalade is a good match for a rich crepe.

Why it tastes this way

Marmalade’s bitterness keeps the dessert from being only sugar. The crepe is dairy and egg, mild and browned at the edge. Walnuts, when used, reflect an inland filling shared with Hungary and Serbia. Keeping Croatia’s page focused on marmalade avoids copying the Serbian page word for word while staying accurate.

How it is made

Thin crepes are spread with marmalade and rolled.

Authentic touch

Warm the marmalade. Its peel is firmer than jelly and will tear a crepe if you spread it cold and thick.

How it is used

Warm, with tea or coffee.

Field notes

  • Palačinke are shared across the Balkans. The filling habits differ by house.
  • Marmalade is the filling written here.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup flour
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 1/4 cups milk
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • Pinch of salt
  • 1 tablespoon melted butter
  • 1/2 cup marmalade

Steps

  1. Whisk and rest the batter 10 minutes.
  2. Cook thin crepes in butter.
  3. Spread with warm marmalade and roll.
  4. Serve warm.
  5. Do not overfill.
  6. Sugar on top is optional.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A ground walnut and honey filling is traditional and adds tree nuts.
  • The Serbian page uses jam and sparkling water. This one is marmalade and milk.

Pairings

  • marmalade
  • sugar
