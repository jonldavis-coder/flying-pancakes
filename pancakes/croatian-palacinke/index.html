Croatian palačinke

You come in by Train. Croatia · Eastern Europe.

Pronounced pah-lah-CHEEN-keh

Croatian crepes rolled with fresh cheese, lemon, and raisins.

25 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 filled crepes

History

Croatian palačinke are thin crepes eaten across the country, sweet or savory. This page writes a filling of svježi sir, fresh cheese, with lemon and raisins, which is a home dessert. It is not the Serbian plum-jam recipe and not a coastal specialty claimed for one town. Jam and a walnut filling also exist. Walnuts would add a tree-nut allergen and are a variation.

Cultural background

Fresh cheese is ordinary grocery food. The crepes are made thin, filled, and sometimes dusted with powdered sugar. They are dessert, including after a Sunday meal.

Why it tastes this way

Fresh cheese is milder and wetter than aged cheese, so lemon zest is there to brighten it. Raisins add a dark sweetness. The crepe tastes of butter and egg. Powdered sugar on the outside is dry against the soft filling. Drain the cheese or the roll will be watery.

How it is made

Thin crepes are filled with sweetened fresh cheese, lemon zest, and raisins, then rolled.

Authentic touch

Drain the fresh cheese before you sweeten it. This is a cheese roll, not the plum-jam roll of the Serbian page. A walnut version is a different allergen list.

How it is used

As dessert, dusted with powdered sugar.

Field notes

  • This Croatian recipe is a fresh-cheese roll.
  • The Serbian page uses plum jam.
  • Palačinke are eaten across Croatia, not as the property of one town.

Ingredients

  • 150 g plain wheat flour
  • 1 pinch fine salt
  • 2 large eggs
  • 350 ml whole milk
  • 30 g unsalted butter, for the pan
  • 300 g fresh cheese, drained
  • 3 tbsp sugar
  • 1 tsp lemon zest
  • 40 g raisins

Steps

  1. Whisk the flour, salt, eggs, and milk. Rest 10 minutes and cook thin crepes in butter.
  2. Beat the drained cheese with the sugar, lemon zest, and raisins.
  3. Fill and roll the crepes.
  4. Dust with extra sugar and serve.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Jam only, closer to the Serbian plate
  • Walnuts, which add a tree-nut allergen

Pairings

  • Powdered sugar
  • A spoon of sour cherry jam
  • Coffee
