Pronounced free-HWEH-lohs

Thin carnival pancakes from Asturias, finished with sugar or honey.

15 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · about 10 frixuelos

History

Frixuelos are the thin pancakes of Asturias, especially at carnival. The batter is flour, egg, and milk, close to Galician filloas and to other Spanish carnival crepes. Honey or sugar is the finish. They are a regional name worth keeping beside filloas rather than folded into one generic Spanish pancake.

Cultural background

Families cook a stack. The cake is thin. Anís appears in some batters and is left out here so the recipe is alcohol-free.

Why it tastes this way

Egg and milk are the richness before Lent, the carnival pattern. Asturias’s dairy is why the batter tastes of milk rather than of water. Sugar or honey is enough. The mountain and coastal kitchens of the region both claim them. The method is a crepe. The name is local.

How it is made

A thin batter is cooked as crepes and finished with sugar or honey.

Authentic touch

A small pan and a thin pour. Rest the batter. Butter, not a deep fry, is the fat.

How it is used

Stacked, warm.

Field notes

  • Frixuelos are Asturias’s carnival crepes.
  • This batter has no alcohol.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup flour
  • 2 eggs
  • 1 1/4 cups milk
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • Pinch of salt
  • Butter for the pan
  • Honey, to serve

Steps

  1. Whisk and rest the batter 15 minutes.
  2. Cook thin pancakes in butter.
  3. Serve stacked with honey or sugar.
  4. Do not make them thick.
  5. Eat them the day they are made.
  6. A small pan is easier.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A splash of anise liqueur is a flavored variation and is alcoholic.
  • Filloas are the Galician kin.

Pairings

  • honey
