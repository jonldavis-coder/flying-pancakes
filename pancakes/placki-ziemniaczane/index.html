Placki ziemniaczane

You come in by Train. Poland · Eastern Europe.

Pronounced PLAHT-skee zhem-nya-CHA-neh

Polish potato pancakes, shredded and fried, with sugar or sour cream.

20 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 pancakes

History

Placki ziemniaczane are Poland's everyday shredded-potato pancakes. They are fried crisp and eaten with sugar, sour cream, or a savory stew. They use the same raw-potato technique as deruny and as Ashkenazi latkes. The latke page is the Hanukkah history. This page is the Polish home and street pancake, not a holiday claim. Flour and egg bind them. They are fried in oil, so the cake itself is dairy-free. Sour cream is a topping, not an ingredient.

Cultural background

They are sold at counters and made at home, often from a bowl of grated potato that has to be cooked before it turns gray. Sugar on a potato pancake surprises people who expect only sour cream. Both are served in Poland.

Why it tastes this way

The crust is salty, browned starch. Onion makes it savory. Sugar on top is a direct contrast, grainy and sweet against the salt. Sour cream is the softer contrast. The inside should be tender, not raw. Too much flour makes them bready.

How it is made

Potato and onion are grated, mixed with egg, flour, and salt, and fried in oil until crisp.

Authentic touch

Offer sugar and sour cream as two real options, not as a mistake. Keep the cakes thin. This is not the Hanukkah latke page, even though a cook could mistake one pan for the other.

How it is used

Hot, with sugar or with sour cream. A meat goulash beside them is a meal and adds meat to the plate.

Field notes

  • Placki ziemniaczane are the Polish potato pancakes of ordinary days.
  • Sugar and sour cream are both served on them.
  • The Hanukkah latke has its own page and its own history.

Ingredients

  • 600 g starchy potatoes, peeled
  • 1 small onion
  • 1 large egg
  • 2 tbsp plain wheat flour
  • 1 tsp fine salt
  • Neutral oil, for frying
  • Sugar or sour cream, for serving

Steps

  1. Grate the potatoes and onion, squeeze lightly, and mix with the egg, flour, and salt.
  2. Heat a shallow layer of oil over medium-high heat.
  3. Fry thin rounds until both sides are brown and the potato is cooked.
  4. Serve at once with sugar or sour cream.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Sugar only, which keeps the plate dairy-free
  • Latkes, the Ashkenazi Hanukkah page

Pairings

  • Sugar
  • Sour cream
  • A mushroom or meat stew, as a separate choice
