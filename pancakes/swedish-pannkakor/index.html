Arriving in Sweden

Swedish pannkakor

You come in by Boat. Sweden · Northern Europe.

Pronounced PAHN-kah-kor

Thin Swedish pancakes, pan-cooked, served with lingonberry jam.

15 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 4 · 10 pancakes

History

Swedish pannkakor are thin pancakes, cooked in a pan or, in another method, baked in the oven. This page is the pan cake. Thursday yellow pea soup followed by pancakes is a living Swedish habit. A common explanation ties that Thursday plate to a Friday fast. Treat the explanation as common, not settled. The thin pancake also traveled with Swedish immigrants to the American Upper Midwest, where it has a separate journal page. This recipe stays in Sweden. Whipped cream and lingonberry jam are the usual finish.

Cultural background

Pannkakor are home food and school-lunch food. They are dessert after soup more often than they are a tall breakfast stack. Plättar, the tiny pan cakes, are the next page.

Why it tastes this way

A thin egg batter browns in butter and tastes of milk and a little salt. Lingonberry jam is tart, which keeps the butter from feeling heavy. Sugar in the batter is modest. The cake should bend. A thick, dry pancake is a different recipe.

How it is made

Flour, egg, milk, salt, and a little sugar are whisked thin and cooked as crepes in butter.

Authentic touch

Serve lingonberry jam, or another tart jam if you cannot find lingonberries. Do not turn this into the Upper Midwest page. If you bake the batter in a dish, you have started the oven variation, not the pan cake written here.

How it is used

Rolled or folded, with jam and sometimes whipped cream, often after pea soup on Thursdays.

Field notes

  • Thursday pea soup and pancakes are a Swedish habit. The fast-day explanation is not settled.
  • The Upper Midwest Swedish pancake is a different page. This one stays in Sweden.
  • Plättar are the tiny ones, cooked in a special pan.

Ingredients

  • 150 g plain wheat flour
  • 1 tbsp sugar
  • 1/2 tsp fine salt
  • 3 large eggs
  • 500 ml whole milk
  • 30 g unsalted butter, for the pan
  • Lingonberry jam, for serving

Steps

  1. Whisk the flour, sugar, and salt. Beat in the eggs and milk until the batter is thin and smooth. Rest 15 minutes.
  2. Heat a pan and grease it with butter.
  3. Cook thin pancakes until spotted brown on both sides.
  4. Serve with lingonberry jam, rolled or folded.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Ugnspannkaka, the oven pancake, often with pork, which is a different method and a meat dish
  • A savory fold of cheese

Pairings

  • Lingonberry jam
  • Whipped cream
  • Yellow pea soup, if this is the Thursday plate
