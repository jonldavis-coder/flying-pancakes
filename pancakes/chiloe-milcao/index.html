Pronounced mil-KAH-oh

A dense potato cake from Chiloé, boiled, baked, or pan-cooked. This one is pan-cooked.

History

Milcao is a potato bread of Chiloé in southern Chile, made from a mix of raw grated potato and cooked mashed potato, similar in idea to Irish boxty. It can be boiled, baked, or cooked in a pan. Chiloé’s potato diversity is famous. This recipe is the pan cake and does not claim to cover every island method.

Cultural background

It is a bread beside curanto, the island’s pit cook, and beside everyday meals. The flavor is potato, not wheat. Some milcaos are stuffed. This one is plain.

Why it tastes this way

Raw potato brings starch that binds, and cooked potato brings a soft, earthy flavor, the same logic as boxty in a completely different potato culture. Chiloé’s dozens of potato varieties are why a potato bread, not a wheat loaf, is the local starch. A pan toasts the outside. The inside stays dense. It is a bread.

How it is made

Grated raw potato and mashed potato are mixed, shaped, and cooked in a skillet.

Authentic touch

Squeeze the raw potato very dry and add the settled starch back. If the dough will not hold, it is too wet. Cook it slowly so the raw starch turns translucent.

How it is used

Warm, as a bread.

Field notes

  • Milcao mixes raw and cooked potato, a Chilote bread.
  • This page is the pan-cooked cake, not every island method.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups grated raw potato, squeezed dry
  • 1 cup mashed potato
  • 1 teaspoon salt

Steps

  1. Mix the squeezed raw potato, its settled starch, the mash, and the salt.
  2. Shape thick cakes.
  3. Cook in a lightly oiled skillet over medium-low, covered, turning once, until the raw starch is cooked and the outside is browned.
  4. Serve warm.
  5. If they will not hold, the raw potato was too wet.
  6. They are dense on purpose.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A sweet cooked version exists in some households.
  • Boxty is the Irish parallel, not the source.
