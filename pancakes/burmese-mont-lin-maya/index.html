Mont lin maya

You come in by Plane. Myanmar · Southeast Asia.

Pronounced mont lin mah-YAH

A pair of small rice cakes from a dimpled pan, one plain and one topped with split peas and shallot, joined together.

20 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 joined pairs

History

Mont lin maya are Myanmar's small batter cakes cooked in a dimpled pan and sold as snacks. The name means husband and wife and refers to the pair: one plain cake and one with a topping, cooked in neighboring cups and then pressed together. Yangon street stalls turn them out through the day. The batter is rice flour. This recipe tops the second cake with boiled split peas and shallot, with no egg and no wheat. A quail egg is another common topping and would add an egg allergen.

Cultural background

The pan is the same idea as other Southeast Asian dimpled pans, with a Burmese batter and a paired serving. The cakes are finger food. A little chile in the pea topping keeps the heat mild. They are a snack, not a feast loaf.

Why it tastes this way

Rice batter fried in a little oil tastes lightly sweet and toasted at the rim. Split peas add an earthy, mealy contrast. Shallot and a green chile make the topping savory. Pressing the plain cake onto the topped one traps that filling and softens the crust just enough. Coconut milk in the batter, used here in a small amount, rounds the grain. The pair is the dish, which is what the name is describing.

How it is made

Cook rice batter in a dimpled pan, leave half the cups plain, top the other half with peas and shallot, and join each pair.

Authentic touch

Cook a plain cup and a topped cup at the same time, then clap them together while they are still flexible. Two plain cakes miss the point of the name.

How it is used

Eat them warm as a street snack. They are savory and mildly hot from the chile in the peas.

Field notes

  • The name mont lin maya refers to the pair of cakes, one plain and one topped, joined together.
  • They are cooked in a dimpled pan and sold as snacks in Myanmar.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup rice flour, with no wheat added
  • 1 cup water
  • 1/2 cup coconut milk
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 cup cooked yellow split peas
  • 1 shallot, minced
  • 1 green chile, minced
  • Neutral oil

Steps

  1. Whisk rice flour, water, coconut milk, and salt into a pourable batter.
  2. Mix the split peas with shallot and green chile.
  3. Oil a hot dimpled pan. Fill the cups halfway.
  4. Leave half the cups plain. Spoon peas onto the other half. Cook until the bottoms are brown and the centers are set.
  5. Press a plain cake onto a topped cake to make a pair. Repeat with the rest.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A quail egg on the topped half, which adds egg
  • A pinch of turmeric in the batter for color

Pairings

  • A cup of tea
  • A squeeze of lime
  • More fried shallot
