History

Surinamese roti is an Indo-Caribbean flatbread, part of the same history of Indian indenture that shaped Guyanese and Trinidadian roti. It is cooked on a tawa and used to eat curry and long beans. A split-pea filling, like dhalpuri, is one style. This recipe is the plain layered roti, so the pea-filled Guyanese page stays distinct.

Cultural background

It should be soft and lightly blistered. Curry is the partner. The bread is the utensil.

Why it tastes this way

A soft wheat dough and a hot tawa make a bread that folds around a wet curry without cracking. Oil in the dough keeps it pliable. The flavor is mild because the curry is spicy. Suriname’s roti shops are a real everyday food, not a rare festival bread. The Indian technique and the Caribbean life are both the story.

How it is made

A soft dough is rolled and cooked on a hot tawa with a little oil.

Authentic touch

Rest the dough so it rolls without springing back. Cook it until it puffs in spots, then keep it wrapped.

How it is used

With curry.

Field notes

  • Surinamese roti belongs to Indo-Caribbean cooking.
  • This page is the plain bread. The pea-filled roti is the Guyana page.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups flour
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 3/4 cup water
  • 2 tablespoons oil

Steps

  1. Mix a soft dough and rest 20 minutes.
  2. Divide and roll into rounds.
  3. Cook on a hot tawa with a little oil until spotted and puffed in places.
  4. Wrap in a towel.
  5. Serve with curry.
  6. Keep them soft.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A split-pea filling makes it closer to Guyanese dhalpuri.
  • Clapping it into flakes makes it closer to buss-up-shut.

Pairings
