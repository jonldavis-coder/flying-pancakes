Arriving in Bucaramanga

Arepa santandereana

You come in by Train. Colombia · South America.

Pronounced ah-REH-pah sahn-tahn-deh-reh-AH-nah

A thick yellow-corn cake, griddled, served with crisp pork chicharrón.

20 min prep · 50 min cook · Serves 4 · 4 arepas and chicharrón

History

In Santander the arepa is often yellow corn, thicker than the white arepa of Antioquia, and it is eaten with chicharrón. The pork is part of this plate, so the recipe contains meat and is not vegetarian. Yellow corn dough here is cooked corn that has been ground, or yellow precooked cornmeal at home. It is not nixtamalized masa and not a fresh-corn choclo cake. The corn cake as a form is older than wheat hotcakes in the region. Serving it with fried pork belly is a Santander breakfast, not a stuffed coastal egg arepa.

Cultural background

Bucaramanga breakfasts pair the yellow arepa with chicharrón, sometimes with hogao or avocado. This recipe keeps the cake plain and puts the pork on the same plate, because that is how the dish is recognized. The arepa can be eaten alone, but then you are no longer cooking the plate this page describes.

Why it tastes this way

Yellow corn tastes deeper and a little sweeter than white precooked meal. A thick cake stays moist inside and toasts outside. Chicharrón is salt, rendered fat, and a crackling crust, which is why the mild corn can stand next to it without salsa. There is no chile in the written recipe. The richness is pork fat, not cheese.

How it is made

Form thick cakes from yellow precooked cornmeal and salt water and griddle them. Simmer pork belly, then fry it until crisp, and serve the two together.

Authentic touch

Simmer the pork belly until it is tender, dry it, then fry it in its own fat until the skin blisters and the meat is cooked through. Griddle the arepas separately so they do not sit in that fat. Season the dough only with salt.

How it is used

Serve one or two arepas with a piece of chicharrón for breakfast. Avocado on the side is common. The pork is still part of the plate.

Field notes

  • Santander breakfast arepas are yellow corn and are often eaten with chicharrón.
  • The pork in this recipe means the plate is not vegetarian.
  • Yellow precooked cornmeal stands in for cooked, ground yellow corn. It is not masa harina.

Ingredients

  • 250 g yellow precooked cornmeal
  • 340 ml warm water
  • 1 teaspoon salt, plus more for the pork
  • 400 g skin-on pork belly, in four pieces

Steps

  1. Cover the pork belly with water, salt it, and simmer until the meat is tender, about 30 minutes. Drain and pat dry.
  2. Stir the cornmeal, 1 teaspoon salt, and warm water into a soft dough. Rest 5 minutes. Form 4 thick cakes and smooth the cracks.
  3. Griddle the arepas over medium heat until both sides are crusty and the center is cooked, about 7 minutes a side.
  4. Fry the dried pork in a skillet over medium heat, turning, until the skin is crisp and the meat is cooked through. Pour off excess fat as it renders.
  5. Serve the arepas with the chicharrón.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Skip the pork and the plate is a plain yellow arepa, which can be vegetarian. This written recipe includes the chicharrón.
  • A little cheese on the arepa adds dairy.
  • White precooked cornmeal changes the color Santander cooks expect from this cake.

Pairings

  • Sliced avocado
  • Hogao, if you want a sauce
  • Small black coffee
