Irish oatmeal pancakes

You come in by Train. Ireland · Western Europe.

Pronounced Irish OAT-meal pancakes

Soft oatmeal pancakes with buttermilk, a breakfast cake from Irish kitchens.

20 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · 10 pancakes

History

Oatmeal pancakes in Ireland use the porridge grain in a softer, egg-and-buttermilk batter. They are not Scottish oatcakes, which are thin and dry, and they are not Staffordshire's yeasted shop cakes. Soaking the oatmeal first keeps the texture from turning gritty. Wheat flour in this batter means the cakes are not gluten-free.

Cultural background

They are a home breakfast. Oats were everyday food, and a pancake was one way to serve them with butter and honey or jam. Honey, if you add it, is its own allergen.

Why it tastes this way

Soaked oats stay tender and taste toasted once the pan browns them. Buttermilk gives a quiet tang. The cakes are thicker than a crepe and less fluffy than a baking-powder drop scone. Butter on top is the richness. Sugar is not required in the batter.

How it is made

Oatmeal soaks in buttermilk, then flour, an egg, salt, and a pinch of baking soda are stirred in. The batter is cooked as small thick pancakes.

Authentic touch

Soak the oatmeal in the buttermilk for at least 15 minutes. Skipping the soak leaves hard flecks in the crumb.

How it is used

At breakfast, with butter and jam. They are a morning cake.

Field notes

  • The oatmeal is soaked so the pancake is not gritty.
  • This is a soft breakfast pancake, not a crisp Scottish oatcake.
  • Wheat flour in the batter means it is not gluten-free.

Ingredients

  • 100 g medium oatmeal
  • 250 ml buttermilk
  • 80 g plain wheat flour
  • 1/2 tsp baking soda
  • 1 pinch fine salt
  • 1 large egg
  • Butter, for the pan and for serving

Steps

  1. Stir the oatmeal into the buttermilk and leave it for 15 minutes.
  2. Whisk in the flour, baking soda, salt, and egg.
  3. Cook spoonfuls in a buttered pan over medium heat until the bubbles set, then turn and finish.
  4. Serve warm with butter and jam.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A handful of raisins
  • All oatmeal and no wheat flour, which is a different cake and would need checking before you call it gluten-free

Pairings

  • Butter
  • Jam
  • Honey, which adds a honey allergen
