Arriving in Cádiz

Tortillitas de camarones

You come in by Boat. Spain · Southern Europe.

Pronounced tor-tee-YEE-tahs deh kah-mah-ROH-nes

Crisp Cádiz fritters of tiny shrimp and chickpea flour.

20 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · 12 fritters

History

Tortillitas de camarones are shrimp fritters from the Bay of Cádiz, especially Cádiz and San Fernando. The batter is chickpea flour and water, crowded with tiny shrimp, parsley, and onion, fried crisp in olive oil. They are bar food and festival food. There is no wheat in this recipe, so it is gluten-free if the chickpea flour was not milled with wheat. The shrimp make it a shellfish dish, not vegetarian. They are not a French socca and not a potato tortilla.

Cultural background

Camarones are the tiny local shrimp. If you cannot buy them that small, chop the smallest shrimp you can find and say that is a substitute. The fritter should be lacy, not a dense fish cake.

Why it tastes this way

Chickpea flour fries nutty and sweet. Shrimp taste briny and sweet. Parsley and onion keep it fresh. Olive oil in a hot pan makes the edge crisp and lacy. A thick, pale fritter means the oil was too cool or the batter too heavy. Salt and a squeeze of lemon are the finish. There is no chile on this page.

How it is made

Chickpea flour, water, onion, parsley, and chopped shrimp rest briefly, then spoonfuls are fried in hot olive oil until crisp.

Authentic touch

Use chickpea flour, not wheat. Fry thin and hot so the fritter laces. Tiny shrimp are the point. A wheat batter is a different tapa.

How it is used

Hot, as a bar snack or a first plate, with lemon. They are street and table food.

Field notes

  • These are Cádiz shrimp fritters. The flour is chickpea.
  • They are a shellfish dish.
  • They are not the potato omelet called a Spanish tortilla.

Ingredients

  • 150 g chickpea flour
  • 250 ml cold water
  • 1/2 tsp fine salt
  • 1 small onion, very finely chopped
  • 2 tbsp chopped parsley
  • 200 g tiny shrimp, or small shrimp chopped fine
  • Olive oil, for frying
  • Lemon wedges, for serving

Steps

  1. Whisk the chickpea flour, water, and salt. Stir in the onion, parsley, and shrimp. Rest 15 minutes. The batter should be thick but spoonable.
  2. Heat a shallow layer of olive oil until a drop of batter sizzles at once.
  3. Fry spoonfuls, spreading them thin, until the edges are brown and crisp. Turn once.
  4. Drain and serve with lemon.

The shrimp must be fully cooked and the oil hot. Do not leave the pan. If you have a shellfish allergy, skip this recipe.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A pinch of hot paprika, which would raise the heat
  • Socca, the Nice chickpea flatbread, which has no shrimp

Pairings

  • Lemon wedges
  • A cold drink
  • Nothing creamy on top
