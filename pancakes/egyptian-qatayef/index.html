Arriving in Cairo

Egyptian qatayef

You come in by Plane. Egypt · North Africa.

Pronounced KA-tah-yef

Yeasted pancakes stuffed with cinnamon walnuts and soaked in a lemon-rose syrup.

35 min prep · 40 min cook · Serves 8 · About 16 stuffed pancakes

History

Qatayef are the pancakes of Ramadan evenings in Egypt, and they are also a feast sweet in Lebanon, Palestine, and Syria. A yeasted batter is cooked on one side, filled, pinched shut, and fried or baked before a bath of syrup. Walnuts are a classic filling. Cheese is another. The smallest pancakes, asafiri, are left unstuffed and dipped in syrup. The name asafiri refers to little birds. This recipe is the walnut one.

Cultural background

During Ramadan, qatayef move from bakery windows to night streets. At home, one person pours, another fills and pinches. The syrup is cooked first so it can cool. The pancakes must be stuffed while the pale side is still flexible, or the seam splits.

Why it tastes this way

The batter itself is only lightly sweet. The flavor arrives twice: cinnamon and walnut inside, and a lemon-rose syrup outside. Frying toasts the pinched edge. Because the pancake was cooked on one side first, the inside stays soft enough to hold the filling instead of cracking like a cracker.

How it is made

Cook a sugar syrup and cool it. Blend a thin yeast batter and rest it until foamy. Pour small rounds and cook them on one side only. Fill each with a cinnamon walnut mixture, pinch it into a half-moon, and fry until golden. Dip the hot pastries in the cool syrup.

Authentic touch

Cook the pancakes on one side until bubbles cover the top, and fill them before they cool and turn brittle. Walnuts make this batch a tree-nut dish. It is not nut-free. Asafiri, the little unstuffed ones, are a different shape of the same batter.

How it is used

Qatayef are a feast dessert, especially on Ramadan nights, and they are street food in that season. They are not a breakfast stack. Cheese-filled and asafiri versions sit beside this walnut one in the same bakeries.

Field notes

  • Qatayef are the pancakes most associated with Ramadan evenings.
  • Asafiri are the small unstuffed ones. The name refers to little birds.
  • This walnut filling contains tree nuts, so the recipe is not nut-free.

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 cups wheat flour (about 190 g)
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • 1 teaspoon instant yeast
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/4 teaspoon fine salt
  • 1 1/2 cups warm water
  • 1 cup finely chopped walnuts
  • 2 tablespoons sugar, for the filling
  • 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 1 cup sugar, for the syrup
  • 1/2 cup water, for the syrup
  • 1 teaspoon lemon juice
  • 1 teaspoon rose water
  • Oil for shallow frying

Steps

  1. Simmer the syrup sugar and syrup water for 8 minutes, then add the lemon juice and rose water. Cool the syrup completely.
  2. Whisk the flour, the tablespoon of sugar, yeast, baking powder, salt, and warm water into a thin batter. Rest it for 20 minutes, until foamy.
  3. Cook small rounds on a lightly oiled nonstick pan, one side only, until the top is bubbly and set. Do not flip. Cover the pancakes with a towel so they stay flexible.
  4. Mix the walnuts, filling sugar, and cinnamon. Place a spoon on the pale side of each pancake and pinch the edges into a half-moon. This filling contains tree nuts.
  5. Fry the pinched pancakes in shallow oil until golden, drain them, and dip them in the cool syrup while they are hot.

Walnuts are a tree nut. The syrup is very hot while it cooks, and the frying oil is hot. This recipe is a feast sweet, not a nut-free food.

Keep syrup-soaked qatayef covered in the refrigerator and eat them within a couple of days. They soften as they sit. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A white-cheese filling is the dairy version and is not vegan.
  • Asafiri are left small and unstuffed, then dipped in syrup.
  • Baking the pinched pancakes instead of frying dries them a little and uses less oil.

Pairings

  • Unsweetened tea
  • A smaller extra dip of the same syrup
  • Chopped pistachios, which are also a tree nut
