Spanish tortilla de patatas

You come in by Train. Spain · Southern Europe.

Pronounced tor-TEE-yah deh pah-TAH-tahs

A potato omelet. It is not a pancake. This page says so and still cooks the omelet.

15 min prep · 40 min cook · Serves 8 · 1 omelet, 8 wedges

History

Spanish tortilla de patatas is a potato omelet, cooked in olive oil and served in wedges. It is not a pancake. It is on this journal because the word tortilla is constantly confused with tortillas and with tortitas, and because the dish is a real Spanish egg cake. Whether it should contain onion is a genuine disagreement. This recipe uses onion. Leaving the onion out is the other defended style, not a failed tortilla. There is no flour and no dairy.

Cultural background

Tortilla is tapas, picnic food, and a meal. It is cooked slowly so the potato becomes soft in olive oil, then set with egg. The center may be just firm or slightly damp. Both are served. This recipe sets the center.

Why it tastes this way

Potato that has stewed in olive oil tastes sweet and rich, not fried like a chip. Egg binds it into a custard. Onion, when you use it, adds sweetness. Salt is essential. The outside is only lightly browned. It does not taste like a crepe, because there is no milk batter and no browned flour.

How it is made

Potatoes and onion are simmered in plenty of olive oil until tender, drained, folded into beaten eggs, and set in a frying pan into a round omelet.

Authentic touch

Say, before you cook, that this is an omelet. Use plenty of olive oil and cook the potatoes gently. Onion is included. The version without onion is a real tortilla, not a correction of this one.

How it is used

Warm or at room temperature, in wedges, as a tapa or a meal. It is not a breakfast pancake stack.

Field notes

  • Tortilla de patatas is an omelet, not a pancake.
  • Onion or no onion is a real disagreement. This recipe uses onion.
  • There is no flour in the omelet.

Ingredients

  • 800 g waxy potatoes, peeled and sliced thinly
  • 1 onion, thinly sliced
  • 300 ml olive oil, for cooking the potatoes
  • 6 large eggs
  • 1 tsp fine salt

Steps

  1. Put the potatoes and onion in a pan, cover with the olive oil, and cook gently until the potatoes are tender and not browned. This is a confit, not a fry. Drain them and save the oil.
  2. Beat the eggs with the salt. Fold in the warm potatoes and onion. Rest 5 minutes.
  3. Heat 2 tablespoons of the saved oil in a nonstick pan. Add the egg mixture and cook over medium-low heat until the bottom is set and the top is almost set.
  4. Invert onto a plate, slide it back, and cook until the center is set. Rest 5 minutes and cut in wedges.

The potatoes cook in a deep layer of oil. Stay with the pan. Strain leftover oil only if it is not burnt, and do not pour hot oil down a drain.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • No onion, the other defended style
  • A few roasted red peppers folded in

Pairings

  • Bread, which adds wheat if you eat it
  • A green salad
  • Aioli, which adds garlic and more egg
