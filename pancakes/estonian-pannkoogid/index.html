Pronounced PAHN-koh-git

Thin pancakes, rolled with jam or served with savory fillings.

10 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · about 8 pancakes

History

Pannkoogid are Estonian pancakes, thin and crepe-like, influenced by the same Baltic and Nordic habits as Finland’s lettu and Russia’s blini, without being either. Vastlapäev, Estonia’s Shrove Tuesday, is associated with pea soup and whipped-cream buns more than with these pancakes. This page does not claim pannkoogid as the Vastlapäev dish. They are ordinary dessert and supper crepes.

Cultural background

Jam, condensed milk in some modern homes, or a savory minced filling are ways to eat them. The recipe is the plain cake with jam.

Why it tastes this way

A thin egg batter tastes mild and buttery, a base for jam in a kitchen that also bakes cardamom rolls. Estonia’s place between Nordic and Russian food shows up in the pancake’s thinness, closer to a Nordic crepe than to a thick yeasted blin. The holiday honesty matters: Vastlapäev has its own buns, and this cake is not those buns.

How it is made

A thin batter is cooked as crepes and rolled with jam.

Authentic touch

Cook them thin and roll with a modest amount of jam. Condensed milk is a modern sweetness and much heavier.

How it is used

Warm, with jam.

Field notes

  • Vastlapäev is more strongly tied to pea soup and cream buns than to these pancakes.
  • Pannkoogid are still the Estonian crepe for ordinary days.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup flour
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 1/4 cups milk
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1 tablespoon melted butter
  • Jam, to serve

Steps

  1. Whisk the batter.
  2. Cook thin pancakes in a buttered pan.
  3. Roll with jam.
  4. Serve warm.
  5. Flip only long enough to set the second side.
  6. Keep them pliable.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A savory meat filling makes them a meal.
  • Finnish lettu is the close thin pancake across the gulf.

Pairings

  • jam
  • sour cream
