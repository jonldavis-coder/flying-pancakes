Irish potato farls

You come in by Train. Ireland · Western Europe.

Pronounced potato farls

Mashed-potato farls, cut in quarters and cooked on a griddle.

15 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 farls

  • No chili heat

History

Potato farls are a mashed-potato bread cooked on a griddle and cut into quarters. Farl means a fourth. They are an Ulster breakfast bread eaten in Ireland, which is why this page is pinned to Ireland. The wheat soda farl, a different dough, is on the Ulster soda farl page under the United Kingdom. Potato farls use mash, flour, and fat. They do not use the raw potato that defines pan boxty.

Cultural background

They are weekend breakfast food beside eggs, bacon, or soda bread. The dough should be soft and the cakes thin enough to cook through on a dry griddle.

Why it tastes this way

Mashed potato makes a mild, stretchy crumb once it meets a hot griddle. Flour keeps the shape. Butter in the dough tastes nutty when the surface toasts. They are saltier and drier than a fried potato cake, because the pan is only lightly greased.

How it is made

Cold mash is mixed with butter, salt, and flour, pressed into rounds, quartered, and cooked on a hot griddle until spotted brown.

Authentic touch

Cut the round into four before you cook it. Do not grate in raw potato. If you do, you have started a boxty, and that recipe is the other page.

How it is used

At breakfast, with butter, eggs, or a fry. The farls themselves are vegetarian.

Field notes

  • A farl is a quarter of the round.
  • This cake is mashed potato. Boxty also uses raw potato.
  • The pin is Ireland. Ulster soda farls are the wheat cake on the United Kingdom page.

Ingredients

  • 500 g cold mashed potato
  • 40 g unsalted butter
  • 1 tsp fine salt
  • 100 g plain wheat flour, plus more for pressing

Steps

  1. Beat the mash with the butter and salt. Work in the flour until you have a soft dough that does not stick.
  2. Divide in two. Press each half into a round about 1 cm thick on a floured board and cut into quarters.
  3. Cook on a hot dry griddle until brown spots show, then turn and cook the other side.
  4. Serve warm with butter.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A spoon of chopped spring onion
  • Tattie scones, the Scottish thin triangle of the same family

Pairings

  • Butter
  • Eggs
  • Bacon or sausage on a meat plate
