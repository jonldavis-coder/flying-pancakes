Arriving in Xi'an

Xianbing

You come in by Train. China · East Asia.

Pronounced shyen-BING

A round northern wheat cake sealed around a juicy pork and scallion filling, pan-fried until spotted.

30 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 stuffed cakes

History

Xianbing are stuffed savory pancakes of northern China. A wheat dough, often made with hot water, is filled with minced pork, scallion, ginger, and seasoning, then pleated shut and pan-fried or griddled. Xi'an is one city where stuffed breads are everyday food. The same family of cakes shows up across the north under local habits. This page calls the cake what it is: a stuffed griddled pancake, not a rolled scallion bread and not an open-faced crepe.

Cultural background

The filling should be juicy and the dough strong enough to hold it. A tear in the pan spills meat and burns. Street cooks press the rounds gently so they cook through without bursting. Beef and lamb fillings exist. This recipe writes pork.

Why it tastes this way

Pork and scallion are the northern savory pair: fat, salt, and a sharp green bite. Ginger and soy sauce season the juice that collects inside the cake. The hot-water dough stays soft and a little chewy, then the pan spots it with toast. You taste the filling first and the crust second. Mild heat comes from ginger and white pepper, not from a chile paste.

How it is made

Fill hot-water dough with seasoned pork and scallion, seal the rounds, and pan-fry them covered until the meat cooks through.

Authentic touch

Pleat and pinch the seam firmly, and cook on medium with a lid for part of the time. An open high flame browns the dough and leaves the pork underdone.

How it is used

Eat them hot, with vinegar or garlic water if you want a dip. They are street food and a savory snack.

Field notes

  • Xianbing are northern stuffed savory pancakes, sealed and then griddled.
  • The pork stays inside the dough. It is not a topping.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups all-purpose wheat flour
  • 3/4 cup hot water
  • 200 grams ground pork
  • 3 scallions, chopped
  • 1 tablespoon soy sauce
  • 1 teaspoon grated ginger
  • 1/4 teaspoon white pepper
  • Neutral oil
  • Salt

Steps

  1. Mix the flour with hot water and a pinch of salt into a dough. Knead until smooth and rest 20 minutes.
  2. Mix the pork with scallion, soy sauce, ginger, white pepper, and a teaspoon of oil.
  3. Divide the dough into eight. Flatten each piece, add filling, and pleat it shut. Press gently into a thick round.
  4. Pan-fry in a filmed pan over medium heat, covered, until the underside is spotted and the pork is cooked through. Flip and finish the other side.
  5. Rest two minutes so the juice settles, then serve.

Cook until the pork in the center is no longer pink.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Beef in place of pork, still a meat filling
  • A vegetarian filling of egg and chive, which would drop the meat tag and add egg

Pairings

  • Black vinegar
  • A smashed cucumber salad
  • Hot millet porridge
