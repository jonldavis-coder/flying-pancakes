Arriving in La Ceiba

Honduran baleadas

You come in by Plane. Honduras · Mexico & Central America.

Pronounced bah-leh-AH-dahs

A soft griddled wheat tortilla folded over refried beans and crumbled cheese.

35 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 6 · 6 baleadas

History

Baleadas are a north-coast Honduran meal, especially around La Ceiba and San Pedro Sula. A large wheat tortilla is griddled, spread with refried beans and cheese, and folded. The tortilla is the bread, cooked on a comal, not fried. Corn tortillas are older in the region than this wheat wrap, and older than wheat hotcakes. The baleada became the everyday breakfast and late-night plate of the coast. This recipe keeps it to beans and cheese.

Cultural background

Stands sell baleadas simples, just beans and cheese, and baleadas especiales with egg, avocado, or crema added. The simple one is the recipe here. It is breakfast, lunch, and a snack after a dance, depending on the hour.

Why it tastes this way

The wheat tortilla tastes soft and slightly toasted, with enough fat in the dough to bend without cracking. Refried red beans are salty and earthy. Fresh cheese crumbles rather than stretching, so the bite stays milky and dry against the beans. There is no chile in the written filling, which is why a plain baleada is gentle. Hot sauce is a choice at the table.

How it is made

Make a soft wheat dough with oil, rest it, and griddle large thin rounds. Mash beans with onion, season them, and fold each tortilla over beans and crumbled cheese.

Authentic touch

Griddle the tortilla until it puffs in spots and is still flexible. A crisp tortilla will not fold. Spread the beans while the bread is hot so the cheese softens. Fold once, like a taco, not a tight roll.

How it is used

Eat them hot, folded, for breakfast or a street meal. Two make a plate. Avocado on top is common and does not change the allergens.

Field notes

  • The tortilla for a baleada is griddled wheat bread, then folded. It is not a corn pupusa.
  • The simple street version is beans and cheese. Eggs and meat are additions.
  • Corn cakes in this region are older than the wheat tortilla used here.

Ingredients

  • 250 g wheat flour, plus more for rolling
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt, plus more for the beans
  • 2 tablespoons neutral oil
  • 120 ml warm water
  • 300 g cooked red beans
  • 1 small onion, chopped
  • 100 g crumbled fresh white cheese

Steps

  1. Mix the flour, 1/2 teaspoon salt, oil, and warm water into a soft dough. Cover and rest 20 minutes.
  2. Fry the onion in a spoon of oil, add the beans, and mash them with a little bean broth until spreadable. Salt to taste.
  3. Divide the dough into 6 pieces and roll thin rounds.
  4. Cook each round on a dry or lightly oiled comal until it blisters and browns in spots, under a minute a side. Keep them soft.
  5. Spread hot beans and cheese on one half and fold. Serve at once.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A baleada especial adds egg, avocado, and crema. The egg adds an allergen. The crema is more dairy.
  • A spoon of hot sauce raises the heat above none.
  • Lard in the tortilla dough adds meat and is not vegetarian. This dough uses oil.

Pairings

  • Honduran sour cream, which is still dairy
  • Sliced avocado
  • A fried egg, which adds the egg allergen
