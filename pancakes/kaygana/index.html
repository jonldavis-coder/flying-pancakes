Pronounced KAI-gah-nah

A Black Sea herb pancake, more egg than flour, soft and green.

10 min prep · 12 min cook · Serves 2 · one cake

History

Kaygana is a herb pancake or thick omelette of Turkey’s Black Sea coast, made with eggs and a handful of flour or cornmeal, and a large quantity of scallions, leeks, or herbs. It is a regional breakfast. This version uses a spoon of cornmeal so it can stay gluten-free, and it is still a pancake rather than a plain omelette.

Cultural background

It is cut into wedges. The herbs are the body. Cheese on top is common and adds dairy. The written cake has none.

Why it tastes this way

The Black Sea kitchen puts greens into eggs because the coast is wet and green. A little cornmeal sets the eggs into a cake you can cut. The flavor is scallion and egg, not a sweet batter. It is a regional dish, not a national crepe, which is why it has its own page.

How it is made

Eggs, cornmeal, and a mass of scallions are cooked as one thick pancake.

Authentic touch

Cook it covered on medium-low so the egg sets without burning. The herbs should wilt. Do not stir it into scramble.

How it is used

In wedges, warm.

Field notes

  • Kaygana is a Black Sea herb and egg pancake.
  • This version uses cornmeal so it contains no wheat.

Ingredients

  • 4 eggs
  • 2 tablespoons cornmeal
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 6 scallions, chopped
  • 1 tablespoon oil

Steps

  1. Beat the eggs with cornmeal and salt. Stir in the scallions.
  2. Heat oil in a skillet and pour in the batter.
  3. Cook covered over medium-low until set.
  4. Brown the top briefly if you like.
  5. Cut into wedges.
  6. Serve warm.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A handful of grated cheese on top adds dairy.
  • Wheat flour instead of cornmeal is common and is not gluten-free.
