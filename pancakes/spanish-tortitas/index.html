Small, thick Spanish pancakes, milder than a carnival crepe.

10 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · about 10 tortitas

History

Tortitas are the word many Spanish kitchens use for small thick pancakes, as opposed to filloas or the potato omelette called tortilla española. They are a breakfast or a children’s snack with sugar, honey, or chocolate. They are not the potato tortilla, which is an omelette and is not given a false pancake recipe here.

Cultural background

Baking powder makes them thick. They are closer to drop scones than to filloas. Chocolate a la taza is a fair partner and is a separate drink.

Why it tastes this way

A thicker batter tastes more of egg and sugar than a carnival crepe, a modern home breakfast rather than a Lenten custom. The word tortita is the clue. Tortilla, in Spain, usually means the potato omelette. Keeping the names apart avoids a real confusion.

How it is made

A thick sweetened batter is cooked as small pancakes.

Authentic touch

Drop them small and thick. Flip when the bubbles hold. They are not meant to be paper-thin.

How it is used

With honey, sugar, or jam.

Field notes

  • Tortitas are thick pancakes. Tortilla española is a potato omelette.
  • The names are kept apart on purpose.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup flour
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 2 tablespoons sugar
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1 egg
  • 3/4 cup milk
  • 1 tablespoon melted butter

Steps

  1. Mix a thick batter.
  2. Cook small thick rounds on a buttered griddle.
  3. Flip when the bubbles hold.
  4. Serve with honey or jam.
  5. They are not crepes.
  6. Keep the heat at medium.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Filloas are the thin carnival crepes.
  • Tortilla española is a potato omelette, not this recipe.

Pairings

  • honey
  • jam
