Spanish tortitas

You come in by Train. Spain · Southern Europe.

Pronounced tor-TEE-tahs

Small thick Spanish pancakes with lemon, a home breakfast, not a tortilla.

10 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · 12 small pancakes

  • No chili heat
  • Vegetarian

History

Tortitas in this sense are small thick pancakes cooked in homes across Spain, served with sugar, jam, or chocolate. They are not tortilla de patatas, which is an omelet and has its own page that says so. They are not Galician Carnival filloas and not the shrimp fritters of Cádiz. This is the everyday wheat pancake, raised with baking powder, scented with lemon.

Cultural background

They are a breakfast and a children's snack. The name tortita is also used for other small round sweets. On this page it means the poured pancake. A heavy pan and a spoon are the tools.

Why it tastes this way

Baking powder makes them softer and thicker than a filloa. Lemon zest keeps the sweetness bright. Butter browns the outside. Sugar on top melts into the crust. They taste like a small breakfast cake, not like olive oil and potato.

How it is made

A thick batter of flour, egg, milk, baking powder, and lemon is cooked as small rounds and sugared.

Authentic touch

Do not confuse the name with a Spanish tortilla. If someone asks for tortilla, they may want the omelet. These are tortitas, plural of the small pancake, served with sugar.

How it is used

At breakfast or as a snack, with sugar or jam.

Field notes

  • Tortitas here means small pancakes, not a potato omelet.
  • The omelet is the Spanish tortilla page.
  • Filloas and frixuelos are the thin Carnival crepes.

Ingredients

  • 150 g plain wheat flour
  • 1 tsp baking powder
  • 1 tbsp sugar, plus more for serving
  • 1 pinch fine salt
  • 1 large egg
  • 200 ml whole milk
  • 1 tsp lemon zest
  • 30 g unsalted butter, for the pan

Steps

  1. Whisk the flour, baking powder, sugar, and salt. Beat in the egg, milk, and lemon zest until just smooth.
  2. Cook spoonfuls in butter over medium heat until bubbles set, then turn.
  3. Cook until the centers spring back.
  4. Sprinkle with sugar and serve.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A banana slice on the batter
  • Filloas, the thin Carnival crepe, which are a different page

Pairings

  • Sugar
  • Jam
  • A square of chocolate, melted on the hot cake if you want it
