Arriving in Kaunas

Lithuanian bulviniai blynai

You come in by Train. Lithuania · Eastern Europe.

Pronounced bool-VIN-ay BLEE-nay

Lithuanian potato pancakes with kefir in the batter, fried and served with sour cream.

20 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 pancakes

History

Bulviniai blynai means potato pancakes in Lithuanian. They are grated potato cakes, a different dish from the Ashkenazi farmer-cheese latke pinned to Lithuania on that page. Kefir in the batter is the method here. A little wheat flour binds them, so they are not gluten-free. They are everyday food, served with sour cream or with bacon on a meat plate. Bacon is not in this recipe.

Cultural background

Potato pancakes are a home supper. The cheese latke is a holiday dairy cake with a separate history. Keeping the names apart matters, because both pages use Lithuania and they are not the same food.

Why it tastes this way

Kefir tangs the potato slightly and loosens the batter. Onion fries sweet at the edge. The crust is crisp and the center soft. Sour cream doubles the dairy note. They taste fresher and less purely starchy than a flour-only bind, because the kefir is sour.

How it is made

Grated potato and onion are mixed with kefir, egg, flour, and salt, then fried in oil.

Authentic touch

Use kefir, not sweet milk. Do not call these cheese latkes. Those are the Hanukkah page. Flour in this batter means it is not gluten-free.

How it is used

Hot, with sour cream, as a savory meal. Bacon on the side makes it a meat plate.

Field notes

  • Bulviniai blynai are potato pancakes. The cheese latke page is a different food.
  • Kefir is in this batter.
  • They are everyday food, not the Hanukkah cheese cake.

Ingredients

  • 600 g starchy potatoes, peeled
  • 1 small onion
  • 1 large egg
  • 80 ml kefir
  • 2 tbsp plain wheat flour
  • 1 tsp fine salt
  • Neutral oil, for frying
  • Sour cream, for serving

Steps

  1. Grate the potatoes and onion and squeeze lightly. Stir in the egg, kefir, flour, and salt.
  2. Heat a shallow layer of oil over medium-high heat.
  3. Fry rounds until both sides are brown and the potato is cooked through.
  4. Serve with sour cream.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • The farmer-cheese latke, a different cake and a different holiday story
  • No kefir, loosened with potato water only

Pairings

  • Sour cream
  • Fried bacon, for a meat plate
  • Dill
