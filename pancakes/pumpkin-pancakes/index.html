Arriving in Circleville

Pumpkin pancakes

You come in by Hot air balloon. United States · North America.

Soft pumpkin, cinnamon, and ginger in a lightly sweet wheat cake.

15 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 12 pancakes

History

Pumpkin pancakes are a seasonal American breakfast made with pumpkin puree and warm spices in a wheat batter. Canned puree made the cake an easy autumn diner special, and roasted pumpkin, mashed and drained, works the same way. The cake does not have a single town of origin. Circleville, Ohio, is the map pin because the town grows pumpkins, not because the recipe was invented there.

Cultural background

Pumpkin pie spice blends vary. This recipe uses cinnamon, ginger, and nutmeg so the amounts are clear. The puree is wet, so the batter needs less milk than a plain pancake.

Why it tastes this way

Pumpkin tastes sweet and vegetal, and the spices make it read as pie rather than as squash soup. Browned butter at the edge is the contrast. Too much puree and too little flour makes a gummy cake that never browns.

How it is made

Griddled wheat batter mixed with pumpkin puree and spices.

Authentic touch

Use plain pumpkin puree, not a can of sweetened pie filling. The sugar and spices are already in the recipe.

How it is used

Hot, with butter and maple syrup.

Field notes

  • Canned pumpkin puree turned this into a standard autumn diner special.
  • Circleville is a pumpkin-growing town, and the pin is there for the crop, not for an invention claim.

Ingredients

  • 3/4 cup plain pumpkin puree
  • 1 1/2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 2 tablespoons sugar
  • 2 teaspoons baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 1/2 teaspoon ground ginger
  • 1/4 teaspoon ground nutmeg
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 cup buttermilk
  • 1 large egg
  • 2 tablespoons unsalted butter, melted, plus more for the griddle

Steps

  1. Whisk the flour, sugar, baking powder, baking soda, cinnamon, ginger, nutmeg, and salt.
  2. Whisk the pumpkin, buttermilk, egg, and melted butter until smooth. Stir into the flour until just combined.
  3. Butter a griddle over medium-low heat. Pumpkin browns quickly.
  4. Pour 1/4 cup cakes. Flip when the underside is brown and the top looks set.
  5. Cook the second side until the center springs back.
  6. Serve with butter and maple syrup.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Roast a sugar pumpkin, mash it, and drain it in a sieve if you are not using canned puree.
  • A darker molasses in place of the sugar makes a heavier cake.

Pairings

  • Butter
  • Maple syrup
  • Toasted pecans only if a tree-nut topping is wanted
