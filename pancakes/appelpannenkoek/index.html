A plate-sized pancake with tender apple cooked into it.

15 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 2 · 2 large pancakes

History

An appelpannenkoek is a Dutch pancake with apple, either sliced into the batter as it cooks or sautéed and laid on the cake. It is a standard sweet pannenkoek, served as a meal or a hearty dessert. Cinnamon is common and not universal.

Cultural background

Apple varieties that hold their shape are the right fruit. The cake is large, like other pannenkoeken, and is eaten with syrup or sugar.

Why it tastes this way

Apple sweetens the cake and adds water, so the center stays soft while the edge crisps. Dutch cooking puts fruit in the pancake rather than only beside it, which is the difference from a plain pannenkoek with applesauce. A cool climate apple, tart rather than a dessert banana, keeps the richness in check.

How it is made

A pannenkoek batter is cooked with thin apple slices set into it, then flipped.

Authentic touch

Slice the apple thin and press the slices into the batter at the start of cooking. Thick chunks stay raw when the cake is brown.

How it is used

Powdered sugar or stroop, and a little cinnamon if you like.

Field notes

  • The apple goes in the cake, not only on the side.
  • Keep the slices thin so they cook with the batter.

Ingredients

  • 3/4 cup flour
  • 1 large egg
  • 3/4 cup milk
  • Pinch of salt
  • 1 apple, thinly sliced
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • Butter for the pan

Steps

  1. Whisk flour, egg, milk, and salt.
  2. Butter a skillet and lay in the apple slices. Sprinkle with sugar.
  3. Pour the batter over the apples and cook covered on medium until set.
  4. Flip and brown the apple side.
  5. The fruit should be tender.
  6. Serve with sugar or stroop.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Pear works the same way and should be called a pear pancake.
  • Raisins with the apple are a home addition.

Pairings

  • powdered sugar
  • stroop
  • cinnamon
