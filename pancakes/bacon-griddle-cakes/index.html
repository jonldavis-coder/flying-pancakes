Arriving in Madison

Bacon griddle cakes

You come in by Train. United States · North America.

Salty, smoky bacon in a lightly sweet wheat pancake, cooked in bacon fat.

20 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 4 · 12 cakes

History

Bacon griddle cakes cook chopped bacon into a wheat pancake so the breakfast meat and the cake come off the same griddle. The idea is a savory American morning plate. Nothing about it is older than the habit of serving bacon beside pancakes and then putting the bacon inside them. Madison is the map pin for a widespread plate, not a local invention.

Cultural background

The bacon is cooked first, and some of its fat greases the griddle. The batter is a plain buttermilk pancake. This recipe contains meat, so it is not vegetarian.

Why it tastes this way

Rendered bacon fat browns the cake and carries smoke and salt into the crumb. Pieces of bacon stay chewy if they were cooked crisp before they went in. Maple syrup at the table is the sweet against that salt. A batter that is very sweet fights the bacon.

How it is made

Buttermilk pancakes with chopped cooked bacon, griddled in bacon fat.

Authentic touch

Cook the bacon first and use a spoon of the fat on the griddle. Raw bacon stirred into batter will not crisp.

How it is used

Hot, with maple syrup if you want the salty-sweet plate.

Field notes

  • The bacon has to be cooked before it is added, or it stays flabby inside the cake.
  • This is a meat pancake. The diet list does not call it vegetarian.

Ingredients

  • 6 slices bacon
  • 1 1/2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • 2 teaspoons baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 1/2 cups buttermilk
  • 1 large egg
  • 1 tablespoon unsalted butter, melted

Steps

  1. Cook the bacon in a skillet until crisp. Drain it on a rack, reserve 2 tablespoons of the fat, and chop the bacon.
  2. Whisk the flour, sugar, baking powder, baking soda, and salt.
  3. Stir in the buttermilk, egg, and melted butter. Fold in the chopped bacon.
  4. Heat a griddle over medium heat and brush it with the reserved bacon fat.
  5. Pour 1/4 cup cakes. Flip when the tops set and the undersides are brown.
  6. Cook until the center is done. Serve hot, with maple syrup if you want it.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A fully savory cake leaves out the sugar.
  • Sausage in place of bacon is a different meat and still not vegetarian.

Pairings

  • Maple syrup
  • Fried eggs
  • Black coffee
