Arriving in Cape Cod

Cape Cod cranberry hotcakes

You come in by Boat. United States · North America.

Tart cranberry pieces in a lightly sweet, orange-scented wheat cake.

20 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 12 hotcakes

History

Cape Cod cranberry hotcakes put chopped cranberries in a wheat batter. Cranberries are native to the bogs of southeastern Massachusetts, and Wampanoag people used them long before commercial bogs. The hotcake is a later breakfast. Commercial cranberry agriculture expanded on the Cape in the nineteenth century.

Cultural background

Fresh cranberries are sharp. Chopping them keeps that tartness in pieces instead of staining every bite the same color. A little orange zest is a common local pairing with the berry.

Why it tastes this way

Cranberries are high in acid and pectin, so they stay bright against a sweet crumb instead of tasting like jam. Extra sugar in this batter is there to meet that acid. Orange zest smells floral and keeps the cake from tasting only sour.

How it is made

Griddled wheat batter studded with chopped cranberries.

Authentic touch

Chop fresh or frozen cranberries. Whole berries burst and leave you with raw, hard centers unless the cakes overcook.

How it is used

Hot, with butter. A little maple syrup is enough.

Field notes

  • Cranberries are native to southeastern Massachusetts bogs.
  • Wampanoag use of the berry is far older than the wheat hotcake.

Ingredients

  • 3/4 cup fresh or frozen cranberries, chopped
  • 1 1/2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 3 tablespoons sugar
  • 2 teaspoons baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 teaspoon finely grated orange zest
  • 1 1/2 cups buttermilk
  • 1 large egg
  • 2 tablespoons unsalted butter, melted, plus more for the griddle

Steps

  1. Chop the cranberries into small pieces. If frozen, chop them while frozen.
  2. Whisk the flour, sugar, baking powder, baking soda, salt, and orange zest.
  3. Stir in the buttermilk, egg, and melted butter. Fold in the cranberries.
  4. Butter a griddle over medium heat and pour 1/4 cup cakes.
  5. Flip when the underside is golden. Cook until no wet batter remains.
  6. Serve with butter and a light pour of maple syrup.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Dried cranberries, chopped and soaked 10 minutes, work when fresh berries are out of season.
  • Leave out the orange zest if you want the berry alone.

Pairings

  • Butter
  • Maple syrup
  • Hot tea
