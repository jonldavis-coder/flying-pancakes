Arriving in Santa Fe

New Mexico blue corn hotcakes

You come in by Hot air balloon. United States · North America.

Toasted blue corn in a tender buttermilk cake, nutty and a little sweet.

15 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 12 hotcakes

History

New Mexico blue corn hotcakes are a cafe and home breakfast that mixes blue cornmeal into a wheat batter. Blue corn is a Southwestern crop with a long history in Pueblo farming, while a thick hotcake with wheat flour, buttermilk, and egg is a later plate. It is not Hopi piki, which is a paper-thin sheet on a stone. Santa Fe is the map pin.

Cultural background

Blue cornmeal tastes toastier than yellow meal and colors the batter gray-blue or lavender. The wheat flour and buttermilk make a risen cake, which is why this page is not gluten-free and not a piki recipe.

Why it tastes this way

Blue corn has a deeper, toastier flavor than a standard yellow meal. Buttermilk softens that flavor and lifts the cake. Butter browns the surface. Syrup will cover the corn if you pour it on heavily, so the corn shows up best with a light hand.

How it is made

Griddled blue-cornmeal and wheat batter.

Authentic touch

Use blue cornmeal, not food coloring in a yellow-meal batter. Keep this cake distinct from piki: it is thick, wheat-based, and cooked like a pancake.

How it is used

Stacked, with butter and a light pour of syrup.

Field notes

  • Blue corn in New Mexico is an old Pueblo crop. The thick wheat hotcake is a later cafe breakfast.
  • Hopi piki is a different food: a paper-thin sheet, not this stack.

Ingredients

  • 3/4 cup blue cornmeal
  • 3/4 cup all-purpose flour
  • 2 tablespoons sugar
  • 2 teaspoons baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 1/2 cups buttermilk
  • 2 large eggs
  • 2 tablespoons unsalted butter, melted, plus more for the griddle

Steps

  1. Whisk the blue cornmeal, flour, sugar, baking powder, baking soda, and salt.
  2. Stir in the buttermilk, eggs, and melted butter until just combined. Rest 5 minutes.
  3. Butter a griddle over medium heat.
  4. Pour 1/4 cup cakes. They brown a little faster than all-wheat pancakes.
  5. Flip when the top is set and cook until the center springs back.
  6. Serve with butter and a light pour of syrup so the corn still shows.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A higher share of blue cornmeal makes a more fragile, stronger-tasting cake.
  • This batter cannot be made gluten-free by swapping in more cornmeal unless the wheat flour is removed and the recipe is rebuilt.

Pairings

  • Butter
  • Maple syrup or honey only if honey fits the table
  • Piñon only as an optional tree-nut topping
