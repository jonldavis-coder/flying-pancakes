Pronounced PAH-nen-koo-ken

A large, thin-but-sturdy pancake, often eaten with bacon or apple.

15 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 4 large pancakes

History

Pannenkoeken are the Dutch pancake: larger and a bit thicker than a French crêpe, cooked one to a plate, and eaten with sweet or savory toppings. Pannenkoeken restaurants serve them as a meal. Bacon cooked into the batter, or apple, or a syrup pour, are all ordinary. They are not the American Dutch baby, whose name comes from Deutsch, and they are not poffertjes.

Cultural background

A family pan cooks one large cake. You eat it with a knife and fork. Stroop, a syrup, or powdered sugar are sweet finishes. Cheese and bacon are the savory ones.

Why it tastes this way

A batter with more flour than a crêpe stays sturdy enough to carry bacon or apple and to be eaten as dinner. Dairy and pork on the same plate reflect a farming kitchen that had milk, eggs, and a slice of spek. The cake tastes mildly of egg and butter, and the topping does the rest. Plate-sized cooking is why the edge is crisper than the center.

How it is made

A pourable batter is cooked as one large pancake, with apple or bacon added in the pan if you are using them.

Authentic touch

Spread the batter to the edge of a wide pan and do not stack them if you can help it. A pannenkoek is one cake on a plate. Cover the pan so the center cooks before the edge dries out.

How it is used

With stroop or powdered sugar, or with cheese. Bacon makes it not vegetarian. The batter below is the plain cake.

Field notes

  • Dutch pannenkoeken are not Dutch babies. The American name points at German pancakes.
  • One large cake per person is the usual serving.

Ingredients

  • 1 1/4 cups all-purpose flour
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 1/2 cups milk
  • Pinch of salt
  • 1 tablespoon melted butter
  • Butter for the pan

Steps

  1. Whisk into a smooth, pourable batter. Rest 15 minutes.
  2. Heat a wide skillet and butter it.
  3. Pour a generous layer and tilt to cover.
  4. Cook covered on medium until the top sets, then flip.
  5. The center should be cooked, not wet.
  6. Serve flat on a plate with stroop, sugar, or cheese.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Spekdik is the bacon pancake of the northeast, on its own page.
  • Appelpannenkoek has its own page too.
