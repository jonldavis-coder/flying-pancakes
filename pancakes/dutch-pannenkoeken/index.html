Dutch pannenkoeken

You come in by Train. Netherlands · Western Europe.

Pronounced PAH-nuh-koo-kuh

Large, thin Dutch pancakes, plate-sized, with syrup, apple, or bacon.

10 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 4 large pancakes

History

A Dutch pannenkoek is a large thin pancake, often the size of the plate. Pannenkoeken restaurants cook them to order, sweet or savory. Stroop, a dark Dutch syrup, and powdered sugar are the usual sweet finish. Bacon and cheese are the usual savory ones. This page writes a plain batter and a syrup finish, and names bacon as a variation so the base recipe stays vegetarian. Poffertjes, the small yeast cakes, are a different pan and a different page.

Cultural background

They are family restaurant food and home food. Children and adults share the same batter. The pancake is dinner as often as it is dessert, which is why both uses are tagged.

Why it tastes this way

The batter is a little thicker than a French crepe, so the crumb is soft with a lacier edge. Butter browns it. Syrup adds bitterness and molasses-like depth that maple syrup does not quite copy. A pinch of salt in a sweet pancake keeps it from tasting flat.

How it is made

A batter of flour, egg, and milk is cooked as one wide pancake, then finished with syrup or sugar.

Authentic touch

Cook one large pancake per person rather than a stack of small ones. Spread inclusions such as apple on the raw batter in the pan, instead of stirring a heavy handful into the bowl.

How it is used

Flat on a plate, sweet with stroop or sugar, or savory if you add bacon and cheese. Cut into wedges if you share.

Field notes

  • A pannenkoek is often cooked one to a plate.
  • Poffertjes are the small dimpled cakes, not this pancake.
  • Stroop, a dark syrup, is the usual sweet topping.

Ingredients

  • 200 g plain wheat flour
  • 2 large eggs
  • 500 ml whole milk
  • 1 pinch fine salt
  • 30 g unsalted butter, for the pan
  • Dark syrup, for serving

Steps

  1. Whisk the flour, eggs, milk, and salt into a smooth batter, a little thicker than a crepe batter.
  2. Heat a wide pan and melt a little butter.
  3. Pour enough batter to cover the pan in a thick crepe layer. Cook until the top is set and the underside is brown, then turn and finish.
  4. Slide onto a plate and serve with syrup. Repeat for each person.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Bacon and cheese, which adds meat and is no longer vegetarian
  • Apple pieces pressed into the uncooked top

Pairings

  • Dutch stroop or another dark syrup
  • Powdered sugar
  • Apple slices cooked on the batter
