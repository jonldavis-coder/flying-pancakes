Cachapas

You come in by Plane. Venezuela · South America.

Pronounced kah-CHAH-pahs

Coarse sweet-corn pancakes folded over fresh white cheese, juicy rather than bready.

15 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · 4 cachapas

History

A cachapa is a Venezuelan sweet-corn pancake, sold at roadside stands and folded over fresh cheese. It is not an arepa. An arepa is made from precooked or cooked dried corn. A cachapa is made from fresh kernels blended until coarse. It is not masa. Corn cakes of this region are older than wheat hotcakes, and the cachapa sits in the fresh-corn branch of that family, beside Colombia's arepa de choclo but with its own shape: a wide, folded pancake rather than a thick griddled round of dried corn.

Cultural background

Stands along the roads out of Maracay and across the center of the country grind corn to order. The cheese is often queso de mano or another fresh white cheese that softens in the heat. People eat cachapas as breakfast, a drive-out lunch, or a rainy-afternoon snack.

Why it tastes this way

Fresh corn carries sugar and juice, so the pancake tastes sweet without a dessert amount of sugar. Leaving the grind coarse keeps bursts of kernel instead of a uniform custard. Butter browns at the edge. Fresh cheese is salty and milky and melts only enough to soften. The fold traps steam, which is why a cachapa should be eaten before it slumps.

How it is made

Pulse fresh corn with salt, a little sugar, and a splash of milk. Cook wide pancakes in butter and fold them over slices of fresh white cheese.

Authentic touch

Pulse the corn. Do not liquefy it. The batter should be spoonable and full of pieces. Cook it thicker than a wheat crepe, on moderate heat, and fold it over the cheese while the pancake is hot. Do not add precooked arepa flour and call it the same thing.

How it is used

Serve them at once, folded, from the pan or wrapped in paper the way a stand does. They are a snack or a breakfast, not a stuffed dried-corn arepa.

Field notes

  • A cachapa is not an arepa. One is fresh corn. The other is cooked dried corn.
  • The batter should stay coarse. A smooth puree loses the kernel texture.
  • Fold the cheese in while the pancake is hot so it softens without a long melt.

Ingredients

  • 4 cups fresh sweet-corn kernels
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 teaspoon sugar
  • 2 tablespoons milk
  • 150 g fresh white cheese, sliced
  • Butter for the skillet

Steps

  1. Pulse the corn, salt, sugar, and milk until coarse and thick. Stop before it is a smooth liquid.
  2. Heat a skillet over medium heat and add butter.
  3. Spread a wide, thick pancake of batter. Cook until the underside browns and the top sets.
  4. Flip, lay on the cheese, and cook until the center is done.
  5. Fold and serve at once.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Queso de mano is the classic melting fresh cheese. Another mild white cheese still works.
  • A sweeter batter, with more sugar, is a home choice. Roadside cachapas are only gently sweet.
  • An arepa uses dried precooked corn. Do not substitute that flour here.

Pairings

  • More fresh cheese
  • A pat of butter
  • Black coffee or a cold fruit juice
