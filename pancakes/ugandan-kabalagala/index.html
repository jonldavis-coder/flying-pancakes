Arriving in Kampala

Ugandan kabalagala

You come in by Plane. Uganda · East Africa.

Pronounced kah-bah-lah-GAH-lah

Sweet fried cakes of ripe banana and wheat flour, crisp outside and soft within.

20 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · About 12 small cakes

History

Kabalagala are ripe banana street cakes in Uganda, sold around Kampala and cooked at home. Street versions are often fried, and this recipe is fried. Many street cooks bind the mash with cassava flour. This recipe uses wheat flour instead and does not use cassava. It does not describe how to process cassava. The flavor is the small sweet banana, mashed and fried in rounds.

Cultural background

The bananas for kabalagala are ripe and aromatic, not green cooking bananas. Vendors fry batches through the day and sell them as a snack. At home the same mash is a way to use a bunch that has ripened all at once.

Why it tastes this way

Ripe banana sweetens the dough without much added sugar. Frying turns the surface brown and slightly caramelized while the center stays moist. Wheat flour makes the crumb a little bready compared with a pure fruit mash. There is no chile and no savory spice in this version.

How it is made

Mash very ripe bananas, stir in wheat flour, a pinch of salt, and a little sugar until you have a soft dough. Shape small patties and fry them in oil until golden on both sides. Drain and eat them warm.

Authentic touch

Fry the cakes. A baked disk is not the street method. Use wheat flour as written. Do not substitute raw cassava, and do not look to this page for a cassava process.

How it is used

Kabalagala are street snacks, eaten hot from a paper bag or a rack. They can accompany tea. They are sweet, not a stew starch. This home batch uses the fried street method.

Field notes

  • Street kabalagala are often fried, and this recipe fries them.
  • The binder here is wheat flour, not cassava.
  • Use ripe sweet bananas. Green bananas will not mash into the same cake.

Ingredients

  • 4 very ripe bananas, mashed
  • 1 cup wheat flour (about 125 g)
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • 1/4 teaspoon fine salt
  • Oil for frying

Steps

  1. Mash the ripe bananas until soft and mostly smooth.
  2. Stir in the wheat flour, sugar, and salt to make a soft dough you can shape. There is no cassava in this recipe.
  3. Form small patties with wet hands.
  4. Fry the patties in hot oil, turning once, until both sides are golden and the center is cooked. Street versions are often fried, and this is that method.
  5. Drain and serve warm.

The dough contains wheat. Frying oil is hot. This page does not use cassava and does not explain cassava processing.

Eat kabalagala the day they are fried. Refrigerate leftovers and reheat them in a pan. The banana crumb does not keep a crisp crust. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Very ripe bananas need less sugar. Greener fruit will taste starchy and are the wrong bunch.
  • A spoon of ground cardamom is optional and not required for the street cake.
  • Cassava flour is what some stalls use. It is not in this recipe, and this page gives no cassava method.

Pairings

  • Tea
  • A little extra sugar on the hottest cakes
  • Fresh passion fruit on the side
