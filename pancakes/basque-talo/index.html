Arriving in the Basque Country

Basque talo

You come in by Train. Spain · Southern Europe.

Pronounced TAH-lo

A griddled corn flatbread from the Basque Country, often wrapped around sausage or eaten with cider.

20 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 6 · 6 flatbreads

History

Talo is a corn flatbread of the Basque Country, cooked on a griddle from maize flour and water. The same bread is made on both sides of the border. The pin is Spain. It is not nixtamalized masa and not a wheat tortilla. At cider houses and festivals it is wrapped around txistorra, a thin pork sausage, or eaten with chocolate. Those fillings are not in the dough. The dough here is vegan and gluten-free. Txistorra would add meat, and chocolate is a separate serving.

Cultural background

Corn arrived in Basque farming and became talo flour, a fine maize meal. The bread is pliable when warm and cracks when cold. Cider, sagardoa, is the drink beside it in a cider house.

Why it tastes this way

Toasted corn tastes sweet and a little smoky from the dry griddle. Salt is the only seasoning in the dough. It is bland on purpose so sausage, cheese, or chocolate can be the flavor. A hot talo is flexible. A cold one is a cracker. There is no wheat gluten to make it stretchy, so it tears rather than pulls.

How it is made

Fine corn flour, salt, and warm water are kneaded into a soft dough, rested, pressed into thin rounds, and cooked on a hot griddle.

Authentic touch

Use fine corn flour, not a nixtamalized masa harina, unless that is all you have, and then say you have changed the grain treatment. Cook it dry or with a wipe of oil. Fill it after cooking. The dough itself has no wheat.

How it is used

Warm, plain, or wrapped around txistorra or cheese, or with a piece of chocolate. The bread is the recipe. The sausage is a meat filling.

Field notes

  • Talo is a Basque corn flatbread, griddled, not a masa tortilla.
  • Cider and txistorra are the classic partners. The dough is only corn, water, and salt.
  • It is cooked on both the Spanish and French sides of the Basque Country. The pin is Spain.

Ingredients

  • 250 g fine corn flour
  • 1/2 tsp fine salt
  • 250 ml warm water
  • A little oil, for your hands if the dough sticks

Steps

  1. Mix the corn flour, salt, and warm water into a soft dough. Rest 15 minutes.
  2. Divide into six pieces and press or roll each into a thin round between sheets of parchment if it sticks.
  3. Cook on a hot dry griddle until brown spots appear and the bread is flexible, then turn.
  4. Serve warm. Add sausage, cheese, or chocolate after cooking if you want a filling.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A little wheat flour, which some modern talo includes and which removes the gluten-free diet
  • Cheese inside, which adds dairy

Pairings

  • Txistorra or another thin cooked sausage, a meat filling
  • Cider
  • A square of chocolate
