Finnish Pannukakku

Pronounced PAHN-noo-kahk-koo

A cardamom-scented baked pancake, custardy in the center.

10 min prep · 30 min cook · Serves 8 · one 9-by-13-inch pan

History

Pannukakku is Finland’s oven pancake, baked in a buttered pan and cut into squares. Cardamom is a frequent flavor, as it is in Finnish pulla. The Upper Peninsula of Michigan bakes a community version of the same dish, written separately because the American context is part of that story.

Cultural background

It is breakfast or a simple dessert with jam. One pan replaces a stack of flipped cakes.

Why it tastes this way

Cardamom’s perfume in a milky baked batter is the Finnish signature, a spice that became ordinary in Nordic baking through trade and stayed in Sunday buns and this pancake. The oven gives a browned rim and a soft middle. Jam adds the berry. The method is practical in a kitchen that is already heating the oven.

How it is made

Eggs, milk, flour, sugar, and cardamom are baked in a hot buttered pan.

Authentic touch

Melt the butter in the pan in the oven so the batter sizzles when it goes in. That sound is the bottom crust forming.

How it is used

With jam or berries.

Field notes

  • Cardamom is a traditional Finnish baking spice.
  • Pannukakku is baked. Lettu is cooked in a skillet.

Ingredients

  • 3 large eggs
  • 2 cups milk
  • 1 cup flour
  • 1/4 cup sugar
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 teaspoon cardamom
  • 3 tablespoons butter

Steps

  1. Heat the oven to 425°F and melt the butter in the pan in the oven.
  2. Whisk the rest into a smooth batter.
  3. Pour into the hot pan.
  4. Bake 25 to 30 minutes until puffed at the edges and set.
  5. It will fall when cut.
  6. Serve with jam.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • The Upper Peninsula page is the Finnish American pan.
  • Lettus are the thin flipped version.

Pairings

  • jam
  • berries
