Arriving in Caracas

Venezuelan arepas

You come in by Plane. Venezuela · South America.

Pronounced ah-REH-pahs

Thick white-corn cakes, griddled until they crackle, split and filled with fresh cheese.

15 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 4 arepas

History

The corn cake is older than the stuffed breakfast sandwich. People in Venezuela were cooking ground corn on a griddle long before wheat hotcakes, and long before a Caracas counter split the cake and filled it. There is no single pre-Columbian recipe that matches today's stuffed arepa. What is old is the corn cake. Splitting it and stuffing it is a later way of eating it. Precooked white cornmeal, the flour most homes use now, is a twentieth-century shortcut: the corn is cooked and dried before it is milled. It is not nixtamalized masa, and it is not fresh sweet corn.

Cultural background

An arepa in Caracas is breakfast, a packed lunch, and a night-time snack. Fillings run from cheese to black beans to shredded beef. This recipe is the corn cake plus fresh white cheese, the vegetarian counter version. The dough is only precooked cornmeal, water, and salt.

Why it tastes this way

Precooked cornmeal cooks up dense and corny, with a skin that toasts and a center that stays steamy if the dough was wet enough. Salt is the only seasoning in the cake, so the flavor is plain corn, not chile and not wheat. Fresh cheese melts at the edges and salts the interior once the cake is split. The contrast is the point of the modern sandwich: a bland, toasted shell and a rich filling.

How it is made

Stir precooked white cornmeal into warm salted water, rest it, and form thick cakes. Griddle them until a crust forms and the inside is set, then split and fill with cheese.

Authentic touch

The dough should feel like soft clay that does not stick to your hands and does not crack when you form a ball. Seal every crack before the cake hits the griddle, or it will split in the pan. Split it only after it is cooked, with a knife around the edge.

How it is used

Eat them hot for breakfast, plain or filled. A thinner Colombian arepa, a fresh-corn cachapa, and this thick stuffed cake are three different plates.

Field notes

  • The stuffed breakfast arepa is later than the corn cake itself. Do not treat them as one ancient recipe.
  • Precooked cornmeal is not nixtamalized Mexican masa and not the fresh corn in a cachapa.
  • If the dough cracks on the griddle, it needed more water or a wetter hand before cooking.

Ingredients

  • 250 g precooked white cornmeal
  • 360 ml warm water
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 150 g sliced fresh white cheese
  • 1 teaspoon oil for the griddle, if needed

Steps

  1. Stir the salt into the warm water. Add the precooked cornmeal and mix until a soft dough forms. Cover and rest 5 to 10 minutes.
  2. If the dough cracks, wet your hands and knead in a spoon of water. Divide into 4 pieces and form thick cakes about 2 centimeters tall. Smooth every crack.
  3. Cook on a lightly oiled or dry skillet over medium heat for about 7 minutes a side, until a crust forms and the cake sounds slightly hollow.
  4. Cool for a minute, then split each arepa around the edge with a knife, leaving a hinge if you can.
  5. Fill with the cheese and return the cake to the pan just long enough to warm the filling. Serve hot.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Shredded beef or chicken is a usual filling. Either one adds meat and is not vegetarian.
  • Leave the cheese out and the cake is vegan and dairy-free. It is still an arepa.
  • Yellow precooked cornmeal makes a deeper-colored cake. It is still not masa harina.

Pairings

  • Black beans as a second filling
  • Guasacaca, a green avocado sauce
  • Black coffee or a small cup of hot chocolate
