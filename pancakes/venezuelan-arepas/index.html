History

Arepas are a daily bread of Venezuela and Colombia, with roots in Indigenous maize cakes of the region. The modern Venezuelan arepa is usually made from precooked corn flour, masarepa, shaped into thick disks, griddled, and often finished in the oven, then split and filled. Fillings are a whole cuisine. This page is the corn cake. Reina pepiada and other meat fillings are variations, not the dough.

Cultural background

The dough should feel like clay. A griddle browns the face. A few minutes in a hot oven, if you have one, sets the center. They are split while warm.

Why it tastes this way

Precooked corn flour is a twentieth-century product that made a much older maize cake fast to cook at home. The taste is toasted corn, mild and a little sweet, a container for salty fillings. The thickness is why an arepa is not a tortilla. Venezuela’s habit of stuffing them turned the bread into breakfast and dinner.

How it is made

Masarepa and water are shaped into thick disks and cooked on a griddle, then split.

Authentic touch

Use masarepa, not masa harina for tortillas and not cornmeal. The dough should be moist and not crack. Cook the crust, then finish the center. A raw white line in the middle means they need more time.

How it is used

Split, with cheese, beans, or eggs. Meat fillings are a separate choice.

Field notes

  • Use masarepa, the precooked corn flour, not tortilla masa.
  • Stuffing them is the Venezuelan habit.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups masarepa
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 2 1/2 cups warm water
  • Oil

Steps

  1. Stir masarepa, salt, and water. Rest 5 minutes. The dough should be moist and not crack.
  2. Shape 6 thick disks.
  3. Cook on a medium griddle until a crust forms, then bake at 400°F for 10 minutes or continue on the griddle until the center is set.
  4. Split.
  5. Fill with beans, cheese, or eggs.
  6. Do not use tortilla masa harina.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Colombian arepas are often left unsplit and served beside the meal.
  • Andean arepas use wheat and are a different bread.

Pairings

  • cheese
  • beans
  • eggs
