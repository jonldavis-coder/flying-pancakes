Pronounced ROAR-ah-kor

Crisp grated-potato cakes with almost no batter.

20 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · about 8 cakes

History

Rårakor are Swedish cakes of grated raw potato, fried until lacy, with little or no flour. They are the plainer relative of raggmunk. The name points at raw potato. They are served with the same partners in many houses: lingonberry and, traditionally, pork. This recipe is the potato cake alone, so it stays meatless. Add pork if you want the full old plate.

Cultural background

They are a side or a light meal. Salt is the seasoning. They should be thin.

Why it tastes this way

Without batter, the taste is browned potato and salt, crisper than raggmunk and more fragile. Potato was the northern staple that made a meal when bread flour was scarce. A hot pan and a thin layer are what create the lace. Lingonberry on the side is the acid a fried potato wants.

How it is made

Grated, squeezed potato is fried in thin cakes in a hot pan.

Authentic touch

Grate coarsely, squeeze hard, and spread thin. Do not pile the potato or the center steams. Flip once, with confidence.

How it is used

Salt, lingonberry jam, and sour cream or a fried egg if you eat dairy and eggs.

Field notes

  • Rårakor are raw potato, fried, not a mashed potato cake.
  • This written recipe has no meat. The traditional full plate often includes pork.

Ingredients

  • 4 medium potatoes
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • Oil for frying

Steps

  1. Grate the potatoes and squeeze them dry. Toss with salt.
  2. Heat a thin film of oil until it shimmers.
  3. Spread thin rounds.
  4. Fry until deep golden, then flip.
  5. Salt again.
  6. Serve at once with lingonberry jam.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Raggmunk adds batter and is usually served with pork.
  • A whisper of flour helps them hold and removes the gluten-free label.

Pairings

  • lingonberry jam
  • salt
