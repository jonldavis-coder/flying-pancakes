Paper dosa

You come in by Train. India · South Asia.

Pronounced PAY-per DOH-sah

A very large, very thin fermented rice sheet that shatters at the edge and tastes toasted.

40 min prep · 30 min cook · Serves 4 · 4 very large dosas

History

Paper dosa is a size and a cook, not a different grain. The batter is the fermented rice and urad batter of a plain dosa. The cook spreads it across a wide griddle until the sheet is almost translucent, then keeps it on the heat until the rim turns brittle. Restaurant menus sometimes call that spread a paper roast. The spectacle is the diameter. The cereal is unchanged.

Cultural background

Homes with a smaller pan still make a paper-style dosa by using less batter and a hotter surface. The restaurant version is larger because the griddle is larger. Either way, the test is thinness and a dry crackle, not a new recipe of flours.

Why it tastes this way

More of the batter touches iron, so toast and a light ferment sourness dominate. There is almost no soft center to cushion the taste. Oil at the edge fries the lace. Because the grain is still rice and urad, the aroma matches a plain dosa that has been cooked further and thinner.

How it is made

Spread fermented rice and urad batter very thin on a broad hot griddle and cook it until the sheet is crisp enough to crack.

Authentic touch

Use less batter than pride suggests, and spread in one confident spiral. A second pass piles batter back onto the center and ruins the paper thickness.

How it is used

Bring it to the table whole, standing or folded once, with chutney and sambar. It is a shared breakfast more than a stuffed one.

Field notes

  • Paper dosa does not introduce wheat, rava, or millet. It is a thin cook of rice and urad batter.
  • The wide restaurant griddle is why the famous ones look longer than a home dosa.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup idli rice
  • 1/3 cup skinned urad dal
  • 1/4 teaspoon fenugreek seeds
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • Water for soaking and grinding
  • Neutral oil for a wide griddle

Steps

  1. Soak rice, and soak urad with fenugreek, in separate bowls for 4 to 6 hours. Grind the urad fluffy and the rice fine. Mix with salt.
  2. Ferment until bubbly and mildly sour, commonly 8 to 14 hours. Active prep does not include that rest. Discard spoiled batter.
  3. Loosen the batter with water until it spreads easily in a thin film. Heat a wide griddle until a water drop dances.
  4. Grease lightly. Pour a small ladle and spread it at once to the full width of the pan, as thin as the batter will allow.
  5. Oil the edge and cook without flipping until the sheet is pale gold, dry, and brittle at the rim. Fold once and serve.

Discard batter that is moldy or smells putrid.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A brush of oil only, no filling
  • Ghee on the last ten seconds of cooking, which adds dairy

Pairings

  • Coconut chutney
  • Sambar
  • Molaga podi with sesame oil
