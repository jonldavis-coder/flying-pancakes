Arriving in Machias

Maine blueberry pancakes

You come in by Boat. United States · North America.

Tart wild blueberries bursting into a buttered, lightly sweet wheat cake.

15 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 12 pancakes

History

Maine blueberry pancakes fold lowbush wild blueberries into a wheat batter. Those berries are native to the region, and Wabanaki communities have long gathered them. The pancake is a breakfast use of the berry. Machias sits in Washington County, where the wild blueberry harvest is part of the local economy.

Cultural background

Lowbush berries are smaller and more tart than many cultivated highbush berries. Frozen wild berries are the honest off-season choice. Do not thaw them before they go into the batter.

Why it tastes this way

Wild berries have more acid and deeper color than large cultivated ones, so they stain the crumb purple and taste sharper. A thicker batter keeps the juice from spreading into one gray cake. Butter and a light syrup keep the fruit in front.

How it is made

Griddled wheat batter with whole wild blueberries folded in.

Authentic touch

Use wild blueberries, fresh or frozen. Fold them in at the end and do not mash them.

How it is used

Hot, with butter and maple syrup.

Field notes

  • Lowbush wild blueberries are native to Maine and the Maritimes.
  • Wabanaki people gathered them long before this wheat pancake existed.

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 2 tablespoons sugar
  • 2 teaspoons baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 1/2 cups buttermilk
  • 1 large egg
  • 2 tablespoons unsalted butter, melted, plus more for the griddle
  • 1 cup wild blueberries, fresh or frozen

Steps

  1. Whisk the flour, sugar, baking powder, baking soda, and salt.
  2. Stir in the buttermilk, egg, and melted butter until just combined. The batter should be a little thick.
  3. Fold in the blueberries. If they are frozen, do not thaw them first.
  4. Butter a griddle over medium heat. Pour 1/4 cup cakes.
  5. Cook until you see berries at the surface and the underside is brown, then flip. Turn the heat down if the berries scorch.
  6. Serve when the center is set, with butter and maple syrup.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Frozen berries go in still frozen so they do not dye the whole batter.
  • Cultivated blueberries work, with a milder taste.

Pairings

  • Maple syrup
  • Butter
  • A spoon of extra berries
