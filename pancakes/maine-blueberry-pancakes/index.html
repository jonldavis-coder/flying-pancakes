Maine Blueberry Pancakes

Tart wild blueberry bursting through a simple batter.

10 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · about 8 pancakes

History

Lowbush wild blueberries are a Maine crop, managed in barrens and gathered by Wabanaki peoples long before they were a commercial can and a tourist breakfast. Folding the berries into wheat pancakes is a modern New England breakfast that depends on that fruit. The cake is not a Wabanaki recipe. The berry is the local fact.

Cultural background

In August the berries are fresh. The rest of the year, Mainers use frozen wild berries, which are smaller and tarter than cultivated highbush blueberries.

Why it tastes this way

Wild lowbush berries are smaller and more acidic than a large cultivated blueberry, so the cake tastes tart rather than jammy. Their skins stain the batter purple if you stir hard. Acid in the fruit also softens the crumb around each berry. The barrens and the short northern summer are why this fruit, and not peach, defines the plate.

How it is made

A buttermilk batter is cooked with wild blueberries folded in or dotted on top.

Authentic touch

Fold the berries in at the end, or drop them onto the poured batter. Stirring breaks them and turns the whole batch gray.

How it is used

Butter and maple syrup. Extra berries on the side if you have them.

Field notes

  • Wild Maine blueberries and large grocery blueberries are different plants in practice: smaller, tarter, and often sold frozen.
  • The pancake is a modern use of a much older Indigenous food.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup wild blueberries, fresh or frozen
  • 1 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1 cup buttermilk
  • 1 large egg
  • 2 tablespoons melted butter

Steps

  1. Stir the dry ingredients together, then stir in the wet ingredients until the batter is combined.
  2. Fold the berries in gently after the batter is mixed.
  3. Heat a griddle or heavy skillet over medium heat and film it with butter.
  4. Spoon on the batter. Cook until the underside is browned and the top looks set.
  5. Flip once and cook the second side through.
  6. Flip when the berries have set into the surface so they do not stick to the griddle.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Frozen berries should be used frozen or barely thawed, or they bleed.
  • Cultivated blueberries are milder. They still make a pancake. They are not the Maine berry.

Pairings

  • maple syrup
  • wild blueberries
  • butter
