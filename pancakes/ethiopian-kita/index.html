A quick, unfermented teff flatbread, milder than injera.

10 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · about 6 rounds

History

Kita is a quick Ethiopian flatbread, often teff or other flour mixed and cooked without the long ferment of injera. It is everyday bread, softer and less sour. Chechebsa, or kita firfir, is torn kita cooked with spices and is a breakfast. This page is the plain kita. It is not a failed injera.

Cultural background

When there is no time to ferment, kita is the bread. It can be torn and dressed with spiced butter for breakfast. This version is teff and salt, vegan as written.

Why it tastes this way

Without fermentation, teff tastes toasted and grainy rather than sour. That is the point of kita: the same grain, a different schedule. Injera’s sourness takes days. Kita takes minutes. The flavor is the grain itself, which is why a spiced butter breakfast exists, to dress a plain bread.

How it is made

Teff flour and water are mixed into a batter and cooked on both sides.

Authentic touch

Cook it on a medium pan like a thick crepe, and flip it, unlike injera. It will not grow eyes. Do not wait for them.

How it is used

Plain, or torn and finished with a spiced butter if you eat dairy. The bread itself is vegan.

Field notes

  • Kita is the quick bread. It is not supposed to taste like injera.
  • Teff keeps it gluten-free.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup teff flour
  • 1 cup water
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1 teaspoon oil

Steps

  1. Stir flour, water, and salt into a thick batter.
  2. Oil a skillet lightly over medium.
  3. Spread a round and cook until the underside browns.
  4. Flip and cook through.
  5. Stack under a cloth.
  6. Serve warm.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Chechebsa is torn kita warmed with spices.
  • Injera is the fermented bread.

Pairings

  • spiced butter
  • honey
