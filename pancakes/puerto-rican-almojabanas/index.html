Puerto Rican almojábanas

You come in by Boat. Puerto Rico · Caribbean.

Pronounced al-moh-hah-BAH-nahs

Fried rice-flour and cheese fritters, crisp at the edge, salty and chewy inside.

15 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · 12 fritters

History

Almojábanas in Puerto Rico are fritters of rice flour and cheese, fried and eaten at breakfast or as a snack. The name is older than the island recipe and is also used in Colombia, where the dough is often cheese and a starch such as corn starch, and the cakes may be baked rather than fried. This page is the Puerto Rican fritter: rice flour, cheese, egg, and milk, with no wheat. Rice flour is a later pantry staple here. It is not masa and not cassava.

Cultural background

Bakeries and home kitchens in San Juan fry them in small batches. The cheese is often a firm cow-milk cheese that can be grated, including the Edam-style cheeses long sold on the island. Any firm, mildly salty cheese that grates will work. The Colombian cake with the same name is a cousin, not a copy.

Why it tastes this way

Rice flour fries to a thin crust and a chewy, almost stretchy center, different from a wheat doughnut. Cheese melts into that center and salts it. Egg and milk bind the batter and brown at the edge. There is little sugar, so the fritter eats like a bread, not like a dessert. A gluten-free baking powder lifts it just enough that the middle is not a solid lump.

How it is made

Stir rice flour, gluten-free baking powder, salt, grated cheese, egg, and milk into a thick batter. Fry spoons of it in oil until golden and cooked through.

Authentic touch

Grate the cheese fine so it disperses. A chunk of cheese in a loose batter leaks into the oil. Fry until the center is set, not only until the outside colors. Use a gluten-free baking powder if you need the fritters to stay free of wheat.

How it is used

Eat them hot, at breakfast with coffee, or as a street snack. They dry out as they cool, so the pan and the plate should be close.

Field notes

  • Puerto Rican almojábanas are fried rice-flour fritters. The Colombian cake of the same name is often a different starch.
  • There is no wheat in this recipe if the baking powder is gluten-free.
  • Cheese is the main flavor. The fritter is savory-salty, not a sugar doughnut.

Ingredients

  • 150 g rice flour
  • 1 teaspoon gluten-free baking powder
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 120 g grated firm white cheese
  • 1 egg
  • 80 ml milk
  • Neutral oil for frying

Steps

  1. Stir the rice flour, baking powder, and salt. Toss in the grated cheese.
  2. Beat the egg with the milk and stir it in until the batter is thick enough to hold a spoon.
  3. Heat 2 centimeters of oil over medium heat until a drop of batter sizzles at once.
  4. Fry spoons of batter, turning once, until golden and cooked through, about 3 minutes.
  5. Drain and serve hot.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Colombian almojábanas often use corn starch and cheese and may be baked. That is a related cake, not this batter.
  • A little sugar in the batter makes them closer to a sweet fritter.
  • Wheat flour in place of rice flour means the fritters contain gluten.

Pairings

  • Café con leche
  • Guava paste on the side
  • A cup of hot chocolate
