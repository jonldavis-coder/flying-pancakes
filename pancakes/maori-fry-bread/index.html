Pronounced parāoa parai

Fried dough, blistered and soft, a Māori takeaway and hāngī bread.

20 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 6 · 6 pieces

History

Parāoa parai, Māori fry bread, is a fried wheat dough associated with Māori communities, hāngī gatherings, and takeaways. Wheat and frying fat are post-contact ingredients. The bread is a living Māori food, not a pre-contact recipe and not the same as Australian damper. This page gives a simple home fry bread and does not invent a ritual around it.

Cultural background

It is eaten hot, plain, with butter, or alongside a boil-up or hāngī foods. The shape is a flat round or an oval. It should blister.

Why it tastes this way

Hot fat puffs a soft dough so it tastes rich and toasted, a festive bread. Wheat flour became part of Māori cooking after European contact, and fry bread is one of the foods built from that. It is claimed and cooked by Māori cooks now. The flavor is fried dough and salt. Honey or butter is the finish some families use.

How it is made

A soft baking-powder dough is rolled and fried until blistered.

Authentic touch

Fry at a steady medium heat so the center cooks and the outside blisters without burning. Drain on a rack. Eat it hot.

How it is used

Hot, as a bread.

Field notes

  • Māori fry bread is a living food made with post-contact wheat and fat.
  • It is not the same bread as Australian damper.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups flour
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 2 tablespoons oil
  • 3/4 cup water
  • Oil for frying

Steps

  1. Mix a soft dough and rest 10 minutes.
  2. Roll or press into ovals about 1/2 inch thick.
  3. Fry in hot oil until blistered and cooked through.
  4. Drain.
  5. Serve hot.
  6. The center should not be raw.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Damper is baked or campfire-cooked and is a different settler bread.
  • A sweeter dough is a home choice and less typical.

Pairings

  • butter
  • hāngī foods
