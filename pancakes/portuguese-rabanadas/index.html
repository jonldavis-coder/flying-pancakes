Portuguese rabanadas

You come in by Boat. Portugal · Southern Europe.

Pronounced hah-bah-NAH-dahs

Portuguese Christmas fried bread, soaked in milk and egg, with cinnamon sugar. Not a poured pancake.

15 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 slices

History

Rabanadas are slices of bread soaked in milk and egg, fried, and coated with cinnamon sugar. They are Christmas food in Portugal, close to Spanish torrijas. They are not a poured pancake. The journal includes them because they share the Christmas table with sweeter batters and because leaving them out would pretend Portugal only has panquecas. The poured crepe is the next page. Day-old bread is the right bread. Fresh soft bread falls apart.

Cultural background

They are a convent-and-home Christmas sweet in the sense that egg sweets are a Portuguese Christmas pattern. This page does not assign them to one convent. Cinnamon and sugar are the coat. A light syrup is another serving and is not required here.

Why it tastes this way

The crust fries crisp while the inside stays custardy from milk and egg. Cinnamon sugar sticks to the hot surface. The bread's own flavor matters, so a plain white loaf is better than a sweet cake. They taste of fried custard and spice, not of a thin crepe.

How it is made

Slices of day-old bread are dipped in sweetened milk, then in egg, fried in oil, and tossed in cinnamon sugar.

Authentic touch

Use day-old bread and say plainly that this is fried bread, not a pancake batter. Cook them at Christmas or whenever you want the same sweet. Do not call them panquecas.

How it is used

Warm or cold, as a Christmas dessert. They are vegetarian.

Field notes

  • Rabanadas are fried, egg-soaked bread, a Christmas dessert.
  • They are not poured pancakes. Panquecas are the crepe page.
  • Day-old bread holds together. Fresh bread collapses.

Ingredients

  • 8 slices day-old white bread
  • 300 ml whole milk
  • 1 tbsp sugar, plus 4 tbsp for the coating
  • 1 tsp ground cinnamon
  • 2 large eggs, beaten
  • Neutral oil or olive oil, for frying

Steps

  1. Stir 1 tbsp sugar into the milk. Dip each slice of bread until it is soaked but not collapsing. Dip it in the beaten egg.
  2. Fry in hot oil until both sides are golden. Drain.
  3. Mix the remaining sugar with the cinnamon.
  4. Coat the hot slices in the cinnamon sugar and serve.

Fry only as many slices as the pan can hold in one layer. Do not leave the oil.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A splash of port in the milk
  • Portuguese panquecas, the poured crepe

Pairings

  • Cinnamon sugar
  • A spoon of sugar syrup, if you want them wetter
  • Coffee
