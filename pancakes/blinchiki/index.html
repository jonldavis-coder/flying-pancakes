Arriving in Russia

Blinchiki

You come in by Train. Russia · Eastern Europe.

Pronounced bleen-CHEE-kee

Thin Russian crepes rolled around a sweet farmer-cheese filling.

25 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 filled crepes

History

Blinchiki are the thin unleavened Russian crepes, often filled and rolled, as distinct from thicker yeasted blini. A sweet filling of tvorog, the farmer cheese, is written here. Meat fillings exist and would change the diet. The Ukrainian nalysnyky page is the rolled crepe baked in sour cream. These blinchiki are filled and served after a brief second fry, not baked in a dish of cream.

Cultural background

They are home dessert food and a way to use tvorog. The crepe has to be thin enough to roll without cracking. A dusting of sugar or a spoon of sour cream finishes them.

Why it tastes this way

The crepe is mild and a little eggy. Tvorog is tangy, sweetened just enough, with lemon to lift it. A second fry browns the outside of the roll so you get a crisp seam and a warm cheese center. They taste fresher than a heavy baked pasta, because the sauce is only what you add at the table.

How it is made

Thin wheat crepes are filled with sweetened tvorog, rolled, and fried briefly in butter.

Authentic touch

Drain the tvorog or the filling will burst the crepe. Roll seam down and fry lightly. For the baked sour-cream dish, use the nalysnyky page instead.

How it is used

Warm, with sour cream or sugar, as dessert. A savory meat filling is a different version and not vegetarian.

Field notes

  • Blinchiki are thin and unleavened. Yeasted blini are the other page.
  • This filling is sweet tvorog, a farmer cheese.
  • They are fried after rolling, not baked in a pan of sour cream.

Ingredients

  • 120 g plain wheat flour
  • 2 large eggs
  • 300 ml whole milk
  • 1 pinch fine salt
  • 40 g unsalted butter, divided
  • 300 g tvorog or farmer cheese, drained
  • 2 tbsp sugar
  • 1 tsp lemon zest

Steps

  1. Whisk the flour, eggs, milk, and salt. Rest 10 minutes. Cook thin crepes in a little butter.
  2. Mix the tvorog, sugar, and lemon zest. If it is wet, drain it first.
  3. Fill and roll the crepes, seam side down.
  4. Fry the rolls in the remaining butter until the outsides are golden. Serve with sour cream or sugar.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A savory mushroom filling
  • Nalysnyky, the Ukrainian crepes baked in sour cream

Pairings

  • Sour cream
  • Sugar
  • A little jam
