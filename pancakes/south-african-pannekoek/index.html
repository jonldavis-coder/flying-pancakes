South African pannekoek

You come in by Plane. South Africa · Southern Africa.

Pronounced PAN-uh-kook

Thin wheat pancakes rolled around cinnamon sugar, with a buttery edge.

15 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 4 · About 8 thin pancakes

History

Pannekoek is the thin cinnamon-sugar pancake of South African homes and market stalls. The name is the Dutch and Afrikaans word for pancake, and the cake is a thin batter cooked in a wide pan, then rolled. In South Africa and in Britain, the word flapjack can mean a baked oat bar. This page is the thin pannekoek, not that bar. Market stands and church fetes still cook them to order.

Cultural background

A pannekoek stall is a griddle, a jug of batter, and a shaker of cinnamon sugar. At home the same batter is a weekend pan. The cake is thin enough to roll. It is a national snack, not a single city's secret, even though the Dutch name has a Cape history.

Why it tastes this way

Egg and milk make a flexible sheet that browns in spots. There is little baking powder, so the pancake stays flat instead of rising into a pikelet. Cinnamon sugar melts against the hot bread and tastes toasted rather than like a raw spice. Lemon is a sharper finish some cooks prefer. This recipe stays with cinnamon sugar.

How it is made

Rest a thin batter of flour, milk, egg, and a spoon of melted butter. Cook wide pancakes in a buttered pan, one at a time, until spotted. Sprinkle each with cinnamon sugar and roll it. Keep the rolls warm under a cloth.

Authentic touch

Cook the batter thin and roll it around cinnamon sugar while it is hot. If you bake a tray of oats and syrup, you have made the other flapjack, the oat bar, and not this pannekoek.

How it is used

Pannekoek are a snack, a dessert, and stall food. They are eaten by hand once rolled. They can be a weekend treat rather than a daily bread. Ice cream is a later fairground addition and not required.

Field notes

  • In South Africa and Britain, flapjack can mean a baked oat bar. This page is the thin pannekoek.
  • The cake is rolled around cinnamon sugar while it is hot.
  • The name is Dutch and Afrikaans for pancake.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup wheat flour (about 125 g)
  • 1 1/4 cups milk
  • 1 egg
  • 1 tablespoon melted unsalted butter, plus more for the pan
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • 1/4 teaspoon fine salt
  • 2 tablespoons sugar mixed with 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon

Steps

  1. Whisk the flour, milk, egg, melted butter, sugar, and salt into a thin batter. Rest it for 15 minutes.
  2. Heat a wide pan, add a little butter, and pour enough batter to coat the bottom in a thin sheet. This is a thin pannekoek, not an oat bar.
  3. Cook until the top looks set and the underside is spotted, then flip once and cook briefly.
  4. Sprinkle cinnamon sugar over the hot pancake and roll it up.
  5. Repeat with the rest of the batter. Serve the rolls warm.

The batter contains wheat, egg, and dairy. Cinnamon sugar is sweet. This is not the baked oat flapjack.

Rolled pannekoek are best fresh. Refrigerate leftovers and reheat them covered. The sugar will weep a little. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Lemon and sugar instead of cinnamon is the other classic finish.
  • A savory mince filling exists at some stalls and is a different dish.
  • Whole wheat in part of the flour makes a heartier cake and still contains wheat.

Pairings

  • More cinnamon sugar
  • Lemon wedges, if you want the sharper finish
  • Rooibos tea
