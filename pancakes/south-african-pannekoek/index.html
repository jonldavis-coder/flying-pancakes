South African Pannekoek

Pronounced PAH-nuh-kook

A thin, large pancake with cinnamon sugar, a fair and church-fete food.

15 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 6 · about 6 large pancakes

History

South African pannekoek are thin pancakes in the Dutch tradition, renamed and kept at church fetes, school events, and home kitchens. Cinnamon sugar is the usual finish. The Dutch settlement at the Cape is why the name and the crepe method are there. They are a social food: someone stands at a large pan and rolls them to order.

Cultural background

A pannekoek stall at a fete is a recognizable scene. Lemon and cinnamon sugar are both used. The cake is thin and large.

Why it tastes this way

The batter is the Dutch thin pancake, and cinnamon sugar is the spice the Cape kitchen put on it. Cinnamon was a trade spice. The cake itself is mild egg and flour, a carrier. Cooking one large cake and rolling it is why the edge is crisper than the middle. The fete context is the culture: a shared pan, not a single plated dessert.

How it is made

A thin batter is cooked as a large pancake, sugared with cinnamon, and rolled.

Authentic touch

Spread the batter thin in a wide pan and roll the pancake while it is hot so the cinnamon sugar sticks and the cake does not crack.

How it is used

Rolled, warm, in a paper if you are pretending you are at the fete.

Field notes

  • Church fetes and school events are a real home of the South African pannekoek.
  • Cinnamon sugar is the classic sweet finish.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup flour
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 1/4 cups milk
  • Pinch of salt
  • 1 tablespoon melted butter
  • 2 tablespoons sugar
  • 1 teaspoon cinnamon

Steps

  1. Whisk flour, eggs, milk, salt, and butter. Rest 15 minutes.
  2. Cook thin large pancakes in a buttered pan.
  3. Mix sugar and cinnamon and sprinkle each pancake.
  4. Roll and serve.
  5. The first one tests the heat.
  6. Eat them the day they are made.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A savory mince filling exists and adds meat.
  • Dutch pannenkoeken are the European source of the method.

Pairings

  • cinnamon sugar
  • lemon
