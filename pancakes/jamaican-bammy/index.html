History

Bammy is a Jamaican cassava flatbread, associated with the island’s Taíno heritage and with later Jamaican cooking, especially beside fried fish. Traditional bammy uses grated bitter cassava that has been pressed. This page uses commercial cassava flour or frozen peeled yuca labeled ready to cook, not a home detox of bitter root. The cake is griddled, and a short soak and fry is a common way to reheat it.

Cultural background

It is a bread, round and dense. Fried fish and bammy are a recognized plate. The bread itself is plain.

Why it tastes this way

Cassava tastes mild and starchy, a foil for spicy fish and pickled pepper. Pressing or using a dry flour keeps the cake from being glue. Griddling toasts the outside. A soak in milk or coconut milk before a second fry is a later richness. Coconut milk is written as optional and keeps the base recipe free of dairy if you use water.

How it is made

Cassava flour, salt, and water are shaped into rounds and cooked on a griddle.

Authentic touch

Cook the first side until a crust forms before you flip, or the cake breaks. Use ready-to-cook yuca or commercial flour only.

How it is used

With fried fish or on its own. A pepper sauce on the side is the heat, not the bread.

Field notes

  • Bammy is Jamaica’s cassava bread, often served with fish.
  • This recipe does not process bitter cassava at home.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup commercial cassava flour
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 cup water
  • Oil for the pan

Steps

  1. Mix flour, salt, and water into a firm dough.
  2. Shape four small rounds about 1/2 inch thick.
  3. Cook on a medium oiled griddle, covered, turning once, until both sides are golden and the center is cooked.
  4. Serve warm.
  5. The center should not look wet.
  6. Pepper sauce belongs on the side.

Use commercial cassava flour or yuca sold ready to cook. Do not process bitter cassava at home from these pages.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Soaking a cooked round in coconut milk and frying it again is a common finish.
  • Casabe is the drier Caribbean cracker.

Pairings

  • fried fish
  • pepper sauce
