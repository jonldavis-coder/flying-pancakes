Soft wheat tortillas with a few browned spots.

30 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 tortillas

History

Flour tortillas are the wheat flatbread of northern Mexico, where wheat farming and a different history from the masa south made a soft tortilla of flour and fat the daily bread. They are cooked on a comal. Burritos and quesadillas in the north often use them. They are a griddled bread, included beside corn tortillas rather than instead of them.

Cultural background

Lard is traditional in many northern recipes. Oil is the fat in this version so it can be dairy-free and meatless. The dough should be soft.

Why it tastes this way

Wheat and fat make a stretchy, mild bread that browns in spots and stays pliable, good for wrapping. The north’s wheat agriculture is the reason this tortilla exists beside the corn one. A comal, not an oven, is the tool. The flavor is toasted flour and a little salt.

How it is made

Flour, salt, fat, and warm water are rolled and cooked on a hot comal.

Authentic touch

Rest the dough and roll it thin. Cook it until it puffs and spots, then keep the stack covered or it becomes a cracker.

How it is used

Warm, as a wrap or a bread.

Field notes

  • Flour tortillas are the wheat bread of northern Mexico.
  • Keep the stack covered or they turn into crackers.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups flour
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 3 tablespoons oil
  • 3/4 cup warm water

Steps

  1. Mix a soft dough and rest 20 minutes.
  2. Divide into 8 and roll thin.
  3. Cook on a hot dry comal until spotted and puffed.
  4. Stack in a towel.
  5. Serve warm.
  6. Do not brown them hard.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Lard is a traditional fat and adds meat.
  • Corn tortillas are the masa bread.

Pairings

  • eggs
