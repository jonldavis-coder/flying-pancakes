Arriving in Honolulu

Hawaiian banana-macadamia hotcakes

You come in by Plane. United States · North America.

Ripe banana and toasted macadamia in a rich diner hotcake.

20 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 10 hotcakes

History

Banana-macadamia hotcakes are twentieth-century Hawaiian diner food, a wheat breakfast piled with banana and macadamia nuts. The macadamia was introduced to Hawaii from Australia and later became a local crop and a diner ingredient. This is not an ancient Hawaiian dish. Honolulu is the map pin.

Cultural background

The plate is a tourist-era and local-diner breakfast that uses ingredients that were available in twentieth-century Hawaii. Macadamia nuts are tree nuts. The batter is a wheat hotcake, not a taro or coconut bread.

Why it tastes this way

Macadamias are very oily, so they taste buttery when they are toasted and bitter if they burn. Banana adds perfume and moisture. A richer batter, with milk and butter, matches the nut. The combination is sweet and rich, which is why the cakes are small.

How it is made

Griddled wheat hotcakes with banana and toasted macadamia.

Authentic touch

Toast the macadamias and chop them. Use a ripe banana. Do not describe the plate as ancient.

How it is used

A short stack with butter, banana slices, and more nuts.

Field notes

  • Macadamia trees were introduced to Hawaii. They are not an ancient Hawaiian crop.
  • The banana-macadamia hotcake is twentieth-century diner food.

Ingredients

  • 1/2 cup chopped roasted macadamia nuts
  • 1 ripe banana, mashed, plus 1 banana, sliced
  • 1 1/2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 2 tablespoons sugar
  • 2 teaspoons baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 1/4 cups whole milk
  • 1 large egg
  • 2 tablespoons unsalted butter, melted, plus more for the griddle

Steps

  1. If the macadamias are raw, toast them in a dry skillet until pale gold, then chop them. If they are already roasted, chop them.
  2. Whisk the flour, sugar, baking powder, and salt.
  3. Stir in the mashed banana, milk, egg, and melted butter. Fold in half of the nuts.
  4. Butter a griddle over medium-low heat. Pour 1/4 cup cakes and lay a banana slice and a few nuts on each.
  5. Flip carefully when the underside is golden. The nuts burn if the pan is too hot.
  6. Serve with butter, the remaining banana, and the remaining nuts.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Leave the nuts out only if you are cooking for someone who cannot eat tree nuts, and use a clean pan.
  • Coconut flakes are a different topping and are not required here.

Pairings

  • Butter
  • Banana slices
  • More chopped macadamias
