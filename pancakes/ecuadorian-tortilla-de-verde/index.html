History

Tortillas de verde are Ecuadorian cakes of mashed green plantain, cooked on a griddle, a coastal staple different from the highland potato llapingacho. Green plantain is starchy, not sweet. The cakes are a bread beside fish or eggs.

Cultural background

The plantain is boiled, mashed, salted, and shaped. Some versions add cheese. This one does not, so it stays vegan.

Why it tastes this way

Green plantain is starch, which is why the cake tastes savory and filling rather than like a ripe-plantain tatale. The coast grows the plantain. A griddle toasts it. Salt is the seasoning. It is a bread in a plantain country, the way a potato cake is a bread in the highlands.

How it is made

Boiled green plantains are mashed with salt and pan-cooked as small cakes.

Authentic touch

Boil the plantains until they are truly soft, mash them while warm, and salt them. A stiff mash fries cleanly. A wet one sticks.

How it is used

Warm, as a side.

Field notes

  • Green plantain is starch. Ripe plantain would be a different, sweet cake.
  • These are a coastal bread, not the highland potato cakes.

Ingredients

  • 3 green plantains, boiled until soft
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • Oil

Steps

  1. Mash the warm plantains with salt until smooth.
  2. Shape small cakes.
  3. Pan-cook in a little oil until both sides are browned and the center is hot.
  4. Serve as a side.
  5. Green plantains only.
  6. A wet mash will stick. Dry it in the pan first.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Cheese in the mash adds dairy.
  • Ripe plantain would make a sweet cake and is not this recipe.

Pairings

  • eggs
  • fried fish
