A savory pancake of soaked yellow moong dal, ginger, and green chile.

20 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 3 · about 6 pancakes

History

Moong dal cheela is a North Indian breakfast pancake made from soaked split yellow moong, ground with ginger and chile. It is a home and diet-conscious tiffin food, high in the lentil and free of wheat when no flour is added. It is related to pesarattu and milder than adai.

Cultural background

It is cooked like a thick crepe and eaten with green chutney. A spoon of rice flour can help it hold. This version uses only the dal.

Why it tastes this way

Yellow moong tastes sweeter and lighter than besan, and grinding it fresh keeps that sweetness. Ginger and chile are the seasoning because the dal itself is gentle. No ferment means it tastes clean. It is a lentil breakfast from a pantry that already soaked dals for other dishes.

How it is made

Soaked moong dal is ground with ginger and chile and cooked as pancakes.

Authentic touch

Grind until almost smooth, with enough water to spread. A coarse grind stays gritty. Cook covered for a minute so the dal sets.

How it is used

With green chutney.

Field notes

  • Split yellow moong is the dal for this cheela.
  • It is an unfermented lentil pancake.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup split yellow moong dal, soaked 3 hours and drained
  • 1 inch ginger
  • 1 green chile
  • 1/2 teaspoon cumin
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • Water, to grind
  • Oil

Steps

  1. Grind the dal, ginger, chile, cumin, salt, and a little water to a smooth spreadable batter.
  2. Cook pancakes on a medium oiled tawa, covered briefly.
  3. Flip and cook until both sides are spotted and the center is set.
  4. Serve with chutney.
  5. Thin the batter if it will not spread.
  6. Eat them hot.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Pesarattu is the Andhra whole-green-gram crepe, a close cousin.
  • A spoon of rice flour makes the batter easier and is still gluten-free.

Pairings

  • green chutney
