Moong dal cheela

You come in by Train. India · South Asia.

Pronounced moong dahl CHEE-lah

A soft yellow-lentil pancake, bright with ginger and green chile, less nutty than a besan cheela.

20 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 small cheelas

History

Moong dal cheela is a North Indian pancake made from soaked split yellow moong, ground and cooked the same day. It is not a besan cheela. The dal is the whole batter, not chickpea flour stirred with water. Rajasthani and other northern kitchens use it as a light breakfast. Andhra pesarattu uses whole green moong and has its own page. This one is the split yellow dal of the north.

Cultural background

Soaking is the only wait. There is no ferment to plan the night before. Ginger in the grind is what makes a plain dal batter taste finished. Jaipur homes serve it with a sharp chutney or a spoon of pickle.

Why it tastes this way

Split yellow moong grinds sweet and mild, with a legume aroma that stays fresher than toasted besan. Ginger and green chile cut that sweetness. Cumin adds a warm note without the thyme-like ajwain of a Delhi gram cheela. The pan dries the surface so the cake tastes toasted at the rim and custardy in the center.

How it is made

Soak yellow moong dal, grind it with ginger and chile, and cook small pancakes at once.

Authentic touch

Drain the dal well before grinding. Extra soak water makes a watery crepe that never browns. The batter should drop from a spoon in a ribbon.

How it is used

Serve warm with cilantro chutney. It is breakfast food, eaten with the hands or a fork the same morning it is ground.

Field notes

  • This cheela is ground moong dal, not chickpea flour.
  • Split yellow moong is the usual dal. Whole green moong is the Andhra pesarattu.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup split yellow moong dal
  • 1 inch ginger
  • 1 green chile
  • 1/2 teaspoon cumin seeds
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • Water for soaking and a splash for grinding
  • Neutral oil for the pan

Steps

  1. Rinse the dal and soak it for 2 to 3 hours. Drain thoroughly.
  2. Grind with ginger, chile, cumin, salt, and as little water as you need for a thick, smooth batter.
  3. Cook without an overnight ferment. If the batter looks stiff, add a teaspoon of water.
  4. Oil a medium-hot pan. Spread a small ladle into a neat round and cook until the underside is golden.
  5. Flip and cook until the center is set. Repeat with the remaining batter.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Chopped spinach folded through the batter
  • A pinch of turmeric for color

Pairings

  • Cilantro chutney
  • A lime wedge
  • Sliced tomato
