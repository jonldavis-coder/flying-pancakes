Arriving in Brittany

Breton crepes

You come in by Hot air balloon. France · Western Europe.

Pronounced breh-TON krep

Thin wheat crepes from Brittany, butter and sugar, the cake of Chandeleur.

20 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 4 · 10 crepes

History

The French crepe day is Chandeleur, Candlemas, on 2 February. It is not Mardi Gras, and it is not Shrove Tuesday. Breton wheat crepes are the sweet crepe of Brittany, usually eaten with butter and sugar or a spoon of jam. The savory buckwheat cake is a galette and has its own page. A custom at Chandeleur is to flip a crepe with one hand while holding a coin in the other, as a wish for the year. The wish is not a cooking step.

Cultural background

Crepes are street food and home food in Brittany, cooked on a flat billig. Cider is the regional drink beside them. The wheat crepe is the one for sugar. Buckwheat is the one for ham and cheese.

Why it tastes this way

A thin batter browns in spots and stays flexible. Butter on the crepe, then sugar, makes a sauce as the sugar melts. The egg keeps it tender. Resting the batter hydrates the flour so the sheet does not tear. The flavor is toasted milk and butter, not spice.

How it is made

Flour, egg, milk, water, salt, and melted butter are whisked into a thin batter, rested, and cooked in a very thin layer.

Authentic touch

Say Chandeleur, 2 February, when you mean the French crepe day. Do not call this a Mardi Gras recipe. Spread the batter with a wooden T or swirl the pan. The first crepe still counts as the test.

How it is used

Folded with butter and sugar, or with jam. At a creperie they are the sweet side of the menu. The savory side is the galette.

Field notes

  • Chandeleur, Candlemas, is 2 February. It is the French crepe day, not Mardi Gras.
  • In Brittany the wheat crepe is usually sweet. The buckwheat galette is usually savory.
  • Holding a coin while you flip the crepe is a Chandeleur custom, not part of the batter.

Ingredients

  • 125 g plain wheat flour
  • 2 large eggs
  • 250 ml whole milk
  • 100 ml water
  • 1 pinch fine salt
  • 30 g unsalted butter, melted, plus more for the pan and for serving
  • 3 tbsp sugar, for serving

Steps

  1. Whisk the flour, eggs, milk, water, salt, and melted butter into a smooth thin batter. Rest 20 minutes.
  2. Heat a crepe pan and brush it with butter. Pour a thin layer and swirl.
  3. Cook until the edge lifts and the underside is spotted, then turn and cook the second side for a few seconds.
  4. Brush with butter, sprinkle with sugar, and fold. Repeat with the rest of the batter.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A squeeze of lemon
  • Normandy apple crepes, which are the filled apple version

Pairings

  • Butter and sugar
  • Jam
  • Dry cider
