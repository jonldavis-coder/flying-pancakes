Arriving in Gaziantep

Gaziantep katmer

You come in by Plane. Turkey · Middle East.

Pronounced KAHT-mer

Crisp layered flatbread filled with pistachio and kaymak, which is clotted cream.

40 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · 2 large folded pastries

History

Gaziantep katmer is a layered pastry of thin dough, pistachio, and kaymak, cooked on a griddle or a saj. Kaymak is clotted cream, the thick cream skimmed from heated milk. The city is known for its pistachios, and this breakfast pastry uses them crushed, not as a flavoring extract. It is a stuffed, folded flatbread, not a batter pancake. Bakers stretch the dough, fill it, and cook it to order in the morning.

Cultural background

Katmer is often a shared morning plate, cut into squares beside tea. The dough has to be thin or the cream turns heavy. Home cooks who cannot find kaymak sometimes use a thick cream, and the pastry is then a cousin rather than the Gaziantep version. This recipe asks for kaymak and pistachio.

Why it tastes this way

Pistachio is buttery and slightly bitter, and it stays green if you crush it rather than toast it to a powder. Kaymak tastes of cooked milk, richer and less sour than yogurt. Thin dough on a hot saj shatters at the edge and stays flexible where the cream is. Sugar is light. The pastry should not eat like a soaked baklava.

How it is made

Make a soft dough and rest it. Stretch it very thin on an oiled table, spread kaymak and crushed pistachio and a little sugar in the center, and fold it into a square. Cook on a hot saj or griddle until blistered and golden. Cut and serve warm.

Authentic touch

Use kaymak, and say what it is: clotted cream. Crush pistachios so they stay in pieces. Cook the folded bread on a griddle or saj. A poured pancake cannot hold this filling.

How it is used

Katmer is breakfast in Gaziantep and sweet enough to be dessert. Tea is the partner. It is not a stew bread. The filling is dairy and tree nut, so the plate is neither dairy-free nor nut-free.

Field notes

  • Kaymak is clotted cream, not a soft cheese and not butter.
  • Gaziantep katmer is cooked on a griddle or a saj, a convex metal dome.
  • Pistachio in the filling is a tree nut.

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 cups wheat flour (about 190 g)
  • 1/2 teaspoon fine salt
  • 1/2 cup water
  • 2 tablespoons oil, plus more for stretching
  • 150 g kaymak, clotted cream
  • 3/4 cup crushed pistachios
  • 1 tablespoon sugar

Steps

  1. Mix the flour, salt, water, and oil into a soft dough. Knead until smooth and rest it for 20 minutes.
  2. Divide the dough in half. Oil the table and stretch one piece until it is thin enough to see the table through the center.
  3. Spread kaymak over the center. Kaymak is clotted cream. Scatter pistachios and sugar over the cream. The pistachios are a tree nut.
  4. Fold the dough into a square that seals the filling. This is a layered griddle pastry, not a pourable batter.
  5. Cook on a hot saj or griddle, turning once, until both sides blister and turn golden. Cut into pieces and serve warm. Repeat with the second half.

Pistachios are a tree nut. Kaymak is a fresh dairy food and should be kept cold until you use it. The dough contains wheat.

Eat katmer the morning it is cooked. Refrigerate leftovers promptly because of the cream, and reheat them on a dry pan. The layers will be less crisp. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A thinner layer of kaymak keeps the pastry crisp.
  • Some shops add a little semolina sugar between the folds.
  • Without pistachio the pastry is a different, plainer katmer and would not carry the tree-nut tag.

Pairings

  • Black tea
  • More crushed pistachio
  • A glass of cold water, because the cream is rich
