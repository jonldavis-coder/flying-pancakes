Malawach

You come in by Plane. Israel · Middle East.

Pronounced mah-lah-WAHKH

Flaky laminated flatbread, salty and toasted, with a pull-apart layer at the edge.

35 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 4 skillet flatbreads

History

Malawach is a Yemeni Jewish laminated flatbread. Families brought it from Yemen, and many arrived in Israel in the 1949 to 1950 airlift often called Operation Magic Carpet. In Israeli home kitchens it became a breakfast bread: the dough is layered with fat, chilled, and cooked in a skillet. This recipe uses oil, so the written dough has no dairy. Grated tomato, a hard-boiled egg, and zhug are the plate around it. The origin is Yemeni. The weekday pan on this page is an Israeli one.

Cultural background

The dough is often mixed at night and cooked in the morning, which suits a home kitchen more than a stall. It is richer than a plain pita and flakier than a single-layer chapati. Restaurants later put it on menus. The method here stays with the skillet.

Why it tastes this way

Lamination is the flavor: thin sheets of dough fried lightly in their own oil, so the bread tastes toasted and almost pastry-like without sugar. Salt is the only seasoning in the dough. Grated tomato adds acid, and zhug adds chile and herbs at the table. Those condiments are why one bread can taste different from one house to the next.

How it is made

Mix a soft dough, rest it, then stretch it thin, brush it with oil, and fold it into a packet. Chill the packet. Cook it in a dry skillet from cold-ish dough until both sides are browned and the layers pull apart. Serve with grated tomato.

Authentic touch

Layer the dough and chill it before you cook it, so the flakes separate. Remember both kitchens: the bread's Yemeni origin, and the Israeli home breakfast it became. Oil in this recipe keeps the dough free of dairy.

How it is used

Malawach is breakfast. A typical plate is the bread with grated tomato, zhug, and a hard-boiled egg. Honey is the sweet path. Egg and zhug are accompaniments. Zhug is hot even though the bread is not.

Field notes

  • Malawach is a laminated flatbread of Yemeni Jewish origin, cooked in Israeli home kitchens.
  • This dough uses oil, so the bread itself is dairy-free.
  • Grated tomato and zhug are breakfast partners, not fillings baked inside.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups wheat flour (about 250 g)
  • 3/4 teaspoon fine salt
  • 3/4 cup water
  • 4 tablespoons neutral oil, divided

Steps

  1. Mix the flour, salt, water, and one tablespoon of oil into a soft dough. Knead until smooth and rest it for 15 minutes.
  2. Divide the dough into 4. Stretch one piece into a thin sheet, brush it with oil, and fold it into a small layered packet.
  3. Repeat with the rest. Cover and chill the packets for at least 20 minutes so the layers firm up.
  4. Cook each packet in a dry skillet over medium heat, turning, until both sides are browned and the edge flakes. This is laminated dough, not a batter.
  5. Serve in an Israeli home-breakfast way: with grated tomato, and with zhug or egg if you want them.

The dough contains wheat. Zhug served alongside is a chile sauce. A hard-boiled egg adds egg only on plates that include it.

Refrigerate leftover malawach and reheat it in a dry skillet so the layers crisp. Raw packets can be kept refrigerated for a day before cooking. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Butter or margarine instead of oil changes the fat and may add dairy.
  • Honey instead of tomato makes a sweet breakfast.
  • A thinner packet cooks faster and flakes more. A thick one stays bready.

Pairings

  • Grated fresh tomato
  • Zhug
  • A hard-boiled egg
