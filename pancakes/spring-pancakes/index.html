Spring pancakes

You come in by Train. China · East Asia.

Pronounced chwen BING

Soft wheat wrappers for the start of spring, rolled around shredded vegetables and a savory-sweet sauce.

25 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 wrappers

History

Spring pancakes, chun bing, belong to lichun, the solar term that marks the start of spring, usually near the beginning of February. Northern Chinese tables set out a stack of thin wheat pancakes and a spread of small dishes to roll inside them. The custom is seasonal. It is distinct from the mandarin pancakes served with Peking duck, even though both are thin wheat wrappers. Chun bing come with an assortment, often shredded vegetables, egg, or meat, to welcome the new season. This recipe writes a vegetable filling.

Cultural background

The pancakes are cooked one by one and kept soft under a cloth, ready to wrap. They are not paired and peeled for duck. Lichun food is about the bite-size fillings as much as the cake. A sweet bean sauce or a savory sauce holds the shreds together.

Why it tastes this way

The pancake is mild wheat, a little chewy, so the fillings can lead. Cucumber and carrot taste fresh and sweet, which fits a holiday that celebrates the turn toward spring. Sweet bean sauce adds salt, soy, and a dark sweetness. Scallion sharpens the roll. Because the sheet is cooked only until flexible, it tastes steamed and soft rather than like a fried scallion cake.

How it is made

Griddle soft wheat pancakes and roll them around shredded vegetables and sweet bean sauce for lichun.

Authentic touch

Cook the pancakes pale and stack them covered. A crisp fried bread will not wrap. Warm the stack again in a steamer if they stiffen.

How it is used

Set the pancakes and the fillings out together and let people wrap their own. It is a feast dish for the start of spring, and an accompaniment to the rest of the meal.

Field notes

  • Chun bing are eaten at lichun, the start of spring in the Chinese solar calendar.
  • They are a seasonal wrapping custom, distinct from the pancakes served with Peking duck.

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 cups all-purpose wheat flour
  • 1/2 cup boiling water
  • 2 tablespoons cold water
  • A pinch of salt
  • 1 small cucumber, cut in thin strips
  • 1 carrot, cut in thin strips
  • 2 scallions, shredded
  • 3 tablespoons sweet bean sauce

Steps

  1. Mix the flour and salt with boiling water, then the cold water. Knead to a smooth dough and rest 20 minutes.
  2. Divide into eight pieces and roll each into a thin round.
  3. Cook the rounds one at a time on a dry pan until they look dry and flexible, with only a few brown spots. Stack them under a cloth.
  4. Set out cucumber, carrot, scallion, and sweet bean sauce.
  5. Spread a little sauce on a pancake, add vegetables, and roll. Eat at the lichun table.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A thin omelet shredded into the wrap, which adds egg
  • Mustard tuber for a sharper bite

Pairings

  • Sweet bean sauce
  • Shredded cucumber and carrot
  • A meat dish on the same feast table, kept separate from this filling
