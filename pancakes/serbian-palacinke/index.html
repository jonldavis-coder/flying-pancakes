Pronounced pah-lah-CHEEN-keh

Thin crepes filled with jam, walnuts, or Eurocrem-style chocolate spread.

10 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 crepes

History

Palačinke are the everyday crepe of Serbia and much of the Balkans. Fillings include jam, ground walnuts, and a chocolate-hazelnut spread that became a standard late-twentieth-century filling. The spread is a modern pantry item, not an ancient one. This recipe uses jam. Walnuts and chocolate-hazelnut spread are variations, and both bring nuts.

Cultural background

They are street food and home food, folded in triangles or rolled. A glass of yogurt on the side is common.

Why it tastes this way

The crepe is a blank, slightly sweet sheet. Jam is the old filling. Chocolate-hazelnut spread is the filling many people now actually eat, and it should be described as modern. Walnuts are the regional nut. The batter’s soda water or mineral water, often used in the Balkans, makes the crepe a little lighter.

How it is made

A thin batter with a splash of sparkling water is cooked and filled with jam.

Authentic touch

Carbonated water in the batter should be stirred in at the end. It goes flat if the batter sits all afternoon, and the lift goes with it.

How it is used

Folded, warm.

Field notes

  • Palačinke are the Balkan crepe. The chocolate spread filling is modern.
  • Sparkling water in the batter is a common lightness trick.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup flour
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 cup milk
  • 1/2 cup sparkling water
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • Pinch of salt
  • 1 tablespoon oil
  • Jam, to serve

Steps

  1. Whisk the batter, adding the sparkling water last.
  2. Cook thin crepes immediately.
  3. Fill with jam and fold.
  4. Serve warm.
  5. The bubbles fade if you wait too long.
  6. A lightly oiled pan is enough.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Ground walnuts and sugar are a nutty filling.
  • Chocolate-hazelnut spread is a modern filling and usually contains nuts and dairy.

Pairings

  • jam
  • yogurt
