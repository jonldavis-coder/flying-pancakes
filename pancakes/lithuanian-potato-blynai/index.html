Potato pancakes, crisp, served with sour cream or bacon.

History

Bulviniai blynai are Lithuanian potato pancakes. Like their neighbors’ potato cakes, they are grated potato fried in fat, served with sour cream, bacon, or sometimes a sweet accompaniment. They are a staple supper.

Cultural background

Bacon on the side is common and adds meat. The written cake is vegetarian. Sour cream is the usual sauce.

Why it tastes this way

Potato, fried, is the calorie and the flavor of a northern winter diet. A little flour keeps the cake together for a family skillet. Sour cream cools it. The Baltic potato pancake is a family of dishes. The Lithuanian name is worth keeping so it is not folded into a single “Eastern European latke.”

How it is made

Grated potato, egg, and a little flour are fried as cakes.

Authentic touch

Squeeze, then fry in a real film of oil. Pale, soft cakes are underfried bulviniai blynai.

How it is used

Sour cream, hot.

Field notes

  • These are the potato blynai, not the yeasted wheat ones.
  • Sour cream is the partner to write down first.

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 pounds potatoes
  • 1 small onion
  • 1 large egg
  • 1 tablespoon flour
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • Oil for frying

Steps

  1. Grate and squeeze potato and onion.
  2. Mix with egg, flour, and salt.
  3. Fry until crisp on both sides.
  4. Drain.
  5. Serve with sour cream.
  6. Keep cakes in a single layer so they stay crisp.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Bacon fat is a traditional frying fat and adds meat.
  • Wheat blynai are the yeasted pancakes.

Pairings

  • sour cream
