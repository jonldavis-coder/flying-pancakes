Arriving in Friesland

Flensjes

You come in by Boat. Netherlands · Western Europe.

Pronounced FLEN-syuhs

Paper-thin Frisian crepes, stacked or rolled with sugar.

15 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 12 thin pancakes

History

Flensjes are very thin pancakes, a word and a habit strongly tied to Friesland and also used elsewhere in the Netherlands. They are thinner than a standard pannenkoek and closer to a crepe. They are stacked with sugar or rolled. This page keeps them as a dessert crepe, distinct from the plate-sized pannenkoek and from poffertjes.

Cultural background

A Frisian table might stack them with butter and sugar or with a fruit filling. They are home cooking, made in a small pan so each cake stays thin.

Why it tastes this way

A loose batter cooks fast and tastes of browned butter and milk. Sugar between layers melts into a light syrup. Because they are thin, there is no fluffy crumb to hide undercooking or overmixing. The flavor is delicate, so burnt butter shows immediately.

How it is made

A thin egg batter is cooked in small rounds and stacked with butter and sugar, or rolled.

Authentic touch

Use less batter than you think. The cooked cake should be translucent at the edge. Stack them with sugar while they are hot so the sugar softens.

How it is used

As a dessert or a rich snack, stacked or rolled. They are vegetarian.

Field notes

  • Flensjes are thinner than a Dutch pannenkoek.
  • The name is strongly associated with Friesland.
  • They are often stacked with sugar rather than served one to a plate.

Ingredients

  • 100 g plain wheat flour
  • 2 large eggs
  • 250 ml whole milk
  • 1 pinch fine salt
  • 30 g unsalted butter, plus more for the pan
  • 3 tbsp sugar

Steps

  1. Whisk the flour, eggs, milk, and salt into a very thin batter. Rest 10 minutes.
  2. Cook small thin pancakes in a buttered pan, turning once.
  3. Melt the 30 g butter. Brush each pancake and sprinkle with sugar.
  4. Stack them and serve warm, cut in wedges, or roll them individually.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • An apple cooked soft and layered between the flensjes
  • A lemon squeeze, in the English manner

Pairings

  • Butter and sugar
  • A spoon of jam between layers
  • Tea or coffee
