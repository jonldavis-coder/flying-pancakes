Very thin Dutch pancakes, stacked with sugar or jam.

15 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · about 10 thin pancakes

History

Flensjes are the thin Dutch pancake, closer to a crêpe than a plate-sized pannenkoek. They are often stacked with sugar, jam, or a sweet filling. The word is the diminutive form. They are home dessert cooking rather than the big savory pancake of a pannenkoeken restaurant.

Cultural background

A stack, sugared between the layers, is a familiar way to serve them. They cook fast and are easy to tear if the pan is dry.

Why it tastes this way

More egg and a looser batter than a pannenkoek make a flexible cake that tastes of butter and sugar instead of a meal. Stacking with sugar lets the sugar melt between warm layers. They are the Dutch cake that sits nearest the French crêpe, and the Dutch still keep the name.

How it is made

A thin egg batter is cooked like crêpes and stacked with sugar or jam.

Authentic touch

Pour less batter than you would for a pannenkoek and swirl. If you cannot see the pan color through the batter, it is too thick for a flensje.

How it is used

Warm, in a stack, with sugar or jam.

Field notes

  • Flensjes are the thin ones. Pannenkoeken are the large meal pancakes.
  • Sugar between the layers melts only if the cakes are hot.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup flour
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 1/2 cups milk
  • 1 tablespoon melted butter
  • Pinch of salt
  • Sugar or jam, to serve

Steps

  1. Whisk a thin batter and rest 15 minutes.
  2. Cook very thin pancakes in a buttered pan.
  3. Sprinkle each with sugar or spread jam.
  4. Stack and serve.
  5. Flip only briefly.
  6. The edges should spot brown, not turn brittle.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A cheese stack is a savory turn.
  • Pannenkoeken are larger and sturdier.

Pairings

  • sugar
  • jam
  • powdered sugar
