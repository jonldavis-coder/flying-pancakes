Arriving in Seoul

Bindaetteok

You come in by Plane. South Korea · East Asia.

Pronounced bin-DET-tuck

A coarse mung-bean pancake with pork, sour kimchi, and a toasted sesame edge.

25 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 4 · 6 pancakes

History

Bindaetteok, also called nokdujeon, is Seoul's mung-bean pancake. Split yellow mung beans are soaked, ground coarsely, and fried with pork and kimchi. A spoon of wheat flour appears in some pans. This recipe leaves that flour out, so the gluten-free tag matches the bowl. The pork and a kimchi made with fish sauce and salted shrimp mean it is not vegetarian. It is street food and home food, especially in colder months.

Cultural background

The grind should stay a little gritty. A silky batter is a different cake. Vendors press the rounds flat so the beans crust. The name bindaetteok is the one Seoul menus use. Nokdu simply means mung bean.

Why it tastes this way

Mung bean tastes earthy and sweet when it fries, closer to a fritter than to a wheat jeon. Pork adds fat and salt. Kimchi adds acid and mild chile. Sesame oil on the pan perfumes the crust. Because there is no wheat gluten, the cake crumbles at the edge in a way a pajeon does not, and that crumble is part of the pleasure.

How it is made

Soak and coarsely grind mung beans, fold in pork and kimchi, and pan-fry thick savory cakes.

Authentic touch

Grind the beans coarse and drain them well. A wet, fine puree will not crust. Fry in enough oil that the edge sputters.

How it is used

Eat them hot, cut or whole, with a vinegary dip. They are a snack or a side with rice, not a dessert.

Field notes

  • Bindaetteok is a mung-bean pancake. Nokdu is the bean.
  • This version skips wheat flour so the gluten-free tag is honest. Many stalls add a spoonful.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup split yellow mung beans
  • 80 grams ground pork
  • 1/3 cup chopped kimchi with fish sauce and salted shrimp
  • 2 scallions, chopped
  • 1 garlic clove, minced
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • Sesame oil and neutral oil

Steps

  1. Rinse and soak the mung beans for 3 hours. Drain very well.
  2. Grind coarsely with a spoon or two of water. The texture should be nubbly, not a smooth milk.
  3. Mix in pork, kimchi, scallions, garlic, and salt.
  4. Heat a pan with neutral oil and a teaspoon of sesame oil. Spread a small thick round.
  5. Fry on medium until both sides are deep brown and the pork is cooked through. Repeat.

Cook the pork until no pink remains in the center of each cake.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A spoon of wheat flour, which would remove the gluten-free tag
  • Bean sprouts folded in, a common addition

Pairings

  • Vinegar-soy dip
  • Steamed rice
  • Raw onion
