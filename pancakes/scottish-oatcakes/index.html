Scottish oatcakes

You come in by Train. United Kingdom · Western Europe.

Pronounced SKOT-ish OAT-cakes

Thin, dry, unleavened oatcakes from a Scottish girdle.

15 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 6 · 12 oatcakes

History

Scottish oatcakes are thin unleavened oat breads cooked on a girdle. They are a staple accompaniment, not a dessert pancake, and they are not the yeasted oatcakes of Staffordshire or Derbyshire. Oats were the grain that reliably grew in much of Scotland. This recipe uses oatmeal, salt, a little fat, and hot water. There is no wheat flour in it.

Cultural background

An oatcake is travel food and table food. It is eaten with cheese, soup, or fish, and it keeps better than a soft pancake. The texture should be crisp after cooling, not floppy.

Why it tastes this way

Toasted oatmeal is nutty even though the recipe has no nuts. Salt and a little fat keep it from tasting raw. The girdle dries the cake. Hot water softens the oats just enough to roll. They taste plain on purpose, because cheese or a salty fish is supposed to do the seasoning.

How it is made

Medium oatmeal is mixed with salt, a pinch of baking soda, oil, and hot water, rolled thin, cut, and baked on a dry or lightly oiled girdle until firm.

Authentic touch

Roll them thin and cook them slowly. Use oats milled apart from wheat if you need them gluten-free. Ordinary oatmeal is often handled near wheat, so the gluten-free label depends on the oats you buy.

How it is used

With soup, cheese, or smoked fish. The cakes themselves are vegan. A herring or a buttered slice changes that plate.

Field notes

  • These are unleavened and crisp. Staffordshire oatcakes are yeasted and soft.
  • They are an accompaniment as often as they are a snack.
  • The recipe has no wheat unless you choose the variation that adds it.

Ingredients

  • 200 g medium oatmeal, plus more for rolling
  • 1/2 tsp fine salt
  • 1/4 tsp baking soda
  • 20 ml neutral oil
  • 120 ml hot water

Steps

  1. Stir the oatmeal, salt, and baking soda. Add the oil and hot water and mix to a stiff dough. Rest 5 minutes.
  2. Roll or press the dough thin on an oatmeal-dusted board. Cut rounds or triangles.
  3. Cook on a medium-low girdle until the edges lift and the cake is dry and pale gold, then turn and finish the other side.
  4. Cool on a rack until crisp. Eat with cheese, soup, or fish.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A spoon of wheat flour, which is common and removes the gluten-free diet
  • Fine oatmeal for a smoother cake, or pinhead oats kept coarse

Pairings

  • Cheddar or a hard cheese
  • Smoked fish
  • A bowl of soup
