Scottish Oatcakes

History

Scottish oatcakes are one of the old breads of Scotland, cooked on a girdle, made of oats because oats ripened in a cold, wet climate where wheat was less reliable. They were everyday bread and travel food. A modern oatcake can be baked or griddled. This one is cooked on a dry girdle and uses only oats, so it stays free of wheat.

Cultural background

They are eaten with cheese, butter, herring, or soup. They are a bread, not a porridge and not a Staffordshire pancake.

Why it tastes this way

The flavor is the oat itself, especially if the meal is freshly ground. Little fat carries that flavor and helps them crisp. Salt is the seasoning. The agricultural reason is the climate: oats tolerate cool summers. A crisp oatcake keeps, which mattered in a kitchen without soft bread every morning.

How it is made

Oat flour, salt, a little fat, and hot water are rolled thin and cooked on a dry griddle.

Authentic touch

Roll them thin and cook them slowly until they are dry, not browned black. They finish crisping as they cool. If they bend, they need another minute.

How it is used

With cheese, butter, or soup. They are a staple bread.

Field notes

  • This version is oats only, so it is gluten-free if your oats are not contaminated with wheat.
  • Scottish oatcakes and Staffordshire oatcakes share a grain and not a texture.

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 cups oat flour
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 tablespoon oil
  • 1/3 cup hot water

Steps

  1. Mix oat flour and salt. Add oil and hot water to a dough.
  2. Roll thin between parchment if they crack, and cut rounds.
  3. Cook on a dry medium-low griddle until the edges look dry and both sides are faintly colored.
  4. Cool on a rack until crisp.
  5. Store airtight.
  6. Serve with cheese or butter.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A spoon of wheat flour makes them easier to roll and adds gluten.
  • Ulster oatcakes are the Irish relative, often a little softer.

Pairings

  • soup
