Syrniki

You come in by Train. Russia · Eastern Europe.

Pronounced syr-NEE-kee

Pan-fried Russian farmer-cheese cakes, with sour cream and jam.

15 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 cakes

History

Syrniki are Russian pan-fried cakes of tvorog, egg, a little flour, and sugar. They are breakfast and dessert, not yeasted blini and not potato latkes. The same curd-cheese idea appears in other countries, including the Estonian kohupiim cakes, which have their own page. This one is the Russian breakfast cake. Flour is the binder, so they are not gluten-free.

Cultural background

Tvorog is a staple cheese. Frying it into cakes is a home method, done in butter, and served as soon as the crust is brown. Sour cream and jam are the plate.

Why it tastes this way

The cheese is tangy and rich. Sugar makes them dessert-like without hiding the tang. A thin flour coating and a little flour inside create a crust. The center stays soft, almost custardy, if the cheese was not overworked. Burnt butter tastes harsh, so the pan should be medium, not smoking.

How it is made

Drained farmer cheese is mixed with egg, sugar, and flour, shaped into small cakes, floured, and fried in butter.

Authentic touch

Drain the tvorog until it is closer to a paste than a yogurt. Wet cheese will not hold a cake. Flour the outside lightly so the crust browns.

How it is used

Hot, with sour cream and jam, at breakfast or as a sweet course.

Field notes

  • Syrniki are fried cheese cakes, not blini.
  • Tvorog, a dry farmer cheese, is the main ingredient. Flour only binds them.
  • Sour cream and jam are the usual plate.

Ingredients

  • 400 g tvorog or farmer cheese, well drained
  • 1 large egg
  • 2 tbsp sugar
  • 1/4 tsp fine salt
  • 40 g plain wheat flour, plus more for shaping
  • 30 g unsalted butter, for frying
  • Sour cream and jam, for serving

Steps

  1. Mash the cheese with the egg, sugar, salt, and flour until it holds together. Do not beat it into a glue.
  2. Shape eight small cakes with floured hands and dust the outsides with flour.
  3. Fry in butter over medium heat until both sides are golden and the center is hot.
  4. Serve with sour cream and jam.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Raisins in the cheese
  • Estonian kohupiim pancakes, a related curd cake on its own page

Pairings

  • Sour cream
  • Jam
  • Honey, which adds a honey allergen
