Pronounced SYR-nee-kee

Pan-fried farmer-cheese cakes, lightly sweet.

15 min prep · 12 min cook · Serves 4 · about 8 cakes

History

Syrniki are fried cakes of tvorog, the farmer cheese of Russia and Ukraine, bound with egg and a little flour. The name comes from syr, for the cheese. They are a breakfast across the region, served with sour cream, jam, or honey. They are not blintzes. The cheese is the cake, not a filling.

Cultural background

Tvorog is drier than many supermarket ricottas. If you use ricotta, drain it overnight or the cakes will not hold. That is a substitute, and it should be drained.

Why it tastes this way

Tvorog tastes mildly sour and milky, which is the flavor under the browned crust. A little sugar makes them breakfast-sweet without turning them into dessert pastries. Flour is only the binder. The cheese is there because dairy regions kept tvorog as a way to use milk. Sour cream on top doubles the dairy and softens the crust.

How it is made

Farmer cheese, egg, sugar, and a little flour are shaped into thick patties and fried.

Authentic touch

Drain the cheese until it is crumbly, not wet. Flour your hands. Fry on medium-low so the center heats before the crust blackens.

How it is used

Sour cream and jam or honey.

Field notes

  • Syrniki are cheese cakes, in the literal sense, not a New York cheesecake.
  • Wet ricotta must be drained or the patties collapse.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup well-drained tvorog or farmer cheese
  • 1 large egg
  • 2 tablespoons sugar
  • 1/4 cup flour, plus more for shaping
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • Oil or butter for frying

Steps

  1. Mash the cheese with egg, sugar, salt, and flour.
  2. Shape thick patties with floured hands.
  3. Fry over medium-low until deep golden on both sides and hot in the center.
  4. Serve with sour cream.
  5. They should not ooze liquid. If they do, the cheese was too wet.
  6. A few raisins can be mixed in.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Raisins in the cheese are common.
  • Ukrainian and Russian breakfasts both claim syrniki. The cake is shared.

Pairings

  • sour cream
  • jam
  • honey
