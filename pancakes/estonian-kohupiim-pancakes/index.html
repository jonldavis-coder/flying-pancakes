Estonian kohupiim pancakes

You come in by Boat. Estonia · Eastern Europe.

Pronounced KO-hu-peem

Small fried cakes of Estonian kohupiim, a curd cheese, with jam.

15 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 cakes

History

Kohupiim is Estonia's curd cheese, an acid-set fresh cheese. These pancakes fry that cheese with egg, a little flour, and sugar. They sit near Russian syrniki on the map and they are not the same page. This is the Estonian breakfast or dessert cake, served with jam or sour cream. Flour means they are not gluten-free. They are vegetarian and not vegan.

Cultural background

Kohupiim is used in pastries and eaten plain. Frying it into small cakes is a home method, close to other curd cakes of the region without being a copy of one city's recipe. Jam is the usual sweet note.

Why it tastes this way

The cheese is tangy and slightly grainy if you do not mash it to glue. Sugar rounds it. A thin crust from flour and butter contrasts with the soft center. Jam adds fruit acid. They taste fresher than a cheesecake and richer than a flour pancake.

How it is made

Kohupiim is mashed with egg, sugar, and flour, shaped into small cakes, and fried in butter.

Authentic touch

Use a dry curd. If you only have cottage cheese, drain it thoroughly and mash it. Call it a stand-in, not kohupiim. Serve jam.

How it is used

Warm, at breakfast or as a sweet snack, with jam or sour cream.

Field notes

  • Kohupiim is Estonian curd cheese.
  • These are fried cheese cakes, related to syrniki and written as their own recipe.
  • Jam or sour cream is the serving.

Ingredients

  • 400 g kohupiim or well-drained farmer cheese
  • 1 large egg
  • 2 tbsp sugar
  • 1/4 tsp fine salt
  • 3 tbsp plain wheat flour
  • 30 g unsalted butter, for frying
  • Jam, for serving

Steps

  1. Mash the cheese with the egg, sugar, salt, and flour until it holds together.
  2. Shape eight small cakes.
  3. Fry in butter over medium heat until both sides are golden and the center is hot.
  4. Serve with jam.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Raisins in the cheese
  • Russian syrniki, the neighboring curd cake

Pairings

  • Jam
  • Sour cream
  • A spoon of honey, which adds a honey allergen
