This stop is on Ramadan and the griddle

Pronounced KAH-tah-yef

Small yeasted pancakes, cooked on one side, filled with cheese or nuts and sweetened.

1 hr 10 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 8 · about 16 pieces

History

Qatayef are Ramadan pancakes of the Levant and Egypt, cooked on one side so they stay flexible, filled, and either fried or baked and soaked in syrup. Cheese and walnut fillings are both traditional. This recipe uses a sweet cheese filling so the written cake has no tree nuts. Walnuts are the variation. No one capital owns the pancake. It is a shared Ramadan sweet.

Cultural background

Street vendors and home kitchens both make them in the evenings of the fast. The bubbles on one side are the pocket that holds the filling.

Why it tastes this way

A yeasted batter cooked on one side stays soft enough to pinch shut around a filling. Syrup of sugar, water, and a little orange blossom or lemon is the perfume of Levantine sweets. Ramadan nights are when the cake appears, after the fast, which is why it is a feast food and not a weekday breakfast. Cheese tastes milky and mild under the syrup. Walnuts taste of oil and bitterness.

How it is made

A yeast batter is cooked on one side, filled with sweet cheese, pinched, baked, and dipped in syrup.

Authentic touch

Cook only one side, until the top is matte and bubbly. Pinch them shut as soon as they are cool enough to handle, before they dry. Bake or fry after filling, then dip briefly in syrup.

How it is used

Warm or at room temperature, during Ramadan and whenever you make the effort.

Field notes

  • Qatayef are cooked on one side so they can be pinched around a filling.
  • They are a shared Ramadan sweet of the Levant and Egypt.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup flour
  • 1 teaspoon instant yeast
  • 1 teaspoon sugar
  • 1/4 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1 cup warm water
  • 1 cup fresh unsalted cheese or well-drained ricotta
  • 1 tablespoon sugar, for the cheese
  • 1/2 cup sugar and 1/2 cup water, for syrup
  • 1 teaspoon lemon juice

Steps

  1. Whisk flour, yeast, sugar, baking powder, and water. Rest 45 minutes.
  2. Cook small rounds on a lightly oiled medium pan on one side only, until the top is bubbly and set.
  3. Mix the cheese and 1 tablespoon sugar. Fill and pinch the cakes shut.
  4. Bake at 375°F for 10 minutes.
  5. Simmer the syrup ingredients 5 minutes and dip the cakes briefly.
  6. Serve warm.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Chopped walnuts with cinnamon and sugar are a classic filling and add tree nuts.
  • Frying the filled pancakes is the richer method. Baking is the one written here.

Pairings

  • orange blossom water
