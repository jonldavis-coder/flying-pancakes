Arriving in Osaka

Takoyaki

You come in by Plane. Japan · East Asia.

Pronounced tah-koh-YAH-kee

Crisp-edged batter balls with a piece of octopus in the center, finished with sauce and bonito.

20 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 16 balls

History

Takoyaki are octopus batter balls from Osaka, cooked in a pan of round molds. They are a cousin of the savory batter family that also produced okonomiyaki, and they are not a flat cake. Octopus, pickled ginger, and tenkasu sit in a thin wheat batter. The balls are turned with a pick until the outside browns and the inside stays soft. Octopus is a mollusk. This page tags shellfish and names the octopus in the recipe. Bonito flakes on top add fish. The snack is not vegetarian.

Cultural background

Osaka stalls sell them by the boat-shaped plate. The turn matters: an unturned ball is raw on top and burnt below. Mayonnaise and a brown sauce are the usual finish. No brand is required. Aonori and bonito complete the plate.

Why it tastes this way

The batter takes on dashi, so it tastes lightly marine even before the octopus. Octopus itself is sweet and chewy if it is not overcooked into rubber. Pickled ginger and scallion cut the softness. Crisped edges of batter contrast with a custardy center. Sauce adds sweetness, and bonito adds a smoky fish finish. The shape keeps a pocket of filling that a flat pancake would spread out.

How it is made

Pour a dashi batter into a takoyaki pan, add octopus and ginger, and turn the balls until they are round and browned.

Authentic touch

Fill the molds with batter first, add octopus, then keep turning. If you drop the octopus in late, it sits raw on the surface. Oil the pan well.

How it is used

Eat them hot from the plate, with picks. They are street food. The center stays very hot.

Field notes

  • Takoyaki are molded balls, a cousin of Osaka's savory batters, not a flat pancake.
  • The filling is octopus. The allergen tag is shellfish, and bonito on top is fish.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup all-purpose wheat flour
  • 1 egg
  • 1 1/2 cups bonito dashi
  • 150 grams cooked octopus, cut in bite-size pieces
  • 2 tablespoons pickled ginger, chopped
  • 2 scallions, chopped
  • 2 tablespoons tenkasu (tempura bits), optional
  • Neutral oil
  • Sauce, mayonnaise, aonori, and bonito flakes to finish

Steps

  1. Whisk flour, egg, and dashi into a thin batter. Stir in ginger and scallion.
  2. Oil a hot takoyaki pan. Fill each mold with batter and add a piece of octopus.
  3. Cook until the bottoms set, then break the edges and turn each ball with a pick.
  4. Keep turning until the balls are golden and the batter is cooked through, adding a few tenkasu if you use them.
  5. Pile them on a plate, add sauce, mayonnaise, aonori, and bonito, and eat while hot.

Use octopus that has been kept cold, and cook the batter until the center is no longer liquid. The filling stays hot after cooking.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A cube of cheese in place of one octopus piece, which adds dairy
  • Extra pickled ginger

Pairings

  • Savory brown sauce
  • Mayonnaise
  • Aonori and bonito flakes
