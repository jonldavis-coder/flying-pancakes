Pronounced rah-TSOO-khee

Small yeasted pancakes with apple, dusted with sugar.

1 hr 10 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · about 12 cakes

History

Racuchy are small Polish yeast pancakes, often with apple in the batter, served with sugar or cream. They are a home dessert, fluffier than naleśniki. Yeast distinguishes them from a baking-powder pancake.

Cultural background

Apple racuchy are the version most people mean when they want the fruit. Plain ones exist. This recipe includes the apple.

Why it tastes this way

Yeast gives a slight beer-like aroma and a soft rise that baking powder does not copy exactly. Apple adds moisture and perfume. Sugar on the hot cakes sticks to the crust. They are a fruit pancake from a kitchen that kept apples through winter, which is why dried or storage apples both make sense.

How it is made

A yeast batter with diced apple is risen and fried as small cakes, then sugared.

Authentic touch

Fold in small apple dice, not big chunks, and let the batter rise. Fry on medium-low so the yeasted center cooks.

How it is used

Warm, with sugar or sour cream.

Field notes

  • Racuchy are yeasted and often fruity. They are not naleśniki.
  • Small cakes cook through. Large ones stay doughy.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup warm milk
  • 1 teaspoon instant yeast
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • 1 1/2 cups flour
  • 1 egg
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1 apple, finely diced
  • Oil for frying
  • Sugar, to serve

Steps

  1. Mix milk, yeast, sugar, flour, egg, and salt. Rest 45 minutes.
  2. Fold in the apple.
  3. Fry small cakes in a little oil over medium-low until cooked through.
  4. Drain.
  5. Dust with sugar.
  6. Serve warm.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Without apple they are still racuchy, just plainer.
  • Naleśniki are the thin crepes.

Pairings

  • sugar
  • sour cream
