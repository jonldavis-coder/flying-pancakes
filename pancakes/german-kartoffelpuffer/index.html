Pronounced kar-TOF-fel-poof-er

Crisp potato pancakes served with applesauce.

20 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · about 10 cakes

History

Kartoffelpuffer are German potato pancakes, also called Reibekuchen in the Rhineland. They are Christmas-market food and home food, served with applesauce. Grated potato, onion, egg, and a little flour are fried flat. Pennsylvania Dutch potato pancakes come from this family.

Cultural background

At a market they are eaten standing up, with a cup of applesauce. At home they are a side or a meatless supper. Sour cream is less universal here than applesauce.

Why it tastes this way

Potato starch browns into a sweet crust, and applesauce is the tart partner that makes the richness make sense. A little flour keeps the market cake in one piece while you walk. The Rhineland name Reibekuchen points at grating, which is the whole method. Onion is the savor. Christmas markets did not invent the cake. They kept it in public.

How it is made

Grated potato and onion are mixed with egg and flour and fried crisp.

Authentic touch

Squeeze the potato, and fry in enough fat that the edge laces. Press them thin. Thick puffer steam.

How it is used

Applesauce. Sugar is a lesser habit. Applesauce is the one to write down.

Field notes

  • Applesauce is the classic partner, including at Christmas markets.
  • Reibekuchen and Kartoffelpuffer are regional names, not two unrelated foods.

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 pounds potatoes
  • 1 small onion
  • 1 large egg
  • 2 tablespoons flour
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • Oil for frying

Steps

  1. Grate and squeeze the potato and onion.
  2. Mix with egg, flour, and salt.
  3. Fry thin cakes in hot oil until deep brown.
  4. Drain.
  5. Serve with applesauce.
  6. Salt a little more if the applesauce is very sweet.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Reibekuchen is the Rhineland name for the same idea.
  • Pennsylvania Dutch potato pancakes are the American descendant.

Pairings
