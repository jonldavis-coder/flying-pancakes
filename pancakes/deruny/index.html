Deruny

You come in by Train. Ukraine · Eastern Europe.

Pronounced deh-ROO-nih

Ukrainian grated-potato pancakes, fried crisp and served with sour cream.

20 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 pancakes

History

Deruny are Ukrainian potato pancakes made from grated raw potato, onion, egg, and flour. They are everyday food, served with sour cream. They share a technique with Polish placki, Belarusian draniki, and Ashkenazi latkes, and they are not the same plate. Latkes on this journal are the Hanukkah cake, with that history. Deruny are the Ukrainian home pancake. Flour means they are not gluten-free.

Cultural background

A cast-iron pan and a fine grater are the tools. Some cooks add a little salo, cured pork fat. This recipe fries in oil and keeps the cake vegetarian, with sour cream at the table.

Why it tastes this way

Onion and potato fry together so the edge is sweet and crisp. Egg and flour hold the shreds. Sour cream cools the crust. Salt and pepper are the seasoning. If the batter sits, the potato darkens and the flavor dulls, so cook it promptly.

How it is made

Potato and onion are grated, mixed with egg, flour, and salt, and fried in oil until brown.

Authentic touch

Grate onion into the potato and fry the cakes thin enough to crisp. Sour cream is the serving. A pork-fat fry is a real variation and is not this vegetarian recipe.

How it is used

Hot, with sour cream, as a meal. They are savory.

Field notes

  • Deruny are the Ukrainian grated-potato pancakes.
  • They are related to draniki, placki, and latkes, and the histories are not identical.
  • This recipe is fried in oil and served with sour cream.

Ingredients

  • 600 g starchy potatoes, peeled
  • 1 small onion
  • 1 large egg
  • 2 tbsp plain wheat flour
  • 1 tsp fine salt
  • 1/4 tsp black pepper
  • Neutral oil, for frying
  • Sour cream, for serving

Steps

  1. Grate the potatoes and onion. Squeeze lightly, leaving them moist. Stir in the egg, flour, salt, and pepper.
  2. Heat a shallow layer of oil over medium-high heat.
  3. Fry flattened rounds until deep brown on both sides and cooked through.
  4. Drain briefly and serve with sour cream.

Stay with the hot oil. Cut one cake open to be sure the potato is cooked.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Fried in salo, which adds meat and removes the vegetarian diet
  • Belarusian draniki, the next-door potato cake with its own page

Pairings

  • Sour cream
  • Dill
  • A mushroom sauce
