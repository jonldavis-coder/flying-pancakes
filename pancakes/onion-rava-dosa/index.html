Arriving in Udupi, Karnataka

Onion rava dosa

You come in by Train. India · South Asia.

Pronounced UN-yun RAH-vah DOH-sah

Crisp semolina lace crowded with sweet onion, green chile, and curry leaf.

25 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 onion dosas

History

Onion rava dosa keeps the unfermented semolina batter and puts onion in the sheet itself. Udupi hotel kitchens, which sent many South Indian breakfasts to other cities, treat it as its own dosa on the board, next to a plain rava dosa. The onion is mixed through or scattered on the pour so it fries into the lace. It is still wheat semolina, not a fermented rice and urad dosa with onions on the side.

Cultural background

Cut the onion small enough to cook in the time the lace crisps. Large raw chunks steam and leave pale pockets. A pinch of ginger is common in Udupi batters and keeps the onion from tasting flat.

Why it tastes this way

Onion fries in the oil at the edge of the batter and turns sweet. Semolina still supplies the wheat toast and the brittle holes. Chile and curry leaf cut that sweetness. Because there is no overnight souring, the onion stays bright instead of fading into a fermented tang.

How it is made

Mix a thin semolina and rice-flour batter, fold in plenty of onion, and pour it loosely on a hot griddle.

Authentic touch

Scatter the onion as you pour, then leave the pan alone until the rim lifts. Stirring after that pulls the lace apart.

How it is used

Serve open-faced so the onion stays visible, with coconut chutney. Folding is fine once the sheet is crisp.

Field notes

  • Onion rava dosa is still a semolina batter. The onion does not change the grain.
  • Udupi restaurants often list it apart from both plain dosa and plain rava dosa.

Ingredients

  • 1/2 cup fine wheat semolina
  • 1/3 cup rice flour
  • 1 1/2 cups cold water
  • 1 medium onion, finely chopped
  • 1 green chile, minced
  • 1 teaspoon grated ginger
  • 10 curry leaves, chopped
  • 1/2 teaspoon cumin seeds
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • Neutral oil for the griddle

Steps

  1. Stir semolina, rice flour, salt, and water until smooth and thin. Rest 15 minutes.
  2. Fold in onion, chile, ginger, curry leaves, and cumin. The batter should look busy with onion and still pour from a ladle.
  3. Heat a griddle well. Oil it. Pour the batter in ribbons so holes remain, carrying onion along with each pour.
  4. Add extra onion to any bare spot. Drizzle oil and cook until the onion browns and the sheet snaps at the edge.
  5. Remove it whole. Stir the bowl before the next ladle so the onion does not settle at the bottom.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A teaspoon of grated ginger in the batter
  • A few cashews on one dosa, which adds tree nut

Pairings

  • Coconut chutney
  • Sambar
  • A small bowl of sugar and ghee for people who want a sweet bite
