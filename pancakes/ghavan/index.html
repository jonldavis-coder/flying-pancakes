Pronounced GHAH-vahn

A soft, white rice crepe from the Konkan coast, unfermented.

20 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · about 8 crepes

History

Ghavan is a Maharashtrian rice crepe, especially of the Konkan coast, made from soaked rice ground with water and cooked soft, in the family of neer dosa. It is often served with coconut chutney or a spicy potato dish. There is no dal ferment. The crepe is mild.

Cultural background

It is breakfast. The rice should be ground smooth. A lumpy ghavan is unpleasant and not traditional.

Why it tastes this way

Fresh rice batter without souring tastes sweet and delicate, a coastal choice where rice and coconut are the pantry. The Konkan coast’s food is not the same as a Deccan millet bread, and ghavan shows that: a soft white crepe, not a spicy thick pancake. Chutney brings the heat the batter does not have.

How it is made

Soaked rice is ground fine with water and cooked as soft crepes.

Authentic touch

Grind longer than you think. The batter should feel silky. Cook it pale. Browning it deeply makes it a cracker.

How it is used

With coconut chutney.

Field notes

  • Ghavan is an unfermented Maharashtrian rice crepe.
  • Smooth grinding is the step that matters most.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup rice, soaked 4 hours
  • 3/4 cup water
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • Oil

Steps

  1. Grind rice, water, and salt until completely smooth. The batter should be thin.
  2. Cook soft crepes on a medium oiled nonstick pan until they lift.
  3. Fold and serve.
  4. Do not aim for a dark crust.
  5. Coconut chutney is the partner.
  6. Eat them immediately.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Neer dosa is the Karnataka cousin.
  • A pinch of cumin in the batter is a home variation.

Pairings

  • coconut chutney
