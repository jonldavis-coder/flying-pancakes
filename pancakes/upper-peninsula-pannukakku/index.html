Pronounced PAHN-noo-kahk-koo

A baked custard pancake, browned at the edge and creamy in the center.

10 min prep · 30 min cook · Serves 8 · one 9-by-13-inch pan

History

Finnish immigrants settled in Michigan’s Upper Peninsula and in northern Minnesota to work mines and logging. They brought pannukakku, an oven pancake closer to a custard than to a flipped stack. In the Upper Peninsula it is still a community and home breakfast, sometimes baked in a large pan for a crowd. The Finnish original has its own page. This is the Upper Midwest pan.

Cultural background

It is cut into squares and served with butter, jam, or cardamom sugar. Cardamom is a Finnish baking spice that survived the move.

Why it tastes this way

An oven pancake puffs at the edge and stays custardy because it is mostly milk and egg, with just enough flour to set. That texture fits a cold climate breakfast: one pan, no standing at a griddle, and a dish that feeds a household. Cardamom came into Finnish baking through trade and stayed in American Finnish kitchens as the smell of holiday bread and of this pancake.

How it is made

A thin batter of milk, egg, flour, and sugar is poured into a hot buttered pan and baked.

Authentic touch

Bake it in a preheated buttered pan so the edges fry. If the pan is cold, the bottom steams and turns gray.

How it is used

Butter and jam, or sugar and a pinch of cardamom.

Field notes

  • Finnish settlement in the Upper Peninsula is why this oven pancake is a regional American breakfast.
  • Cardamom is a traditional Finnish baking spice, not a random addition.

Ingredients

  • 3 large eggs
  • 2 cups milk
  • 1 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1/4 cup sugar
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 teaspoon ground cardamom
  • 3 tablespoons butter

Steps

  1. Heat the oven to 425°F. Put the butter in a 9-by-13-inch pan and melt it in the oven.
  2. Whisk eggs, milk, flour, sugar, salt, and cardamom into a smooth batter.
  3. Pour the batter into the hot buttered pan.
  4. Bake about 25 to 30 minutes, until puffed and browned at the edges.
  5. It will settle when cut.
  6. Serve warm with jam.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • The Finnish pannukakku page is the same family baked at home in Finland.
  • A handful of berries in the pan is a summer change.

Pairings

  • butter
  • cardamom sugar
