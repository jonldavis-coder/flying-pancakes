A small, soft potato flatbread used as a wrapper.

25 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 5 · 10 small breads

History

Lompe are small Norwegian potato lefses, softer and smaller than holiday lefse, famously wrapped around a hot dog as a pølse med lompe. The bread itself is potato and flour. The hot dog is a modern filling and is not required to cook the bread. This recipe is the wrapper.

Cultural background

They are everyday bread in many Norwegian homes, cooked on a dry pan and stacked in a towel. Butter is a topping, not part of this dough.

Why it tastes this way

A higher proportion of potato to flour makes lompe softer and milder than a thin festive lefse. They bend around a sausage without cracking, which is why the kiosk version works. The flavor is almost neutral, a warm potato bread, so the filling seasons it.

How it is made

Mashed potato and flour are rolled into small rounds and cooked on a dry griddle.

Authentic touch

Cook them dry, not in a pool of fat, and keep them covered. They are supposed to be pale with a few spots, not toasted hard.

How it is used

As a bread, with butter, or wrapped around a sausage if that is the meal you want.

Field notes

  • Lompe around a hot dog is a real Norwegian snack. The bread can be eaten without the sausage.
  • Keep them soft under a towel.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups plain mashed potato, cooled
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 3/4 cup all-purpose flour

Steps

  1. Mix potato, salt, and flour into a soft dough.
  2. Roll small thin rounds.
  3. Cook on a dry griddle until lightly spotted.
  4. Stack in a towel.
  5. Serve warm.
  6. They stiffen if they cool uncovered.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Lefse is the larger, often richer potato bread.
  • A little barley flour is used in some regions.

Pairings

  • butter
  • sausage
