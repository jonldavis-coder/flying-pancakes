Pronounced KY-zer-shmahrn

A torn, soufflé-like pancake with raisins, dusted with sugar.

20 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · one pan, about 4 servings

History

Kaiserschmarrn is an Austrian torn pancake, associated with the Habsburg table and now a standard Alpine dessert and lunch. The name refers to the emperor. Stories about which emperor and why the pancake was torn are anecdotes, and they are not repeated here as fact. The method is solid: a thick, egg-rich pancake, torn in the pan, sugared, often with raisins, served with plum compote.

Cultural background

It is eaten as a meal in mountain huts as well as a dessert. Plum compote, Zwetschkenröster, is the classic side.

Why it tastes this way

Separated eggs make it puff, and tearing it creates more browned surface for powdered sugar. Raisins plump in the batter. Plum compote is tart, which a sweet torn pancake needs. The Alpine hut version is hearty because people eat it after being outside in the cold, not because the batter is different.

How it is made

A soufflé batter with raisins is cooked in a wide pan, torn, and sugared.

Authentic touch

Whip the whites and fold them in. When the underside is brown, tear the pancake with two forks and turn the pieces. Do not try to flip it whole.

How it is used

With powdered sugar and plum compote or another tart fruit.

Field notes

  • The imperial anecdotes are left out because they conflict. The dish is the torn pancake.
  • Plum compote is the usual fruit.

Ingredients

  • 4 large eggs, separated
  • 2 tablespoons sugar
  • 1 cup milk
  • 1 cup flour
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1/3 cup raisins
  • 2 tablespoons butter
  • Powdered sugar, to serve

Steps

  1. Beat yolks with sugar, milk, flour, and salt. Fold in raisins and the whipped whites.
  2. Melt butter in a wide skillet and pour in the batter.
  3. Cook covered on medium until the bottom is brown and the top is set.
  4. Tear into pieces with forks and turn them to brown.
  5. Dust with powdered sugar.
  6. Serve with plum compote.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Without raisins it is still Kaiserschmarrn.
  • Palatschinken are the thin Austrian crepes, a different dessert.

Pairings

  • powdered sugar
  • plum compote
