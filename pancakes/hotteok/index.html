Pronounced ho-TUHK

A stuffed street pancake with brown sugar, cinnamon, and nuts, pressed crisp.

1 hr 10 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 6 · 6 cakes

History

Hotteok is a Korean street pancake: a yeasted dough filled with brown sugar and cinnamon, pressed flat on a griddle so the sugar melts. Seeds or nuts are common in the filling. Vendors sell them in cold weather. The dough is wheat. This version uses a seed filling so it can stay free of a particular nut, and it notes peanuts and walnuts as the stall variations.

Cultural background

The cake is eaten hot, wrapped in a cup, before the sugar hardens. It is a snack, not a meal.

Why it tastes this way

Yeast dough tastes bready, and the sugar melts into a syrup inside because the cake is pressed on a hot, oiled griddle. Cinnamon is the perfume. The street context is winter: a hot sweet cake in the hand. Nuts at a stall add crunch and are optional. The contrast of a crisp face and a molten center is the whole trick.

How it is made

A yeasted dough is filled with brown sugar and cinnamon, then cooked and pressed on a griddle.

Authentic touch

Seal the filling well and press the cake with a spatula only after the bottom has set. If you press too soon, the sugar leaks and burns. Eat it carefully. The center is hot.

How it is used

Immediately, in a napkin.

Field notes

  • Hotteok is a filled street pancake, eaten hot.
  • The sugar center stays molten. Warn anyone you hand it to.

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 cups flour
  • 1 teaspoon instant yeast
  • 1 teaspoon sugar
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 cup warm water
  • 1 tablespoon oil
  • 1/4 cup brown sugar
  • 1 teaspoon cinnamon
  • 1 teaspoon sesame seeds

Steps

  1. Mix flour, yeast, sugar, salt, water, and oil into a soft dough. Rise 1 hour.
  2. Mix brown sugar, cinnamon, and sesame.
  3. Divide the dough into 6, stuff each with filling, and seal.
  4. Cook on a medium oiled griddle, pressing flat, until both sides are browned and the center is cooked.
  5. Rest 1 minute.
  6. Eat while warm, carefully.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Chopped peanuts are a common stall filling and add a peanut allergen.
  • Walnuts add tree nuts.

Pairings
