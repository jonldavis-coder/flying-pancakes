Pronounced bee-BEENG-kah

A baked rice cake, tender, traditionally cooked in a clay pan lined with banana leaf.

15 min prep · 35 min cook · Serves 8 · one 8-inch cake

History

Bibingka is a Filipino rice cake especially associated with Christmas dawn masses, Simbang Gabi, when it is sold outside churches. Traditional bibingka is baked in a clay pot lined with banana leaf, with heat above and below. Banana leaf is ideal and not required for a home pan. This is a baked rice cake, included as a holiday cousin of the pancake, not as a crepe.

Cultural background

Salted egg or cheese on top are common finishes. Butter and sugar too. The crumb is tender and a little sweet.

Why it tastes this way

Rice flour and coconut milk taste of the Philippine celebration table, richer than a daily tortilla and softer than a cracker. Christmas season is why the cake is a special breakfast. Banana leaf, when you have it, perfumes the edge. Cheese or salted egg on top is salty against the sweet crumb, a Filipino contrast. A home oven is an adaptation of the clay pot.

How it is made

Rice flour, coconut milk, egg, and sugar are baked in a lined pan until set.

Authentic touch

Bake it until the center is just set. It dries out if you treat it like a brownie. A banana leaf in the pan is traditional. Parchment works and will not taste of leaf.

How it is used

Warm, with butter and grated cheese or a slice of salted egg if you eat them.

Field notes

  • Bibingka is the Christmas rice cake sold around Simbang Gabi.
  • A banana leaf perfumes the edge. Parchment will not.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup rice flour
  • 1 cup coconut milk
  • 1/3 cup sugar
  • 2 eggs
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 2 tablespoons butter

Steps

  1. Heat the oven to 375°F. Butter a pan. Line it with banana leaf if you have one.
  2. Whisk the batter and pour it in.
  3. Bake about 30 minutes, until just set.
  4. Brush with butter.
  5. Cool 10 minutes.
  6. Serve warm.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Salted egg on top is a classic salty finish.
  • Bibingka is baked. It is not a street crepe.

Pairings
