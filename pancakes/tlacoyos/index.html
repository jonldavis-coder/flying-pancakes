Arriving in Xochimilco

Tlacoyos

You come in by Plane. Mexico · Mexico & Central America.

Pronounced tlah-KOH-yohs

An oval masa cake with a seam of mashed beans, topped with nopales, onion, and salsa.

35 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 tlacoyos

History

Tlacoyos are oval masa cakes from the Valley of Mexico. The filling, often mashed beans or habas, which are fava beans, goes inside the raw dough, and the cake is sealed before it meets the comal. That is different from a gordita, which is split after cooking, and from a sope, which is an open round with a pinched rim. The masa is nixtamalized corn, older in this region than wheat hotcakes. This recipe uses habas and keeps cheese off the cake.

Cultural background

Street cooks around Xochimilco and across Mexico City pat tlacoyos to order and set them on a clay or metal comal. Toppings are added after the cake is cooked. Queso fresco and crema are common. They are not in this version.

Why it tastes this way

The sealed bean filling steams inside the masa, so each bite is toasted corn and then a soft, salty center. Fava beans taste earthier and a little sweeter than black beans. A raw onion and a cooked nopal on top add bite and a green, tart note. The mild salsa brings acid and a small amount of chile without covering the corn.

How it is made

Season mashed cooked favas. Tuck them into oval masa cakes, seal, and griddle dry. Top with nopales, onion, and salsa.

Authentic touch

Shape a log, press a groove, lay in a thin rope of beans, and close the dough so the filling does not leak. The finished cake is oval and flatter than a gordita, with pointed or rounded ends.

How it is used

Eat them hot from the comal as a street snack or a light meal. Because this batch has no cheese or crema, it stays vegan. Add cheese only if you mean to change that.

Field notes

  • Habas, fava beans, are a classic tlacoyo filling along with black beans and requesón.
  • The oval shape and the sealed raw filling are what separate a tlacoyo from a gordita or a sope.
  • This batch is masa, beans, nopales, and salsa, so it stays vegan if you skip cheese and crema.

Ingredients

  • 250 g masa harina
  • 320 ml warm water
  • 1 teaspoon salt, divided
  • 200 g cooked fava beans, mashed with a spoon of their cooking liquid
  • 1 cup cooked nopales, drained
  • 1 small white onion, finely chopped
  • 2 tomatoes and 1 jalapeño for salsa

Steps

  1. Mix the masa harina, 1/2 teaspoon salt, and warm water. Knead, cover, and rest 20 minutes.
  2. Season the mashed favas with the remaining salt. They should be thick, not soupy. Chop the tomatoes and jalapeño with a pinch of salt for a mild salsa.
  3. Divide the dough into 8 pieces. Form each into an oval, press a groove down the center, add a spoon of favas, and seal the dough around them. Pat into a flat oval.
  4. Cook on a dry comal over medium heat for about 4 minutes a side, until the masa is spotted and cooked through.
  5. Top with nopales, raw onion, and salsa. Serve hot.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Mashed black beans can replace the favas. Keep the filling dry enough to seal.
  • Crumbled fresh cheese and crema are a common topping. They add dairy, so the plate is no longer vegan or dairy-free.
  • A hotter salsa is a table choice. The cake itself is the same.

Pairings

  • Sliced radishes and lime
  • A cup of hot corn atole, if you want a drink beside them
  • Black coffee
