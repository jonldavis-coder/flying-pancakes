Arriving in Kutztown

Pennsylvania Dutch funnel cakes

You come in by Train. United States · North America.

Crisp fried batter, lightly eggy, buried under powdered sugar.

15 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 6 · 6 funnel cakes

History

Pennsylvania Dutch funnel cakes are batter fried in hot fat after it is poured through a funnel in looping strands. The name Pennsylvania Dutch means Deutsch, the German-speaking settlers of the region, not the Netherlands. Fair stands later carried the fried batter well beyond Lancaster County. Kutztown is the map pin.

Cultural background

The funnel is a tool for making a lacy shape, not a flavor. A squeeze bottle does the same job if you do not own a funnel. The cake is fried, which is why it sits with fritters in this journal.

Why it tastes this way

Hot oil dehydrates the thin streams of batter and turns them crisp while the crossings stay slightly chewy. Powdered sugar melts into the hot fat on the surface. Milk and egg make the interior taste richer than a water dough.

How it is made

Batter poured in loops into hot oil and fried.

Authentic touch

Fry loops, not a solid disk. The oil should be hot enough that a drop of batter puffs in a second. Powdered sugar goes on while the cake is hot.

How it is used

Immediately, dusted with powdered sugar.

Field notes

  • Funnel cakes are fried batter, not griddled pancakes.
  • Pennsylvania Dutch means Deutsch, and this cake is one of the foods those communities brought to fairs.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 2 tablespoons sugar
  • 2 teaspoons baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 1/2 cups whole milk
  • 4 cups neutral oil, for frying
  • 1/2 cup powdered sugar, for serving

Steps

  1. Whisk the flour, sugar, baking powder, and salt.
  2. Whisk in the eggs and milk until the batter is smooth and can flow in a stream.
  3. Pour 1 inch of oil into a wide pan and heat it to 350°F. A drop of batter should puff at once without smoking.
  4. Pour batter through a funnel or a squeeze bottle in overlapping loops, about 1/3 cup for each cake.
  5. Fry until golden, about 1 minute, then turn and brown the other side. Drain on a rack.
  6. Dust with powdered sugar and serve immediately.

Fry in a deep, steady pan and do not leave hot oil. If the oil smokes, turn the heat off and let it cool.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A pinch of cinnamon in the sugar is a fair-stand habit.
  • A savory batter without sugar is a different food and not the fair cake.

Pairings

  • Powdered sugar
  • A squeeze of lemon
  • Fruit preserves
