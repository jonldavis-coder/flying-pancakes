Arriving in Osaka

Osaka okonomiyaki

You come in by Plane. Japan · East Asia.

Pronounced oh-sah-kah oh-koh-noh-mee-YAH-kee

A thick savory pancake with cabbage mixed into the batter, topped with sauce, mayonnaise, and bonito flakes.

25 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 4 · 2 thick pancakes

History

Osaka okonomiyaki mixes shredded cabbage into a wheat batter and griddles it as one thick cake. That mix is the Osaka method. Hiroshima's okonomiyaki is a different dish, built in layers, and it has its own page. Pork is a standard add-in. Bonito flakes on top are dried fish. The cake grew up as street and restaurant food in Osaka. This page does not give it a single invention year. It is not the scallion-only negiyaki, and it is not a sweet pancake.

Cultural background

Okonomi means as you like it, which explains the add-ins, not the shape. In Osaka the cabbage is still inside the batter. Grated yam helps the texture in many kitchens. Aonori and a brown savory sauce finish the crust. No trademark sauce is required. A homemade or unlabeled okonomiyaki sauce works.

Why it tastes this way

Cabbage wilts into the batter and tastes sweet and juicy, so the pancake is not a dry flour cake. Wheat and egg make a soft crumb. Pork browns at the edge and adds salt. The sauce is sweet and tangy, mayonnaise is rich, and bonito flakes add a smoky fish note that moves in the heat. The flavor is savory and layered on top of a vegetable-heavy interior.

How it is made

Mix cabbage into wheat batter, griddle it slowly with pork, flip once, and finish with sauce, mayonnaise, aonori, and bonito.

Authentic touch

Fold the cabbage in until every strand is wet, then cook covered on medium-low. A hot rush burns the outside and leaves the cabbage raw.

How it is used

Cut into wedges and share at the griddle if you have one. Eat it hot, while the bonito still twists. It is a savory meal.

Field notes

  • In Osaka the cabbage goes into the batter. Hiroshima stacks its ingredients in layers instead.
  • Bonito flakes are dried fish, so this plate is not vegetarian.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup all-purpose wheat flour
  • 1 egg
  • 3/4 cup water or unsalted bonito dashi
  • 400 grams shredded green cabbage
  • 2 scallions, chopped
  • 80 grams thinly sliced pork belly
  • Neutral oil
  • Okonomiyaki sauce, mayonnaise, aonori, and bonito flakes to finish

Steps

  1. Whisk flour, egg, and dashi or water into a batter. Fold in cabbage and scallions until the shreds are coated.
  2. Oil a wide pan over medium-low heat. Spread half the batter into a thick round. Lay pork on top.
  3. Cover and cook about 8 minutes, until the underside is brown and the batter has set most of the way.
  4. Flip so the pork hits the pan. Cover and cook until the pork is done and the center is not raw.
  5. Turn the cake pork-side up. Spread sauce and mayonnaise, then aonori and bonito. Repeat with the rest.

Cook the pork through. The center of a thick cake needs the covered time.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Squid instead of pork, which swaps meat for more shellfish
  • A handful of shrimp, which adds shellfish

Pairings

  • Okonomiyaki sauce
  • Mayonnaise
  • Aonori and bonito flakes
