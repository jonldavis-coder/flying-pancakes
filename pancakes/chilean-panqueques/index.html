Thin crepes with dulce de leche or manjar.

15 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 crepes

History

Chilean panqueques are thin dessert crepes, filled with manjar, the local name for dulce de leche. They are a home dessert, close to the Argentine plate and worth a separate page because the name manjar and the Chilean table are not identical to Buenos Aires. The batter is a standard crepe batter.

Cultural background

They are rolled. A dust of sugar finishes them. Orange zest in the batter is a home variation and is optional here.

Why it tastes this way

Manjar is sweet, thick, and milky, so the crepe stays thin and not very sweet. The dessert is the same Atlantic idea as Argentine panqueques with a Pacific name. Butter in the pan is the browned note. The filling does the rest.

How it is made

Thin crepes are filled with manjar and rolled.

Authentic touch

Spread a thin ribbon of manjar. A heavy hand makes the roll slide apart.

How it is used

Warm.

Field notes

  • In Chile the caramel is often called manjar.
  • The crepe is the wrapper. The manjar is the flavor.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup flour
  • 2 eggs
  • 1 1/4 cups milk
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • Pinch of salt
  • 1 tablespoon melted butter
  • 3/4 cup manjar

Steps

  1. Cook thin crepes.
  2. Spread with manjar and roll.
  3. Dust with a little sugar if you like.
  4. Serve warm.
  5. Do not overfill.
  6. Eat them the day they are made.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Orange zest in the batter is a bright variation.
  • Argentine panqueques are the neighbor.

Pairings
