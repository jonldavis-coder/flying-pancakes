Chilean panqueques

You come in by Hot air balloon. Chile · South America.

Pronounced pahn-KEH-kehs

Thin wheat crepes filled with manjar and finished with a dry dust of powdered sugar.

15 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 crepes

History

Chilean panqueques are crepes filled with manjar, the milk caramel Chilean cooks keep in the cupboard. They show up at Sunday lunch and at children's birthdays, stacked in the kitchen and filled to order. The batter is wheat, egg, and milk, a twentieth-century crepe habit, not an Indigenous corn cake and not the fried pumpkin sopaipilla that belongs to rainy days. Manjar is the Chilean name on the jar. The crepe is the wrap.

Cultural background

In Santiago the panqueque is a dessert people already know how to make without a recipe card: a thin batter, a hot pan, a spoon of manjar. Once, the evening tea, is another hour when a sweet crepe might appear. This page keeps the filling to manjar and powdered sugar.

Why it tastes this way

Manjar is dense, so a hot crepe melts a spoonful just enough to spread without soaking the cake to pieces. Powdered sugar on the outside adds a dry sweetness that the caramel does not have. Oil in this batter, rather than a large piece of butter, keeps the crepe flexible and a little less rich, so the manjar can be the dairy you taste. The surface spots where it touched the pan.

How it is made

Make a thin batter of wheat flour, egg, milk, oil, and salt. Cook crepes, spread them with manjar, roll, and dust with powdered sugar.

Authentic touch

Spread manjar on a crepe that is still warm and roll loosely. A tight roll squeezes the filling out the ends. Powdered sugar goes on after rolling, or it melts into the pan.

How it is used

Serve them at dessert or with afternoon tea, warm or at room temperature. They are a sweet plate, separate from the pumpkin fritters Chile fries in the rain.

Field notes

  • Chilean cooks call the milk caramel manjar. This crepe is built around that jar.
  • Sopaipillas are the rainy-day pumpkin fritter. They are not this dessert.
  • Powdered sugar stays on the outside so the surface tastes drier than the filling.

Ingredients

  • 100 g wheat flour
  • 1 egg
  • 240 ml milk
  • 1 tablespoon neutral oil, plus more for the pan
  • Pinch of salt
  • 200 g manjar
  • 1 tablespoon powdered sugar

Steps

  1. Whisk the flour, egg, milk, oil, and salt into a thin batter. Rest it for 15 minutes.
  2. Oil a skillet lightly and cook thin crepes over medium heat, flipping once, until they spot.
  3. Spread manjar on each warm crepe and roll it loosely.
  4. Dust the rolls with powdered sugar.
  5. Serve at dessert or with tea.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A slice of banana inside the manjar is a birthday-table addition.
  • Orange zest in the batter is enough perfume. Do not turn the crepe into a juice.
  • A savory crepe with cheese is a different meal and still contains dairy and wheat.

Pairings

  • Tea with lemon
  • A cup of coffee after Sunday lunch
  • Extra manjar for the table
