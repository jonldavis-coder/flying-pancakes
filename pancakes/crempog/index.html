Pronounced KREM-pog

Thick, buttermilk-rich Welsh pancakes, often stacked with butter.

10 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · about 10 pancakes

History

Crempogau are Welsh pancakes, thicker than a crepe, made with buttermilk or sour milk. They are associated with celebrations and with ordinary tea, and recipes vary by household. Some are stacked with butter between the layers. They are a Welsh cake in the pancake family, distinct from griddled Welsh cakes that are a currant dough.

Cultural background

A crempog is cooked in spoonfuls and eaten warm. The plural is crempogau. Butter is part of serving, not only of cooking.

Why it tastes this way

Buttermilk makes them tangy and tender, and a bit of butter in the batter makes them rich enough to stack. Wales’s dairy country is the reason sour milk was the liquid on hand. They taste less of sugar than a Scotch pancake and less of lemon than an English Shrove pancake. Butter between the layers melts and salts the stack.

How it is made

A buttermilk batter is cooked as small thick pancakes and stacked with butter.

Authentic touch

Cook them thicker than a crepe and stack them while hot so the butter you spread between them melts. A cold crempog with hard butter is a lesser thing.

How it is used

Butter, and honey or jam if you want them sweeter.

Field notes

  • Crempog and Welsh cake are both Welsh and are not the same recipe.
  • Stacking with butter is a traditional way to serve them.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/4 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1 large egg
  • 3/4 cup buttermilk
  • 1 tablespoon melted butter, plus more to stack

Steps

  1. Stir the dry ingredients together, then stir in the wet ingredients until the batter is combined.
  2. Rest 5 minutes.
  3. Heat a griddle or heavy skillet over medium heat and film it with butter.
  4. Spoon on the batter. Cook until the underside is browned and the top looks set.
  5. Flip once and cook the second side through.
  6. Stack the cakes with butter between them and serve warm.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Some recipes use a yeast batter. This one uses baking powder and soda for a same-day cake.
  • Welsh cakes are the currant girdle cake on the next page, not a crempog.

Pairings

  • honey
  • jam
