Arriving in Fort-de-France

Martinican accras de morue

You come in by Boat. Martinique · Caribbean.

Pronounced ah-KRAH duh moh-ROO

Salt-cod fritters in a wheat batter, garlicky and medium-hot, fried until the crust shatters.

30 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 6 · about 20 fritters

History

Accras de morue are salt-cod fritters from Martinique, fried for snacks, for parties, and through the Christmas season. Salt cod reached the French Caribbean as preserved fish and became a Creole pantry item. The fritter is wheat batter, soaked cod, garlic, herbs, and chile. It contains fish, so it is not vegetarian. It is not the Haitian malanga accra, and it is not a cassava bread.

Cultural background

In Fort-de-France, trays of accras show up at celebrations and at snack counters. The cod is soaked first, in the refrigerator, until it is pleasant rather than harshly salty. The chile is part of the batter in this version, so the heat is medium.

Why it tastes this way

Soaked salt cod keeps a savory, sea-salt flavor without tasting like a block of salt, if you changed the water. Garlic and scallion perfume the batter. A small hot chile gives a clear medium heat. Wheat flour and egg fry into a lacy crust around flakes of fish. The contrast is crisp shell and moist fish, not a dense fish cake.

How it is made

Soak salt cod, flake it, and fold it into a thick batter of wheat flour, egg, water, garlic, scallion, parsley, and chile. Fry spoons of batter until golden and cooked through.

Authentic touch

Soak the cod in the refrigerator and change the water until a flake tastes salty but not harsh. Dry the flakes well before they go into the batter, or the fritters spit and fall apart. Fry until the center is hot and the fish is opaque.

How it is used

Serve hot as a party snack or a street food, with a cold drink and extra lime. They are not a side vegetable. The fish is the point.

Field notes

  • Morue means salt cod. The fritters contain fish and are not vegetarian.
  • Soak the fish in the refrigerator, not on the counter, and change the water.
  • These are wheat fritters, not the malanga accras of the Haitian page.

Ingredients

  • 200 g salt cod, soaked in the refrigerator
  • 150 g wheat flour
  • 1 egg
  • 120 ml water
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced
  • 2 scallions, chopped
  • 1 small fresh hot chile, minced
  • 2 tablespoons chopped parsley
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking powder
  • Neutral oil for frying

Steps

  1. The day before, cover the salt cod with cold water and refrigerate it. Change the water two or three times. Drain, simmer the cod for 5 minutes if it is still very stiff, then cool, bone, skin, and flake it. Taste and stop soaking when it is seasoned rather than harsh.
  2. Stir the flour and baking powder. Beat in the egg and water. Fold in the garlic, scallions, chile, parsley, and flaked cod. The batter should be thick.
  3. Heat 3 centimeters of oil until a drop of batter sizzles at once.
  4. Fry spoons of batter, turning once, until deep gold and cooked through, about 3 to 4 minutes.
  5. Drain and serve hot with lime.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Leave the chile out and the heat drops. The fish stays, so the fritter is still not vegetarian.
  • A little baking powder makes a puffier accra.
  • Malanga accras without fish are the Haitian page in this journal, a different dish.

Pairings

  • Lime wedges
  • A Creole sauce chien of herbs, onion, and chile
  • Ti' punch or a lime soda at a party
