Tart dried or fresh cherries in a buttermilk cake.

15 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · about 8 pancakes

History

Michigan, especially around Traverse City, grows a large share of the American tart cherry crop. Pancakes with those cherries are a modern regional breakfast served in cherry season and, with dried cherries, the rest of the year. They are not an old voyageur recipe. The orchard is the fact that makes the plate local.

Cultural background

Tart cherries, not sweet binge cherries, are the Michigan product. They need a little sugar so the cake does not taste like a sour candy.

Why it tastes this way

Montmorency and other tart cherries are acidic, which is why cherry pies from this region are sharp rather than floral. Dried cherries concentrate that acid. A buttermilk batter has its own tang, so the fruit should be chopped and sugared or the cake tastes harsh. The Great Lakes climate, with cold winters and a moderated shoreline, is what built the orchards.

How it is made

Chopped tart cherries are folded into a buttermilk batter.

Authentic touch

If you use dried cherries, soak them in hot water for 10 minutes and pat them dry. Unsoaked dried fruit stays leathery.

How it is used

Butter. Syrup can be light because the fruit is already sharp and sweet.

Field notes

  • Michigan’s tart cherry crop is the local reason for this breakfast.
  • The pancake is a modern use of that crop, stated plainly.

Ingredients

  • 1/2 cup dried tart cherries or 1 cup fresh tart cherries, pitted and chopped
  • 1 cup all-purpose flour
  • 2 tablespoons sugar
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1 cup buttermilk
  • 1 large egg
  • 2 tablespoons melted butter

Steps

  1. Soak dried cherries 10 minutes if using them, then drain and chop. Toss fresh cherries with a pinch of the sugar.
  2. Mix the batter and fold in the fruit.
  3. Cook on a buttered medium griddle.
  4. Flip carefully. Cherries scorch.
  5. Cook until the center is set.
  6. Serve warm.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Fresh pitted tart cherries in July are the best version.
  • Sweet cherries are a different, milder fruit.

Pairings

  • butter
  • maple syrup
