Arriving in Pyeongchang, Gangwon

Memiljeon

You come in by Plane. South Korea · East Asia.

Pronounced MEH-mil-juhn

A nutty buckwheat pancake, earthy and a little bitter, bound with egg and scallion.

15 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 small pancakes

History

Memiljeon is a buckwheat pancake associated with Gangwon, the mountainous province where buckwheat is a local grain. Memil means buckwheat. The flour is naturally free of gluten and often milled where wheat dust is present, so this recipe calls for certified gluten-free buckwheat and adds no wheat. An egg binds the cake. It is a savory pancake, kin to other jeon, with a flavor that wheat batter does not have.

Cultural background

Pyeongchang and the towns around it serve buckwheat as noodles and as pancakes. The pancake is the griddled form, good with a sharp radish salad. Because the flour can be dry, the batter needs enough water to spread and enough egg to hold.

Why it tastes this way

Buckwheat tastes earthy, slightly bitter, and toasted even before it browns. That bitterness is the point, not a fault. Egg softens it. Scallion adds a fresh edge. A vinegar dip cuts the grain the way it does for other jeon. There is no chile in the cake. The mountain grain is the flavor.

How it is made

Whisk certified gluten-free buckwheat flour with egg and water, fold in scallion, and fry small cakes.

Authentic touch

Cook the pancakes smaller than a pajeon. Buckwheat batter is fragile and breaks when you try to flip a wide sheet. Certified gluten-free flour is what makes the diet tag true.

How it is used

Serve warm with a vinegar dip and a radish salad. It is savory snack food from a buckwheat region.

Field notes

  • Memil is buckwheat. Gangwon is the province most associated with it.
  • The gluten-free tag depends on certified gluten-free buckwheat and no wheat in the recipe.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup certified gluten-free buckwheat flour
  • 1 egg
  • 3/4 cup water
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 2 scallions, chopped
  • Neutral oil
  • Gluten-free tamari and rice vinegar for dipping

Steps

  1. Whisk the certified gluten-free buckwheat flour, egg, water, and salt into a pourable batter.
  2. Stir in the scallions.
  3. Oil a pan over medium heat and cook small rounds, using less batter than you would for a wheat pancake.
  4. Flip when the underside is brown and the top is set. Buckwheat breaks if you flip too early.
  5. Serve with gluten-free tamari and vinegar.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Shredded potato in the batter
  • A spoon of wheat flour, which would cancel the gluten-free tag

Pairings

  • Soy-vinegar dip made with gluten-free tamari
  • A sharp radish salad
  • Steamed rice
