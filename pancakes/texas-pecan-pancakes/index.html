Arriving in San Saba

Texas pecan pancakes

You come in by Plane. United States · North America.

Buttered wheat cakes with toasted pecan, sweet and slightly bitter at the brown edge of the nut.

20 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 12 pancakes

  • Vegetarian

History

Texas pecan pancakes put a native nut into a wheat breakfast cake. Pecan trees grow wild and in orchards across Texas, and Indigenous peoples of the region used the nut long before a diner pancake did. The cake itself is a twentieth-century breakfast. San Saba is a pecan-growing town and the map pin.

Cultural background

Toasting the nuts first is what makes them taste like pecans rather than pale crumbs. The wheat batter is the usual buttermilk stack with nuts folded through.

Why it tastes this way

Pecans are oily. Heat releases that oil and turns the flavor round and toasted. If the nuts go in raw, the cakes taste flatter. A bitter note from the brown skin is part of a real pecan, so do not expect a bland crunch.

How it is made

Griddled buttermilk batter with toasted pecans in and on the cakes.

Authentic touch

Toast the pecans in a dry skillet until they smell nutty, then chop them. Scatter a few more on the batter after you pour it.

How it is used

Hot, with butter and syrup, and extra pecans on the plate.

Field notes

  • The pecan is native to Texas and the wider South.
  • San Saba built a town identity on pecan growing, which is why this pin sits there.

Ingredients

  • 3/4 cup chopped pecans
  • 1 1/2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 2 tablespoons sugar
  • 2 teaspoons baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 1/2 cups buttermilk
  • 1 large egg
  • 2 tablespoons unsalted butter, melted, plus more for the griddle

Steps

  1. Toast the pecans in a dry skillet over medium heat, stirring, until they smell fragrant. Cool them, then chop if they are still in large pieces.
  2. Whisk the flour, sugar, baking powder, baking soda, and salt. Stir in half of the pecans.
  3. Add the buttermilk, egg, and melted butter. Stir until just combined.
  4. Butter a griddle over medium heat and pour 1/4 cup cakes. Scatter a few of the remaining pecans on each cake.
  5. Flip when the tops set. Cook until the nuts on the bottom are brown, not black.
  6. Serve with butter, syrup, and the rest of the pecans.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Leave the nuts on top only if you need to cook a nut-free cake for someone else, and use a clean pan.
  • A darker sorghum syrup stands up to the nut better than a very light syrup.

Pairings

  • Butter
  • Maple syrup or sorghum
  • More toasted pecans
