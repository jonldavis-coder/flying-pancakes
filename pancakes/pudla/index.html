Arriving in Ahmedabad, Gujarat

Pudla

You come in by Train. India · South Asia.

Pronounced POOD-lah

A turmeric-yellow Gujarati besan pancake, savory and a little sweet, eaten with chutney.

15 min prep · 18 min cook · Serves 3 · 6 pudlas

History

Pudla is the Gujarati besan pancake that opens a breakfast, often with chutney and a cup of tea. Turmeric is mixed into the gram flour, so the cake arrives yellow. Green chile, cilantro, and sometimes sesame sit in the batter. It is usually unfermented and cooked as soon as the bowl is smooth. A Delhi cheela can share the flour and still be a different plate: this one belongs to a Gujarati morning, not to a tiffin counter seasoned with ajwain.

Cultural background

Ahmedabad homes treat pudla as ordinary breakfast cooking, the way other regions treat a toast. The chutney beside it is part of the habit, not a restaurant garnish. Sesame, when it is used, is toasted by the pan rather than ground into a paste.

Why it tastes this way

Turmeric colors the crumb and adds a light bitter note that Gujarati food often balances with a pinch of sugar. Besan still tastes of chickpea. Cilantro and green chile keep it fresh. A little sugar, common on Gujarati savory tables, rounds the spices without turning the pudla into a dessert.

How it is made

Whisk besan with turmeric, chile, cilantro, and a pinch of sugar, then griddle thin yellow pancakes.

Authentic touch

Cook it fully. Besan that is still raw in the center tastes chalky and bitter in a way turmeric did not intend. Medium heat and an extra minute are the fix.

How it is used

Serve straight from the pan with green chutney. Breakfast is the right hour. A sweeter hando version exists and is a different bowl.

Field notes

  • Pudla is a Gujarati breakfast cake. Turmeric in the batter is part of that habit.
  • It is besan, mixed and cooked the same morning, without a ferment.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup besan (chickpea flour), with no wheat added
  • 3/4 cup water
  • 1/4 teaspoon turmeric
  • 1 green chile, minced
  • 2 tablespoons chopped cilantro
  • 1 teaspoon sesame seeds
  • 1/2 teaspoon sugar
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • Neutral oil for the pan

Steps

  1. Whisk besan, turmeric, sugar, salt, and water into a smooth batter. Rest it 10 minutes.
  2. Stir in chile, cilantro, and sesame seeds.
  3. Oil a pan over medium heat. Pour a ladle and spread a thin round.
  4. Cook until the surface looks dry and the underside is speckled deep gold. Flip and cook through so no raw besan remains.
  5. Keep finished pudlas warm under a cloth and send them out with chutney.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Shredded cabbage in the batter
  • Methi leaves when they are in season

Pairings

  • Cilantro-mint chutney
  • Sweet mango pickle
  • Tea
