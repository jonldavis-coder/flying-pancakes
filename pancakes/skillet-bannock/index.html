Arriving in Winnipeg

Skillet bannock

You come in by Plane. Canada · North America.

Pronounced BAN-uk

Plain wheat bread, slightly salty, with a fried face and a soft center.

15 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 8 · 8 skillet cakes

History

Bannock is a Scottish word for a flat griddle bread. Many First Nations and Métis communities in Canada made skillet bannock after wheat flour became a trade good and a ration staple. This recipe is that simple flour version: it is not a claim that one nation owns the bread, and it is not a claim that this flour bread predates flour. Winnipeg is the map pin because it is a Métis center, not because the bread belongs to one city.

Cultural background

The written dough is flour, baking powder, salt, oil, and water, cooked in a skillet. Lard was a common fat after it was available through trade and rations. This batch uses oil, so the written recipe stays free of meat and dairy.

Why it tastes this way

The flavor is wheat and salt, with browning from the skillet. Baking powder lifts it enough to keep the center from turning to hardtack. Oil in the dough makes the crumb shorter. There is no sour ferment and no corn taste.

How it is made

Soft baking-powder dough cooked in a greased skillet and flipped once.

Authentic touch

Cook it in a skillet and turn it once. This is the simple flour bread. Do not add ingredients to make it seem older than flour.

How it is used

Hot, broken into pieces, with jam, stew, or nothing.

Field notes

  • Bannock is the Scottish name for a griddle bread, later made across Canada after flour was a trade and ration staple.
  • This page is the simple flour version and does not assign it to one nation.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1 tablespoon baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 2 tablespoons neutral oil, plus more for the skillet
  • 3/4 cup cold water

Steps

  1. Stir the flour, baking powder, and salt. Add the oil and water and mix to a soft dough. If it is crumbly, add a spoon of water.
  2. Divide the dough into 8 pieces and pat each into a round about 1/2 inch thick.
  3. Film a skillet with oil over medium heat.
  4. Cook the rounds, a few at a time, until the underside is brown and the side looks set, about 4 minutes.
  5. Flip and cook until the center is no longer doughy.
  6. Serve hot.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Raisins in the dough are a common sweet version and still fit this flour bread.
  • Lard in place of oil is closer to many camp kitchens and makes the recipe no longer vegan or meat-free.

Pairings

  • Jam
  • Stew
  • A drizzle of oil or butter if you eat dairy
