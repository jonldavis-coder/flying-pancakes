Arriving in Al Ain

Emirati chebab

You come in by Plane. United Arab Emirates · Middle East.

Pronounced cheh-BAHB

Small yeasted pancakes perfumed with saffron and cardamom, served with date syrup.

25 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · About 16 small pancakes

History

Chebab are Emirati fermented pancakes, small and yeasted, often scented with saffron and cardamom. Date syrup, dibs, is the usual condiment, with butter or a soft cheese nearby. They are a home breakfast across the Emirates, not a restaurant invention from one hotel. The batter rests until it is bubbly, then it is cooked in little rounds. This batch is written from an Al Ain kitchen and eaten the same way in Abu Dhabi or Dubai.

Cultural background

Dates are everyday food, and dibs is the concentrated syrup made from them. Saffron is used by the pinch, steeped in warm milk, not by the handful. Chebab sit at breakfast beside eggs or cheese more often than at the end of a formal meal, though they are sweet enough for dessert.

Why it tastes this way

Yeast gives a mild tang and a soft rise. Saffron tastes floral and hay-like, and cardamom is camphorous and sweet. Milk and egg make the crumb tender. Date syrup is dark, caramelized, and less floral than honey, so the finish is toffee rather than blossom. The cakes are small, so the crust-to-center ratio stays high.

How it is made

Bloom yeast and saffron in warm milk. Whisk in flour, egg, sugar, cardamom, and salt. Rest until foamy. Cook small rounds on a buttered pan until both sides are golden. Serve with date syrup.

Authentic touch

Steep a pinch of saffron in warm milk before it joins the flour, and serve the cakes with date syrup. A large plain pancake with maple syrup is not chebab.

How it is used

Chebab are breakfast, and they work as a sweet snack or a simple dessert. Date syrup is poured or spooned. A salty white cheese on the side is a savory contrast and is still dairy, which this batter already contains.

Field notes

  • Saffron and cardamom are the usual scents in Emirati chebab.
  • Date syrup, not honey, is the condiment written here.
  • The cakes are small and yeasted. They are cooked after the batter has rested.

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 cups wheat flour (about 190 g)
  • 1 teaspoon instant yeast
  • 1 cup warm milk
  • 1 pinch saffron threads
  • 1 egg
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • 1/2 teaspoon ground cardamom
  • 1/4 teaspoon fine salt
  • Butter for the pan
  • 1/2 cup date syrup, to serve

Steps

  1. Stir the saffron into the warm milk and add the yeast and sugar. Let it stand for 5 minutes.
  2. Whisk in the egg, flour, cardamom, and salt until you have a thick batter. Cover it for 45 minutes, until bubbly.
  3. Heat a pan and grease it with butter. Drop small rounds, a generous spoon each.
  4. Cook until bubbles set on top, then flip and cook until the second side is golden.
  5. Serve warm with date syrup. The syrup is the condiment, not a glaze cooked into the batter.

The batter contains wheat, egg, and dairy. Saffron should be a pinch. Date syrup is a concentrated sweetener.

Refrigerate leftover chebab and reheat them in a covered pan. Keep date syrup covered at room temperature or in the refrigerator, apart from the cakes. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Rose water instead of saffron is a different perfume and not this batch.
  • A thicker batter makes a puffier cake. The traditional ones stay small.
  • Leaving out the egg will change the crumb. This recipe includes it.

Pairings

  • Date syrup
  • A salty white cheese
  • Cardamom coffee
