Chocolate chip pancakes

You come in by Train. United States · North America.

A plain sweet pancake with pockets of melted semisweet chocolate.

15 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 12 pancakes

History

Chocolate chip pancakes are a late twentieth-century American diner cake, wheat batter with pieces of chocolate melted into the crumb. They have no older regional origin. Columbus, Ohio, is the map pin only as a stand-in for that diner breakfast. Most semisweet chocolate chips contain soy lecithin, so soy is listed here.

Cultural background

The cake is the milk-and-egg diner pancake with chocolate added. It is not a cocoa batter. The chips should stay in pieces so the crumb is not uniformly brown.

Why it tastes this way

Chocolate melts at the griddle face and stays in pockets inside. If the chips are stirred too long they streak and sink. Sugar in the batter and sugar in the chocolate make this one of the sweeter cakes in the set. Butter at the edge is the plain contrast.

How it is made

Griddled wheat batter with semisweet chocolate chips.

Authentic touch

Scatter the chips on the cakes after pouring, or fold them in gently at the end. Do not blend them into a cocoa paste.

How it is used

Hot, when the chips are still soft. Syrup is optional because the chocolate is already sweet.

Field notes

  • This is a late twentieth-century diner pancake, not an older regional recipe.
  • Standard semisweet chips usually contain soy lecithin, which is why soy is an allergen here.

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 2 tablespoons sugar
  • 2 teaspoons baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 1/4 cups whole milk
  • 1 large egg
  • 2 tablespoons unsalted butter, melted, plus more for the griddle
  • 3/4 cup semisweet chocolate chips, which usually contain soy lecithin

Steps

  1. Whisk the flour, sugar, baking powder, and salt.
  2. Stir in the milk, egg, and melted butter until just combined.
  3. Butter a griddle over medium-low heat. Chocolate scorches easily.
  4. Pour 1/4 cup cakes and scatter chocolate chips over each one.
  5. Flip when the underside is golden and the chips look glossy. Cook briefly on the second side.
  6. Serve while the chocolate is soft.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Chop a semisweet chocolate bar if you want larger melts.
  • A cocoa-powder batter is a different, fully chocolate cake.

Pairings

  • Butter
  • A little maple syrup
  • Cold milk
