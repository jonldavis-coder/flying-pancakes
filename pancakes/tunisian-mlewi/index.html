Pronounced m-LEH-wee

A soft, oiled Tunisian flatbread cooked on a griddle.

30 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 6 · 6 breads

History

Mlewi, also spelled mlawi, is a Tunisian pan flatbread related to msemen, oiled and folded or rolled, then cooked. It is street and home breakfast food, often eaten with harissa, tuna, or honey. Harissa adds chile. This recipe is the bread. Tuna would add fish.

Cultural background

It should be soft and layered. Vendors cook it to order. Honey and harissa are two different breakfasts, sweet and hot.

Why it tastes this way

Oil in the layers makes mlewi rich and pliable, a wrap for harissa or tuna rather than a dry cracker. Tunisia’s breakfast culture puts chile paste on bread, which is why the same dough can be sweet with honey or hot with harissa. The dough itself is mild. The condiment is the decision.

How it is made

A soft dough is oiled, flattened, and cooked on a griddle until supple and spotted.

Authentic touch

Keep the dough soft and the griddle moderate. A hard crust means the bread will not fold around a filling.

How it is used

Warm, with honey or with harissa. Harissa makes the plate spicy. The bread is not.

Field notes

  • Mlewi is the Tunisian oiled flatbread in this family.
  • Harissa is a condiment, not part of the dough.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups flour
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 3/4 cup water
  • 3 tablespoons oil, plus more for the pan

Steps

  1. Mix flour, salt, water, and oil into a soft dough. Rest 15 minutes.
  2. Divide, oil, and flatten into rounds.
  3. Cook on a medium griddle until spotted and cooked through.
  4. Keep them soft under a towel.
  5. Serve with honey or harissa.
  6. They are best warm.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A tuna and egg filling is a common street meal and adds fish and egg.
  • Msemen is the Moroccan square cousin.

Pairings

  • honey
  • harissa
