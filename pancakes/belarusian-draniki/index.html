Belarusian draniki

You come in by Train. Belarus · Eastern Europe.

Pronounced drah-NEE-kee

Belarusian potato pancakes bound with potato starch and egg, sour cream on the side.

20 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 pancakes

History

Draniki are the potato pancakes of Belarus, grated and fried. They are a national home dish, served with sour cream. A pork gravy called machanka is a traditional accompaniment and is not in this recipe, so the cakes stay vegetarian. This batter uses the potato's own starch and an egg, not wheat flour, so it is gluten-free. Sour cream is a topping. The cakes are fried in oil and the recipe is dairy-free until you add that topping.

Cultural background

Potato is the staple. Draniki are everyday food, not a single holiday. They are cousins of deruny, placki, and latkes, with a Belarusian name and this flourless method.

Why it tastes this way

Crisp fried potato and onion, salted, with a soft center. Egg binds the shreds without flour. The flavor is direct. Sour cream cools the crust if you add it. Machanka, if you serve it, makes a heavier meat plate. The oil should be hot enough to crust the cake before it absorbs grease.

How it is made

Potatoes and onion are grated, mixed with their starch, an egg, and salt, and fried in oil.

Authentic touch

Do not add wheat flour if you want this gluten-free version. Keep the starch that settles out of the grated potato. Sour cream is the usual condiment and is optional if the meal must stay dairy-free.

How it is used

Hot, with sour cream. Machanka, a pork gravy, is the meat accompaniment and is not part of this batter.

Field notes

  • Draniki are the Belarusian potato pancakes.
  • This recipe uses no wheat flour.
  • Sour cream is the usual topping. Machanka is a pork gravy served beside them, not in this batter.

Ingredients

  • 700 g starchy potatoes, peeled
  • 1 small onion
  • 1 large egg
  • 1 tsp fine salt
  • Neutral oil, for frying

Steps

  1. Grate the potatoes and onion. Save the liquid, let the starch settle, pour off the water, and mix the starch back in.
  2. Stir in the egg and salt.
  3. Fry flattened rounds in hot oil until both sides are brown and the centers are cooked.
  4. Serve hot. Add sour cream at the table if you want it.

The potato must cook through. Do not leave the frying pan.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A spoon of wheat flour, which removes the gluten-free diet
  • Ukrainian deruny, which use flour on their page

Pairings

  • Sour cream
  • Machanka, a pork gravy, for a meat meal
  • Salt
