Arriving in Romagna

Piadina

You come in by Train. Italy · Southern Europe.

Pronounced pyah-DEE-nah

The griddled flatbread of Romagna. It is a flatbread, folded around a filling.

30 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · 4 flatbreads

History

Piadina is the flatbread of Romagna, griddled, folded, and filled. It is a flatbread, not a poured pancake and not a Bolognese pasta dough. The older fat in many Romagna kitchens is lard. This recipe uses olive oil so the bread itself stays vegetarian and dairy-free. Lard is named as a variation and would add a meat allergen. The usual filling, cured pork and a soft fresh cheese such as squacquerone, is put on after cooking. That filling is not vegan. The dough is.

Cultural background

Piadina is street food and home food, cooked on a testo, a flat griddle. It is eaten folded, often standing up. The bread should be pliable and spotted, with a few bubbles.

Why it tastes this way

Hot griddle spots taste toasted. Olive oil makes the crumb short rather than lean. Salt is noticeable because the dough is simple. The filling does the rest: salty cured meat, sharp greens, and mild creamy cheese. Without a filling, the bread still tastes good with olive oil and salt, which is how you can judge the dough.

How it is made

Flour, salt, a pinch of baking soda, olive oil, and water are kneaded briefly, rested, rolled into thin rounds, and cooked on a hot griddle.

Authentic touch

Call it a flatbread. Cook it on a dry or lightly oiled griddle and fold it while warm. If you use lard, say that you have left the vegan dough. Do not put the filling in the dough.

How it is used

Folded around greens and cheese, or around cured meat, as a street meal. The dough recipe is vegan. The classic filled plate usually is not.

Field notes

  • Piadina is a flatbread from Romagna, griddled and folded.
  • It is not a pancake batter and not a pasta dough.
  • This dough uses olive oil. Lard is the older fat in many kitchens and changes the diet.

Ingredients

  • 250 g plain wheat flour
  • 1/2 tsp fine salt
  • 1/4 tsp baking soda
  • 40 ml olive oil
  • 120 ml warm water

Steps

  1. Mix the flour, salt, and baking soda. Add the oil and enough water to make a soft dough. Knead briefly and rest 20 minutes.
  2. Divide into four and roll each piece into a thin round.
  3. Cook on a hot griddle until brown spots appear and the bread puffs in places, then turn.
  4. Fold while warm around the filling you have chosen, or serve plain with oil and salt.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Lard instead of olive oil, the older fat, which is not vegetarian
  • A whole-wheat share of the flour

Pairings

  • Squacquerone or another fresh soft cheese, a dairy filling
  • Arugula
  • Cured pork, a meat filling
