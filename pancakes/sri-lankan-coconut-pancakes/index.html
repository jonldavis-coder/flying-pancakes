Sri Lankan coconut pancakes

You come in by Train. Sri Lanka · South Asia.

Pronounced KOH-koh-nut PAN-kakes

A thin sweet crepe rolled with grated coconut and jaggery, meant for tea time.

20 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 small rolls

History

Sri Lankan coconut pancakes are a tea-time crepe, sweet and rolled, often filled or topped with grated coconut and jaggery or treacle. They are not hoppers. Hoppers are fermented rice bowls from a curved pan. These are thin wheat-and-egg pancakes cooked on a flat griddle, the kind a Kandy kitchen might bring out with afternoon tea. Coconut is the filling, not a bowl of coconut milk left to ferment.

Cultural background

Tea time is the hour, not a claim that a plantation invented the cake. Homes use kitul treacle when they have it and jaggery when they do not. The pancake is soft enough to roll around a spoon of coconut.

Why it tastes this way

Jaggery or treacle gives the coconut a dark caramel edge. Fresh coconut keeps the filling moist. The crepe tastes of egg and a little sugar, with wheat in the background. Because the batter is not a rice ferment, there is no sour lace. The pleasure is the contrast of a tender pancake and a sandy-sweet coconut center.

How it is made

Make small sweet egg crepes and roll them around coconut cooked with jaggery.

Authentic touch

Cook the coconut with the jaggery until the grains look moist and separate, not swimming. A syrupy filling bursts out of the roll.

How it is used

Serve warm with tea, two rolls to a plate. They are a snack, not a hopper breakfast.

Field notes

  • These are tea-time coconut crepes, not bowl-shaped hoppers.
  • Jaggery or kitul treacle is the usual sweetener with the coconut.

Ingredients

  • 1/2 cup all-purpose wheat flour
  • 1 egg
  • 3/4 cup coconut milk
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • A pinch of salt
  • 1 cup grated coconut
  • 1/3 cup jaggery, chopped
  • Neutral oil for the pan

Steps

  1. Cook the grated coconut with jaggery and a pinch of salt over low heat until the jaggery melts and the mixture clumps. Cool.
  2. Whisk flour, egg, coconut milk, sugar, and a pinch of salt into a thin batter. Rest 10 minutes.
  3. Oil a pan and cook small thin crepes until the tops set and the undersides color lightly.
  4. Add a spoon of coconut filling and roll each crepe while it is warm.
  5. Serve the same afternoon.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A spoon of kitul treacle instead of jaggery
  • A pinch of salt and lime zest in the coconut

Pairings

  • Black tea with milk on the side
  • Kitul treacle
  • A slice of plantain
