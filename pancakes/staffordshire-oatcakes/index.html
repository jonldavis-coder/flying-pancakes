Arriving in Stoke-on-Trent, Staffordshire

Staffordshire oatcakes

You come in by Train. United Kingdom · Western Europe.

Pronounced STAFF-erd-sher OAT-cakes

Large, soft, yeasted oatmeal pancakes from the Potteries, rolled around a savory filling.

1 hr 20 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 large oatcakes

History

Staffordshire oatcakes are yeasted oatmeal pancakes from the Potteries, the towns around Stoke-on-Trent. They are large, thin, and flexible, sold in oatcake shops and rolled around bacon, cheese, or sausage. They are not the dry Scottish oatcake, and they are not the Derbyshire batter on the next page. Both counties use yeast and oats. The Staffordshire cake is oat-heavy and meant to fold.

Cultural background

An oatcake shop batter is a local product. Home cooks keep a jug of it warm so the yeast stays alive. The cake is breakfast and lunch food, eaten hot.

Why it tastes this way

Oatmeal makes the crumb soft and a little sweet, and the yeast adds a mild sour note. The griddle dries the surface without turning it into a biscuit. A salty filling, cheese or bacon, is the usual contrast. This recipe leaves the filling for the plate so the batter stays meat-free.

How it is made

Fine oatmeal and a smaller share of wheat flour are mixed with yeast and warm milk, left until bubbly, then poured as large thin rounds on a greased bakestone or frying pan.

Authentic touch

Cook them large and thin enough to roll. A cake that cracks when folded needs a looser batter or a slightly hotter pan and a shorter cook.

How it is used

Hot, rolled or folded with cheese. Bacon or sausage is the shop-counter filling and adds meat. The batter itself is vegetarian.

Field notes

  • Potteries oatcakes are soft and rolled. Scottish oatcakes are crisp and dry.
  • Oatcake shops in Stoke-on-Trent still sell them as everyday food.
  • The batter is yeasted. It is not a baking-powder pancake.

Ingredients

  • 200 g fine oatmeal
  • 80 g plain wheat flour
  • 1 tsp instant yeast
  • 1 tsp sugar
  • 450 ml warm milk and water, mixed half and half
  • 1/2 tsp fine salt
  • Oil or butter, for the pan
  • 100 g cheddar, for serving

Steps

  1. Stir the oatmeal, flour, yeast, sugar, salt, and warm liquid into a batter like thin cream. Cover and leave until foamy, about 1 hour.
  2. Heat a wide pan or griddle and grease it lightly.
  3. Pour a thin round and cook until the top sets and the underside is pale gold. Turn once and cook through.
  4. Stack the cakes in a cloth so they stay flexible. Fill with cheese, roll, and eat hot.

Keep the rising batter covered. Discard it if it smells rotten.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Derbyshire oatcakes, a related but thicker local batter
  • A sweet roll with butter and honey, less typical than the savory shop cake

Pairings

  • Melted cheddar
  • Bacon or a pork sausage, if you eat meat
  • A grilled tomato
