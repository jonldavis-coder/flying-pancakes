Arriving in Oaxaca

Oaxacan memelas

You come in by Plane. Mexico · Mexico & Central America.

Pronounced meh-MEH-lahs

An elongated masa cake spread with oil, black beans, and a mild salsa, toasted at the edges.

30 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 memelas

History

Memelas are an Oaxacan comal cake: masa patted into a long oval, cooked, then brushed with fat and salsa. In many Oaxacan kitchens the fat is asiento, the soft drippings left after pork is rendered, or lard. This recipe uses neutral vegetable oil instead, so the plate stays vegetarian and vegan and the allergen list has no meat. The dough is still nixtamalized masa, a corn food older in Mexico than wheat hotcakes. A memela is longer than a sope and is not split like a gordita.

Cultural background

On a Oaxacan breakfast table, memelas sit beside eggs, beans, and salsa, or they are eaten from a market comal. The shape is elongated on purpose. A round sope from another state is a different antojito.

Why it tastes this way

The masa toasts along the thin edges and stays soft in the thicker middle. Oil carries the aroma of the salsa into the surface. Black beans add an earthy layer. The salsa, tomato and a small chile, is acidic and only mildly hot. Without asiento, you lose the porky depth those drippings give, and you taste the corn and the salsa more directly.

How it is made

Griddle oval masa cakes, brush with vegetable oil, spread a thin layer of black beans and salsa, and toast them again until the edges crisp.

Authentic touch

Pat the dough into a long oval, not a circle. Brush the oil on after the first cook, then return the memela to the comal so the salsa dries a little and sticks.

How it is used

Eat them for breakfast or as a market snack, with extra salsa if you want it. If you switch the oil for asiento, say so, because the dish is then made with pork.

Field notes

  • Asiento is pork fat, not a spice. This page leaves it out and uses vegetable oil.
  • Memelas are elongated. Sopes are smaller rounds with a pinched rim.
  • The masa is nixtamalized. Plain cornmeal will not stretch into the same oval.

Ingredients

  • 250 g masa harina
  • 320 ml warm water
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 3 tablespoons neutral vegetable oil
  • 200 g cooked black beans, mashed smooth
  • 2 tomatoes, 1 jalapeño, and a pinch of salt, chopped into a mild salsa

Steps

  1. Mix the masa harina, salt, and warm water. Knead, cover, and rest 20 minutes.
  2. Divide into 8 pieces and pat each into an elongated oval, thicker in the center.
  3. Cook on a dry comal over medium heat until both sides are set and spotted, about 3 minutes a side.
  4. Brush each memela with vegetable oil. Spread a thin layer of black beans and a spoon of salsa.
  5. Return them to the comal for a minute so the toppings heat and the edges crisp. Serve hot.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Asiento or lard in place of vegetable oil is the older Oaxacan spread. It adds meat and is not vegetarian or vegan.
  • Crumbled fresh cheese is a common finish. It adds dairy.
  • A red salsa de chile pasilla oaxaqueño is a local sauce. Use a mild amount if you want to keep the heat down.

Pairings

  • Black coffee or hot chocolate
  • Eggs cooked in salsa, on the side
  • Sliced avocado
