Palak cheela

You come in by Train. India · South Asia.

Pronounced PAH-luck CHEE-lah

A green spinach and gram-flour pancake, savory with ginger and cumin, soft rather than crisp.

20 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 3 · 6 green cheelas

History

Palak cheela is a North Indian gram-flour pancake with spinach ground or finely chopped into the batter. Besan is still the structure. The spinach is the vegetable, not a dye from somewhere else. Kitchens from Punjab through Delhi make it as a breakfast that uses a bunch of greens. It is unfermented. It is not the plain onion besan cheela, and it is not a moong dal cake.

Cultural background

Chandigarh markets sell spinach through the cool months, which is when this cheela tastes sweetest. The leaf should be washed well. Grit in the batter is the usual failure, not the spice. A mild green chile is enough.

Why it tastes this way

Spinach sweetens and loosens besan, and it turns the crumb green and moist. Ginger keeps the green from tasting grassy in a raw way. Cumin is the savory line. Because the cake is thicker and wetter than a plain cheela, the chickpea flavor is softer and the spinach reads in every bite.

How it is made

Blend spinach with besan, ginger, and spices, then cook small green pancakes on a moderate pan.

Authentic touch

Squeeze the cooked or very finely chopped spinach so the batter does not become a soup. Extra leaf water steams the cake and stops it browning.

How it is used

Serve hot with a spoon of pickle or a dairy-free chutney. It is a savory breakfast.

Field notes

  • Palak is spinach. The flour is still besan.
  • The cake is mixed and cooked at once, with no ferment.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups spinach leaves, washed
  • 1 cup besan (chickpea flour), with no wheat added
  • 1 inch ginger
  • 1 green chile
  • 1/2 teaspoon cumin seeds
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • Water only if the batter is stiff
  • Neutral oil for the pan

Steps

  1. Blend spinach, ginger, and green chile to a coarse puree.
  2. Stir in besan, cumin, and salt. Add a spoon of water only if you cannot spread the batter. Rest 10 minutes.
  3. Oil a pan over medium heat. Spread a small round.
  4. Cook until the underside browns and the top loses its raw shine. Flip and cook through so the besan is done.
  5. Repeat. The green darkens as it cooks, which is expected.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A handful of fenugreek leaves with the spinach
  • Grated carrot for sweetness

Pairings

  • Mint chutney
  • Lime
  • A side of yogurt, which is dairy
