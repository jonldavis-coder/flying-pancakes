Hungarian palacsinta

You come in by Train. Hungary · Eastern Europe.

Pronounced PAH-lah-chin-tah

Thin Hungarian crepes rolled with apricot jam.

15 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 crepes

History

Palacsinta are Hungary's thin pancakes, related by name to Austrian palatschinken and to Slavic palačinke. The everyday sweet version is rolled with jam, often apricot, or dusted with cocoa. A walnut filling is common and would add a tree-nut allergen, so it is a variation, not this recipe. Hortobágyi palacsinta, the savory paprika pancake, is its own page and is not the ancient origin of every palacsinta. These jam crepes are home and restaurant dessert.

Cultural background

A Hungarian home stacks palacsinta for an afternoon sweet or a Sunday lunch dessert. They are thin and flexible. Cocoa and sugar, shaken together in a dry jar, is a filling that needs no brand of chocolate spread.

Why it tastes this way

The crepe is lightly eggy and browned in spots. Apricot jam is tart and fragrant, which keeps the butter from cloying. A thin batter means you taste jam and toasted surface in the same bite. Sugar in the batter is small so the filling can be sweet.

How it is made

A thin batter is cooked as crepes and rolled around warmed apricot jam.

Authentic touch

Roll apricot jam inside, not a trademark chocolate spread. If you fill them with ground walnuts, they are no longer nut-free. The Hortobágy meat pancake is a different recipe.

How it is used

Rolled, as dessert or a snack, sometimes dusted with sugar.

Field notes

  • Palacsinta are thin crepes. Jam is the everyday sweet filling.
  • Walnut fillings are traditional and are a tree-nut variation, not this recipe.
  • The paprika meat pancake of Hortobágy is a separate page.

Ingredients

  • 150 g plain wheat flour
  • 1 tbsp sugar
  • 1 pinch fine salt
  • 2 large eggs
  • 300 ml whole milk
  • 100 ml sparkling water
  • 30 g unsalted butter, for the pan
  • 200 g apricot jam

Steps

  1. Whisk the flour, sugar, salt, eggs, milk, and sparkling water into a thin batter. Rest 10 minutes.
  2. Cook thin crepes in a buttered pan.
  3. Warm the jam so it spreads easily.
  4. Roll each crepe around a spoon of jam and serve.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Ground walnuts and sugar, which adds a tree-nut allergen
  • Hortobágyi palacsinta, the savory meat page

Pairings

  • Apricot jam
  • A cocoa and sugar sprinkle
  • Coffee
