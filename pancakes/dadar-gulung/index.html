A green pandan crepe rolled around sweet coconut.

History

Dadar gulung are Indonesian pandan crepes rolled around a coconut and palm-sugar filling. The green color comes from pandan leaf if you can juice or blend it, or from a drop of pandan extract. They are a snack and a market sweet. The crepe is rice flour. The filling is coconut.

Cultural background

They should be bright but not lurid. A little green is enough. The filling is cooked until it holds together. They are rolled, which is the gulung.

Why it tastes this way

Pandan tastes floral and grassy, a Southeast Asian leaf that wheat crepes do not have. Coconut and palm sugar are the sweet, the same family as many Indonesian snacks. Rice flour keeps the crepe soft and gluten-free. The roll shows the green outside and the brown filling at the end. It is a sweet, not a savory meal.

How it is made

Rice-flour crepes, green if you have pandan, are filled with cooked coconut and palm sugar and rolled.

Authentic touch

Cook the coconut filling until it is dry enough to roll. A wet filling bursts the crepe. If you have no pandan, leave the crepe white and say so. Do not use food coloring and call it pandan.

How it is used

At room temperature or warm, as a snack.

Field notes

  • Pandan should be real extract or leaf, not green food coloring.
  • The filling has to be dry enough to roll.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup rice flour
  • 1 1/4 cups coconut milk
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 teaspoon pandan extract, optional
  • 1 cup grated coconut
  • 1/3 cup palm sugar or brown sugar

Steps

  1. Cook coconut and palm sugar until the filling holds together. Cool.
  2. Whisk rice flour, coconut milk, salt, and pandan if you have it.
  3. Cook thin crepes.
  4. Fill and roll.
  5. Serve warm or cool.
  6. If you skip pandan, the crepe is plain. Say so.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Without pandan they are still coconut crepes. Call them unflavored.
  • Kue dadar is another name you will meet.

Pairings
