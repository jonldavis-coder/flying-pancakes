Dadar gulung

You come in by Train. Indonesia · Southeast Asia.

Pronounced DAH-dar GOO-loong

A green pandan crepe rolled around grated coconut and palm sugar.

25 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 green rolls

History

Dadar gulung is an Indonesian crepe colored with pandan and rolled around a coconut and palm-sugar filling. The name describes the roll. Javanese snack tables and market stalls both sell it, and Yogyakarta is a fair place to meet the green spiral in a display case. The crepe is rice flour, not wheat, in this recipe. Pandan gives the color and the floral smell. It is a different sweet from serabi, which is a small thick pancake with syrup, and from kue ape, which is molded.

Cultural background

The filling is cooked until the coconut looks moist and separate, like a Bengal coconut pitha filling, with palm sugar instead of jaggery. The crepe must be flexible and green all the way through. A wheat crepe would change the diet tags. This one stays gluten-free and, with no egg, vegan.

Why it tastes this way

Pandan tastes floral and grassy, a perfume that coconut and palm sugar underline rather than hide. Palm sugar is smoky and dark. Fresh coconut is rich. The rice crepe is mild, a green wrapper, so the filling leads. Because the roll is thin, you taste pandan and caramel coconut in the same bite. There is no chile and no dairy.

How it is made

Color a rice batter with pandan, cook thin crepes, and roll them around coconut cooked with palm sugar.

Authentic touch

Blend pandan leaves with a little water and strain the juice if you can get the leaves. A few drops of extract work, but the fresh leaf smells rounder. Roll the crepe while it is warm.

How it is used

Serve at room temperature as a snack, two rolls each. They keep covered for a day.

Field notes

  • Dadar gulung is a green rolled crepe. Pandan supplies the color when you use the leaf or a pandan extract.
  • The filling is coconut and palm sugar, not a meat or egg stuffing.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup rice flour, with no wheat added
  • 1 cup water or thin coconut milk
  • 2 tablespoons sugar
  • A pinch of salt
  • Pandan juice from 6 leaves blended with water and strained, or 1/2 teaspoon pandan extract
  • 1 cup grated coconut
  • 1/3 cup palm sugar, chopped
  • Neutral oil

Steps

  1. Cook the coconut, palm sugar, and a pinch of salt until the sugar melts and the filling clumps. Cool it.
  2. Whisk rice flour, water or coconut milk, sugar, salt, and pandan juice or extract into a thin green batter.
  3. Oil a pan and cook thin crepes until the tops set and the undersides color lightly. Keep them flexible.
  4. Spread a spoon of coconut filling on each crepe and roll it up.
  5. Serve the same day.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A pinch of salt in the coconut, which this recipe already uses
  • Extra pandan juice for a deeper green

Pairings

  • Hot tea
  • A glass of water after the palm sugar
  • A plate of other market sweets
