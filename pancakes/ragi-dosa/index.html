Ragi dosa

You come in by Train. India · South Asia.

Pronounced RAH-gee DOH-sah

An earthy, malt-dark finger-millet dosa with a mild chile edge and a crisp skin.

20 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 ragi dosas

History

Ragi dosa is a finger-millet griddle cake of Karnataka and Tamil kitchens, where ragi is an everyday grain. This version mixes ragi flour with a little rice flour and ferments the batter so it belongs with the dosa family. It is gluten-free only when neither flour has wheat added, which is how the ingredients here are written. Instant ragi dosas mixed with yogurt are another home habit. They are a different bowl from this ferment.

Cultural background

Ragi porridge and ragi mudde are the better-known millet dishes. The dosa is the griddle form, useful when cooks want the same grain in a crisp sheet. The color is naturally dark. That darkness is the millet, not burnt batter.

Why it tastes this way

Finger millet tastes earthy and slightly malty, with a bitterness that toasting on the pan turns pleasant. A little rice flour keeps the sheet from crumbling. Fermentation softens the bitterness and adds a small tang. Onion and green chile, if you scatter them on top, brighten a grain that can otherwise taste flat.

How it is made

Stir ragi flour and rice flour into a batter, ferment it until mildly sour, and cook thin seasoned dosas.

Authentic touch

Oil the pan well. Ragi batter sticks more than a rice-and-urad batter. Cook it a shade longer than a pale dosa so the millet flavor rounds out.

How it is used

Serve with coconut chutney. The dark color looks fully cooked even early, so trust the edge: it should lift cleanly.

Field notes

  • Ragi is finger millet, a different grain from rice, wheat, and sorghum.
  • The dosa is gluten-free in this recipe because the flours are ragi and rice, with no wheat.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup ragi flour (finger millet), with no wheat added
  • 1/4 cup rice flour, with no wheat added
  • 1 1/2 cups water
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 green chile, minced
  • 2 tablespoons chopped cilantro
  • Neutral oil for the pan

Steps

  1. Whisk both flours with water and salt into a smooth batter about as thick as crepe batter.
  2. Cover and ferment 8 to 12 hours until mildly sour and a few bubbles show. Active prep is only the mixing. Discard the batter if it spoils.
  3. Stir in chile and cilantro. Add a splash of water if the ferment made it stiff.
  4. Heat a griddle, oil it generously, and spread a thin round.
  5. Cook until the edge lifts and the underside smells toasted. Flip briefly if the center is still wet. Oil the pan again before the next dosa.

Discard the ferment if it smells rotten or grows mold.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Chopped onion and cilantro pressed on top
  • A pinch of cumin in the batter

Pairings

  • Coconut chutney
  • A dry potato palya
  • Plain yogurt on the side, which is dairy and separate from this batter
