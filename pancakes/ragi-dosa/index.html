An earthy finger-millet dosa, darker than a rice dosa.

History

Ragi, finger millet, is a traditional grain in parts of Karnataka and Tamil Nadu. Ragi dosa is a crepe that uses ragi flour with rice or with a rice-dal batter. It is both an older grain in a newer breakfast format and a home recipe people cook for the millet. This version mixes ragi flour into a fermented rice-dal batter.

Cultural background

The color is brown-gray. The taste is earthier than plain dosa. Chutney is still the partner.

Why it tastes this way

Finger millet tastes mineral and slightly bitter, a dryland grain that stores well. Mixed with fermented rice and dal, it keeps that earthiness and gains the sourness of the batter. The cake is a way millet enters the dosa breakfast without pretending ragi was always cooked as a crisp crepe in every century. The flavor is the grain.

How it is made

Ragi flour is stirred into fermented dosa batter and cooked as crepes.

Authentic touch

Ragi browns fast. Keep the heat at medium and cook the dosa through, because the millet can taste raw if it is only browned on the outside.

How it is used

With coconut chutney.

Field notes

  • Ragi is finger millet, a real South Indian grain.
  • The earthy taste is the millet, not a burnt pan, if you cook it moderately.

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 cups fermented dosa batter
  • 1/2 cup ragi flour
  • 1/4 cup water
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • Oil

Steps

  1. Whisk ragi, water, and salt into the dosa batter. Rest 10 minutes.
  2. Cook thin dosas on a medium oiled tawa until the underside is browned and the center is cooked.
  3. Flip if needed.
  4. Serve with chutney.
  5. They will be darker than rice dosas.
  6. Do not use high heat.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • An instant ragi dosa with yogurt is a different, quicker batter and adds dairy.
  • Plain dosa is the rice-dal cake without millet.

Pairings

  • coconut chutney
