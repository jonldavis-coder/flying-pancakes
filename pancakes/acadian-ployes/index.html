Arriving in Madawaska

Acadian ployes

You come in by Boat. Canada · North America.

Pronounced plwah

Mild buckwheat, a little sour from the grain, with a holey top and a soft underside.

10 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 10 ployes

History

Acadian ployes are buckwheat pancakes of the Madawaska region, on the New Brunswick side of the Saint John River and in nearby Maine. The batter is usually cooked on one side. Buckwheat suited the cool seasons of that border country. The map pin is in Canada, and the same cakes are made across the river in the United States.

Cultural background

A ploye batter is thin, often buckwheat plus some wheat flour, water, and baking powder. Many recipes have no egg and no milk. This one follows that pattern. It is not gluten-free, because of the wheat flour.

Why it tastes this way

Buckwheat tastes earthy, and cooking on one side leaves the top soft and full of holes while the bottom browns. Water keeps the flavor plain. Butter or maple at the table is how many people eat them, and those additions are dairy or sweetness on top of a batter that has neither.

How it is made

Thin buckwheat batter cooked on one side on a hot griddle.

Authentic touch

Cook them on one side only, until the top is dry and bubbled. Flipping them makes a different pancake. Serve them as a bread, not only as a dessert.

How it is used

Hot, often with butter, maple syrup, or baked beans. Cretons, a pork spread, is a local accompaniment for people who eat pork.

Field notes

  • Ployes belong to the Madawaska region of New Brunswick and to nearby Maine.
  • They are traditionally cooked on one side, until the top dries and forms holes.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup buckwheat flour
  • 1/2 cup all-purpose flour
  • 2 teaspoons baking powder
  • 3/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1 1/2 cups cold water
  • 1 teaspoon neutral oil, for the griddle

Steps

  1. Whisk both flours, the baking powder, and the salt.
  2. Whisk in the cold water until the batter is smooth and thin, like cream. Add a spoon of water if it is stiff.
  3. Heat a griddle over medium-high heat and wipe it with a thin film of oil.
  4. Pour about 1/4 cup batter. It should spread.
  5. Cook on one side until the top is dry, the surface is full of holes, and the underside is brown. Do not flip.
  6. Stack them and serve hot.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Some families use a higher share of buckwheat. Unless that flour is certified gluten-free, do not call the cakes gluten-free.
  • An egg in the batter is a later enrichment and removes the vegan label.

Pairings

  • Maple syrup
  • Baked beans
  • Butter, if you eat dairy
