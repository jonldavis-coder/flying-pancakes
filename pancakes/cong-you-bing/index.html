Arriving in Shanghai

Cong you bing

You come in by Plane. China · East Asia.

Pronounced tsong yoh bing

A flaky wheat cake layered with scallion and oil, crisp outside and chewy within.

30 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 4 pancakes

History

Cong you bing is a scallion pancake made from laminated dough, not from a poured batter. Wheat dough is rolled thin, painted with oil, scattered with salt and scallion, rolled into a log, coiled, and rolled out again so the oil and onion form layers. It is then pan-fried. Shanghai is one city strongly associated with it, and northern and Taiwanese kitchens make close versions. The flake comes from the laminating, the same idea as a pastry, done on a griddle.

Cultural background

Street griddles press the coil flat and fry it until the layers separate. At home the dough needs a rest so it rolls without springing back. This is a savory snack or a side, not a dessert and not a crepe. There is no egg in the written recipe.

Why it tastes this way

Oil trapped between sheets of dough fries the layers from the inside, which is why the pancake shatters and then chews. Scallion perfumes those layers instead of sitting in a batter. Salt on the oil makes each leaf savory. The toasted wheat tastes nuttier than a boiled noodle. Because nothing is fermented, the flavor is clean fat, onion, and crust.

How it is made

Laminate a wheat dough with oil, salt, and scallion, coil it, roll it flat, and pan-fry until the layers flake.

Authentic touch

Roll, oil, sprinkle, and coil. If you knead the scallions into a ball of dough, you lose the layers and the cake tastes like a bun. Fry from a cold-medium start or the outside browns before the coil cooks.

How it is used

Cut into wedges and eat hot. It is street food and a side with soup or congee. The crisp does not last.

Field notes

  • Cong you bing is laminated dough. It is not a batter you pour.
  • The coil is what makes the flake. Scallion alone does not.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups all-purpose wheat flour
  • 3/4 cup hot water
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt, plus more for the layers
  • 3 tablespoons neutral oil or sesame oil
  • 1 bunch scallions, finely chopped
  • More oil for the pan

Steps

  1. Stir flour and salt with hot water into a dough. Knead until smooth. Rest 20 minutes.
  2. Divide into four. Roll one piece very thin. Brush with oil, salt it, and cover it with scallion.
  3. Roll the sheet into a log, coil the log into a spiral, and gently roll the spiral into a thick round.
  4. Pan-fry over medium heat in a little oil until both sides are golden and the layers have separated at the edge.
  5. Repeat with the remaining dough. Cut and serve.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A pinch of white pepper in the oil
  • A few drops of chile oil on the finished wedge

Pairings

  • A small dish of black vinegar
  • Soy milk
  • Congee
