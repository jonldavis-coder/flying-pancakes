Arriving in Poland

Racuchy

You come in by Train. Poland · Eastern Europe.

Pronounced rah-TSOO-khee

Small yeasted Polish pancakes with apple, dusted with sugar.

1 hr 20 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 12 small pancakes

History

Racuchy are small Polish yeast pancakes, often with apple in the dough, fried in a pan and dusted with sugar. They are a snack and a home dessert, not the thin naleśnik and not a potato placki. The yeast needs about an hour. Baking powder versions exist. This page keeps the yeast.

Cultural background

Apple racuchy are the version most people mean. The fruit is diced small so it cooks inside the cake. They are eaten the day they are fried.

Why it tastes this way

Yeast makes them slightly tangy and airy. Apple softens and perfumes the crumb. A sugar dusting adds crunch. The fry in oil or butter gives a thin crust. They taste more like a small doughnut pancake than like a crepe. Cinnamon is optional and not required for the cake to be racuchy.

How it is made

A yeast batter rises, diced apple is folded in, and small cakes are fried, then dusted with sugar.

Authentic touch

Let the yeast batter actually rise before you fry. Put apple in the batter, not only on the side. Sugar goes on after frying.

How it is used

Warm, as a snack or dessert, with sugar or a little jam.

Field notes

  • Racuchy are small yeast pancakes, often with apple.
  • They are not thin naleśniki.
  • Sugar is dusted on after they are fried.

Ingredients

  • 200 g plain wheat flour
  • 1 tsp instant yeast
  • 2 tbsp sugar, plus more for dusting
  • 1/4 tsp fine salt
  • 1 large egg
  • 200 ml warm milk
  • 1 apple, finely diced
  • Oil or butter, for frying

Steps

  1. Mix the flour, yeast, sugar, salt, egg, and warm milk into a thick batter. Cover until bubbly, about 1 hour.
  2. Fold in the apple.
  3. Fry spoonfuls in hot oil or butter until both sides are brown and the center is cooked.
  4. Dust with sugar and serve warm.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • No apple, which is a plainer racuch
  • A baking-powder batter, faster and less yeasty

Pairings

  • Powdered sugar
  • Jam
  • Sour cream
