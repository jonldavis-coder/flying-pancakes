Pronounced LUM-mur

Small, thick oatmeal pancakes, lightly sweet.

15 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · about 10 cakes

History

Lummur are Icelandic oatmeal pancakes, thicker than pönnukökur, made with oats and often served with butter, sugar, or jam. They are a homey cake, sometimes associated with Thorri and midwinter festivities, and also with ordinary cooking. Oats make them heartier than the thin pancake.

Cultural background

They are cooked as small rounds. A sour note from buttermilk or skyr in some recipes is pleasant. This version uses milk and a spoon of skyr-style yogurt if you have it, with milk as the measured liquid so the recipe is clear.

Why it tastes this way

Oats in a small thick cake taste toasted and filling, which suits a midwinter table more than a lacy dessert crêpe. Iceland imported grain for a long time, and oats were a practical one. Sugar on top, rather than a very sweet batter, keeps them a breakfast cake. The thickness is the difference from pönnukökur.

How it is made

Oats and flour are mixed with egg and milk, rested, and cooked as small thick pancakes.

Authentic touch

Let the oat batter rest 10 minutes so the flakes swell. Cook the cakes small. A large lumma stays raw inside.

How it is used

Butter and sugar or jam.

Field notes

  • Lummur are the oatmeal pancakes of Iceland.
  • A rest lets the oats hydrate.

Ingredients

  • 1/2 cup rolled oats, ground or fine
  • 1/2 cup flour
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1 large egg
  • 1 cup milk
  • 1 tablespoon melted butter

Steps

  1. Stir the batter and rest 10 minutes.
  2. Cook small cakes on a buttered medium griddle.
  3. Flip when the tops set.
  4. Cook until the center is done.
  5. Serve with butter and sugar.
  6. They are thicker than pönnukökur on purpose.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Pönnukökur are the thin rolled pancakes.
  • A spoon of skyr in the batter adds tang and is still dairy.

Pairings

  • butter
  • sugar
  • jam
