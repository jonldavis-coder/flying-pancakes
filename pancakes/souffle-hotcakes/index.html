Souffle hotcakes

You come in by Plane. Japan · East Asia.

Pronounced soo-FLAY HOT-kakes

Extremely thick, jiggly pancakes with a browned cap and a soft, egg-white interior.

20 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 3 · 3 very thick cakes

History

Souffle hotcakes are a contemporary Japanese cafe trend of the 2010s. Cooks whip egg whites, fold them into a small amount of flour and milk, and bake the batter slowly in rings so the cakes rise very tall. The style moved through city cafes and then into home kitchens. It is a recent fashion in the hotcake story, separate from the kissaten hotcake and from a traditional confection like dorayaki. It is contemporary.

Cultural background

The cakes need time and low heat. A ring keeps the side from flowing away. Powdered sugar and butter are the usual finish, sometimes with fruit. They collapse as they cool, which is expected. The texture is the reason people order them.

Why it tastes this way

Whipped egg white makes the crumb light and a little eggy, closer to a souffle than to a diner pancake. A small measure of flour and milk keeps it from being only meringue. Slow browning adds a toasted cap while the inside stays moist and sweet. The height is steam and foam, so the flavor is delicate. Syrup or cream makes it a dessert even at breakfast.

How it is made

Whip egg whites, fold them into a yolk and flour batter, and cook tall rounds in rings on very low heat under a lid.

Authentic touch

Fold the whites in gently and do not open the lid for the first several minutes. A slam of the pan knocks the rise out. Low heat is the whole trick.

How it is used

Serve immediately. They are best while still tall. Dust with powdered sugar and add butter or cream.

Field notes

  • Souffle hotcakes are a 2010s cafe trend. They are contemporary.
  • The height comes from whipped egg whites cooked slowly in rings.

Ingredients

  • 2 eggs, separated
  • 2 tablespoons all-purpose wheat flour
  • 2 tablespoons milk
  • 2 tablespoons sugar
  • 1/4 teaspoon baking powder
  • A pinch of salt
  • Butter for the pan and rings
  • Powdered sugar to serve

Steps

  1. Stir the yolks with flour, milk, baking powder, and salt into a thick paste.
  2. Whip the whites to soft peaks, adding the sugar gradually. Fold the whites into the yolk paste in two additions.
  3. Butter two or three rings and set them in a buttered pan on the lowest heat. Fill the rings nearly to the top.
  4. Cover and cook about 8 minutes without lifting the lid. Flip the rings carefully, cover again, and cook until the centers are just set.
  5. Remove the rings, dust with powdered sugar, and serve at once.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A few drops of vanilla
  • Matcha in the batter, which turns them green and slightly bitter

Pairings

  • Powdered sugar
  • Butter
  • Whipped cream and berries
