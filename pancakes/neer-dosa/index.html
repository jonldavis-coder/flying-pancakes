Arriving in Mangaluru, Karnataka

Neer dosa

You come in by Train. India · South Asia.

Pronounced neer DOH-sah

A soft, pale, almost translucent rice roll with a clean cereal taste and no sour ferment.

25 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 4 · 10 soft dosas

History

Neer dosa belongs to Mangaluru and the Tulu coast of Karnataka. Neer means water, which matches the batter: soaked rice ground with enough water to look like thin rice milk. No fermentation is required. The dosa is cooked quickly, stays flexible, and is rolled rather than crisped into a wafer. It is a coastal rice bread, separate from the fermented rice-and-urad dosa of the inland hotel menu.

Cultural background

Tulu and other coastal kitchens serve it with coconut chutney, a chicken or fish curry, or a simple scrape of jaggery and grated coconut. This recipe keeps the dosa itself free of fish and meat so the tags match the written batter.

Why it tastes this way

Without urad and without a ferment, there is no tang and no lacy wheat crust. Freshly ground rice tastes sweet and mild. The extra water lets the sheet set thin and pliable instead of brittle. Coconut, if you add a little to the grind, rounds the flavor the way coastal cooking often does, with the fat of the nut rather than with ghee.

How it is made

Soak rice, grind it with plenty of water to a watery batter, and cook soft rounds that can be rolled at once.

Authentic touch

Use a warm, not screaming-hot, pan and spread with the back of the ladle in one or two strokes. The dosa is done when the top turns from shiny to matte. Browning it hard dries it out.

How it is used

Stack them under a clean cloth so they stay flexible. Serve with coconut chutney. A fish curry is a common Mangaluru partner and would add a fish allergen.

Field notes

  • Neer dosa does not need an overnight ferment. The batter is cooked the day it is ground.
  • The sheet is supposed to bend. A cracker-crisp result is a different dosa.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup raw rice
  • 1 1/4 cups water, plus more for grinding
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1 tablespoon grated coconut, optional
  • Neutral oil for the pan

Steps

  1. Rinse the rice and soak it in water for 3 to 4 hours. Soaking is waiting, not active prep.
  2. Drain and grind the rice with the coconut, if using, and fresh water until the batter is completely smooth and as thin as light cream.
  3. Stir in the salt. The batter should pour in a steady stream. Add water if the grind is thick.
  4. Heat a nonstick or well-seasoned pan over medium heat. Grease it lightly. Pour a ladle of batter and tilt the pan, or spread once, to make a thin round.
  5. Cover for a few seconds if the top looks wet. When the surface is matte and the edge lifts, roll the dosa off. Keep the rolls covered while you finish the batch.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A small piece of coconut ground with the rice
  • A pinch of salt only, which is the plainest home version

Pairings

  • Coconut chutney
  • Jaggery and fresh coconut
  • A vegetable coconut curry
