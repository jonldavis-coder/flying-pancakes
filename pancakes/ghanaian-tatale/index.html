Pronounced tah-TAH-leh

Ripe plantain fritters, sweet and spiced, fried in small cakes.

15 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · about 12 fritters

History

Tatale are Ghanaian ripe-plantain fritters, mashed with flour, ginger, and chile, then fried. They are a snack and a side, especially along the coast and in home cooking. They are fritters, not flipped pancakes, and they belong here as the plantain cake of the Gulf of Guinea.

Cultural background

Ripe, spotted plantains are essential. Green plantains will not mash into a sweet batter. They are eaten hot, with beans or on their own.

Why it tastes this way

A ripe plantain is sweet because its starch has turned to sugar, which is why tatale taste like a dessert and a snack at once. Ginger and chile are the heat and perfume of Ghanaian frying. A little flour holds the mash. Palm oil is a traditional frying fat with its own red color and flavor. Neutral oil is the plainer home choice written here, and palm oil is the variation.

How it is made

Mashed ripe plantain is mixed with flour, ginger, and chile and fried as small cakes.

Authentic touch

Use plantains that are yellow with black spots. Mash them fine. Fry spoonfuls in enough oil to brown the sides, and drain them well.

How it is used

Hot, as a snack or beside beans.

Field notes

  • Ripe plantains are the requirement. Green ones are a different, starchier food.
  • Tatale are fritters. They are not kenkey or banku, which are fermented doughs.

Ingredients

  • 2 very ripe plantains
  • 1/4 cup flour
  • 1 teaspoon grated ginger
  • 1 fresh chile, seeded and minced, or 1/4 teaspoon cayenne
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • Oil for frying

Steps

  1. Mash the plantains. Stir in flour, ginger, chile, and salt.
  2. Heat 1/2 inch of oil over medium.
  3. Fry spoonfuls until deep golden on both sides.
  4. Drain.
  5. Serve hot.
  6. The center should be hot and set.

Fry over steady heat. Chile heat is real. Seed the chile if you want it milder.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Palm oil for frying is traditional and changes the color and flavor.
  • A milder cake uses less chile.

Pairings

  • beans
  • stew
