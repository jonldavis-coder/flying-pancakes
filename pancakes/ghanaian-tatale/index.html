Arriving in Accra

Ghanaian tatale

You come in by Plane. Ghana · West Africa.

Pronounced tah-TAH-lay

Sweet ripe plantain fritters with ginger and a mild chile warmth, fried until the edges crisp.

20 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · About 12 fritters

History

Tatale are ripe plantain cakes from Ghana. Very ripe plantain is mashed with ginger, onion, and chile, bound with a little flour, and fried. They are a snack and a street food, not a breakfast stack poured on a dry griddle. The method here is frying, which is how they are often cooked. The chile and ginger put the heat at mild.

Cultural background

Black-spotted plantains are the right fruit. Green plantain is a different, starchy dish. Tatale show up at home and at stalls, eaten hot, sometimes with beans and sometimes on their own. The spice paste is pounded or grated into the mash.

Why it tastes this way

Ripe plantain brings banana-like sweetness and a soft mash. Frying in oil browns the outside and keeps the center custardy. Ginger is floral and warm. A small chile adds mild heat without covering the fruit. Wheat flour only holds the mash together. It is not the flavor.

How it is made

Mash very ripe plantains. Stir in grated ginger, chopped onion, a mild chile, salt, and enough wheat flour to make a thick paste. Fry spoonfuls in oil until deep golden on both sides. Drain and eat warm.

Authentic touch

Use plantains that have gone soft and sweet, and fry the mash. A dry-skillet pancake made from green plantain is not tatale. The ginger and chile are part of the mash, not a sauce.

How it is used

Tatale are a snack or street food, hot from the oil. They can sit beside beans. They are not a dessert with syrup, though the plantain is sweet. This version is mild, not extra-hot.

Field notes

  • Tatale are fried ripe-plantain cakes. This recipe fries them.
  • Ginger and a little chile make the heat mild.
  • Green plantain will not give the sweet mash this dish needs.

Ingredients

  • 3 very ripe plantains, peeled and mashed
  • 1 tablespoon grated fresh ginger
  • 1 small onion, finely chopped
  • 1 mild fresh chile, seeded and chopped
  • 1/2 teaspoon fine salt
  • 1/2 cup wheat flour
  • Oil for shallow frying

Steps

  1. Mash the ripe plantains until mostly smooth, with a few soft lumps left.
  2. Stir in the ginger, onion, chile, salt, and wheat flour. The paste should hold a spoonful. The chile keeps the heat mild.
  3. Heat a finger's depth of oil in a wide pan until a drop of mash sizzles at once.
  4. Fry spoonfuls, turning once, until both sides are deep golden. Do not crowd the pan. This method is frying, not a dry griddle.
  5. Drain on a rack and eat while hot.

Hot oil is the cooking fat. The dish contains wheat and a mild chile. Do not leave the pan unattended.

Tatale are best the hour they are fried. Refrigerate leftovers and reheat in a hot pan. They will not be as crisp the next day. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Leave the chile out for no heat. The ginger will still taste warm.
  • Rice flour can replace wheat for a gluten-free binder. This written recipe uses wheat.
  • A hotter chile is a family choice and would move the heat tag up.

Pairings

  • Stewed beans
  • A squeeze of lime
  • More fried rounds, because the first plate goes quickly
