Arriving in Tunis

Tunisian mlawi

You come in by Boat. Tunisia · North Africa.

Pronounced MLAH-wee

Soft, oiled, layered round with a flaky edge and a mild wheat taste.

35 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 6 · 6 round flatbreads

History

Mlawi, also written mlaoui, is the layered flatbread of a Tunisian breakfast. Oiled dough is coiled or folded into a round, flattened, and cooked on a griddle. It is a cousin of Moroccan msemen and it is not the same bread: the Tunis version is usually a round breakfast flatbread, eaten with harissa, olive oil, honey, or an egg. This page keeps that shape and that table.

Cultural background

In Tunis, mlawi is early food, sold at stalls and cooked at home before the day heats up. The bread itself is not a chile dough. Heat arrives when someone spoons harissa beside it. A tuna or egg breakfast is a stall habit and adds fish or egg.

Why it tastes this way

Oil between the layers fries them lightly, so the bread tastes toasted and rich without butter. Semolina in the dough gives a fine grit. Because it is coiled into a round and then pressed, the flakes spiral instead of sitting in a Moroccan square. Harissa on the side is sour and hot against a mild crumb.

How it is made

Make a soft dough of flour, semolina, salt, water, and oil. Rest it, stretch it into a ribbon, brush it with olive oil, and coil it into a disk. Flatten the disk and griddle it until puffed in places and golden on both sides.

Authentic touch

Coil the oiled strip into a round, then flatten it. Do not cut the recipe across into msemen squares and rename them. The bread is unspiced. Put harissa on the plate, not in the dough, if you want this version.

How it is used

Mlawi is breakfast and street food. Eat it with olive oil and harissa, with honey, or as a wrap around an egg. Tuna is a common stall addition and would add a fish allergen that this dough does not have.

Field notes

  • Mlawi is a Tunisian layered flatbread, distinct from Moroccan msemen.
  • It is also written mlaoui.
  • The dough is mild. Harissa is served beside it rather than mixed in.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups wheat flour (about 250 g)
  • 1/2 cup fine semolina
  • 1 teaspoon fine salt
  • 3/4 cup warm water
  • 3 tablespoons olive oil, plus more for brushing

Steps

  1. Mix the flour, semolina, salt, water, and a spoon of olive oil into a soft dough. Knead briefly and rest it, covered, for 15 minutes.
  2. Divide the dough into 6 pieces. Stretch one piece into a long thin ribbon and brush it with olive oil.
  3. Coil the ribbon into a round, then press or roll the round flat. This layered round is mlawi, not a square of msemen.
  4. Repeat with the remaining dough, keeping the coils covered so the oil does not dry.
  5. Cook on a hot griddle with a trace of oil, turning once, until both sides are golden and the layers at the edge flake.

The dough contains wheat. Harissa served alongside is hot even though the bread is tagged with no chile heat.

Reheat leftover mlawi on a skillet. Refrigerate it once cool. Olive oil on the surface can go stale if the bread sits out. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • The spelling mlaoui points to the same bread.
  • A square fold is closer to msemen. This recipe stays round.
  • Adding tuna or egg at the stall changes the allergens and the diet tags.

Pairings

  • Harissa and olive oil
  • Honey
  • A fried egg, which adds egg
