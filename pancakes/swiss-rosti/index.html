Swiss rösti

You come in by Train. Switzerland · Western Europe.

Pronounced RUHR-shti

A shredded potato cake from Switzerland, pan-fried as one round. Not a batter pancake.

40 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 4 · 1 large cake, 4 wedges

History

Rösti is a Swiss shredded-potato cake, fried as a round and served whole or in wedges. It is not a batter pancake. There is no flour and no egg in this recipe. The potatoes are parboiled, chilled, grated, and fried in butter. Bern's version often includes bacon. This page writes the plain cake and names bacon as a variation, so the main recipe stays vegetarian. The word Röstigraben is a common name for the cultural line between German-speaking and French-speaking Switzerland, taken from this potato cake.

Cultural background

Rösti is a meal beside fried eggs or a supplement beside meat and cheese. It is everyday food in German-speaking cantons, not a festival-only dish. The crust is the point.

Why it tastes this way

Parboiled potato gratings brown into a crust while the inside steams and softens. Butter tastes nutty when it browns. Salt is the seasoning. There is no egg to make it cakey and no flour to make it bready. A raw-potato rösti exists in some kitchens. This page uses the chilled, parboiled method because it shreds cleanly and crisps.

How it is made

Waxy potatoes are boiled in their skins, chilled, peeled, grated, seasoned, and fried in butter as one cake until a deep crust forms on both sides.

Authentic touch

Say plainly that this is a potato cake, not a poured pancake. Press the shreds into one round and leave them alone long enough to crust before you turn the cake. Bacon is the Bern variation, not a requirement.

How it is used

As a savory meal or a side, with eggs, cheese, or a salad. Bacon on top is the Bern-style variation and adds meat.

Field notes

  • Rösti is shredded potato fried as a cake. It is not a batter pancake.
  • This recipe has no flour and no egg.
  • Bacon is typical of Bern's version. The plain cake is written here.

Ingredients

  • 800 g waxy potatoes
  • 1 tsp fine salt
  • 40 g unsalted butter
  • 1 tbsp neutral oil

Steps

  1. Boil the potatoes in their skins until just tender. Drain, chill until cold, then peel and grate coarsely.
  2. Toss the gratings with salt.
  3. Heat the butter and oil in a wide nonstick pan. Add the potato and press it into an even round. Cook over medium heat until the bottom is a deep crust, about 10 minutes.
  4. Turn the cake onto a plate and slide it back to brown the other side. Cut in wedges.

Turning a large rösti is easier with a plate. The pan handle will be hot.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Bärner Rösti, with bacon, which adds a meat allergen and is not vegetarian
  • Raw grated potato instead of parboiled, a different and also real method

Pairings

  • Fried eggs
  • Cheese
  • A green salad
