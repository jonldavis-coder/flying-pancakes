A lacy, crisp instant dosa of semolina and rice flour, with onion and chile.

20 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · about 8 dosas

History

Rava dosa is an instant dosa, made with semolina (rava), rice flour, and water, without an overnight ferment. It became a popular tiffin-room dosa because it is fast and crisp, with a lacy look. It is not a substitute story for fermented dosa. It is its own recipe. Semolina is wheat, so it is not gluten-free.

Cultural background

Onion, cumin, and green chile are scattered on the batter as it cooks. The batter is thin, almost like water, which is why the lace forms.

Why it tastes this way

Semolina browns and crisps, and rice flour keeps the lace from tasting like a wheat pancake. No ferment means no sourness, so cumin, chile, and onion do the flavoring. The thin batter hits a hot tawa and sets in holes. That lace is the texture people order it for. The wheat in semolina is the allergen.

How it is made

A thin unfermented batter of rava and rice flour is poured on a hot tawa and cooked until crisp.

Authentic touch

Stir the batter every time you pour, because the semolina settles. Pour from a height or in a loose spiral and do not spread it like a fermented dosa. Spreading kills the lace.

How it is used

With chutney. It is best immediately. It loses its crispness as it sits.

Field notes

  • Rava dosa is instant. It is not a failed fermented dosa.
  • Semolina means it contains wheat.

Ingredients

  • 1/2 cup semolina
  • 1/2 cup rice flour
  • 2 tablespoons all-purpose flour
  • 1 1/2 cups water
  • 1/2 teaspoon cumin seeds
  • 1 green chile, minced
  • 1 small onion, finely chopped
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • Oil for the tawa

Steps

  1. Whisk the flours, water, cumin, chile, onion, and salt. Rest 15 minutes.
  2. Stir again. The batter should be thin.
  3. Pour onto a hot oiled tawa in a lacy spiral. Do not spread hard.
  4. Drizzle oil and cook until the underside is crisp. A flip is optional and often breaks the lace.
  5. Serve at once with chutney.
  6. Stir before each pour.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Onion rava dosa is this cake with more onion pressed in.
  • It will never taste sour. That is not a flaw.

Pairings

  • coconut chutney
  • sambar
