Rava dosa

You come in by Train. India · South Asia.

Pronounced RAH-vah DOH-sah

A lacy, cracker-crisp semolina sheet scented with cumin, black pepper, and curry leaf.

20 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 crisp dosas

History

Rava dosa is a semolina batter, usually stirred and cooked without the long rice-and-urad ferment. Wheat semolina, often called rava or sooji, is the grain that builds the crisp web. Rice flour is there to help the sheet set. It became a restaurant standard because a cook can mix it to order when a fermented batter has run out. It is a different dosa from the fermented rice and urad kind.

Cultural background

The batter is meant to be thin and a little lumpy. Those lumps and the water around them are what leave the holes. A thick batter makes a pancake, which is not the point of this page.

Why it tastes this way

Semolina browns faster than a wet fermented rice batter and tastes toasted and wheat-y. Cumin and crushed black pepper sit on the surface instead of disappearing into a ferment. Curry leaf, dropped into hot oil or onto the batter, adds a sharp green note. The lack of a sour ferment is the flavor clue that this is rava dosa.

How it is made

Whisk semolina, rice flour, and water into a thin batter, season it, and pour it loosely onto a hot griddle so it laces.

Authentic touch

Pour the thin batter from the ladle in separate streams and let the holes stay open. Do not spiral it into a solid disk.

How it is used

Lift it off while it is still brittle. Coconut chutney is the usual partner. It softens if it waits in a stack.

Field notes

  • Rava here is wheat semolina, so this dosa is not gluten-free.
  • The lacy holes are a pouring method. They are not a sign of fermentation.

Ingredients

  • 1/2 cup fine wheat semolina (rava or sooji)
  • 1/3 cup rice flour
  • 1 1/2 cups cold water, plus more if needed
  • 1/2 teaspoon cumin seeds
  • 1/4 teaspoon crushed black pepper
  • 1 green chile, finely chopped
  • 8 curry leaves, torn
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • Neutral oil for the griddle

Steps

  1. Whisk semolina, rice flour, salt, and cold water into a thin batter. Rest it 15 minutes so the semolina hydrates. It should stay pourable.
  2. Stir in cumin, pepper, green chile, and curry leaves. Thin with a splash of water if it has thickened.
  3. Heat a griddle until very hot. Grease it lightly and pour the batter in separate ribbons rather than one tight spiral.
  4. Fill obvious gaps with a little more batter, then drizzle oil around the lace. Cook until the sheet is golden and rigid.
  5. Loosen the edge and lift the dosa off. Cook the rest, stirring the batter each time so the spices do not sink.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A few cumin seeds extra on the surface
  • Buttermilk in place of part of the water, which adds dairy

Pairings

  • Coconut chutney
  • Allam-style ginger chutney
  • Sambar
