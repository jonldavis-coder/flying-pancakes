Danish Pandekager

Pronounced pahn-eh-KAY-uh

Thin, lightly sweet pancakes rolled with jam or sugar.

15 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · about 8 pancakes

History

Pandekager are Denmark’s thin pancakes, close to a crêpe, served with jam, sugar, or ice cream. They are a common dessert. Æbleskiver are the other famous Danish pancake, round and cooked in a special pan, and they are not the same dish.

Cultural background

Children and adults both expect them to be thin enough to roll. Strawberry jam is a usual filling. The batter is egg and milk.

Why it tastes this way

A thin egg batter tastes delicately of vanilla or sugar if you add them, and of browned butter from the pan. Denmark’s dairy is the richness. Jam supplies acid and fruit in a season when fresh berries are not on the table. The cake is a vehicle for that jam, which is why it stays thin.

How it is made

A thin batter is cooked in a buttered pan and rolled with jam.

Authentic touch

Swirl a thin layer. A thick pandekage is just a pancake from somewhere else. Sugar the cake while it is hot if you are not using jam.

How it is used

With jam or a dust of sugar. Ice cream makes it a dessert course.

Field notes

  • Pandekager are thin. Æbleskiver are the round pan cakes.
  • Jam or sugar is the usual finish.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup flour
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 1/2 cups milk
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla
  • Butter for the pan
  • Jam, to serve

Steps

  1. Whisk the batter and rest 10 minutes.
  2. Cook thin pancakes in butter.
  3. Spread with jam and roll.
  4. Serve warm.
  5. Stack extras under a towel.
  6. They reheat in a dry pan.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Æbleskiver are the spherical cousins.
  • A squeeze of lemon is less Danish than jam, and still good.

Pairings

  • jam
  • sugar
  • ice cream
