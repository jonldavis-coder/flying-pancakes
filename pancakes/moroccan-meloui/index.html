A round, coiled flatbread, flaky and rich, cooked on a griddle.

40 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 6 · 6 rounds

History

Meloui, or rghaif in some naming, are round coiled Moroccan pan breads, a cousin of square msemen. The dough is stretched, oiled, and rolled into a spiral, then flattened and cooked. They are breakfast breads, served with honey. Naming varies by region. This page uses meloui for the coil, and msemen for the square, which is a useful distinction even where cooks blur it.

Cultural background

The spiral is the difference you can see. The taste is still oil, dough, and honey.

Why it tastes this way

A coil creates layers in a round instead of a square, so the bread puffs in a spiral and tastes flaky. The same soft dough and the same griddle as msemen are at work. Honey is the sweetener because it soaks the hot layers. Semolina keeps the coil from gluing shut before it cooks.

How it is made

Soft dough is stretched, oiled, coiled, flattened, and cooked on a griddle.

Authentic touch

Roll the oiled strip into a coil, then flatten gently so you can still imagine the spiral. If you mash it paper-thin, the layers vanish.

How it is used

Warm, with honey.

Field notes

  • The coil is what separates this bread from square msemen in this guide.
  • Regional names overlap. The shape is the clear difference.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups flour
  • 1/2 cup fine semolina
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 3/4 cup warm water
  • 1/4 cup oil, plus more for shaping

Steps

  1. Make a soft dough from flour, semolina, salt, water, and 1/4 cup oil. Rest 15 minutes.
  2. Stretch each piece into a strip, oil it, and roll it into a coil.
  3. Flatten the coil into a round.
  4. Cook on a medium oiled griddle until both sides are spotted and the center is cooked.
  5. Serve with honey.
  6. Eat warm.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Msemen are the squares.
  • A savory coil with spicy filling is a different, stuffed bread.

Pairings

  • butter
