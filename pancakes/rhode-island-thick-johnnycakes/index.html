Rhode Island Thick Johnnycakes

Soft corn interior, browned face, and the sweetness of milk.

History

Thick johnnycakes are the other Rhode Island style: cornmeal stirred with cold milk rather than spread as a lace with boiling water. They are associated especially with cooks who want a substantial cake. The shared history with thin cakes is New England cornmeal on a griddle, built on an Indigenous crop and a colonial kitchen. The method is different enough that Rhode Island treats them as a separate cake.

Cultural background

They are breakfast, often with butter and maple syrup, and they are more filling than the thin South County cake. Milk makes them less lean.

Why it tastes this way

Cold milk does not scald the meal, so the texture stays softer and tastes of milk sugar as well as corn. A thicker pour browns outside and stays moist inside, closer to a pancake than to a cracker. Maple syrup on top is a northern New England habit that fits this thicker, milder cake better than it fits a lacy thin one.

How it is made

White cornmeal is stirred with cold milk and salt and cooked as thick, small cakes.

Authentic touch

Do not add boiling water to this version. Heat is what turns it into the thin cake. Keep the batter thick and the cakes small.

How it is used

Butter and maple syrup, or beside baked beans.

Field notes

  • Thick cakes use cold liquid. Thin cakes are scalded and spread.
  • Both are johnnycakes. Arguing about that is a Rhode Island pastime with a real basis in method.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup white cornmeal
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 cup cold whole milk
  • Butter or oil for the griddle

Steps

  1. Stir meal, salt, and milk into a thick batter. Rest 5 minutes.
  2. Add a spoon of milk only if it cannot leave the spoon.
  3. Cook small thick rounds on a greased medium-low griddle.
  4. Flip when the bottom is well browned.
  5. Cook until the center is no longer raw meal.
  6. Serve with butter.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A teaspoon of sugar is optional and modern.
  • Water instead of milk gives a leaner cake and removes the dairy allergen.

Pairings

  • maple syrup
  • butter
  • baked beans
