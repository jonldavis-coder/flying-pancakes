Arriving in Cartagena

Arepa de huevo

You come in by Boat. Colombia · South America.

Pronounced ah-REH-pah deh WEH-voh

A fried corn cake with a whole egg cooked inside, crisp outside and set in the middle.

15 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 4 · 4 fried arepas

History

Arepa de huevo is a coastal Colombian street food, especially in Cartagena and Barranquilla. A corn cake is fried, slit, filled with a raw egg, sealed, and fried again until the egg sets. It is fried, not griddled dry like an Antioquia breakfast arepa. The egg is the filling, so this recipe is not vegetarian. The plain corn cake is older than this stuffed, fried way of eating it. Frying an egg inside is a coastal technique, not a single ancient recipe, and the dough is precooked cornmeal, not nixtamalized masa and not fresh choclo.

Cultural background

Stalls along the Caribbean coast fry them to order and hand them over in a napkin. Cheese or a drizzle of suero, a sour cream, sometimes goes on afterward. Those dairy additions are not in this recipe. The written cake is corn, salt, egg, and oil.

Why it tastes this way

Frying gives the corn a deep, crisp crust that a dry griddle does not. The second fry sets the egg so the white is firm and the yolk stays just soft if you move quickly. The corn dough tastes plain and salty, a shell for the egg. There is no cheese in this batch, so the flavor stays with corn and fried egg. Oil, not chile, is the richness.

How it is made

Mix yellow precooked cornmeal with salted water, form disks, and fry them. Slit each cake, slide in an egg, close it, and fry until the egg is set.

Authentic touch

Fry the sealed cake once so it is firm enough to hold a slit. Add only one egg, press the opening closed, and fry again until the egg is cooked. A raw white in the center is not done. Keep the oil deep enough to brown both sides.

How it is used

Eat them immediately, from a napkin or a plate, as a street snack or a quick meal. They are greasy on purpose. Add suero only if you accept the dairy.

Field notes

  • Arepa de huevo is fried twice: once to set the shell, again to cook the egg.
  • The egg means this recipe is not vegetarian, even though there is no meat.
  • The dough is precooked cornmeal. It is not masa and not fresh sweet corn.

Ingredients

  • 250 g yellow precooked cornmeal
  • 360 ml warm water
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 4 eggs
  • Neutral oil for frying

Steps

  1. Stir the cornmeal, salt, and warm water into a soft dough. Rest 5 minutes. Form 4 thick disks and smooth the cracks.
  2. Heat 2 centimeters of oil over medium heat until a pinch of dough sizzles at once.
  3. Fry the disks, turning once, until they are firm and lightly golden. Drain them a moment.
  4. Cut a slit in the side of each cake, pour in one egg, and press the opening closed.
  5. Fry again, turning carefully, until the outside is deep gold and the egg is set. Drain and serve at once.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A slice of cheese in with the egg adds dairy.
  • Ground beef in place of the egg is a different stuffed fry and adds meat.
  • Griddling this dough without the egg gives you a plain coastal arepa, which is a different dish from the fried egg version.

Pairings

  • Suero or sour cream, which adds dairy
  • A fresh tomato and onion relish
  • Lime and a cold drink
