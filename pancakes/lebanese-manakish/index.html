Arriving in Beirut

Lebanese manakish

You come in by Boat. Lebanon · Middle East.

Pronounced mah-NAH-keesh

Soft round flatbread topped with thyme, sumac, sesame, and olive oil.

30 min prep · 12 min cook · Serves 6 · 6 small flatbreads

History

Manakish are Lebanese flatbreads baked with a topping, and the za'atar one is the breakfast loaf. Za'atar here means a mix of dried thyme, sumac, sesame, and salt, stirred with olive oil and spread on dough. Bakeries and home ovens both make them. This recipe uses a hot oven, not a saj. A saj griddle is the other common method and is not the one written out below. The singular is often man'oushe.

Cultural background

In Beirut, za'atar manakish are morning food, eaten on the way to work or at the kitchen table. Cheese manakish are a different topping and contain dairy. The za'atar bread is the one that can stay vegan. Sesame is in the topping, so the allergen list includes it.

Why it tastes this way

Thyme is herbal and dry, sumac is sour, and sesame is nutty and toasty once the oven hits it. Olive oil keeps the topping from tasting like dust and fries it lightly on the dough. The bread underneath is mild and a little sweet from wheat. There is no chile, so the brightness is acid from the sumac, not heat.

How it is made

Mix a soft dough and let it rise. Shape small rounds, dimple them, and spread a paste of thyme, sumac, sesame, salt, and olive oil. Bake on a hot stone or sheet until the rim puffs and the topping looks glossy and toasted.

Authentic touch

Bake these in a very hot oven, which is the method this page gives. Spread the za'atar and oil to the edge. A saj version is real and is not the set of steps below. Sesame is part of the topping.

How it is used

Za'atar manakish are breakfast, street food, and a snack. They are eaten with tea, labneh, or vegetables. Labneh is dairy and is not in the dough. Cheese manakish are a separate pie.

Field notes

  • This recipe bakes the bread in a hot oven. A saj griddle is the other usual method.
  • Za'atar on this page is thyme, sumac, sesame, salt, and olive oil.
  • Man'oushe is the singular. Manakish is the plural.

Ingredients

  • 2 1/2 cups wheat flour (about 310 g)
  • 1 teaspoon instant yeast
  • 1 teaspoon fine salt, divided
  • 1 cup warm water
  • 1 tablespoon olive oil, for the dough
  • 3 tablespoons dried thyme
  • 1 tablespoon ground sumac
  • 2 tablespoons sesame seeds
  • 3 tablespoons olive oil, for the topping

Steps

  1. Mix the flour, yeast, half the salt, water, and the tablespoon of olive oil into a soft dough. Knead until smooth. Cover and let it rise for 45 minutes.
  2. Stir the thyme, sumac, sesame seeds, remaining salt, and the topping oil into a spreadable paste. The sesame is an allergen in this bread.
  3. Heat the oven to 230 C, or as hot as it will go. Divide the dough into 6 rounds and press each into a disk.
  4. Spread the za'atar paste over each disk, leaving a small rim. This method is the oven, not a saj.
  5. Bake until the rims puff and the topping is fragrant and the bottoms are spotted, about 8 to 12 minutes.

The dough contains wheat. The topping contains sesame. Sumac is sour, not a chile, so the heat tag stays at none.

Eat manakish the day they are baked. Wrap leftovers and reheat them in a hot oven. The topping tastes dull from a microwave. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A cheese topping, often akkawi, adds dairy and is not this recipe.
  • More sumac makes a tarter topping. More thyme makes it grassier.
  • Cooking on a saj instead of in the oven is the other method and needs a different pan.

Pairings

  • Tea
  • Sliced tomato and cucumber
  • Labneh, which adds dairy
