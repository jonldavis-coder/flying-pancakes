History

Roti canai is the flaky Malaysian Indian flatbread, cousin to Indian parotta, cooked on a griddle and served with dal or curry. The dough is oiled, rested, stretched, and folded. It is a mamak-stall breakfast and a late-night food. This is the bread, not the curry.

Cultural background

A good roti canai shatters into flakes. The stretch is the skill. A home version that is merely rolled thin is honest if it does not claim circus tossing.

Why it tastes this way

Oil in a soft dough, stretched thin and folded, fries into layers on a hot griddle. That flake is why people pull the bread apart. The Indian Muslim food stalls of Malaysia made it a national breakfast. Dal on the side is savory and thin, a sauce for the bread. The bread itself is mild, salty, and rich.

How it is made

An oiled dough is rested, stretched, folded, and cooked on a griddle.

Authentic touch

Rest the dough until it is very soft. Oil the counter, stretch as thin as you can without theater, fold, and cook on a hot griddle with a little oil. Clap it so the layers open.

How it is used

With dal or a simple curry.

Field notes

  • Roti canai is defined by the stretch and the flake.
  • A home version can be rolled thin without being tossed in the air.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups flour
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 2 tablespoons oil
  • 3/4 cup water
  • Oil for the griddle

Steps

  1. Mix a soft dough and rest 30 minutes.
  2. Oil the counter, stretch each piece thin, and fold it into a layered square.
  3. Cook on a hot oiled griddle until flaky and spotted.
  4. Clap the bread so the layers open.
  5. Serve with dal.
  6. Keep finished roti wrapped.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Roti telur has an egg broken on the bread as it cooks.
  • Trinidadian paratha is the Caribbean cousin of the same technique.

Pairings
