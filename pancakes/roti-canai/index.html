Roti canai

You come in by Boat. Malaysia · Southeast Asia.

Pronounced ROH-tee chah-NIGH

A flaky, laminated wheat flatbread, griddled until the outside shatters and the inside stays stretchy.

30 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 6 flatbreads

History

Roti canai is a Malaysian griddled flatbread made from laminated dough. Oil or ghee is spread through a wheat dough that is stretched, coiled, and cooked on a griddle until it flakes. The bread has Indian-Muslim heritage in Malaysia, carried by cooks and shops rather than by a single named inventor on this page. It is breakfast and late-night food, usually with dal or a curry. Singapore's roti prata is the same family and has its own page about coffee-shop life there. This page is the Malaysian bread.

Cultural background

Mamak stalls clap the dough on the board until it is wide and thin, then crumple it onto the griddle. The clap is skill. At home, rolling and coiling still makes layers if the dough is well oiled and rested. The bread itself is not spicy. The curry beside it may be.

Why it tastes this way

Layers of dough fried in ghee taste rich and wheat-y, with a crisp leaf and a chewy center. Salt is the only seasoning in the bread, so the flavor is fat and toast. A lentil curry or a spicy dal adds the salt and chile the roti does not carry. The lamination is why it tastes different from a single chapati: you get many surfaces of fried dough in one piece.

How it is made

Laminate a wheat dough with ghee, stretch and coil it, and griddle it until flaky.

Authentic touch

Oil the dough generously and rest it. A dry dough will not stretch into layers. Cook it on a moderately hot griddle and press it so the coil makes contact.

How it is used

Serve hot with dal or a chicken curry. The chicken curry would add meat. A lentil dal keeps the meal vegetarian. Tear the bread and dip it.

Field notes

  • Roti canai is laminated dough, stretched and griddled, not a poured batter.
  • Its heritage in Malaysia is Indian-Muslim cooking, and this page does not name one inventor.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups all-purpose wheat flour
  • 3/4 teaspoon salt
  • 2/3 cup water
  • 3 tablespoons ghee or neutral oil, plus more for cooking
  • 1 teaspoon sugar

Steps

  1. Mix flour, salt, sugar, water, and a tablespoon of ghee into a soft dough. Knead until smooth.
  2. Divide into six balls. Coat each with ghee. Rest at least 1 hour. That rest is not active prep.
  3. Stretch or roll one ball thin, spread a little ghee, and fold or coil it into a layered round.
  4. Griddle on medium heat with a film of ghee, pressing, until both sides are flaky and spotted brown.
  5. Repeat. Serve at once with dal.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Roti telur, with an egg cracked on the griddle, which adds egg
  • A banana and sugar version

Pairings

  • Lentil dal
  • Sugar on a piece, for a sweet bite
  • A fish curry, which would add fish
