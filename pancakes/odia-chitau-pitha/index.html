A soft, holey rice cake from Odisha, served with milk or molasses.

15 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · about 8 cakes

History

Chitau pitha is an Odia rice cake cooked to form holes, traditionally on a covered earthen pan, and associated with festivals including the month of Shravan. It is eaten with milk, ghee, or molasses. This home version uses a covered nonstick pan and serves it with molasses so the cake itself stays vegan. Ghee is the dairy variation.

Cultural background

The look is close to chitoi and baghrir: a sponge on top, a soft body. The grain is rice.

Why it tastes this way

Steam in a covered pan makes the holes, and rice batter tastes clean and slightly sweet. Festival calendars in Odisha give the cake a season. Molasses soaks the sponge. Milk, if you use it, makes it a milder breakfast. The covered cook is the technique. An open pan just dries the batter.

How it is made

Rice-flour batter is cooked covered until holey and served with molasses.

Authentic touch

Cover the pan and resist flipping. The top should look set and pitted before you take it off. A thick batter makes a dumpling. Keep it pourable.

How it is used

With molasses, or with warm milk if you eat dairy.

Field notes

  • Chitau pitha is the holey Odia rice cake.
  • A covered pan is the home stand-in for the traditional covered griddle.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup rice flour
  • 1 1/4 cups water
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • Molasses, to serve

Steps

  1. Whisk a thin batter and rest 10 minutes.
  2. Cook small rounds in a lightly oiled covered pan over medium until the top is holey and set.
  3. Do not flip.
  4. Serve with molasses.
  5. Keep the heat moderate.
  6. Eat them fresh.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Ghee on top is a festive finish and adds dairy.
  • Chakuli is the fermented crepe, not this cake.

Pairings

  • molasses
  • milk
