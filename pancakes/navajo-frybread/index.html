Arriving in Window Rock

Navajo frybread

You come in by Plane. United States · North America.

Fried wheat dough, blistered and salty, crisp outside and soft inside.

15 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 8 · 8 pieces

History

Navajo frybread is a food of survival under confinement and commodity rations, not a pre-contact tradition. Wheat flour and fat were issued foods, including during the Long Walk and the 1860s imprisonment at Bosque Redondo, and later commodity programs kept those ingredients in Navajo kitchens. Families still fry the bread at home and at public events. This page gives the plain flour version and does not treat that history as an ancient custom.

Cultural background

Window Rock is the capital of the Navajo Nation and the map pin. The bread is fried, which is why the journal files it as a fritter. Baking powder is part of the later household dough. The written recipe uses oil and water.

Why it tastes this way

Hot oil puffs the dough and makes a blistered crust while the inside stays bready. Salt is the main seasoning. There is no fermented sourness and no corn flavor. Any sweetness comes from a topping, not from the dough in this recipe.

How it is made

Leavened wheat dough fried in hot oil.

Authentic touch

Fry it. A baked disk of the same dough is a different bread. A small slit in the center helps the dough cook evenly. Remember the ration history when you tell it.

How it is used

Hot, as a bread. Beans are a common savory topping. Powdered sugar is a sweet one. Neither topping is required for the dough.

Field notes

  • Frybread came from rationed flour and fat under confinement, including Bosque Redondo, and it is not a pre-contact food.
  • A slit in the center of each disk is a common way to help it fry through.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1 tablespoon baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 3/4 cup warm water
  • 4 cups neutral oil, for frying

Steps

  1. Stir the flour, baking powder, and salt. Add the warm water and mix to a soft dough. If it is dry and cracking, add another tablespoon of water.
  2. Cover and rest 10 minutes.
  3. Divide into 8 pieces. Pat each into a disk about 1/2 inch thick and cut a small slit through the center.
  4. Heat 1 inch of oil in a deep skillet to 350°F.
  5. Fry one or two pieces at a time until blistered and golden, about 1 to 2 minutes per side. Do not crowd the pan.
  6. Drain on a rack and serve hot.

Use a deep pan, keep the oil below smoking, and do not leave it. Drain the bread on a rack, not on a pile of paper that can slide into the fat.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Some cooks use a spoon of powdered milk in the dough, which adds dairy.
  • Lard was a ration fat. This written recipe uses neutral oil.

Pairings

  • Cooked beans
  • Powdered sugar
  • Stew, using the bread as a scoop
