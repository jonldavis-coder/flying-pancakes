Georgia Peach Pancakes

Juicy peach in a lightly sweet buttermilk cake.

15 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · about 8 pancakes

History

Georgia’s peach orchards are a real commercial crop, famous since the late nineteenth century, though South Carolina also grows peaches at scale. Pancakes with fresh peach slices are a modern breakfast that uses the local fruit in season. They are not a documented antebellum recipe. Out of season, the honest move is to wait or use frozen peaches, not a syrup that only tastes like peach candy.

Cultural background

Roadside stands and home kitchens in peach country cook this in summer. The cake is ordinary buttermilk batter. The fruit is the news.

Why it tastes this way

A ripe peach is sweet, acidic, and full of water. That water keeps the crumb tender and can also make the center gummy if the slices are piled on too thick. Summer heat is why the fruit is here at all: peaches want a warm season. A pinch of sugar in the batter is enough because the fruit already carries sugar.

How it is made

Buttermilk pancakes are cooked with ripe peach slices set into the top before the flip.

Authentic touch

Pat the slices dry and lay them on the batter after you pour it, rather than stirring raw peach into the bowl. The cakes brown instead of steaming.

How it is used

Butter, and only a little syrup. A spoon of plain yogurt is a tart contrast. Whipped cream makes it dessert.

Field notes

  • This is a modern seasonal breakfast, stated here as that, not as an old folk recipe.
  • South Carolina grows a large share of the commercial peach crop too.

Ingredients

  • 1 ripe peach, thinly sliced and patted dry
  • 1 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1 cup buttermilk
  • 1 large egg
  • 2 tablespoons melted butter

Steps

  1. Stir the dry ingredients together, then stir in the wet ingredients until the batter is combined.
  2. Pour the batter, then lay two or three peach slices on each cake before bubbles set.
  3. Heat a griddle or heavy skillet over medium heat and film it with butter.
  4. Spoon on the batter. Cook until the underside is browned and the top looks set.
  5. Flip once and cook the second side through.
  6. Flip carefully. The fruit side browns quickly, so keep the heat at medium.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Frozen peaches, thawed and dried, work in winter. Canned peaches in syrup are much sweeter.
  • A dust of nutmeg is enough spice. Peach pie spice mixes muddy the fruit.

Pairings

  • fresh peaches
  • butter
  • yogurt
