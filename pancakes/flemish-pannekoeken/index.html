Arriving in Flanders

Flemish pannekoeken

You come in by Train. Belgium · Western Europe.

Pronounced PAH-nuh-koo-kuhn

Thick Flemish pancakes, finished with brown sugar and butter.

15 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 pancakes

History

Flemish pannekoeken are the pancakes of Flanders, in Belgium. They are home and fair food, often thicker than a French crepe and served with butter and brown sugar. A splash of beer in the batter is a regional habit and is included here. The beer and the wheat mean this is not a gluten-free recipe. These are not Dutch poffertjes and not French galettes. This page does not claim one town invented them.

Cultural background

Brown sugar, or a coarse beet sugar, and butter are the topping. The pancake can be a snack at a fair or a dessert at home. It is sweet on this page.

Why it tastes this way

A thicker batter stays soft inside and toasts outside. Beer adds a faint bitterness that brown sugar answers. Butter carries the sugar into the crumb. They taste rounder and less lacy than a Dutch plate-sized pancake.

How it is made

Flour, egg, milk, and a little beer make a thick batter. Cakes are cooked in butter and topped with brown sugar.

Authentic touch

Use a mild beer and let the batter rest so the flour hydrates. Brown sugar on the hot cake, not only white sugar in the bowl, is the finish.

How it is used

Warm, with butter and brown sugar, as a snack or dessert.

Field notes

  • This is the Flemish pancake, pinned to Belgium.
  • Brown sugar and butter are the usual finish.
  • A little beer in the batter is a regional option, and this recipe uses it.

Ingredients

  • 200 g plain wheat flour
  • 1 tbsp brown sugar, plus more for serving
  • 1 pinch fine salt
  • 2 large eggs
  • 250 ml whole milk
  • 100 ml mild beer
  • 40 g unsalted butter, for the pan and serving

Steps

  1. Whisk the flour, sugar, salt, eggs, milk, and beer into a smooth batter thicker than a crepe batter. Rest 10 minutes.
  2. Cook ladlefuls in butter over medium heat until both sides are brown.
  3. Stack them buttered.
  4. Sprinkle brown sugar on the hot cakes and serve.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Apple slices on the batter
  • No beer, replaced with milk, if you want the cake without alcohol

Pairings

  • Brown sugar
  • Butter
  • A cup of coffee
