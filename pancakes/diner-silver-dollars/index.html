Arriving in Jersey City

Diner silver-dollar pancakes

You come in by Train. United States · North America.

Mild, buttery, and lightly sweet, with more browned edge than center because the cakes are small.

15 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 24 small pancakes

History

Silver-dollar pancakes are ordinary diner pancakes cooked in rounds about the size of an old silver dollar. The small diameter browns the edge while the center stays tender, and a plate holds a pile of them. The batter is the wheat, milk, and egg mix of a twentieth-century diner, not a regional heirloom. Jersey City is the map pin for a habit that was never limited to one town.

Cultural background

Short-order cooks liked the size because the cakes finished quickly and stacked neatly on an oval plate. The name is a comparison to the coin, not a recipe tied to a mint.

Why it tastes this way

Small cakes spend more of their surface against the griddle, so they taste toastier than a wide pancake from the same batter. Milk keeps the flavor milder than buttermilk. Butter in the pan is the main richness.

How it is made

Griddled as small rounds in butter.

Authentic touch

Keep each round near 2 inches across. A full-size pancake from this batter is a different order.

How it is used

A tall pile on a warm plate, with butter and maple syrup.

Field notes

  • The name describes the diameter, about the width of an old silver dollar.
  • Diner cakes of this size cook through in a couple of minutes.

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 2 tablespoons sugar
  • 2 teaspoons baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 1/4 cups whole milk
  • 2 large eggs
  • 2 tablespoons unsalted butter, melted, plus more for the griddle

Steps

  1. Whisk the flour, sugar, baking powder, and salt.
  2. Whisk in the milk, eggs, and melted butter until the batter is smooth and pourable.
  3. Heat a griddle over medium heat and coat it lightly with butter.
  4. Pour tablespoons of batter so each cake stays about 2 inches wide.
  5. Flip when the tops look set and the undersides are golden. Cook the second side briefly.
  6. Stack and serve with butter and maple syrup.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Use buttermilk and a pinch of baking soda if you want them tangy.
  • Cook a blueberry into the center of each round.

Pairings

  • Butter
  • Maple syrup
  • Fried eggs
