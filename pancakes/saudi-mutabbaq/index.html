Arriving in Jeddah

Saudi mutabbaq

You come in by Plane. Saudi Arabia · Middle East.

Pronounced moo-TAB-bak

A thin, pan-fried stuffed flatbread with a savory leek and egg center and a mild chile edge.

40 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 4 · 4 folded breads

History

Mutabbaq is a stuffed flatbread of the Hijaz, sold in Jeddah and other Saudi cities and also known as mutabbaq, a name that means folded. The dough is stretched thin, wrapped around a filling, and pan-fried until blistered. Leek and egg are one home and stall filling. Minced meat is another. A related folded bread is called murtabak in parts of Southeast Asia. This page is the Jeddah street bread, written as a stuffed flatbread, not as a poured pancake.

Cultural background

Mutabbaq is night and late-afternoon street food as much as a home pan. The stretch of the dough is the skill: thin enough to blister, strong enough to hold egg. The mild chile in this filling is a warm note, not a hot sauce bath.

Why it tastes this way

The wrapper tastes of toasted wheat and frying oil, with crisp bubbles where the dough was thin. Leek goes sweet in the pan. Egg sets into a savory custard. Cumin and a little chile make the heat mild and the aroma warm. Because the dough is folded, some bites are all crust and some are mostly filling.

How it is made

Rest a soft, oiled dough. Cook leek with cumin and a mild chile, then stir in beaten egg just until set. Stretch the dough, fold the filling inside an envelope, and pan-fry it in a film of oil until both sides blister.

Authentic touch

Stretch the dough thin and fold it around the filling before it goes into the pan. It is a stuffed flatbread, also known as mutabbaq. A meat filling is a common stall choice and is not the vegetarian recipe written here.

How it is used

Mutabbaq is street food and a savory snack, eaten hot, sometimes with a lime wedge or a simple salad. It is not a dessert. The meat version should be ordered as meat, not assumed from this recipe.

Field notes

  • Mutabbaq means folded, and the bread is also known by that same name.
  • This recipe is a stuffed flatbread, pan-fried, not a batter pancake.
  • The Jeddah filling here is leek and egg, with a mild chile.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups wheat flour (about 250 g)
  • 3/4 teaspoon fine salt, divided
  • 3/4 cup water
  • 3 tablespoons oil, plus more for the pan
  • 2 leeks, sliced and washed
  • 1 mild green chile, chopped
  • 1/2 teaspoon ground cumin
  • 2 eggs, beaten

Steps

  1. Mix the flour, half the salt, the water, and a spoon of oil into a soft dough. Rest it for 20 minutes.
  2. Cook the leeks, chile, cumin, and remaining salt in a spoon of oil until the leeks are soft. Pour in the eggs and stir until just set. Cool the filling. The chile is mild.
  3. Divide the dough into 4. Oil the board and stretch each piece thin. This is a stuffed flatbread, also called mutabbaq.
  4. Spoon filling into the center and fold the dough into an envelope, sealing the edges.
  5. Pan-fry in a film of oil, turning once, until both sides are blistered and golden and the dough is cooked through.

The dough contains wheat and the filling contains egg. Cook the egg in the filling until it is set. The pan oil is hot. Heat from the chile is mild.

Refrigerate leftover mutabbaq and reheat it in a hot pan. Do not leave the egg filling at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Minced meat replaces the egg and leek in many stalls and is not vegetarian.
  • A greener filling uses more leek and less chile, which softens the mild heat further.
  • The Southeast Asian murtabak is a related folded bread, not this Hijazi batch.

Pairings

  • Lime wedges
  • A chopped salad
  • Black tea
