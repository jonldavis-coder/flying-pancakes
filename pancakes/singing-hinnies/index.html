Arriving in Northumberland

Singing hinnies

You come in by Train. United Kingdom · Western Europe.

Pronounced SING-ing HIN-ees

Currant griddle cakes from Northumberland. The fat sizzles, which is the singing.

20 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 cakes

History

Singing hinnies are currant griddle cakes from Northumberland and the north-east of England. The usual kitchen explanation of the name is that the fat sings on a hot griddle, and that hinny is a local word for someone dear. That is an explanation of a name, not a dated record, and this page treats it as that. The cake is a fat-rubbed dough cooked on a girdle, split, and buttered.

Cultural background

They belong to a tea table in the north-east. Currants are the fruit. There is no yeast. Chemical leavener in this recipe is a home convenience so the cake is not leaden.

Why it tastes this way

Butter in the dough and butter on the griddle brown at the edge. Currants sweeten single bites rather than the whole crumb. The inside stays short, closer to a scone than to a crepe. A hot griddle is what makes the fat sing and the base crisp.

How it is made

Fat is rubbed into flour with currants and a little leavener, mixed to a soft dough with milk, rolled into rounds, and cooked on a greased griddle.

Authentic touch

Use enough fat that you can hear it. A dry pan will not sing, and the cake will toughen. Split the hinny while warm and butter the cut face.

How it is used

Split and buttered, as a snack or a substantial tea. They are not a poured pancake.

Field notes

  • The singing is the sound of fat on a hot griddle.
  • They are a Northumberland cake, cut and buttered like a griddle scone.
  • Currants, not chocolate, are the fruit in the dough.

Ingredients

  • 250 g plain wheat flour
  • 1 tsp baking powder
  • 1/2 tsp fine salt
  • 60 g cold unsalted butter, plus more for the griddle
  • 40 g currants
  • 120 ml whole milk

Steps

  1. Mix the flour, baking powder, and salt. Rub in the cold butter until the bowl looks like crumbs. Stir in the currants.
  2. Add the milk and bring the dough together. It should be soft, not wet. Pat into rounds about 1 cm thick.
  3. Cook on a buttered griddle over medium heat until the underside is brown, then turn. You should hear the fat.
  4. Split each cake and butter it. Eat while warm.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A few strips of candied peel with the currants
  • Lard in place of some of the butter, which adds a meat allergen and removes the vegetarian diet

Pairings

  • Extra butter
  • A slice of cheese
  • Tea
