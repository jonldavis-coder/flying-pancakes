Filipino hotcakes

You come in by Boat. Philippines · Southeast Asia.

Pronounced fil-ih-PEE-noh HOT-kakes

Soft wheat breakfast pancakes, browned in margarine and eaten with sugar or condensed milk.

10 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 hotcakes

History

Filipino hotcakes are a 20th century breakfast. American colonial influence brought wheat pancakes and canned milk into Philippine kitchens, and the cakes stayed after that period as a local morning food. Homes and small cafes cook them in margarine and serve them with sugar or sweetened condensed milk more often than with maple syrup. They sit beside rice meals. They did not replace rice. This page tells that history directly. The batter is wheat, egg, and milk, a modern breakfast cake, not a precolonial rice cake.

Cultural background

A Manila breakfast can include hotcakes, eggs, and garlic rice on the same table. The hotcake is the wheat part of that spread. Margarine is the common frying fat. Condensed milk is both an ingredient in some batters and a topping. This recipe uses milk in the batter and condensed milk at the table.

Why it tastes this way

The flavor is a soft, sweet wheat crumb with browning from the pan and a salty dairy note from margarine. Egg and milk make it tender. Sugar on top or condensed milk makes it sweeter than a butter-and-maple stack, with a cooked-milk taste. There is no ferment and no coconut. The taste is a 20th century breakfast that became ordinary in Filipino homes.

How it is made

Mix a simple baking-powder batter and cook thick breakfast pancakes in margarine, finished with sugar or condensed milk.

Authentic touch

Fry them in margarine, not a dry nonstick pan, if you want the breakfast-shop taste. Serve sugar or condensed milk while the cakes are hot enough to melt it.

How it is used

Stack two or three on a plate for breakfast. Offer sugar and condensed milk. Coffee or hot chocolate can sit beside them.

Field notes

  • These hotcakes became a Philippine breakfast in the 20th century, through American colonial influence, and then became local.
  • Margarine, sugar, and condensed milk are the usual fats and sweets, more than maple syrup.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup all-purpose wheat flour
  • 2 teaspoons baking powder
  • 2 tablespoons sugar
  • 1 egg
  • 3/4 cup milk
  • 2 tablespoons margarine, melted, plus more for the pan
  • A pinch of salt
  • Sweetened condensed milk or extra sugar to serve

Steps

  1. Whisk flour, baking powder, sugar, and salt.
  2. Stir in egg, milk, and melted margarine until just combined.
  3. Heat a pan and add a little margarine.
  4. Pour small rounds and cook until bubbles set on top and the undersides are brown. Flip and finish.
  5. Serve hot with sugar or condensed milk.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A ripe banana sliced into the batter
  • A little more margarine for a crisper edge

Pairings

  • White sugar
  • Sweetened condensed milk
  • Fried eggs and garlic rice on the same breakfast table
