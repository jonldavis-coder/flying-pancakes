A soft breakfast pancake, often served with butter and sugar or syrup.

10 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · about 8 pancakes

History

Filipino hotcakes are the breakfast pancakes of home kitchens and fast-food breakfasts, a wheat baking-powder cake served with butter and sugar or syrup. They are a twentieth-century breakfast, not bibingka and not a rice cake. Condensed milk on top is a local sweetness.

Cultural background

They are thick enough to stack and soft rather than crisp. Butter and sugar, not maple, are the usual home finish.

Why it tastes this way

The batter tastes mild and eggy, a café pancake. Condensed milk is the Filipino pour, cooked milk and sugar, closer to the region’s sweets than maple is. The cake is an adopted breakfast that became ordinary. It sits in this journal beside bibingka so the two are not confused.

How it is made

A simple milk batter is cooked as pancakes and finished with butter and condensed milk or sugar.

Authentic touch

Cook on medium and do not press them. A spoon of condensed milk on the hot cake is enough. More makes them soggy.

How it is used

Stacked, warm.

Field notes

  • Filipino hotcakes are the wheat breakfast pancake.
  • Condensed milk is a common local pour.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup flour
  • 2 tablespoons sugar
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1 cup milk
  • 1 egg
  • 2 tablespoons melted butter
  • Condensed milk, to serve

Steps

  1. Mix a batter and rest 5 minutes.
  2. Cook on a buttered medium griddle.
  3. Serve with butter and a spoon of condensed milk.
  4. Do not press them flat.
  5. They should be soft.
  6. Bibingka is a different cake.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Ripe banana mashed into the batter is a home variation.
  • Bibingka is the rice cake of Christmas, not this.

Pairings

  • butter
