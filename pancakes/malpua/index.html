Arriving in Lucknow, Uttar Pradesh

Malpua

You come in by Train. India · South Asia.

Pronounced mahl-POO-ah

A fried sweet pancake, fennel-scented, soaked in sugar syrup until the edges lacquer.

15 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 5 · 10 malpuas

History

Malpua is a fried sweet pancake of North India and Bengal, served at Holi and at other festivals. Bangladesh shares the Bengali feast table, so it is listed here as well. The batter is wheat flour and milk, often with fennel, fried in ghee and bathed in syrup. Some Bengali versions are richer with khoya. This Lucknow-leaning recipe keeps the fennel and the syrup and does not invent a courtly origin story.

Cultural background

Holi tables expect fried sweets after the colors. Malpua sits with gujiya in many northern houses and with other festival sweets in Bengal. It is not a breakfast crepe. The fry and the syrup are the method.

Why it tastes this way

Fennel is the spice that makes malpua taste like itself rather than like a plain doughnut. Milk in the batter browns and softens the crumb. Syrup cooked to a sticky thread clings to the lacy edge. Ghee, or oil if you must, carries that aroma. The contrast is crisp fringe and a soaked, fragrant center.

How it is made

Mix a loose fennel batter with milk and flour, fry small pancakes in ghee, and soak them briefly in cardamom syrup.

Authentic touch

Fry at a moderate heat so the center cooks before the fringe blacks. Dip into warm syrup, not cold syrup, or the crust sheds the sugar.

How it is used

Serve warm at a festival meal, a few to a plate. They are a dessert, rich enough that two each is plenty.

Field notes

  • Malpua is festival food, especially Holi, in North India and Bengal.
  • The cake is fried and then syruped. It is a fritter, not a dry griddle dosa.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup whole wheat flour or all-purpose flour
  • 1 cup milk
  • 1 teaspoon fennel seeds
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • A pinch of salt
  • 3/4 cup sugar for the syrup
  • 1/2 cup water
  • 2 cardamom pods
  • Ghee for frying

Steps

  1. Whisk flour, milk, fennel, the tablespoon of sugar, and salt into a smooth batter about as thick as cream. Rest 10 minutes.
  2. Simmer 3/4 cup sugar, water, and cardamom until the syrup is sticky and coats a spoon. Keep it warm.
  3. Heat a finger of ghee in a wide pan over medium heat.
  4. Pour a small ladle of batter. Fry until the underside is deep gold and the edge looks lacy, then flip and fry the other side.
  5. Dip each malpua in the warm syrup for a few seconds and lift it out. Fry the rest, adding ghee as needed. Serve warm.

Fry where you can see the pan. Do not leave hot ghee unattended.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Khoya mashed into a Bengali-style batter
  • A pinch of black pepper with the fennel

Pairings

  • A bowl of chilled rabri, which is more dairy
  • Plain tea
  • A handful of pomegranate seeds
