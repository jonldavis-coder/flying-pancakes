Besan cheela

You come in by Train. India · South Asia.

Pronounced BAY-sun CHEE-lah

A savory gram-flour pancake with onion, ajwain, and cumin, soft in the middle and spotted brown.

15 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 3 · 6 cheelas

History

Besan cheela is a North Indian gram-flour pancake, mixed and cooked at once. Besan is chickpea flour. Delhi breakfasts and Rajasthani kitchens both keep it as a fast meal, not as a fermented dosa. There is no rice and no urad soak. Onion, ajwain, and a green chile go into the bowl. A Gujarati pudla is a related besan cake with its own page and its own breakfast habits.

Cultural background

Cheela, also spelled chilla, is weeknight cooking: flour, water, spices, a pan. It does not ask for a grindstone or an overnight bowl. That speed is why it sits in a different column from dosa, even though both are round and griddled.

Why it tastes this way

Chickpea flour tastes nutty and slightly sweet once it cooks. Ajwain adds a thyme-like sharpness that North Indian batters use to keep besan from feeling heavy. Cumin and onion make it savory. Because nothing ferments, there is no sour smell. The mild chile is a lift, not a contest.

How it is made

Whisk besan with water, onion, and spices, rest it briefly, and cook small pancakes in a filmed pan.

Authentic touch

Rest the batter 10 minutes so the besan hydrates, then cook on medium. High heat scorches the outside and leaves raw flour inside.

How it is used

Eat hot with mint-cilantro chutney or ketchup. It is a breakfast or a tiffin, not a dessert.

Field notes

  • Besan is chickpea flour. This cheela is unfermented.
  • With only besan and no wheat, the written recipe is gluten-free.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup besan (chickpea flour), with no wheat added
  • 3/4 cup water
  • 1 small onion, finely chopped
  • 1 green chile, minced
  • 1/2 teaspoon cumin seeds
  • 1/4 teaspoon ajwain seeds
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 2 tablespoons chopped cilantro
  • Neutral oil for the pan

Steps

  1. Whisk besan, salt, and water into a lump-free batter about as thick as heavy cream.
  2. Stir in onion, chile, cumin, ajwain, and cilantro. Rest 10 minutes.
  3. Heat a pan and oil it lightly. Pour a small ladle and spread it into a round.
  4. Cook until the top looks set and the underside is brown in spots. Flip and cook the other side.
  5. Repeat, stirring the bowl so the onion stays mixed in.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Grated carrot in the batter
  • A pinch of turmeric if you want color without making the Gujarati pudla

Pairings

  • Mint and cilantro chutney
  • Sliced onion
  • Plain yogurt, a dairy side
