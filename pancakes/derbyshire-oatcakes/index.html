Derbyshire oatcakes

You come in by Train. United Kingdom · Western Europe.

Pronounced DAR-bee-sher OAT-cakes

A yeasted oatmeal pancake from Derbyshire, a little thicker than the Staffordshire shop cake.

1 hr 20 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 10 oatcakes

History

Derbyshire oatcakes are the Peak District's yeasted oatmeal pancakes. They share a method with Staffordshire oatcakes and they are not the same batter. This page uses equal parts oatmeal and wheat flour and an egg, and it cooks a smaller, thicker cake. Bakers in the two counties do not treat the recipes as interchangeable. Neither cake is a Scottish oatcake, which is unleavened and dry.

Cultural background

They are breakfast food in Derbyshire, eaten with butter, cheese, or bacon. The batter is mixed ahead so the yeast can work. A cold kitchen will take longer than an hour.

Why it tastes this way

The extra wheat and the egg make a cake that holds together when it is thicker. Oats keep it soft. Yeast keeps the sweetness in check. You taste grain first, then the salty filling.

How it is made

Oatmeal, wheat flour, yeast, egg, and warm milk ferment into a batter. Spoonfuls cook on a greased griddle until set.

Authentic touch

Do not roll these as thin as a Potteries oatcake. If the batter will not drop from a spoon in a thick ribbon, add a splash of milk.

How it is used

Buttered, or filled with cheese. They are a morning savory cake, not a dessert stack.

Field notes

  • Derbyshire and Staffordshire both keep yeasted oat pancakes, with different local batters.
  • This batter includes an egg. The Staffordshire page does not.
  • They are soft griddle cakes, not the crisp Scottish biscuit.

Ingredients

  • 150 g fine oatmeal
  • 150 g plain wheat flour
  • 1 tsp instant yeast
  • 1 tsp sugar
  • 1 large egg
  • 400 ml warm milk
  • 1/2 tsp fine salt
  • Butter, for the griddle

Steps

  1. Mix the oatmeal, flour, yeast, sugar, egg, milk, and salt. Cover until the batter is bubbly, about 1 hour.
  2. Stir it down. It should drop from a spoon in a ribbon. Add a spoon of milk if it is stiff.
  3. Grease a medium-hot griddle. Cook small rounds until the top sets, then turn and brown the second side.
  4. Serve warm with butter or cheese. They reheat in a dry pan.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • The oat-heavier Staffordshire oatcake, made to fold
  • A spoon of honey on a plain one

Pairings

  • Butter
  • Cheese
  • Bacon, for a meat plate
