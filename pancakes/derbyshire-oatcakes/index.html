A soft oat pancake, a neighbor to the Staffordshire cake.

1 hr 10 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · about 6 oatcakes

History

Derbyshire oatcakes are the neighboring county’s soft oat pancake, sold and cooked at home in a tradition that overlaps Staffordshire’s. They are large, yeasted, and savory. Treating them as a separate named cake respects the county line cooks actually use, without inventing a dramatic difference that is not there. The batter is oat-forward and flexible.

Cultural background

They are breakfast wrappers for cheese or bacon, much like Staffordshire oatcakes. Some Derbyshire versions are a little thicker.

Why it tastes this way

The same industrial Midlands pantry, oats plus a little wheat and yeast, produces a savory pancake because oats were cheaper than a white-flour diet and yeast made a bread you could fold. Small differences in thickness and sweetness are house differences. The flavor is oat and salt.

How it is made

An oat and wheat yeast batter is cooked as soft pancakes on a griddle.

Authentic touch

If you want them a little thicker than Staffordshire cakes, use a bit less water. Do not add sugar. Sweet oatcakes are a different British food.

How it is used

Cheese or eggs. Roll or fold them.

Field notes

  • Derbyshire and Staffordshire oatcakes are close relatives with local names.
  • Neither one is a crisp Scottish oatcake.

Ingredients

  • 1 1/4 cups oat flour
  • 1/4 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1 teaspoon instant yeast
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 1/4 cups warm water
  • 1 egg
  • Oil for the griddle

Steps

  1. Mix all the batter ingredients and rest 1 hour.
  2. Cook thin rounds on a medium oiled griddle.
  3. Flip when the top sets.
  4. Stack under a towel.
  5. Fill while warm.
  6. They keep a day in the fridge and reheat in a dry pan.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • The Staffordshire page is the twin, with the shop tradition of Stoke called out there.
  • A pinch of sugar appears in some home recipes. This one stays savory.
