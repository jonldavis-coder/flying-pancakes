Pronounced GAHM-jah-juhn

A Korean potato pancake, crisp, with a soy dip.

20 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · about 8 cakes

History

Gamjajeon is a Korean potato pancake, gamja meaning potato. Grated potato is mixed with a little flour and egg and fried, then dipped in soy sauce. It is a jeon, in the same broad family as pajeon, and a cousin of latkes with a Korean seasoning and sauce.

Cultural background

It is a side or a snack. The potato should be squeezed. The sauce is salty and vinegary.

Why it tastes this way

Potato starch crisps, and a little wheat flour helps the cake hold in a jeon style. Soy and vinegar are the Korean finish, not applesauce or sour cream, which is the clearest difference from the European potato cakes in this journal. Onion, if you add it, is the same savory note those cakes use. The pan and the dip are what make it gamjajeon.

How it is made

Grated potato, egg, and a little flour are fried and served with a soy dip.

Authentic touch

Squeeze the potato and fry thin. Salt the dip, not the cake too heavily, or it will be briny twice.

How it is used

Hot, with the dipping sauce.

Field notes

  • Gamja means potato. Jeon is the pancake category.
  • The soy dip, not sour cream, is the Korean plate.

Ingredients

  • 2 medium potatoes
  • 1 egg
  • 2 tablespoons flour
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • Oil
  • 2 tablespoons soy sauce
  • 1 tablespoon vinegar

Steps

  1. Grate and squeeze the potatoes. Mix with egg, flour, and salt.
  2. Fry thin cakes until crisp.
  3. Mix soy and vinegar for the dip.
  4. Serve immediately.
  5. A few sesame seeds on the dip are optional and add sesame.
  6. Do not cover the cakes.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A few slices of chile on top are a home addition.
  • Latkes are the Ashkenazi cousin with a different sauce and holiday.

Pairings

  • soy dipping sauce
