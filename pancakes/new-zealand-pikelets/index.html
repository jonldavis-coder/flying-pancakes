New Zealand Pikelets

Small drop scones, a close cousin of the Australian pikelet, served with butter and golden syrup.

10 min prep · 12 min cook · Serves 4 · about 12 pikelets

History

New Zealand pikelets are the same British drop-scone family as Australian pikelets, a standard in home baking and school cooking. Golden syrup and butter are the usual plate. They are not Māori fry bread. Treating them as a separate page keeps the shared recipe from erasing either country’s afternoon-tea habit.

Cultural background

They are small, thick, and best warm. Raspberry jam is a common second topping. The batter matches the Australian one closely, with a spoon more sugar in some New Zealand tins.

Why it tastes this way

The cake tastes of egg, butter, and a little sugar because that is the British tea cake that traveled. Golden syrup is the shared colonial pantry sweet of Australia and New Zealand. A pikelet is not a crepe and not a fry bread. The small size cooks quickly and gives more crust than a large pancake.

How it is made

A thick batter is cooked as small rounds.

Authentic touch

Cook them on medium and serve them in a stack under a cloth so the last one is not cold. Butter goes on while they are warm.

How it is used

With butter and golden syrup or jam.

Field notes

  • New Zealand pikelets belong to the same drop-scone family as the Australian ones.
  • Golden syrup is the usual sweet.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup flour
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 2 tablespoons sugar
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1 egg
  • 1/2 cup milk
  • 1 tablespoon melted butter

Steps

  1. Mix a thick batter.
  2. Cook small rounds on a buttered griddle.
  3. Serve warm with butter and golden syrup or jam.
  4. Keep them covered so the last one stays soft.
  5. They are tea cakes.
  6. Do not thin the batter into crepes.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Australian pikelets are the twin.
  • A large breakfast pancake is a different pour of a thinner batter.

Pairings

  • butter
  • golden syrup
  • jam
