Arriving in Mogadishu

Somali canjeero

You come in by Plane. Somalia · East Africa.

Pronounced can-JEE-roh

Soft, lightly sour wheat cakes full of small holes, mildly sweet at the edge.

20 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 4 · About 10 small cakes

History

Canjeero is the spongy fermented pancake of a Somali breakfast. The same bread is also called laxoox and anjero. Those are names for this cake, not three different recipes on this page. It is cooked in a small pan until the top is pitted with holes, then eaten with ghee, honey, or a light soup. Yemeni lahoh is a related spongy bread with its own page and its own table. This one stays in Mogadishu.

Cultural background

Breakfast can be a stack of canjeero, a spoon of subag, which is ghee, and tea. Some houses prefer honey. Others set the bread next to a thin soup or a bit of stewed meat. The batter is a home ferment, often helped with a pinch of yeast when the kitchen is cool.

Why it tastes this way

An overnight ferment gives a gentle sourness, and a little sugar keeps it from tasting like injera. Wheat makes the crumb softer and less earthy than teff. The holes hold ghee or honey. A soup-soaked piece tastes savory because of the broth, not because the batter was seasoned like a stew.

How it is made

Whisk wheat flour with warm water, a little sugar, and a pinch of yeast. Rest it overnight until bubbly and slightly sour, then salt it. Cook small rounds in a lightly oiled pan on one side until the top is spongy and set. Serve warm. The ghee, honey, and soup stay at the table, not in the batter.

Authentic touch

Cook one side and wait until the holes cover the surface before you lift the cake. A flip makes it bready. Keep the Somali breakfast toppings, ghee, honey, or soup, and do not dress this page as Yemeni lahoh.

How it is used

Canjeero is breakfast. A common plate is the bread with ghee and honey, or the bread torn into a light soup. It is not the liner under a large platter of stews. Sesame oil is another home finish.

Field notes

  • Laxoox and anjero are other names for this Somali breakfast pancake.
  • The cake is fermented and spongy, and it is usually eaten in the morning.
  • Yemeni lahoh is related and has its own page. It is often served with foul or eggs.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups wheat flour (about 250 g)
  • 2 1/2 cups warm water
  • 1 teaspoon sugar
  • 1/2 teaspoon instant yeast
  • 1/2 teaspoon fine salt

Steps

  1. Whisk the flour, warm water, sugar, and yeast into a batter like thin cream. Leave the salt out for now.
  2. Cover the bowl and leave it at room temperature overnight, until it smells faintly sour and the surface is bubbly.
  3. Stir in the salt. The batter should pour. Add a splash of water if it thickened overnight.
  4. Heat a small nonstick pan over medium heat and wipe it with a drop of oil. Pour a thin layer of batter.
  5. Cover and cook on one side until the holes set and the top is no longer wet. Do not flip. Repeat with the rest of the batter and serve warm.

This recipe contains wheat. Ghee and honey at the table add dairy and honey for anyone who uses them.

Refrigerate leftover cakes and reheat them covered so they do not dry. Do not leave cooked cakes in a warm kitchen overnight. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A spoon of yesterday's batter can replace the yeast.
  • Some cooks replace part of the wheat with sorghum. Any wheat left in the bowl means the bread is not gluten-free. This written recipe stays with wheat.
  • A little cardamom in the batter is a family choice, not a requirement.

Pairings

  • Ghee, called subag
  • Honey
  • A light soup or broth
