Buchujeon

You come in by Plane. South Korea · East Asia.

Pronounced BOO-choo-juhn

A garlic-chive pancake, green and savory, with a light wheat crust and no seafood.

15 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · 2 chive pancakes

History

Buchujeon is the garlic-chive pancake. Buchu is the chive, flat and garlicky, not a substitute for the scallion in pajeon. The leaves are cut long, dipped in a thin wheat and egg batter, and fried in loose clusters or one round. It is a side dish on Korean tables and a snack with a soy dip. This recipe has no fish sauce and no shrimp, so it stays vegetarian.

Cultural background

Garlic chives are a spring and summer market bunch. When they are young they are sweet. Older stems are stronger and still correct for this cake. The batter is only a coating. The chive should dominate the pan the way scallion dominates pajeon.

Why it tastes this way

Garlic chive tastes oniony and distinctly garlicky, greener than bulb garlic and wilder than scallion. A thin wheat batter lets that flavor lead and adds a toasted edge. Egg helps the strands hold together. Sesame is absent here so the chive stays clear. A salty, sour dip is the contrast.

How it is made

Coat garlic chives in a thin wheat batter and pan-fry them until the leaves wilt and the batter spots brown.

Authentic touch

Cut the chives long, about the length of a finger, and fry them in a thin coat. A thick dough buries the vegetable that names the dish.

How it is used

Serve as a side with rice and a dip, cut into pieces. It is savory, not a street sweet.

Field notes

  • Buchu is garlic chive. It is a different green from the scallion in pajeon.
  • This recipe stays vegetarian: no fish sauce and no seafood in the batter.

Ingredients

  • 200 grams garlic chives
  • 1/2 cup all-purpose wheat flour
  • 1 egg
  • 1/3 cup cold water
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • Neutral oil
  • Soy sauce and rice vinegar for dipping

Steps

  1. Wash the chives and cut them into finger lengths.
  2. Whisk flour, egg, water, and salt into a thin batter. Fold in the chives.
  3. Heat a film of oil over medium-high heat. Spread half the mixture into a loose round.
  4. Cook until the underside browns and the chives soften. Flip carefully and cook through.
  5. Repeat. Serve with a soy and vinegar dip.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A few sliced red chiles for color and mild heat
  • Carrot matchsticks in the cluster

Pairings

  • Soy and vinegar dip
  • Steamed rice
  • A bowl of doenjang-jjigae on the same table
