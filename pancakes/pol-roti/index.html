Pol roti

You come in by Boat. Sri Lanka · South Asia.

Pronounced pole ROH-tee

A griddled coconut flatbread, lightly sweet from the coconut, with onion and green chile in the dough.

20 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 6 rotis

History

Pol roti is a Sri Lankan coconut roti, griddled rather than baked in a tandoor. Pol means coconut. Scraped coconut is mixed into wheat flour with salt, onion, and often green chile, then patted and cooked on a flat pan. It is everyday breakfast and accompaniment food, especially along the south coast and in home kitchens. It is a flatbread, not a hopper and not a sweet tea-time pancake.

Cultural background

Galle kitchens serve it with lunumiris, with seeni sambol, or beside a curry. The bread itself can be mild. This recipe puts a little green chile in the dough so the heat matches the tag. Coconut here is fresh and unsweetened.

Why it tastes this way

Fresh coconut melts into the dough and tastes sweet and rich against wheat. Onion softens on the griddle. Green chile flickers rather than burns if it is chopped fine. Because the bread is cooked dry with only a film of oil, you taste toasted coconut and flour instead of a fried crust.

How it is made

Mix wheat flour with grated coconut, onion, and green chile, pat thick rounds, and griddle both sides.

Authentic touch

Pat the roti thicker than a chapati. A thin one dries out before the coconut toasts. Medium heat and a patient few minutes are the method.

How it is used

Serve warm, broken by hand, with an onion sambol. It also sits beside a vegetable curry as an accompaniment.

Field notes

  • Pol means coconut in Sinhala. The roti is built around fresh coconut.
  • It is cooked on a griddle, in rounds you pat by hand.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups whole wheat flour or all-purpose wheat flour
  • 1 cup freshly grated coconut
  • 1 small onion, finely chopped
  • 1 green chile, minced
  • 3/4 teaspoon salt
  • About 1/2 cup water
  • Neutral oil for the griddle

Steps

  1. Mix flour, coconut, onion, chile, and salt. Add water gradually until a soft dough forms. Rest 15 minutes.
  2. Divide into six balls. Pat each into a thick round on a floured sheet.
  3. Heat a griddle and film it with oil. Cook a roti on medium until the underside is spotted brown.
  4. Flip and cook until the center is no longer doughy and the coconut smells toasted.
  5. Repeat. Keep the stack covered with a cloth.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A few curry leaves chopped into the dough
  • Shredded carrot for a lunchbox roti

Pairings

  • Onion sambol
  • Seeni sambol
  • A mild dhal curry
