Tattie scones

You come in by Train. United Kingdom · Western Europe.

Pronounced TAT-ee skons

Thin potato triangles from a Scottish girdle, eaten at breakfast.

15 min prep · 12 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 triangles

History

Tattie scones are Scottish potato scones: mashed potato, a little flour and fat, rolled thin, cut into triangles, and cooked on a girdle. Tattie means potato. They are part of a fried breakfast or a weekend plate with eggs. They are not Lancashire's thicker fried rounds, and they are not boxty. Flour in the dough means they are not gluten-free.

Cultural background

Cold mash from the night before is the usual start. The scones are cooked in a dry or lightly greased pan so the outside toasts without becoming a fritter.

Why it tastes this way

Potato makes them mild and soft. A thin roll means the heat reaches the center and the surface dries. Salt and pepper matter because there is little else. Browning at the edge is the flavor. Butter on the hot scone melts into the face that was against the girdle.

How it is made

Mashed potato is kneaded with butter and flour, rolled into a thin round, quartered, and cooked on a hot girdle until spotted.

Authentic touch

Cut the round into quarters. A farl here is a practical cut, not the Ulster soda farl. Keep them thin enough that you can see the girdle color through the dough at the edge.

How it is used

At breakfast, with eggs, bacon, or butter. They are a savory cake.

Field notes

  • Tattie is potato. The scones are potato, not a sweet oven scone.
  • They are rolled thin and cut in triangles or quarters.
  • They belong on a Scottish breakfast plate.

Ingredients

  • 400 g cold mashed potato
  • 30 g unsalted butter, plus more for serving
  • 80 g plain wheat flour, plus more for rolling
  • 1/2 tsp fine salt
  • 1/4 tsp black pepper

Steps

  1. Mash the potato with the butter, salt, and pepper until smooth. Work in the flour to a soft dough.
  2. Roll into two thin rounds on a floured board and cut each into quarters.
  3. Cook on a hot dry girdle until brown spots appear, then turn and cook the other side.
  4. Serve warm with butter, eggs, or bacon.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A spoon of chopped chives
  • Lancashire potato cakes, thicker and pan-fried in more fat

Pairings

  • Eggs
  • Bacon or square sausage, for a meat breakfast
  • Butter
