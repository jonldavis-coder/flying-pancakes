Mild potato flatbread, girdled in spots.

History

Tattie scones are Scottish potato farls: mashed potato and flour, rolled, cut, and cooked on a girdle. They are a breakfast bread in a Scottish fry and a close cousin of Irish potato farls. “Tattie” is potato. They became common once potatoes were a Scottish staple.

Cultural background

They are best hot, with butter. In a fry they sit beside eggs and sausage. The scone itself need not include meat or egg.

Why it tastes this way

Potato makes the bread moist and mildly sweet. Flour makes it hold. A girdle dries the surface in brown spots without an oven. Scotland’s adoption of the potato, as in Ireland, turned a staple into a morning bread rather than only a boiled side.

How it is made

Plain mashed potato and flour are rolled, quartered, and cooked on a griddle.

Authentic touch

Mash the potatoes without milk or butter if you want them vegan, and keep them dry. Roll just thick enough that they do not tear, and cook them through so the flour does not taste raw.

How it is used

Butter, or as part of a breakfast fry.

Field notes

  • Tattie means potato.
  • They are a flatbread, not a fluffy oven scone, despite the word scone.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups plain warm mashed potato
  • 1/2 cup all-purpose flour, plus more for rolling
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt

Steps

  1. Mix mash, flour, and salt.
  2. Roll into a round and cut into quarters.
  3. Cook on a medium dry girdle until spotted on both sides.
  4. Eat hot with butter.
  5. Leftovers can be fried in a little fat the next morning.
  6. Wet mash makes them stick. Dry the potatoes in the pan first.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A little butter in the dough is richer and adds dairy.
  • Irish potato farls are the sister bread.

Pairings

  • butter
  • eggs
