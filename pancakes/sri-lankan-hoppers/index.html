Sri Lankan hoppers

You come in by Boat. Sri Lanka · South Asia.

Pronounced HOP-erz

A crisp-edged rice bowl with a soft coconut center and a light ferment tang.

35 min prep · 30 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 hoppers

History

Hoppers are Sri Lanka's bowl-shaped rice cakes, kin to Kerala appam and still their own daily bread. Fermented rice and coconut milk are poured into a small curved pan, swirled, and covered. Coconut toddy is a traditional ferment. Yeast is the home substitute in this recipe. A hopper plate in Colombo might come with lunumiris or seeni sambol. Egg hoppers, with an egg broken into the center, are a separate page.

Cultural background

Hopper shops pour from a bowl of batter that has worked overnight. The edge laces where it is thin, and the middle stays pale and spongy. String hoppers, which are pressed rice noodles, are a different food and not this pan.

Why it tastes this way

Coconut milk sweetens the rice and keeps the center custardy. Fermentation adds a clean sour note that toddy makes fruitier and yeast makes milder. The hot curved wall fries the thin film into a cracker while the covered center steams. Salt is spare. The sambol beside the hopper carries chile and, often, dried fish.

How it is made

Ferment rice ground with coconut milk, swirl it in a hot hopper pan, and cover until the bowl sets.

Authentic touch

Swirl once and cover. If you leave the batter pooled without climbing the sides, you have a thick pancake, not a hopper.

How it is used

Serve immediately with onion sambol. Some sambols include dried Maldive fish, which would add a fish allergen. The hopper batter here does not.

Field notes

  • Sri Lankan hoppers and Kerala appam share a pan shape and keep different names and tables.
  • An egg cracked into the center is an egg hopper, written on its own page.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup raw rice
  • 1/4 cup cooked rice
  • 3/4 cup coconut milk
  • 1/4 teaspoon dry yeast
  • 1 teaspoon sugar
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • Water as needed
  • Neutral oil for the pan

Steps

  1. Soak the raw rice for 3 hours, drain, and grind it with the cooked rice and a little water until smooth.
  2. Stir in coconut milk, yeast, sugar, and salt. Aim for a batter that pours in a ribbon.
  3. Ferment covered until bubbly, often 6 to 12 hours. Exclude that rest from active prep.
  4. Heat a hopper pan, grease it, pour a ladle, and swirl so the sides film and the center stays thick.
  5. Cover and cook on medium-low until the rim is crisp and the center is spongy and set. Repeat.

Discard batter that smells rotten or grows mold.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Toddy in place of yeast where it is sold fresh and safe
  • A sweeter center with an extra spoon of coconut milk

Pairings

  • Onion sambol without dried fish
  • Seeni sambol
  • A mild coconut gravy
