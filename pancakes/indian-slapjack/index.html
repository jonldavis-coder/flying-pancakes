Arriving in Hartford

Indian slapjack

You come in by Train. United States · North America.

Soft corn, milk, and egg, with only a little wheat to hold the cake.

10 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · 10 slapjacks

History

Amelia Simmons's American Cookery, printed in Hartford in 1796, gives a formula for Indian slapjack made with Indian meal, milk, eggs, a little wheat flour, and salt. In that book, Indian meal means cornmeal. The cake is an early printed American griddle bread. This page does not assign the recipe to a particular Native nation.

Cultural background

American Cookery is often cited as the first cookbook written by an American and printed in the United States. The proportions here are scaled down from the quart of milk and pint of Indian meal in that formula.

Why it tastes this way

Cornmeal is the main flavor, and milk and egg make it custardy instead of dry. The small amount of wheat flour helps the thin batter hold. Because there is little sugar, the toasted corn is easy to taste.

How it is made

A thin cornmeal batter cooked on a griddle, following the 1796 formula in a smaller batch.

Authentic touch

Keep the wheat flour to a few tablespoons, as Simmons's 'spoons of flour' suggests. A modern pancake mix with a handful of cornmeal is not this cake.

How it is used

Hot off the griddle, with butter or molasses if you want them richer than the printed batter.

Field notes

  • In Amelia Simmons's 1796 American Cookery, Indian meal means cornmeal.
  • The printed slapjack is milk, cornmeal, eggs, a little wheat flour, and salt.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup cornmeal
  • 2 cups whole milk
  • 2 large eggs
  • 2 tablespoons all-purpose flour
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 tablespoon unsalted butter, for the griddle

Steps

  1. Whisk the cornmeal, milk, eggs, flour, and salt into a thin batter.
  2. Rest 5 minutes so the meal swells slightly.
  3. Heat a griddle over medium heat and rub it with butter.
  4. Pour about 1/4 cup batter and spread it thin.
  5. Cook until the surface looks dry and the underside is spotted brown, then flip and cook briefly.
  6. Serve hot. The cakes are tender and best eaten at once.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Simmons allows frying in a dry pan or a pan rubbed with suet, lard, or butter. This batch uses butter.
  • More flour makes a sturdier modern pancake and leaves the 1796 balance behind.

Pairings

  • Butter
  • Molasses
  • Maple syrup
