Gingerbread pancakes

You come in by Train. United States · North America.

Dark molasses, ginger, cinnamon, and clove in a soft wheat cake.

15 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 12 pancakes

History

Gingerbread pancakes are a seasonal American breakfast that borrows molasses, ginger, and cinnamon from gingerbread. They are a twentieth-century table idea, a spiced wheat cake for cold mornings. Philadelphia is the map pin because molasses moved through that port, not because a single colonial manuscript holds this pancake. The crumb is darker and sharper than a plain buttermilk pancake.

Cultural background

Molasses and ground ginger are a trade sweetener and a pantry spice. The warmth is from ginger, cinnamon, and clove, not from chili.

Why it tastes this way

Molasses carries bitterness and a dark sweetness, and ginger makes the crumb feel warm. Clove and cinnamon are strong, so the amounts stay small. Buttermilk and soda keep the cake from turning into a dense gingerbread loaf. The edge browns fast because of the sugar in the molasses.

How it is made

Griddled molasses and ginger wheat batter.

Authentic touch

Cook them a step cooler than plain pancakes so the molasses does not scorch before the center sets. Taste the batter: the spice should be clear and not harsh.

How it is used

Hot, with butter. A little whipped cream turns them into dessert.

Field notes

  • These are a seasonal spiced breakfast, not a pancake copied from one colonial manuscript.
  • Molasses browns quickly, so the griddle should be a little cooler than for a plain cake.

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 1/2 teaspoons ground ginger
  • 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 1/4 teaspoon ground cloves
  • 1 1/4 cups buttermilk
  • 1/4 cup molasses
  • 1 large egg
  • 2 tablespoons unsalted butter, melted, plus more for the griddle

Steps

  1. Whisk the flour, baking powder, baking soda, salt, ginger, cinnamon, and cloves.
  2. Whisk the buttermilk, molasses, egg, and melted butter. Stir into the flour until just combined.
  3. Heat a griddle over medium-low heat and butter it.
  4. Pour 1/4 cup cakes. They will darken quickly.
  5. Flip when the underside is deep brown and the top looks set. Cook until the center springs back.
  6. Serve with butter.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A finer grating of fresh ginger can replace half the ground ginger.
  • Leave out the clove if you want a milder cake.

Pairings

  • Butter
  • Whipped cream
  • Applesauce
