Arriving in Apia

Samoan panikeke

You come in by Boat. Samoa · Oceania.

Pronounced pah-nee-KEH-keh

Round fried cakes, lightly sweet, with mashed banana in a soft wheat crumb.

20 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · About 12 fried rounds

History

Panikeke are Samoan round fried cakes, and the name comes from the English word pancake. They are often made with mashed banana, baking powder, and flour, then fried into rounds for breakfast, for church gatherings, and for the shop counter. The borrowed name already places them in the period when English words and wheat flour were part of island cooking. They are not a pre-contact dish. This recipe fries them, which is how they are cooked, and keeps the banana in the dough.

Cultural background

In Apia and in villages, panikeke are a batch food: one bowl, one pan of oil, a plate that empties fast. Ripe banana is the fruit most often mashed in. They are a snack more than a ceremony. The shape is a round fritter, not a thin crepe.

Why it tastes this way

Banana sweetens the dough and keeps it moist in the middle while the oil browns the outside. Wheat and baking powder make a soft, bready crumb rather than a fruit leather. Sugar is light, so the banana remains the sweetener you taste. Salt at the edge of the dough keeps it from eating flat.

How it is made

Mash a ripe banana and stir it with flour, baking powder, sugar, salt, and a little water into a soft dough. Shape rounds and fry them in oil until golden and cooked through. Drain and eat warm.

Authentic touch

Fry rounds, and keep the banana in the dough if you are cooking this version. The name comes from pancake, but the method is a fritter. A thin griddled crepe is not panikeke.

How it is used

Panikeke are a snack and a breakfast cake, at home and at gatherings. They are eaten plain or with a little butter. Butter adds dairy and is not in this dough. They are not a savory stew bread.

Field notes

  • The name panikeke comes from the English word pancake.
  • The cakes are round and fried, often with banana.
  • This recipe is a fritter, not a thin batter cooked on a dry griddle.

Ingredients

  • 1 ripe banana, mashed
  • 1 cup wheat flour (about 125 g)
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 2 tablespoons sugar
  • 1/4 teaspoon fine salt
  • 1/4 cup water
  • Oil for frying

Steps

  1. Mash the banana until smooth. Stir in the flour, baking powder, sugar, salt, and water to make a soft dough you can shape.
  2. Rest the dough for 10 minutes.
  3. Shape small rounds with floured hands.
  4. Fry the rounds in hot oil, turning once, until they are golden and the center is cooked. Panikeke are fried cakes, and the name comes from pancake.
  5. Drain and serve warm.

The dough contains wheat. Frying oil is hot. There is no egg and no dairy in the dough. Butter on the side would add dairy.

Eat panikeke the day they are fried. Refrigerate leftovers and reheat them in a hot oven. The banana crumb does not stay crisp. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Without banana, the dough is a plainer fried round and less like the usual panikeke.
  • A spoon of mashed pumpkin is another home mash and a different flavor.
  • Egg or milk in the dough changes the allergens. This batch has neither.

Pairings

  • Tea or cocoa
  • A ripe banana on the side
  • Butter, which adds dairy
