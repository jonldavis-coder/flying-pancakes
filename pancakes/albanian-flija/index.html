Many paper-thin layers of batter and dairy, baked into a pie.

History

Flija, also fli, is a layered Albanian and Kosovar pie: batter brushed in a round pan, baked, brushed again, for many layers, traditionally under a saç, a metal dome covered with embers. It is a patient dish for gatherings. Kosovo uses the code XK in some lists and is not on every map. This recipe bakes the layers in a home oven, which is an adaptation of the saç, stated openly. The country field stays Albania, where the dish is also central.

Cultural background

Cream, yogurt, or kaymak between layers is what makes it custardy. It is cut in diamonds. It takes time. That is the dish.

Why it tastes this way

Each thin layer dries just enough to stay separate, so the pie tastes of baked cream and toasted batter rather than of one thick pancake. The saç and embers browned the top slowly. An oven is hotter and less smoky, so the home version watches the color. Dairy fat is the luxury. The layering is the technique that makes flija flija.

How it is made

A thin batter and a cream mixture are brushed in alternating layers in a buttered pan and baked until the top is brown.

Authentic touch

Brush a thin layer, bake until it looks set, then repeat. A thick pour turns it into a custard with no layers. Plan on at least eight layers.

How it is used

Warm, in diamonds, as a shared main or a rich side.

Field notes

  • Flija is defined by its layers. One thick pancake is not flija.
  • The oven method is an adaptation of cooking under a saç.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups flour
  • 2 cups water
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1 cup cream
  • 1 cup plain yogurt
  • 4 tablespoons butter, melted

Steps

  1. Whisk flour, water, and salt into a thin batter.
  2. Stir cream and yogurt together.
  3. Heat the oven to 400°F. Butter a round pan.
  4. Brush a thin layer of batter and bake about 8 minutes, until set.
  5. Brush cream mixture, then batter, baking between layers, until you have many layers and a browned top.
  6. Rest 10 minutes and cut into diamonds.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A true saç and coals are the older heat source. This is the oven path.
  • Petulla are the quick fried cousins, not a shortcut to flija.
