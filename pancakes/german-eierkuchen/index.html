Pronounced EYE-er-koo-khen

An eggier German pancake, served with sugar or applesauce.

10 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · about 8 pancakes

History

Eierkuchen are German egg pancakes. In much of Germany a Pfannkuchen is also a pancake, but in Berlin Pfannkuchen means a jam donut. The naming trap is real, so this page uses Eierkuchen for the pancake. They are thicker than a French crepe and are eaten with sugar, cinnamon, or applesauce.

Cultural background

A home supper can be pancakes, applesauce, and nothing else. That is a recognized simple meal, not an incomplete plate.

Why it tastes this way

More egg than a lean crepe makes them taste custardy and browned. Applesauce is the fruit of a German pantry and cuts the egg. Sugar and cinnamon are the other finish. The Berlin naming oddity is why a traveler can order a pancake and receive a donut. The egg in the name is the flavor clue.

How it is made

An egg-rich batter is cooked as medium pancakes and served with applesauce or sugar.

Authentic touch

Cook them in a little butter and serve them flat or folded, not rolled tight. They are sturdier than flensjes and will crack if you treat them like lace.

How it is used

Applesauce, or sugar and cinnamon.

Field notes

  • In Berlin, Pfannkuchen usually means a donut. Eierkuchen is the safer word for the pancake.
  • Applesauce is a standard partner.

Ingredients

  • 3 large eggs
  • 3/4 cup flour
  • 3/4 cup milk
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • Butter for the pan

Steps

  1. Whisk a smooth batter.
  2. Cook medium pancakes in butter until browned on both sides.
  3. Serve with applesauce or cinnamon sugar.
  4. They should be set in the center.
  5. Do not thin them to crepe consistency.
  6. Eat them fresh.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Berliner Pfannkuchen are donuts. Do not use that recipe for this cake.
  • Kartoffelpuffer are the potato pancakes.

Pairings

  • applesauce
  • cinnamon sugar
