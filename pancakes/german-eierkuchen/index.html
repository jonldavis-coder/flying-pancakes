Arriving in Berlin

German Eierkuchen

You come in by Train. Germany · Western Europe.

Pronounced EYE-er-koo-khen

Thin German egg pancakes. In Berlin, do not call them Pfannkuchen.

15 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 pancakes

History

Eierkuchen means egg cake, and in much of Germany it is the clear word for a pancake. The naming trap is real: in Berlin and parts of eastern Germany, Pfannkuchen means a jam-filled doughnut. In much of the rest of the country, Pfannkuchen means this pancake, and the doughnut is a Berliner. Order carefully. The cake itself is a thin egg batter, served with sugar, applesauce, or a savory filling. This page uses sugar and lemon.

Cultural background

They are home food, cooked one after another and eaten as they come out of the pan. The Berlin doughnut that shares a confusing name is fried yeast dough, not this batter.

Why it tastes this way

Eggs make the sheet richer than a flour-and-water flatbread. Milk softens it. Butter browns the spots. Lemon and sugar brighten the fat, in the same family as an English Shrove pancake, with a German name and a German naming problem. The crumb is thin and flexible.

How it is made

Flour, eggs, and milk are whisked into a thin batter and cooked in butter, then finished with sugar and lemon.

Authentic touch

If you are in Berlin, ask for Eierkuchen when you want this pancake. Pfannkuchen may bring you a doughnut. Cook the batter thin.

How it is used

Folded, with sugar and lemon, as dessert or a weekend breakfast. Applesauce is another honest side.

Field notes

  • In Berlin, Pfannkuchen often means a jam doughnut. Eierkuchen means the pancake.
  • In much of western and southern Germany, Pfannkuchen means the pancake.
  • The doughnut and the pancake are different doughs that share a confusing name.

Ingredients

  • 125 g plain wheat flour
  • 2 large eggs
  • 300 ml whole milk
  • 1 pinch fine salt
  • 1 tbsp sugar, plus more for serving
  • 30 g unsalted butter, for the pan
  • 1 lemon, cut into wedges

Steps

  1. Whisk the flour, eggs, milk, salt, and sugar into a thin batter. Rest 10 minutes.
  2. Cook thin pancakes in a buttered pan until spotted on both sides.
  3. Sprinkle with sugar and add lemon.
  4. Fold and serve. If you are shopping in Berlin, remember the name trap.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A savory filling of mushrooms or cheese
  • Apple slices cooked on the batter

Pairings

  • Sugar and lemon
  • Applesauce
  • Cinnamon sugar
