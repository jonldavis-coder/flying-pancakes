Polish Potato Placki

Pronounced PLATS-kee

Crisp potato pancakes, served with sour cream or sugar.

20 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · about 10 cakes

History

Placki ziemniaczane are Polish potato pancakes. Some families eat them with sour cream or goulash. Others dust them with sugar, a sweet-savory habit that surprises people who only know latkes. Both are real. The cake is shredded potato, onion, and egg.

Cultural background

They are a supper, sometimes a meatless one. This recipe is the cake. Goulash would add meat.

Why it tastes this way

Potato and onion, fried, taste sweet enough that sugar on top is not as strange as it sounds, and savory enough for sour cream. Poland’s potato kitchen produced several cakes. The sugar habit is a home custom worth reporting without forcing every plate to do it. Oil, not butter, is the usual fry.

How it is made

Grated potato and onion are fried as thin cakes.

Authentic touch

Squeeze dry, fry crisp, and decide before you cook whether the meal is sour cream or sugar. Mixing both on one cake is a personal matter.

How it is used

Sour cream, or sugar, or beside a stew.

Field notes

  • Sugar on potato pancakes is a real Polish home habit, and sour cream is the other.
  • The cake is the same either way.

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 pounds potatoes
  • 1 small onion
  • 1 large egg
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • Oil for frying

Steps

  1. Grate and squeeze the potato and onion.
  2. Mix with egg and salt.
  3. Fry thin cakes in hot oil until crisp.
  4. Drain.
  5. Serve with sour cream or a pinch of sugar.
  6. Salt if you are not sugaring them.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A spoon of flour helps them hold and adds gluten.
  • Deruny and draniki are the regional cousins.

Pairings

  • sour cream
  • sugar
