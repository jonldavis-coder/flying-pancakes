Arriving in Portsmouth

New England cornmeal cakes

You come in by Train. United States · North America.

Coarse corn and molasses against a tangy buttermilk crumb.

20 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 12 cakes

History

New England cornmeal cakes mix cornmeal with wheat flour and molasses, a Yankee breakfast that uses both the region's cornmeal habit and molasses from Atlantic trade. They are thicker and sweeter than a Rhode Island johnnycake. Portsmouth, New Hampshire, is the map pin for a coastal New England kitchen. The wheat flour means these cakes are not gluten-free.

Cultural background

Molasses was a common sweetener in New England because it moved through northern ports. The cornmeal gives the crumb a sandy texture that all-wheat pancakes do not have.

Why it tastes this way

Cornmeal toasts faster than wheat and tastes fuller. Molasses adds bitterness and iron-dark sweetness. Buttermilk and soda keep the cake from tasting heavy even with the coarser meal.

How it is made

Griddled cornmeal and wheat batter sweetened with molasses.

Authentic touch

Let the cornmeal sit in the buttermilk for 10 minutes before you add the egg and flour, so the meal swells. Skip that soak and the cakes taste gritty.

How it is used

Hot, with butter and maple syrup or more molasses.

Field notes

  • Molasses in New England cooking follows Atlantic trade into northern ports.
  • Soaking the cornmeal briefly softens the grit before the cakes hit the griddle.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup yellow cornmeal
  • 1 1/2 cups buttermilk
  • 2 tablespoons molasses
  • 1 cup all-purpose flour
  • 2 teaspoons baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 large egg
  • 2 tablespoons unsalted butter, melted, plus more for the griddle

Steps

  1. Stir the cornmeal, buttermilk, and molasses. Rest 10 minutes.
  2. Whisk the flour, baking powder, baking soda, and salt in another bowl.
  3. Stir the egg and melted butter into the cornmeal, then add the flour mixture and stir until just combined.
  4. Butter a griddle over medium heat. Pour 1/4 cup cakes.
  5. Flip when the tops are set and the undersides are brown. Cook until the center is done.
  6. Serve with butter and maple syrup or molasses.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • All-cornmeal johnnycakes are the Rhode Island page, and they are a different texture.
  • A spoon less molasses makes them milder.

Pairings

  • Butter
  • Maple syrup
  • Molasses
