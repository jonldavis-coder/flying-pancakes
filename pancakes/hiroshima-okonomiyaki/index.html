Arriving in Hiroshima

Hiroshima okonomiyaki

You come in by Plane. Japan · East Asia.

Pronounced hee-ROH-shee-mah oh-koh-noh-mee-YAH-kee

A layered savory crepe: thin batter, a mound of cabbage, noodles, pork, and egg, finished with sauce.

25 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 2 · 2 layered cakes

History

Hiroshima okonomiyaki is built in layers on the griddle. A thin crepe is spread first. Cabbage goes on in a high pile, then pork and yakisoba noodles, and the stack is finished on a fried egg. The cabbage is not mixed into the batter the way it is in Osaka. Both cities call the result okonomiyaki. They are different dishes. This recipe writes the layers in order and keeps them separate from the Osaka page.

Cultural background

Hiroshima shops cook the stack on a wide teppan and flip it with large spatulas. At home a big pan works if you keep the portions small. The noodles are wheat. The egg is its own layer, not a binder stirred through cabbage.

Why it tastes this way

Each layer stays distinct. The crepe tastes toasted. The cabbage steams and turns sweet under the lid of the other ingredients. Yakisoba adds a seasoned, chewy wheat note. Pork salts the stack. The egg fries into a soft sheet that holds everything. Sauce on the outside is sweet and savory. You taste the sequence, not a single mixed batter.

How it is made

Layer a thin crepe, cabbage, pork, noodles, and a fried egg, flipping the stack so the egg becomes the base, then sauce it.

Authentic touch

Start with a truly thin crepe and a taller pile of cabbage than looks reasonable. The cabbage cooks down. If you mix it into the batter, you have left Hiroshima.

How it is used

Slide the whole stack onto a plate and cut it with a spatula. Eat it hot as a savory street meal.

Field notes

  • Hiroshima okonomiyaki is layered. Osaka mixes the cabbage into the batter.
  • The usual layers are crepe, cabbage, noodles, and egg, with pork in the pile.

Ingredients

  • 1/2 cup all-purpose wheat flour
  • 1/2 cup water
  • A pinch of salt
  • 300 grams shredded green cabbage
  • 80 grams thinly sliced pork
  • 1 portion cooked yakisoba wheat noodles, about 150 grams
  • 2 eggs
  • Neutral oil
  • Okonomiyaki sauce and aonori

Steps

  1. Whisk flour, water, and salt into a thin crepe batter.
  2. Oil a large pan. Spread a thin crepe of about half the batter. On it pile half the cabbage, then the pork.
  3. Cover and cook until the cabbage shrinks and the pork loses its raw color. Season the noodles and set them on the pile.
  4. In a clear spot of the pan, fry one egg as a flat sheet. Flip the whole stack onto the egg.
  5. Slide the cake onto a plate egg side down, spread sauce and aonori, and repeat for the second serving.

Cook the pork until it is done before you add the noodles and egg.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Bean sprouts with the cabbage
  • A second crepe on top if your pan runs dry

Pairings

  • Okonomiyaki sauce
  • Aonori
  • Pickled ginger
