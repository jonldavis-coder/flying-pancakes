Milk-soaked bread, fried, with cinnamon and honey or syrup.

20 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 8 · 8 slices

History

Torrijas are Spain’s soaked and fried bread, strongly associated with Lent and also with other feasts. Stale bread, milk, egg, cinnamon, and honey or a wine syrup are the classic pieces. This version uses milk and honey and leaves out wine so it is not an alcoholic recipe. Mexican torrijas and Portuguese rabanadas are the relatives.

Cultural background

They are made ahead for Holy Week in many houses. The bread should be a day or two old. Cinnamon is essential to the Spanish taste.

Why it tastes this way

Lent tables used bread, milk, and egg when meat was limited, which is why a fried slice became a seasonal sweet. Cinnamon is the spice. Honey is a traditional sweetener and is not vegan. The crust is the fry. The soft center is the milk. Stale bread is what makes the soak work.

How it is made

Stale bread is soaked in cinnamon milk, dipped in egg, fried, and finished with honey.

Authentic touch

Use dry bread and a short soak. Coat with cinnamon after frying, while the surface is still damp with syrup or honey.

How it is used

Cool or slightly warm, as a Lenten sweet.

Field notes

  • Spanish torrijas are strongly tied to Lent.
  • Honey and cinnamon are the finish in this alcohol-free version.

Ingredients

  • 8 slices stale bread
  • 1 cup milk
  • 1 cinnamon stick
  • 2 tablespoons sugar
  • 2 eggs
  • Oil for frying
  • Honey
  • 1 teaspoon cinnamon

Steps

  1. Heat milk with cinnamon and sugar. Cool and strain.
  2. Dip the bread, then the eggs.
  3. Fry until golden.
  4. Drizzle with honey and dust with cinnamon.
  5. Serve warm or cool.
  6. Use stale bread.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A wine syrup is traditional and alcoholic.
  • The Mexican page uses piloncillo.

Pairings

  • cinnamon
