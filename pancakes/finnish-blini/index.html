Finnish Blini

Small yeasted buckwheat cakes for the Shrovetide table.

1 hr 30 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · about 16 small cakes

History

Finland’s Laskiainen is the Shrovetide festival, with pea soup and cream buns as the foods most often named. Blini are also served in Finland, especially in communities with Russian influence, as small yeasted buckwheat cakes with toppings such as sour cream or fish roe. This page is those blini, not a claim that they replaced pea soup. The Orthodox and eastern connection explains the cake better than a generic “Finnish pancake” label.

Cultural background

They are small, risen, and eaten with savory or rich toppings. Roe adds fish. This recipe stops at the cake and sour cream.

Why it tastes this way

Buckwheat and yeast taste deeper and tangier than a baking-powder pancake, which is why blini suit sour cream and salt rather than maple. The eastern European method moved into Finnish tables through shared borders and Orthodox tradition. Shrovetide is a late-winter feast before the fast, which is why rich toppings show up then.

How it is made

A yeasted batter of buckwheat and wheat is risen and cooked as small cakes.

Authentic touch

Let the batter rise until it is bubbly. Flat blini are just buckwheat pancakes. The rise is the dish. Cook them small.

How it is used

Sour cream, melted butter, or fish roe if you eat fish. The roe is not in the batter.

Field notes

  • Laskiainen foods most famously include pea soup and buns. Blini are also served and are not a substitute story.
  • The yeast rise is what makes them blini rather than lettu.

Ingredients

  • 1/2 cup buckwheat flour
  • 1/2 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1 teaspoon instant yeast
  • 1 teaspoon sugar
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 cup warm milk
  • 1 large egg
  • 2 tablespoons melted butter

Steps

  1. Stir the batter and rest it 1 hour, until bubbly.
  2. Cook small cakes on a buttered medium griddle.
  3. Flip when the tops are full of holes and set.
  4. Serve warm with sour cream.
  5. They should taste faintly tangy.
  6. Do not make them large or the center stays heavy.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Russian blini are the larger tradition this cake belongs to.
  • A purely wheat batter is milder and less typical.

Pairings

  • sour cream
  • melted butter
