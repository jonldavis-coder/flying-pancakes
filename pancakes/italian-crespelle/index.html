Pronounced kres-PEL-leh

Thin Italian pancakes, usually rolled around a savory filling and baked.

30 min prep · 30 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 rolls

History

Crespelle are the Italian crêpe, used more often as a savory wrapper than as a lemon-sugar street snack. Baked crespelle with spinach and ricotta, or with a meat ragù, appear in several regions. Cannelloni are sometimes made from crespelle instead of from pasta tubes. The pancake itself is a thin egg batter.

Cultural background

This recipe is the plain crespelle and a simple spinach and ricotta filling, which is a meatless classic. A meat ragù is a different dish.

Why it tastes this way

An Italian table that already made pasta used the same flour and eggs to make a thin cake when a baked dish was wanted. Ricotta and spinach taste mild and green, and a little nutmeg is the traditional perfume. Baking under tomato or butter keeps the crêpe from drying. The cake is a vessel. The filling is the flavor.

How it is made

Thin pancakes are filled with seasoned spinach and ricotta, rolled, and baked with a little butter and cheese.

Authentic touch

Cook the crespelle only until they release. If they are brittle, they crack around the filling. A damp towel over the stack keeps them flexible while you fill.

How it is used

Hot, as a first course or supper.

Field notes

  • Crespelle are the Italian crêpe, most often baked with a filling.
  • Cannelloni can be pasta or crespelle. This page is the pancake.

Ingredients

  • 3/4 cup flour
  • 2 eggs
  • 1 cup milk
  • Pinch of salt
  • 1 tablespoon butter
  • 1 cup ricotta
  • 1 cup cooked chopped spinach, squeezed dry
  • 1/4 teaspoon nutmeg
  • 1/4 cup grated Parmesan
  • Salt and pepper

Steps

  1. Make thin crespelle from the flour, eggs, milk, salt, and butter.
  2. Mix ricotta, spinach, nutmeg, half the Parmesan, salt, and pepper.
  3. Fill and roll the crespelle. Lay them in a buttered dish.
  4. Dot with butter and the rest of the cheese.
  5. Bake at 375°F about 15 minutes.
  6. Serve hot.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A meat filling is common and changes the allergens.
  • Unfilled, with sugar, they are closer to a French crêpe and less typical.

Pairings

  • tomato sauce
  • parmesan
