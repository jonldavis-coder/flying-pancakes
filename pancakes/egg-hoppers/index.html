Egg hoppers

You come in by Boat. Sri Lanka · South Asia.

Pronounced eg HOP-erz

A coconut rice hopper with a soft egg set in the center, crisp at the rim.

30 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 4 · 4 egg hoppers

History

An egg hopper is a Sri Lankan hopper with an egg broken into the thick center so the white sets against the rice and the yolk stays soft. The batter is the same fermented rice and coconut milk used for plain hoppers. The egg is the addition that gives this page its own name. It is a breakfast from hopper stands in Colombo and elsewhere on the island. It is not a string hopper, and it is not a wheat omelet.

Cultural background

The cook pours and swirls the batter, then cracks the egg at once so it cooks under the lid with the rice. A firm yolk is a missed hopper. Chile sambol is served beside it, not baked inside, in the usual plate.

Why it tastes this way

The rice bowl is lightly sour and sweet from coconut. The egg adds richness the plain hopper does not have, and a soft yolk sauces the spongy center. The crisp rim stays free of egg if the crack is careful, so you still get the cracker edge. Salt and the heat of a sambol finish the plate.

How it is made

Swirl fermented rice batter in a hopper pan, crack an egg into the center, and cover until the white sets.

Authentic touch

Crack the egg into the center puddle, not onto the thin wall. An egg on the lace burns and welds the hopper to the pan.

How it is used

Eat at once with a spicy onion sambol. The yolk should still move. Dried-fish sambols add a fish allergen on the side.

Field notes

  • The egg goes into the center of a rice hopper. It does not replace the rice batter.
  • String hoppers are pressed rice noodles, a different dish from this pan.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups fermented rice and coconut-milk hopper batter
  • 4 eggs
  • Salt and pepper
  • Neutral oil for the pan

Steps

  1. Use hopper batter that has fermented until bubbly, as on the plain hopper page.
  2. Heat and grease a hopper pan. Pour a ladle and swirl so the center remains deep.
  3. Immediately crack one egg into that center. Sprinkle a little salt and pepper.
  4. Cover and cook on medium-low until the rim is crisp, the egg white is set, and the yolk is still soft.
  5. Loosen the rim and slide the hopper out. Repeat with the remaining eggs.

Cook the egg white until it is set. People who need fully cooked yolks should leave the lid on longer.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A pinch of salt and pepper on the yolk
  • A firmer yolk if you cover the pan longer

Pairings

  • Onion sambol
  • Seeni sambol
  • Banana and a second plain hopper
