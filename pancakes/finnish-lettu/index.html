Pronounced LET-too

A thin Finnish pancake, often served with jam or whipped cream.

10 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · about 8 pancakes

History

Lettuja, also called ohukaisia, are Finland’s thin pancakes, related to Swedish pancakes across the Baltic. They are a home dessert, rolled or folded with jam. A thicker baked pannukakku is the other Finnish pancake and has its own page, including an Upper Peninsula version.

Cultural background

They are everyday food, not only holiday food. Berry jam from the summer, especially lingonberry or strawberry, is the filling.

Why it tastes this way

A thin batter cooks quickly in butter and tastes mild, which is why the jam matters. Finland’s berry woods are the fruit source, preserved for winter. The pancake itself is egg and milk and flour, a dairy-country dessert. Cardamom is optional here. Many Finnish pancakes are plain and let the berries speak.

How it is made

A thin egg batter is cooked in a buttered pan and folded with jam.

Authentic touch

Cook them thin enough to fold without a crack. Resting the batter helps. The first lettu will look worse than the rest.

How it is used

Jam, whipped cream, or a both.

Field notes

  • Lettu and ohukainen both name the thin Finnish pancake.
  • The baked pannukakku is a different dish.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup flour
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 1/2 cups milk
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1 tablespoon melted butter
  • Jam, to serve

Steps

  1. Whisk the batter smooth.
  2. Cook thin pancakes in a buttered skillet.
  3. Fill with jam and fold.
  4. Serve warm.
  5. Whipped cream is optional.
  6. Do not overbrown them or they will not fold.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Pannukakku is baked.
  • A savory filling is less usual than jam.

Pairings

  • jam
  • whipped cream
  • berries
