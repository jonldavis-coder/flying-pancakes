Arriving in Finland

Finnish lettu

You come in by Boat. Finland · Northern Europe.

Pronounced LET-too

Thin Finnish pancakes, served with jam, sugar, or whipped cream.

15 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 10 pancakes

History

Lettu, plural letut, is the thin Finnish pancake. Ohukainen is a related name, often for a smaller cake. This page is the thin lettu cooked in a pan. It is everyday home food, served with jam, sugar, or whipped cream. The Åland oven pancake, made from porridge, is a different page. This batter is flour, egg, and milk, not a baked porridge.

Cultural background

Letut are a dessert and a weekend treat more than a ceremonial calendar food. A thin flexible cake is the goal. Families argue about jam versus sugar. Both are honest.

Why it tastes this way

They taste of egg, milk, and browned butter, with almost no spice. A thin sheet means more browned surface. Jam adds acid. Whipped cream adds richness the batter only hints at. If the batter is thick, you lose the lettu and gain a different pancake.

How it is made

A thin batter is rested and cooked like crepes, then served with jam or sugar.

Authentic touch

Cook them thin enough to roll. Ohukaiset, smaller and sometimes a little thicker, are the cousin. Do not bake this batter as an Åland pancake.

How it is used

Rolled or folded, with jam, sugar, or whipped cream, as dessert or a weekend breakfast.

Field notes

  • Lettu is the thin Finnish pancake. Ohukainen is a close name for a smaller cake.
  • The Åland pancake is a baked porridge cake and a different page.
  • Jam, sugar, or whipped cream are the usual finishes.

Ingredients

  • 150 g plain wheat flour
  • 1 tbsp sugar
  • 1/4 tsp fine salt
  • 2 large eggs
  • 400 ml whole milk
  • 30 g unsalted butter, for the pan
  • Jam, for serving

Steps

  1. Whisk the flour, sugar, salt, eggs, and milk into a thin batter. Rest 15 minutes.
  2. Cook thin pancakes in a buttered pan until both sides are spotted.
  3. Spread with jam or sprinkle with sugar.
  4. Roll or fold and serve, with whipped cream if you want it.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Ohukaiset, cooked smaller
  • A savory filling of cheese, less typical than jam

Pairings

  • Jam
  • Whipped cream
  • Sugar
