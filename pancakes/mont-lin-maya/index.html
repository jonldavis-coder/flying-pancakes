Pronounced mont lin MAY-ah

Small rice cakes cooked in pairs in a well pan, crisp-edged and soft inside.

10 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · about 12 cakes

History

Mont lin maya, sometimes translated as husband-and-wife snack, are Burmese rice cakes cooked in a dimpled pan, often sold in pairs. A rice batter, sometimes with chickpea, is cooked in the wells. They are a street snack. The pair is how they are served. This recipe uses rice flour and a little chickpea flour.

Cultural background

They are savory-leaning and mild, eaten as a snack. Some vendors add a quail egg. This version has no egg.

Why it tastes this way

Rice and chickpea flours fry in a small well into a cake with a crisp rim, the same pan logic as khanom krok, with a Burmese batter that is less dessert-sweet. The pair serving is the name’s story in the market. The flavor is toasted rice and a little gram, not coconut custard.

How it is made

A rice and chickpea batter is cooked in a well pan until crisp-edged.

Authentic touch

Oil the wells. Cook until the bottoms are brown and the centers are set. Serve them in pairs, which is the customary way, not a joke.

How it is used

Warm, as a snack, two to a portion.

Field notes

  • Mont lin maya are often sold in pairs.
  • The well pan is the tool.

Ingredients

  • 3/4 cup rice flour
  • 1/4 cup chickpea flour
  • 1 cup water
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • Oil

Steps

  1. Whisk a pourable batter.
  2. Oil a hot well pan.
  3. Cook until the bottoms brown and the centers set.
  4. Turn if you can.
  5. Serve in pairs.
  6. They are a snack, not a dessert.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A quail egg in the well is a richer street version and adds egg.
  • Khanom krok is sweeter and more coconut-forward.
