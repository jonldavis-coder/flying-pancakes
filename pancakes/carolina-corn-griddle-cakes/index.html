Carolina Corn Griddle Cakes

Sweet-corn meal, buttermilk, and a crisper edge than a wheat cake.

15 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · about 8 cakes

History

Corn was the daily grain of the Carolinas long before wheat pancakes filled a diner menu. Indigenous farmers in the region grew corn, and later Black and white Southern cooks turned meal into breads, mush, and griddle cakes. This cake is the breakfast form: cornmeal loosened with buttermilk and an egg, cooked in rounds.

Cultural background

It belongs with breakfast or with a plate of vegetables. It is closer to a hoe cake than to a fluffy flour pancake, with enough egg and buttermilk to make it tender.

Why it tastes this way

Coarse meal keeps a sandy, toasted flavor that fine wheat flour cannot. Buttermilk softens that grit and adds acid. The coastal and piedmont South could rely on corn when wheat was a bought luxury, which is why so many Carolina breads start with meal.

How it is made

Cornmeal, buttermilk, egg, and a little chemical leavener are cooked as small thick cakes.

Authentic touch

Let the batter stand 10 minutes so the meal swells. If you cook it at once, the center tastes raw.

How it is used

Butter, honey, or a savory plate of eggs and greens. They do not need a heavy sweet syrup.

Field notes

  • Resting the meal is the step that keeps the center from tasting like dry polenta.
  • Plain cornmeal keeps the cake gluten-free. Corn muffin mix usually does not.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup plain cornmeal
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/4 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • 1 cup buttermilk
  • 1 large egg
  • 1 tablespoon melted butter

Steps

  1. Stir the dry ingredients. Whisk in buttermilk, egg, and butter.
  2. Rest 10 minutes.
  3. Cook on a buttered medium griddle as small thick rounds.
  4. Flip when the edges look dry.
  5. Cook until the center is set.
  6. Serve hot.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A spoon of flour makes them softer and removes the gluten-free label.
  • Cracklings in the batter are an older savory turn and add meat.

Pairings

  • butter
  • honey
  • eggs
  • greens
