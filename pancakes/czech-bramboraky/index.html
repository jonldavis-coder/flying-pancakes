Pronounced BRAM-boh-rah-kee

Potato pancakes seasoned with marjoram and garlic.

20 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · about 8 cakes

History

Bramboráky are Czech potato pancakes, and the seasoning is the point: marjoram and garlic, sometimes a little caraway. They are not plain latkes. They are served as a side or a meatless main, and they are a recognized national potato dish.

Cultural background

Marjoram is the herb Czech cooks reach for in potato dishes and soups. The cake should taste of it, not of a generic fry.

Why it tastes this way

Marjoram and garlic make the potato savory and slightly perfumed, which is why bramboráky do not need applesauce to have a point. The herb is dried in winter, when potatoes are the staple. Flour and egg bind a cake that is thicker than a lace cookie and crisp at the edge. The flavor is potato plus that herb, a combination worth keeping distinct from German puffer.

How it is made

Grated potato is mixed with egg, flour, garlic, and marjoram and fried.

Authentic touch

Use dried marjoram if that is what you have, and crush it so it does not stay in needles. Fry the cakes through. Raw garlic and raw potato in the center are the failure.

How it is used

Hot, as a side or a main, with a salad or with meat.

Field notes

  • Marjoram is the characteristic seasoning of bramboráky.
  • They are a different dish from palačinky.

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 pounds potatoes
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 teaspoon dried marjoram
  • 1 large egg
  • 2 tablespoons flour
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • Oil for frying

Steps

  1. Grate and squeeze the potatoes.
  2. Mix with garlic, marjoram, egg, flour, and salt.
  3. Fry flattened cakes until deep brown and cooked through.
  4. Drain.
  5. Serve hot.
  6. The center should not taste starchy-raw.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A pinch of caraway is a regional addition.
  • Without marjoram they are just potato cakes. Keep the herb.

Pairings

  • salad
  • sour cream
