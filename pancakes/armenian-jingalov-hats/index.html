Arriving in Goris

Jingalov hats

You come in by Plane. Armenia · Caucasus.

Pronounced jin-gah-LOHV hahts

A thin griddled flatbread packed with a large handful of salted herbs and greens.

40 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 4 stuffed flatbreads

History

Jingalov hats is an herb-stuffed flatbread associated with Artsakh and with Armenian kitchens, including those in Syunik. The pin is Goris. The filling is a pile of finely chopped greens and herbs, lightly salted, sealed in a thin dough and cooked on a griddle. It is a flatbread, not a batter pancake. Cooks use a mix of what is in season. The point is the quantity of greens, not a single spice blend.

Cultural background

The bread is everyday food where the herbs grow, and it is also a bread people make to show what the place tastes like. A thin filling is considered a missed loaf. The dough is only the envelope. No cheese is required, and this recipe does not add any.

Why it tastes this way

A large mass of parsley, cilantro, dill, scallion, and chard tastes green, slightly bitter, and fresh, because the griddle wilts the herbs inside the dough instead of frying them hard. Salt is the seasoning. The toasted wheat wrapper is mild, so the herbs stay in front. Different seasonal leaves change the bitterness from batch to batch.

How it is made

Rest a soft dough. Finely chop a mix of tender herbs and greens, salt them, and squeeze out any heavy liquid. Fill a thin oval of dough, seal it, and cook it on a dry griddle, pressing so the center heats through.

Authentic touch

Chop a large volume of greens, salt them, and taste the filling before it goes into the dough. If it tastes watery or shy, add more herbs, not more flour. This is an herb-stuffed flatbread.

How it is used

Jingalov hats is a savory snack or a light meal, eaten hot, sometimes with tea or a simple salad. It does not need a sauce. Pickles on the side are a home choice and are not in the dough.

Field notes

  • Jingalov hats is an herb-stuffed flatbread, associated with Artsakh and with Armenian home cooking.
  • The filling should be a large amount of greens, not a sprinkle of herbs.
  • The pin is Goris, in Syunik. The bread is eaten well beyond that town.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups wheat flour (about 250 g)
  • 3/4 teaspoon fine salt, divided
  • 3/4 cup water
  • 1 cup finely chopped parsley
  • 1 cup finely chopped cilantro
  • 1/2 cup finely chopped dill
  • 1 cup finely chopped scallions
  • 2 cups finely chopped chard or spinach
  • 1 tablespoon oil

Steps

  1. Mix the flour, half the salt, and the water into a soft dough. Rest it for 15 minutes.
  2. Combine the parsley, cilantro, dill, scallions, and chard with the remaining salt and the oil. You want a generous pile. Squeeze out excess liquid.
  3. Divide the dough into 4 and roll each piece into a thin oval. Spread a thick layer of herbs over it, leaving a border.
  4. Fold and seal the dough around the herbs, then flatten gently so the packet is even. This is a stuffed flatbread.
  5. Cook on a dry griddle over medium heat, turning and pressing, until both sides are spotted and the dough is cooked. The herbs should steam inside, not burn.

The dough contains wheat. Taste a pinch of the salted herbs before cooking so the filling is not overly salty. Wild herbs you cannot identify do not belong in the bowl.

Eat the bread the day it is cooked. Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry pan. The greens darken as they sit. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Sorrel or other seasonal greens can join the mix when you have them.
  • A few walnut pieces appear in some fillings. They would add a tree nut and are not in this recipe.
  • A slightly thicker dough is easier and less like the thin market bread.

Pairings

  • Tea
  • A tomato and cucumber salad
  • Pickled vegetables
