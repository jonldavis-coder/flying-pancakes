Arriving in Boca Chica

Dominican yaniqueques

You come in by Boat. Dominican Republic · Caribbean.

Pronounced yah-nee-KEH-kehs

Small wheat rounds, salty and toasted, cooked in a slick of oil until both sides spot.

20 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 5 · 10 cakes

History

Yaniqueque is the Dominican form of the name johnny cake. The cakes are small wheat-flour rounds, fried or cooked in a skillet, and they are beach and street food around Boca Chica and other coasts. This recipe cooks them in a skillet with a thin layer of oil rather than deep-frying a sweet dough. They are a wheat bread. Cassava casabe on the same island is the older starch. These are the later flour cakes.

Cultural background

Vendors press the dough into irregular rounds and cook them fast. People eat them plain, with ketchup, or next to fried fish. The dough in this version is flour, water, salt, and a little baking powder, so it stays vegan.

Why it tastes this way

Wheat, salt, and hot oil are the whole flavor. Baking powder opens a few holes so the cake is not a hard cracker. The surface browns where it sits in the oil, and the center stays bready. There is no sugar in this batch, which keeps it on the savory side of the johnny-cake family.

How it is made

Mix wheat flour, baking powder, salt, and water. Rest the dough, roll small rounds, and cook them in a lightly oiled skillet.

Authentic touch

Roll them thicker than a tortilla and thinner than a bun. A very thin yaniqueque hardens as it cools. Cook in a shallow film of oil, turning once, until both sides are spotted.

How it is used

Eat them hot at a beach stall or with a stew at home. They are a snack bread, not a dessert.

Field notes

  • The name yaniqueque is the island form of johnny cake.
  • This batch is pan-cooked wheat dough, not cornmeal and not cassava.
  • Casabe, the Taíno cassava bread, is a different Dominican page in this journal.

Ingredients

  • 240 g wheat flour, plus more for rolling
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 120 ml water
  • 3 tablespoons neutral oil for the skillet

Steps

  1. Stir the flour, baking powder, and salt. Add the water and knead into a soft dough. Rest 15 minutes.
  2. Divide into 10 pieces and roll each into a small thick round.
  3. Heat the oil in a skillet over medium heat.
  4. Cook the rounds in a single layer, turning once, until both sides are browned in spots and the center is cooked.
  5. Drain on a rack and eat them while they are still flexible.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Deep-frying the same dough is the other street method. They come out darker and richer.
  • A pinch of sugar pulls them toward Jamaican festival. This recipe leaves the sugar out.
  • Butter in the dough adds dairy, so the cakes are no longer vegan or dairy-free.

Pairings

  • Fried fish from the same stall, which adds fish
  • A spoon of mashed avocado
  • Hot sauce on the side
