Crisp fried johnnycakes, salty and blistered.

20 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · about 12 pieces

History

Yaniqueques are Dominican fried breads in the johnnycake family, the island word sitting close to the New England cake and to other Caribbean journey cakes. A simple flour or cornmeal dough is rolled and fried. Beach vendors sell them. This version uses flour, which is a common vendor dough, and it is fried rather than dry-griddled.

Cultural background

They are a snack with fish or with nothing. The blister is the sign of hot oil. They are not a sweet pancake.

Why it tastes this way

Hot oil blisters a thin dough so it tastes toasted and salty, a portable beach bread. The connection to johnnycake is the journey of a word and a simple dough around the Atlantic, not a claim that a Rhode Island recipe was copied line for line. Salt is the seasoning. The fry is the Dominican street method.

How it is made

A simple dough is rolled thin and fried until blistered.

Authentic touch

Roll them thin and fry hot. Thick yaniqueques stay doughy. Drain them or they go limp.

How it is used

Hot, salted, as a snack.

Field notes

  • Yaniqueque is the Dominican fried johnnycake.
  • Thin and hot is the texture.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups flour
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1 tablespoon oil
  • 3/4 cup water
  • Oil for frying

Steps

  1. Mix flour, baking powder, salt, oil, and water into a soft dough. Rest 15 minutes.
  2. Roll thin and cut rounds. Slit each one.
  3. Fry in 1/2 inch of hot oil until blistered.
  4. Drain and salt.
  5. Serve hot.
  6. Do not fry them thick.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A cornmeal dough is closer to some johnnycakes and can be gluten-free if it has no wheat. This one is wheat.
  • Baking them makes a different, softer bread.

Pairings

  • fried fish
  • salt
