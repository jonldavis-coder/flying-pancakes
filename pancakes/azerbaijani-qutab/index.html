Arriving in Baku

Azerbaijani qutab

You come in by Train. Azerbaijan · Caucasus.

Pronounced koo-TAHB

Thin half-moon flatbread stuffed with herbs, finished with sumac.

35 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 half-moons

History

Qutab is an Azerbaijani stuffed flatbread, cooked on a saj or a dry griddle and folded into a half-moon. The two fillings people ask for are herbs, göy qutab, and pumpkin. This recipe is the herb one. A dusting of sumac after cooking is part of the plate. It is a griddled flatbread, not a batter pancake, and it belongs to the Caucasus on this map, with Baku as the pin.

Cultural background

Qutab is everyday food and guest food. The dough is rolled thin enough that the green of the herbs shows as a shadow. Yogurt or sour cream is a common side and is dairy, so it is kept off this vegan recipe and mentioned as a pairing. The bread itself is oil, flour, herbs, and sumac.

Why it tastes this way

The herbs taste green and fresh because they are barely cooked inside the dough, not fried first into a paste. Wheat from the thin wrapper toasts on the saj. Sumac on the finished bread is sour and fruity, which is why a separate sauce is optional. There is no chile in this version.

How it is made

Make a soft dough and rest it. Chop herbs and scallions finely, salt them, and bind them with a spoon of oil. Fill thin rounds, fold them into half-moons, and cook on a hot griddle. Dust with sumac.

Authentic touch

Roll the dough thin, use a generous handful of herbs, and sprinkle sumac after the bread leaves the pan. Pumpkin qutab is the other canonical filling and is listed as a variation, not cooked in this batch.

How it is used

Qutab is a savory snack or a light meal, eaten hot. Yogurt on the side is traditional and adds dairy. Sumac is the finish on the bread. It is not a sweet pancake.

Field notes

  • Qutab is a stuffed griddled flatbread, folded into a half-moon.
  • Herbs and pumpkin are the two fillings most often named.
  • Sumac is the sour red sprinkle on the finished bread.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups wheat flour (about 250 g)
  • 3/4 teaspoon fine salt, divided
  • 3/4 cup water
  • 1 cup finely chopped scallions
  • 1 cup finely chopped cilantro
  • 1 cup finely chopped spinach
  • 2 tablespoons oil
  • 1 teaspoon ground sumac

Steps

  1. Mix the flour, half the salt, and the water into a soft dough. Rest it, covered, for 15 minutes.
  2. Mix the scallions, cilantro, spinach, remaining salt, and one tablespoon of oil. The herbs should look abundant, not like a garnish.
  3. Divide the dough into 8 and roll each piece thin. Place a spoon of herbs on one half and fold into a half-moon. Seal the edge.
  4. Cook on a hot dry griddle, turning once, until both sides are spotted and the dough is no longer raw. This is a stuffed flatbread.
  5. Brush lightly with the remaining oil and sprinkle with sumac.

The dough contains wheat. Sumac is a sour spice made from dried berries. Yogurt on the side is dairy and is not required.

Eat qutab soon after cooking. Refrigerate leftovers and reheat them on a dry pan. The herbs dull after a day. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Pumpkin qutab uses grated, salted, squeezed pumpkin instead of herbs.
  • A meat filling exists and is not vegetarian.
  • Melted butter instead of the oil finish adds dairy and is not in this written dough.

Pairings

  • Sumac, already on the bread
  • Plain yogurt, which adds dairy
  • Black tea
