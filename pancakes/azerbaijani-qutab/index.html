Thin folded flatbreads filled with greens or meat, cooked dry on a griddle.

History

Qutab are Azerbaijani stuffed flatbreads, folded into half-moons and cooked on a dry griddle called a saj. Greens qutab, with herbs, and meat qutab are the two main kinds. This recipe is the herb one, so it stays meatless. Sumac and yogurt are common at the table. The dough is unleavened and thin.

Cultural background

They are a staple and a street food. The herb mix often includes cilantro, dill, and green onion. Pomegranate or sumac on the finished bread is a typical sour note.

Why it tastes this way

A thin dough cooks in a minute and tastes of the herbs inside, not of a thick batter. Greens in quantity are the dish, a way to eat herbs as a meal. The dry saj browns the dough in spots without frying it. Sumac’s lemony acidity is the finish, a Caucasus flavor that replaces a heavy sauce.

How it is made

Unleavened dough is filled with seasoned herbs, folded, and cooked on a dry griddle.

Authentic touch

Roll the dough thin enough to see the herbs faintly. A thick qutab is doughy. Cook it dry or with the faintest oil, and press the edges so the filling does not leak.

How it is used

Warm, with sumac and yogurt if you eat dairy. The bread itself has no dairy.

Field notes

  • Herb qutab and meat qutab are both traditional. This is the herb one.
  • The saj is a griddle. A heavy skillet is the home stand-in.

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 cups flour
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 cup water
  • 1 tablespoon oil
  • 2 cups chopped herbs: cilantro, dill, and green onion
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt, for the herbs
  • Sumac, to serve

Steps

  1. Mix flour, salt, water, and oil into a dough. Rest 15 minutes.
  2. Toss the herbs with salt.
  3. Divide the dough into 6, roll thin, and fill half of each round with herbs.
  4. Fold and seal.
  5. Cook on a dry medium griddle until spotted on both sides.
  6. Sprinkle with sumac and serve warm.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A lamb filling is traditional and adds meat.
  • A spoon of soft cheese in the herbs adds dairy.

Pairings
