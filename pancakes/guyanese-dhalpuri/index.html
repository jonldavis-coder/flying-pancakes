Guyanese Dhalpuri Roti

History

Dhalpuri is a Guyanese roti filled with ground split peas, cooked on a tawa. It is Indo-Caribbean bread, cousin to Trinidadian roti, and a standard partner for curry. The filling is spiced yellow split peas, dry enough to roll inside the dough. It is a staple, not a sweet pancake.

Cultural background

The peas are boiled, dried in the pan, and ground with cumin and pepper. The dough is soft. The roti is cooked with a little oil.

Why it tastes this way

Split peas taste earthy and peppery, a meal inside the bread, which is why dhalpuri is more than a plain wrap. Cumin is the spice that marks the filling. The indenture-era Indian kitchen in Guyana made roti a daily bread and filled it so one round could be lunch. The griddle toasts the outside and steams the pea center.

How it is made

Boiled, seasoned split peas are sealed in dough and cooked on a griddle.

Authentic touch

The pea filling must be dry and fine. Wet peas tear the dough and steam it into a lump. Roll carefully so the filling reaches the edge without bursting.

How it is used

With curry, or on its own if the peas are well seasoned.

Field notes

  • Dhalpuri is defined by the split-pea filling.
  • Dry the peas before you grind or mash them.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups flour
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 3/4 cup water
  • 1 tablespoon oil
  • 1/2 cup yellow split peas, boiled until soft and drained dry
  • 1/2 teaspoon cumin
  • 1/4 teaspoon cayenne
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt, for the peas

Steps

  1. Mash or grind the dry cooked peas with cumin, cayenne, and salt. The filling should not be wet.
  2. Make a soft dough from flour, salt, water, and oil. Rest 15 minutes.
  3. Stuff and seal 6 rounds. Roll them gently.
  4. Cook on a hot oiled tawa until spotted on both sides.
  5. Wrap in a towel.
  6. Serve warm.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Trinidadian paratha is the flaky unfilled cousin.
  • A hotter pepper in the peas is a house choice.

Pairings
