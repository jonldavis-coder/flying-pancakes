Arriving in Trabzon

Turkish kaygana

You come in by Train. Turkey · Middle East.

Pronounced kai-gah-nah

A soft flour-and-egg skillet cake, herb-flecked, with a tender center and a browned rim.

10 min prep · 12 min cook · Serves 2 · 1 skillet cake

History

Kaygana is a Turkish breakfast cake of flour, egg, and milk, cooked in a skillet. Herbs sometimes go in, and so do cheese or pepper in other houses. It is quicker and thinner than a baked herb frittata. This version is the herb one, the kind a Black Sea or inland home might cook on a weekday morning, written here with a Trabzon pin because that coast still keeps a strong breakfast pan. It is not gözleme and not kuku sabzi.

Cultural background

Kaygana uses pantry ingredients when there is no time to roll dough. It is cut in the pan and eaten with bread or instead of it, with tea. The herbs should be fresh. Dried mint is a different, sharper cake.

Why it tastes this way

Egg is the main flavor, with flour taking the edge off so the cake is less like an omelette and more like a tender pancake. Milk softens it. Parsley and dill taste green and slight, not like the huge mass of herbs in an Iranian kuku. The pan butter browns the rim. There is no syrup.

How it is made

Whisk eggs, milk, flour, salt, parsley, and dill into a pourable batter. Cook it in a buttered skillet over moderate heat, covered, until just set. Fold or cut it in the pan. Serve at once with tea.

Authentic touch

Keep it a flour-and-egg skillet cake for breakfast. Do not bake it into a thick frittata and do not stuff it like gözleme. Fresh herbs, if you use them, are mixed into the batter.

How it is used

Kaygana is breakfast and a savory meal. It is eaten with tea, yogurt, or a simple salad. It is not a dessert. Cheese on top is extra dairy on a dish that already has milk.

Field notes

  • Kaygana is a Turkish flour-and-egg pancake, usually cooked for breakfast.
  • Herbs are mixed into the batter in this version.
  • It is thinner and quicker than a baked herb frittata.

Ingredients

  • 3 eggs
  • 1/2 cup milk
  • 1/3 cup wheat flour
  • 1/4 teaspoon fine salt
  • 2 tablespoons chopped parsley
  • 2 tablespoons chopped dill
  • 1 tablespoon butter

Steps

  1. Whisk the eggs, milk, flour, and salt until smooth. Stir in the parsley and dill.
  2. Heat the butter in a skillet over medium heat until it foams.
  3. Pour in the batter and tilt the pan. This is a flour-and-egg cake, not a rolled flatbread.
  4. Cover and cook until the center is just set and the rim is browned. Do not rush it over high heat.
  5. Cut it in the pan and serve for breakfast.

The cake contains wheat, egg, and dairy. Cook it until the egg is set, not liquid in the center.

Refrigerate leftovers the same day and reheat them in a covered pan. Do not leave the egg batter waiting at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A spoon of white cheese in the batter is a saltier breakfast.
  • Leave the herbs out for a plain flour-and-egg cake.
  • A little black pepper is enough seasoning. A chile paste would change the heat tag.

Pairings

  • Black tea
  • Plain yogurt
  • Sliced tomato
