Arriving in Ayvalık

Aegean akıtma

You come in by Boat. Turkey · Middle East.

Pronounced ah-kuht-mah

Thin Aegean semolina pancakes with a tangy crumb, finished with walnuts and syrup.

25 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · About 8 thin pancakes

History

Akıtma is a semolina pancake from the Aegean coast of Turkey. The name sits next to the verb for pouring, and the batter is meant to be poured thin. Around Ayvalık, Izmir, and the nearby towns it is breakfast or a sweet snack, often with crushed walnuts or a light syrup. Semolina is wheat. Walnuts make this version a tree-nut dish. It is not gözleme, which is a stuffed flatbread, and it is not a layered katmer.

Cultural background

Aegean kitchens keep semolina, yogurt, and olive oil close at hand. Akıtma uses that pantry. Some houses eat it with cheese instead of walnuts. This page chooses the walnut and syrup finish and says so, because the allergen is different.

Why it tastes this way

Semolina gives a fine grit and a mild sweetness. Yogurt makes the batter tangy, and yeast lifts it without a long sour ferment. The pancake is thin, so the griddle browns a larger share of the crumb. Walnuts add bitterness and fat, and the syrup makes those pieces glossy. The Aegean habit is this contrast, not a stack of thick cakes.

How it is made

Whisk fine semolina, a little flour, yeast, yogurt, water, and salt into a pourable batter. Rest it until foamy. Cook thin rounds on an oiled griddle. Top with crushed walnuts and a warm sugar syrup.

Authentic touch

Pour the batter thin, in the Aegean way, and finish with walnuts. If you leave the nuts off, say so, because this written recipe is not nut-free. Cheese instead of walnuts is a real alternative and drops the tree-nut allergen only when the nuts are actually absent.

How it is used

Akıtma is an Aegean breakfast or a sweet snack. Tea goes with it. It can be dessert when the syrup is generous. It is not a street stuffed bread.

Field notes

  • Akıtma is an Aegean Turkish semolina pancake, poured thin.
  • This version includes walnuts, so it is not nut-free.
  • The place on the map is Ayvalık, on the Aegean coast.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup fine semolina (about 160 g)
  • 1/2 cup wheat flour
  • 1 teaspoon instant yeast
  • 1/2 cup yogurt
  • 1 cup warm water
  • 1/2 teaspoon fine salt
  • 1/2 cup chopped walnuts
  • 1/2 cup sugar
  • 1/4 cup water, for the syrup
  • Oil for the pan

Steps

  1. Stir the semolina, flour, yeast, yogurt, warm water, and salt into a thin batter. Cover it for 30 to 40 minutes, until foamy.
  2. Simmer the sugar and the quarter-cup of water for 5 minutes to make a light syrup. Keep it warm.
  3. Oil a griddle over medium heat and pour a thin round. This is a pourable Aegean batter, not a stuffed flatbread.
  4. Cook until the top sets and the underside spots, then flip once and cook briefly.
  5. Top each pancake with walnuts and a spoon of syrup. The walnuts are a tree nut.

Semolina and flour are wheat. Yogurt is dairy. Walnuts are a tree nut. This plate is not nut-free.

Eat akıtma the day it is cooked. Refrigerate plain pancakes separately from the syrup and nuts, and finish them when you reheat them. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Cheese instead of walnuts removes the tree nut and keeps the dairy.
  • More yogurt makes a tangier batter.
  • A savory herb finish is a home choice and not this sweet plate.

Pairings

  • Black tea
  • Extra crushed walnuts
  • A salty white cheese, if you want a savory contrast
