Arriving in Groningen

Spekdik

You come in by Boat. Netherlands · Western Europe.

Pronounced SPEK-dik

A thick Groningen buckwheat pancake with bacon in the batter, served with syrup.

15 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 4 · 1 thick pancake, 4 wedges

History

Spekdik is a buckwheat pancake from Groningen with diced bacon cooked in the batter. It is winter food in that province, especially around the turn of the year. It is not spekkoek. Spekkoek is a spiced layer cake tied to Dutch-Indonesian cooking and does not belong on this page. The batter here is buckwheat, milk, and egg, so it is gluten-free only if the buckwheat was milled apart from wheat. The bacon makes it a meat dish.

Cultural background

Sweet syrup on a bacon pancake is the Groningen combination. The cake is thick, closer to a hearth bread than to a French crepe. It is cut in wedges.

Why it tastes this way

Buckwheat is earthy. Bacon fat seasons the crumb and crisps at the edge. Syrup cuts the salt. Milk and egg make the center soft. If the cake is thin, you have made a different pancake. Spekdik should slice.

How it is made

Bacon is rendered in a pan. A thick buckwheat batter is poured over it and cooked slowly, covered, then turned, and served with syrup.

Authentic touch

Dice the bacon small and cook it in the pan until the fat runs, then pour the batter over it so the meat stays in the cake. Serve with dark syrup.

How it is used

In wedges, as a winter or New Year plate in the Groningen style, with syrup. It is savory and rich.

Field notes

  • Spekdik is a Groningen bacon buckwheat pancake. It is not the layered spice cake called spekkoek.
  • Syrup on a bacon pancake is the local sweet-salty plate.
  • It is associated with the dark months and New Year in Groningen.

Ingredients

  • 150 g diced smoked bacon
  • 200 g buckwheat flour
  • 2 large eggs
  • 400 ml milk
  • 1/2 tsp fine salt
  • Dark syrup, for serving

Steps

  1. Whisk the buckwheat flour, eggs, milk, and salt into a thick batter.
  2. Fry the bacon in a wide ovenproof pan until the fat renders and the pieces start to brown.
  3. Pour the batter over the bacon, spread it, and cook over medium-low heat, covered, until the top is almost set.
  4. Slide it onto a plate, return it to the pan raw side down, and finish until cooked through. Serve with syrup.

The center must be set, not raw batter. Covering the pan helps it cook through.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A meat-free buckwheat pancake, which is no longer spekdik
  • Extra bacon on the side

Pairings

  • Dark syrup
  • Coffee
  • A pickled gherkin if you want something sharp
