A thick bacon pancake, salty and substantial.

History

Spekdik is a bacon pancake from the northeast of the Netherlands, especially Drenthe and Groningen. Pieces of spek, pork belly or bacon, are cooked in the batter so the fat flavors the cake. It is a regional farm dish, heartier than a city crêpe, and it is still served at local events and at home.

Cultural background

It is a meal, not a dessert. Syrup on a corner of the plate is a Dutch habit even with bacon, and some people do that. Others keep it savory.

Why it tastes this way

Pork fat from the bacon bastes the batter as it cooks, which is why the crumb tastes salty and rich without a lot of butter. The northeast’s farming table had pork and flour and a pan big enough for one thick cake. The thickness is the difference from a thin pannenkoek: spekdik is meant to be filling in cold, flat country.

How it is made

Bacon is set in a pan, a thick batter is poured over, and the cake is cooked slowly, then flipped.

Authentic touch

Start the bacon in the pan so some fat renders, then pour the batter over it. If the bacon goes in raw on top of a thick batter, it stays pale.

How it is used

Cut into wedges. Mustard or syrup, depending on the house.

Field notes

  • Spekdik belongs to Drenthe and Groningen, not to every Dutch pancake menu.
  • It is a bacon dish. The plain pannenkoek page is the meatless cake.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1 large egg
  • 1 cup milk
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 4 ounces bacon, cut into pieces

Steps

  1. Whisk flour, egg, milk, and salt into a thick batter.
  2. Cook the bacon in a skillet until it starts to brown.
  3. Pour the batter over the bacon and spread it.
  4. Cook covered over medium-low until the top is set.
  5. Flip carefully and cook until the center is done.
  6. Cut into wedges.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A plain pannenkoek with bacon on the side is not quite spekdik. The meat should be in the cake.
  • Thick-cut bacon works better than paper-thin slices.

Pairings

  • mustard
  • syrup
