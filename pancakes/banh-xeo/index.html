Banh xeo

You come in by Plane. Vietnam · Southeast Asia.

Pronounced bahn SAY-oh

A turmeric-yellow rice crepe, crisp at the edge, filled with pork, shrimp, and bean sprouts.

25 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 4 · 4 folded crepes

History

Banh xeo is a Vietnamese turmeric rice crepe filled with pork and shrimp, eaten with herbs. The batter is rice flour and coconut milk, poured onto a hot pan so it crackles. The name is often explained as the sizzle of that pour. Central cities such as Da Nang and southern kitchens both have their own size and filling. A related crepe is also eaten in Cambodia, so Cambodia is listed beside Vietnam. The written recipe includes pork, shrimp, and fish sauce. It is not vegetarian.

Cultural background

The crepe is folded over the filling and brought to the table with a pile of lettuce, mint, and perilla. Diners tear pieces and wrap them. Nuoc cham, a dipping sauce of fish sauce, lime, sugar, and chile, is the usual bowl. The cake itself is only mildly hot. The dip can be hotter.

Why it tastes this way

Turmeric colors the rice and adds a light bitter note. Coconut milk softens that bitterness and helps the edge fry crisp. Pork and shrimp make the center savory and briny. Bean sprouts stay watery and fresh against the crackle. Herbs and the lime in the fish-sauce dip are what make a bite taste complete. The rice batter has no wheat, which is why a careful batch is gluten-free.

How it is made

Fry pork and shrimp, pour a turmeric rice batter over them in a hot pan, add bean sprouts, and fold the crepe.

Authentic touch

Use a hot pan and a thin pour, then leave it alone until the edge lifts and sounds dry. Stirring turns it into a scramble. The pork and shrimp should be nearly cooked before they are covered by the batter if they are in large pieces.

How it is used

Eat it in herb and lettuce wraps with nuoc cham. It is a savory street meal, shared from the pan.

Field notes

  • Banh xeo is a turmeric rice crepe with pork and shrimp, eaten with herbs.
  • Cambodia eats a related crepe, so it is listed beside Vietnam.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup rice flour, with no wheat added
  • 1 cup coconut milk
  • 1/2 teaspoon turmeric
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 150 grams thinly sliced pork belly or pork shoulder
  • 150 grams shrimp, peeled
  • 1 tablespoon fish sauce
  • 2 cups bean sprouts
  • Neutral oil
  • Lettuce and herbs to serve

Steps

  1. Whisk rice flour, coconut milk, turmeric, salt, and a splash of water into a thin batter. Rest 15 minutes.
  2. Season pork and shrimp with a teaspoon of fish sauce. Stir the rest of the fish sauce into a small dip with lime, sugar, water, and chile if you want nuoc cham.
  3. Oil a hot pan. Fry a portion of pork and shrimp until the pork loses its pink and the shrimp turn opaque.
  4. Pour a ladle of batter over them and swirl a thin crepe. When the edge crisps, add bean sprouts, fold, and cook another minute.
  5. Repeat. Serve with herbs, lettuce, and the dip.

Cook the pork through and the shrimp until opaque.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A smaller crepe, one shrimp each, closer to some southern plates
  • Mung beans soaked and scattered in, a central-region habit

Pairings

  • Lettuce, mint, and perilla
  • Nuoc cham
  • Pickled carrot and daikon
