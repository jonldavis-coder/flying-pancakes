Arriving in Brittany

Buckwheat galette

You come in by Hot air balloon. France · Western Europe.

Pronounced gah-LET duh sah-rah-SAN

A savory Breton buckwheat crepe, lacy and a little bitter, cooked on a billig.

20 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 galettes

History

In Brittany a galette is the buckwheat crepe, and it is usually savory. Buckwheat became a staple grain of inland Brittany. This page does not assign a century to that shift. The batter here is buckwheat flour, water, egg, and salt, cooked with butter. Wheat crepes are the other page. A galette complète, with ham, cheese, and egg, is also its own page. Use buckwheat milled apart from wheat if you need the cake to be gluten-free.

Cultural background

Galettes are everyday food, sold at markets and creperies with cider. The word galette means other flat cakes elsewhere in France. On this page it means the Breton buckwheat crepe.

Why it tastes this way

Buckwheat tastes earthy and slightly bitter, which is why sugar is the wrong default. Water, not milk, keeps that flavor clear. Butter on the billig browns the lace. An egg makes the sheet less fragile. Salt is not optional. The holes and crisp edge come from a hot surface and a thin batter.

How it is made

Buckwheat flour is whisked with salt, egg, and water, rested, and cooked in a thin lacy round on a buttered griddle.

Authentic touch

A galettière spreads the batter with a rozell, a wooden T, and lifts it with a spanell, a small spatula. A hot pan and a quick spiral are the home version of that pair of tools.

How it is used

Plain with a rub of butter, or as the base for cheese, egg, or ham. It is a savory meal, not a dessert crepe.

Field notes

  • In Brittany, galette means buckwheat and usually savory.
  • The wheat crepe is a different page, and so is the ham-and-egg complète.
  • This batter has no wheat. The gluten-free claim depends on buckwheat that was not milled with wheat.

Ingredients

  • 200 g buckwheat flour
  • 1 large egg
  • 450 ml cold water
  • 1/2 tsp fine salt
  • 30 g unsalted butter, for the pan

Steps

  1. Whisk the buckwheat flour, egg, water, and salt into a thin batter. Rest 30 minutes. It will look darker than a wheat batter.
  2. Heat a wide pan until hot and grease it with butter.
  3. Pour a thin layer, swirl, and cook until the edge crisps and the top looks dry. Turn if you want both sides colored. Many cooks leave the top soft.
  4. Rub with a little butter and serve, or add a savory filling before folding.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Galette complète, with ham, cheese, and an egg
  • A wheat-flour blend, which some shops use and which removes the gluten-free diet

Pairings

  • Salted butter
  • Dry cider
  • Grated firm cheese
