Latvian potato pancakes

You come in by Train. Latvia · Eastern Europe.

Pronounced Latvian potato pancakes

Latvian pancakes of half mashed and half raw potato, with dill, fried in butter.

25 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 pancakes

History

Latvian potato pancakes, kartupeļu pankūkas, are grated potato cakes served with sour cream. This page uses half raw grated potato and half mashed potato, with dill, so the texture is softer than an all-raw draniki and different from the Lithuanian kefir batter. Flour binds them. They are home food, not a copy of the Belarusian recipe. Butter in the pan means the cakes contain dairy.

Cultural background

Potato is a staple, and pancakes are a supper that uses it up. Dill is the herb. Sour cream is the sauce. A meat gravy is a possible side and is not required.

Why it tastes this way

Mashed potato makes the center soft. Raw potato still crisps at the edge and supplies starch. Dill tastes green and slightly sharp. Butter browns and tastes sweeter than a neutral oil. They are less brittle than a fully raw pancake and more herb-scented than the Lithuanian page.

How it is made

Raw grated potato and cold mash are mixed with egg, flour, dill, and salt, then fried in butter.

Authentic touch

Keep the half-and-half potato mix. All raw potato is a different cake. Dill goes into the batter, not only on the plate as a garnish you might forget.

How it is used

Hot, with sour cream, as a savory meal.

Field notes

  • This Latvian recipe mixes raw grated potato with mashed potato.
  • Dill is in the batter.
  • The Lithuanian page uses kefir and all raw potato. The Belarusian page uses no wheat flour.

Ingredients

  • 300 g raw potatoes, peeled
  • 300 g cold mashed potato
  • 1 large egg
  • 2 tbsp plain wheat flour
  • 2 tbsp chopped fresh dill
  • 1 tsp fine salt
  • 30 g unsalted butter, for frying
  • Sour cream, for serving

Steps

  1. Grate the raw potatoes and squeeze them lightly. Mix with the mash, egg, flour, dill, and salt.
  2. Fry flattened cakes in butter over medium heat until both sides are brown and the raw potato is cooked.
  3. Keep the heat moderate so the butter browns instead of burning.
  4. Serve with sour cream.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • All raw potato, closer to draniki
  • Bacon fat for frying, which adds meat and removes the vegetarian diet

Pairings

  • Sour cream
  • More dill
  • A beet salad
