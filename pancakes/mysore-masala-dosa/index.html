Arriving in Mysuru, Karnataka

Mysore masala dosa

You come in by Train. India · South Asia.

Pronounced my-SORE mah-SAH-lah DOH-sah

A crisp fermented dosa spread inside with red chile-garlic chutney and a line of potato masala.

45 min prep · 40 min cook · Serves 4 · 6 chutney dosas

History

Mysore masala dosa is a restaurant style associated with Mysuru and the wider Karnataka hotel kitchen. The batter is still fermented rice and urad. What marks the plate is a red chutney, usually dried red chile and garlic, spread on the dosa before the potato goes in. This page does not name an inventor or a founding year. The red smear is the distinction from a plain potato masala dosa.

Cultural background

The chutney is cooked or ground smooth enough to paint with the back of a spoon. It should season the sheet, not drown it. Kitchens in Mysuru vary the chile and the amount of garlic. The potato remains the familiar dry masala.

Why it tastes this way

Dried red chile brings heat and a brick-red color that fresh green chile does not. Garlic, raw or lightly cooked depending on the cook, stays sharp against the sour rice batter. Potato and turmeric soften those edges. Because the chutney is spread on the cooked surface, it fries for a few seconds and tastes toasted rather than like a cold sauce.

How it is made

Cook a thin fermented dosa, paint it with red chile chutney, add a strip of potato masala, and fold.

Authentic touch

Spread a thin film of red chutney on the dosa as soon as the top sets, then add potato. A thick layer of chutney burns before the potato warms.

How it is used

Serve immediately, chutney side in, with coconut chutney if you want a cool contrast. The interior is medium-hot.

Field notes

  • The red chutney spread is what makes a masala dosa a Mysore masala dosa.
  • The grain is still fermented rice and urad, the same family as a plain dosa.

Ingredients

  • 1 batch fermented rice and urad dosa batter, about 3 cups
  • 4 dried red chiles, stemmed
  • 3 garlic cloves
  • 1 tablespoon neutral oil, plus more for cooking
  • Salt
  • 3 boiled potatoes, crumbled
  • 1 small onion, sliced
  • 1/2 teaspoon mustard seeds
  • 1/4 teaspoon turmeric
  • 8 curry leaves

Steps

  1. Have bubbly fermented rice and urad batter ready, made as for a plain dosa.
  2. Soak the dried chiles in hot water for 10 minutes. Grind them with garlic, a pinch of salt, and a teaspoon of water to a thick paste.
  3. For the potato, heat a tablespoon of oil, pop the mustard seeds, add curry leaves and onion, then turmeric and potato. Salt it and cook until dry.
  4. Spread a thin dosa on a hot oiled griddle. When the top sets, smear a thin layer of red chutney over it.
  5. Add a line of potato, fold, and cook another half minute so the chutney heats through. Serve hot.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A spoon of jaggery on the side for a sweet-hot plate
  • Less dried chile for a milder chutney

Pairings

  • Coconut chutney
  • Sambar
  • A ripe banana, a common South Indian breakfast companion
