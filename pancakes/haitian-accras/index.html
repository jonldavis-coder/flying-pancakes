Arriving in Port-au-Prince

Haitian accras

You come in by Boat. Haiti · Caribbean.

Pronounced ah-KRAH

Fried grated malanga, herb-heavy and crisp, with a soft center that has to be cooked through.

25 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · 16 fritters

History

Haitian accras, often akra, are street fritters. This page is the malanga or taro version: a peeled root sold for cooking, grated, mixed with herbs and egg, and fried. Salt-cod accras are a different fritter, common on other Caribbean islands and also known in Haiti. They are not in this bowl. The recipe does not use cassava and does not teach cassava processing. Cook the fritters until the center is soft.

Cultural background

Vendors in Port-au-Prince fry accras and sell them with a spicy sauce on the side. The heat lives in that sauce more than in this batter. Scallion, garlic, and parsley are the seasoning inside the fritter. Leave the fish out and the recipe stays vegetarian.

Why it tastes this way

Malanga fries to a nutty, starchy crust. The inside stays moist and slightly earthy if it is cooked through, and it stays harsh and chalky if it is not. Garlic, scallion, and parsley perfume the batter without making it hot. Egg holds the shreds together. Oil browns the herbs at the edge, which is where a lot of the aroma comes from.

How it is made

Grate peeled malanga, mix with egg, scallion, garlic, parsley, and salt, and fry spoons of the mixture until the center is soft.

Authentic touch

Use peeled malanga or taro that was sold for cooking. Grate it, squeeze out only a little liquid, and fry until a broken fritter is soft in the center, not glassy or raw. A golden crust is not enough.

How it is used

Eat them hot as a street snack, with pikliz or a pepper sauce if you want heat. Those sauces are extra. The fritter on this page is not spicy.

Field notes

  • This accra is malanga or taro, not a cassava recipe and not a salt-cod fritter.
  • Cook the center through. Color on the outside is not a test of doneness.
  • With no fish in the bowl, the fritters stay vegetarian.

Ingredients

  • 400 g peeled malanga or taro, sold for cooking
  • 1 egg
  • 2 scallions, finely chopped
  • 1 garlic clove, minced
  • 2 tablespoons chopped parsley
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • Neutral oil for frying

Steps

  1. Grate the peeled malanga. Squeeze lightly if it weeps a lot of liquid. Leave it moist.
  2. Mix in the egg, scallions, garlic, parsley, and salt until the shreds hold together.
  3. Heat 2 centimeters of oil over medium heat until a shred sizzles immediately.
  4. Fry spoons of the mixture, turning, until deep gold, about 4 minutes. Break one open. The center should be soft and cooked, not raw or glassy.
  5. Drain and serve hot.

Use peeled taro or malanga sold for cooking. Cook the fritters until the center is soft all the way through. This recipe does not use cassava and does not teach cassava processing.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A small hot chile in the batter raises the heat and is a common vendor version.
  • Salt cod mixed in is a different accra. It adds fish and is not vegetarian.
  • A spoon of wheat flour is a binder some cooks use. Those fritters contain gluten.

Pairings

  • Pikliz, the cabbage pickle, on the side
  • A tomato sauce
  • Fried plantain
