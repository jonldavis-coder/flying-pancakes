Tangy sourdough cakes, richer than the Alaskan camp version.

History

San Francisco’s sourdough bakers, and the prospectors who kept starters before them, made the city proud of a sour loaf. Sourdough pancakes are the breakfast use of that starter, common in California kitchens that keep a jar on the counter. They are related to Alaska sourdough hotcakes and are written here with milk and butter, the settled-kitchen version rather than the camp one.

Cultural background

A starter, flour, and milk are mixed the night before or an hour before, then eggs and soda finish the batter in the morning. Bakeries and home cooks both do it.

Why it tastes this way

The Bay Area’s bakers built a reputation on a tart starter, and the pancake borrows that acid. Milk and butter round the sourness so it tastes dairy-rich rather than like a lean camp cake. Baking soda at the end is the same lift used in the North. The flavor is wheat, acid, and butter.

How it is made

Starter, flour, and milk ferment briefly, then egg, butter, and baking soda finish a griddle batter.

Authentic touch

If the overnight batter smells sharply of nail polish or anything rotten, throw it out. A healthy sour smell is tangy and bready, not chemical.

How it is used

Butter and fruit or maple syrup.

Field notes

  • A pleasant tang is the goal. A spoiled starter is not a more authentic one.
  • California and Alaska sourdough cakes are the same idea in two kitchens.

Ingredients

  • 1/2 cup active sourdough starter
  • 1 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1 cup milk
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 large egg
  • 2 tablespoons melted butter
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking soda

Steps

  1. Stir starter, flour, milk, sugar, and salt. Cover and rest 1 hour, or overnight in the refrigerator.
  2. Stir in egg and butter. Heat the griddle.
  3. Stir in the baking soda and cook at once.
  4. Flip when the surface is bubbly and set.
  5. Serve hot.
  6. Save a spoon of starter, not of this egg batter, to keep your jar going.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • The Alaska page is the leaner, water-based camp cake.
  • Skip the overnight rest and use a cup of very active starter the same morning, as in the Alaska method.

Pairings

  • butter
  • berries
  • maple syrup
