Arriving in North End, Boston

Lemon ricotta pancakes

You come in by Train. United States · North America.

Soft, custardy ricotta cakes with bright lemon zest and a light crumb.

15 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · 10 small pancakes

History

Lemon ricotta pancakes are an Italian-American restaurant breakfast, a soft wheat cake enriched with ricotta and brightened with lemon. They became a brunch-menu item in American cities in the late twentieth century. This page does not trace them to a single village in Italy. The North End of Boston is the map pin for an Italian-American neighborhood.

Cultural background

Whole-milk ricotta makes a moister cake than a lean wheat batter. The lemon is zest plus a little juice, so the flavor is in the crumb and not only in a syrup.

Why it tastes this way

Ricotta adds fat and small curds, which taste milky and keep the center soft. Lemon zest holds the fragrant oil. Juice adds acid and helps the baking powder along. Overcooking dries the curds, so the cakes come off the heat as soon as they set.

How it is made

Small, thick ricotta cakes griddled in butter.

Authentic touch

Use whole-milk ricotta and fresh lemon zest. Bottled juice without zest will not smell like lemon. Keep the cakes small so they cook before the outside burns.

How it is used

Warm, with a little powdered sugar or berry sauce.

Field notes

  • These became an American brunch item in Italian-American restaurants in the late twentieth century.
  • The lemon flavor depends on zest. Juice alone tastes flatter.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup whole-milk ricotta
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1/2 cup whole milk
  • 1 tablespoon finely grated lemon zest
  • 2 tablespoons lemon juice
  • 2 tablespoons sugar
  • 1 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 2 tablespoons unsalted butter, for the griddle

Steps

  1. Whisk the ricotta, eggs, milk, lemon zest, lemon juice, and sugar until smooth.
  2. Stir in the flour, baking powder, and salt until just combined.
  3. Butter a griddle over medium-low heat.
  4. Drop 1/4 cup mounds. These cakes stay thicker than a poured diner pancake.
  5. Cook until the underside is golden and the side looks set, then flip and finish briefly.
  6. Serve warm with powdered sugar or berries.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Orange zest in place of lemon is a milder cake.
  • Drained ricotta makes a thicker batter if the cheese you bought is watery.

Pairings

  • Powdered sugar
  • Fresh berries
  • Lemon wedges
