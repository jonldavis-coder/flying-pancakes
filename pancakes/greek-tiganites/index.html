Arriving in Greece

Greek tiganites

You come in by Boat. Greece · Southern Europe.

Pronounced tee-gah-NEE-tes

Small Greek fried pancakes finished with honey and cinnamon.

10 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · 16 small pancakes

History

Tiganites are small Greek pancakes, fried in a pan and finished with honey. Greek writers in antiquity already used a name in this family for fried batter. That is not a recipe you can follow line for line into a modern kitchen, and this page does not pretend the batter never changed. Today's tiganites are spoonfuls of flour batter, fried and honeyed. Honey is an allergen here, and the cakes are not vegan. Cinnamon is the spice on the plate.

Cultural background

They are a home sweet, made for snacks and for festive tables. Olive oil is the frying fat in this recipe, which fits a Greek pantry. A spoon, not a ladle for a crepe, is the portion.

Why it tastes this way

The crust fries in olive oil and tastes toasted rather than like butter. Honey soaks the hot surface and perfumes it with flowers or pine, depending on the honey. Cinnamon adds warmth without chile. The inside stays soft if the spoonful is small. They are sweeter than a breakfast crepe because the honey is generous.

How it is made

A thick batter of flour, egg, milk, and a little leavener is fried in olive oil as small cakes and drizzled with honey and cinnamon.

Authentic touch

Fry small spoonfuls, not large crepes. Honey goes on while they are hot. The ancient name is a clue, not a complete recipe. Do not claim an unbroken formula from a single ancient author.

How it is used

Hot, with honey, as a snack or a sweet plate.

Field notes

  • Tiganites are small fried pancakes finished with honey.
  • An ancient Greek name for fried batter is not the same as this modern recipe card.
  • Honey is part of the dish, so the cakes are not vegan.

Ingredients

  • 150 g plain wheat flour
  • 1 tsp baking powder
  • 1 pinch fine salt
  • 1 large egg
  • 180 ml whole milk
  • Olive oil, for frying
  • 4 tbsp honey
  • 1/2 tsp ground cinnamon

Steps

  1. Whisk the flour, baking powder, and salt. Beat in the egg and milk until you have a thick batter.
  2. Heat a shallow layer of olive oil over medium heat.
  3. Fry small spoonfuls until both sides are golden and the centers are cooked. Drain briefly.
  4. Drizzle with honey and sprinkle with cinnamon. Serve hot.

Honey is not for infants under one year. Fry in a shallow layer and stay with the pan.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A spoon of sesame on top, which adds a sesame allergen
  • No cinnamon, honey only

Pairings

  • Honey
  • Cinnamon
  • A few chopped walnuts, which would add a tree-nut allergen and are not in this recipe
