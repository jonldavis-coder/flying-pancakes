Pronounced tee-gah-NEE-tes

Small fried batter cakes with honey and cinnamon.

10 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · about 12 cakes

History

Tiganites are Greek fried batter cakes, known by that name in medieval Greek sources and still made at home. They are closer to a fritter than to a flipped pancake, served with honey, nuts, or cinnamon. Honey and sesame or walnut are common. This version keeps the nuts on the side so the cake itself can be nut-free, and it says so.

Cultural background

They are a sweet snack, sometimes associated with holidays and sometimes simply with a jar of honey. Olive oil is a traditional frying fat. Neutral oil also works.

Why it tastes this way

Honey is the historic Greek sweetener, which is why the cake tastes floral and sticky rather than like powdered sugar. A simple flour-and-egg batter fries into something airy if the oil is hot enough. Cinnamon is a later trade spice that stuck. The fry, rather than a dry griddle, is the method the name points at: tigani is the frying pan.

How it is made

A thick batter is fried in spoonfuls and drizzled with honey and cinnamon.

Authentic touch

Fry spoonfuls in enough oil to come halfway up the cakes. A dry pan makes tough disks. Honey should go on while they are hot so it soaks the crust.

How it is used

Hot, with honey. Walnuts are traditional and are a tree nut if you add them.

Field notes

  • Tiganites are an old Greek batter cake, fried rather than flipped dry.
  • Honey is the finish. Nuts are optional and are not in this ingredient list.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1 large egg
  • 3/4 cup water
  • 1 tablespoon olive oil
  • Neutral oil for frying
  • Honey and cinnamon, to serve

Steps

  1. Stir the batter until thick and smooth.
  2. Heat 1/2 inch of oil over medium.
  3. Fry spoonfuls until golden, turning once.
  4. Drain.
  5. Drizzle with honey and cinnamon.
  6. Serve immediately.

Fry over steady heat and do not leave the pan.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Chopped walnuts on top are traditional and add a tree-nut allergen.
  • A little orange zest in the batter is a home perfume.

Pairings

  • cinnamon
