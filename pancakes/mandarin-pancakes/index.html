Mandarin pancakes

You come in by Train. China · East Asia.

Pronounced MAN-duh-rin PAN-kakes

Very thin wheat pancakes, soft and faintly sesame-scented, made to wrap crisp duck skin and meat.

25 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 6 · 12 thin pancakes

History

Mandarin pancakes are the thin wheat wrappers served with Peking duck in Beijing. They are cooked in pairs, with sesame oil between the two sheets, then peeled apart. Some kitchens steam the pairs and some griddle them. Either way they should stay pale and flexible. This page does not give them a founding date. Spring pancakes for the lichun festival are a different custom, with a spread of vegetable fillings, and they have their own page. These are the duck pancakes.

Cultural background

A duck dinner is built around the wrapper: a smear of sweet bean sauce, scallion, cucumber, and a slice of duck. The pancake itself is unleavened hot-water dough. It is an accompaniment. The written recipe is the pancake only, so it carries no meat allergen. The duck is the pairing.

Why it tastes this way

Hot-water dough tastes soft and wheaty, with almost no browning, so it will not compete with the duck. Sesame oil between the pair perfumes both faces. Cooking them together keeps them thinner than a single rolled bread could. The flavor is a neutral, slightly nutty sheet. Salt is minimal. The sauce and scallion at the table do the seasoning.

How it is made

Make a hot-water wheat dough, cook oiled pairs on a dry pan or in a steamer, and pull the pairs apart.

Authentic touch

Roll two rounds, oil one, and press them together before cooking. If you cook a single thick round and try to split it, it will not peel into two thin pancakes.

How it is used

Keep them covered with a cloth so they stay soft. Serve as the wrapper for Peking duck, with sauce, scallion, and cucumber. They are an accompaniment at a feast.

Field notes

  • Mandarin pancakes are cooked in pairs and then peeled apart.
  • They are the wrappers for Peking duck, separate from the spring pancakes of lichun.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups all-purpose wheat flour
  • 3/4 cup boiling water
  • 1 tablespoon toasted sesame oil
  • A pinch of salt

Steps

  1. Stir the flour and salt with boiling water until a dough forms. Knead until smooth. Rest 20 minutes.
  2. Divide into 12 pieces and roll each into a small thin round.
  3. Brush half the rounds with sesame oil. Press an unoiled round onto each oiled one so you have six pairs.
  4. Roll each pair thinner. Cook on a dry pan over medium heat, or steam, until the surface looks dry and a few spots color. Do not brown them hard.
  5. Pull each pair apart into two pancakes while warm. Cover them until serving.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A dry griddle instead of steam
  • A few extra drops of sesame oil

Pairings

  • Peking duck
  • Sweet bean sauce
  • Scallion and cucumber batons
