Arriving in Bangkok

Khanom bueang

You come in by Boat. Thailand · Southeast Asia.

Pronounced kah-NOM boo-ANG

A crisp rice crepe folded around a sweet coconut-egg topping, lacy at the edge.

25 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 5 · 10 small crepes

History

Khanom bueang are Thai crepes, crisp and thin, sold as street sweets. A rice-flour batter is cooked into a delicate sheet and topped with a sweet coconut mixture. The familiar Bangkok version carries a yellow topping of egg yolk and coconut cream and a white sweet foam of egg white. Savory toppings, including shrimp, exist and would add shellfish. This page writes the sweet coconut and egg topping, with no shrimp, so it stays vegetarian. It is a crepe, not the dimpled khanom krok.

Cultural background

Vendors work a special flat pan and fold the crepe while it is still flexible at the center and crisp at the rim. The toppings are put on in a stripe, not spread to the edge. The cake is a snack between meals and a dessert, eaten with the fingers.

Why it tastes this way

The rice sheet fries crisp and tastes lightly of coconut milk. Egg yolk and coconut cream cook into a rich, sweet band. Sugar in the egg white topping is drier and fluffier, so one bite has crackle, cream, and grit. There is no chile. The flavor is sweet and eggy, with coconut as the fat instead of butter.

How it is made

Fry thin rice crepes and top them with a coconut-yolk paste and a sweet egg-white mixture, then fold.

Authentic touch

Cook the crepe until the rim is brittle before you add the topping, then fold at once. A soft wheat-style pancake will not shatter the way khanom bueang should.

How it is used

Eat them the hour they are made. They lose their crisp if they are boxed and carried all day. They are a street dessert.

Field notes

  • Khanom bueang are crisp Thai crepes with a coconut topping, sweet or savory depending on the cook.
  • This recipe is the sweet egg-and-coconut version, without shrimp.

Ingredients

  • 1/2 cup rice flour, with no wheat added
  • 1 tablespoon tapioca starch
  • 3/4 cup coconut milk
  • A pinch of salt
  • 2 egg yolks
  • 3 tablespoons coconut cream
  • 2 tablespoons sugar, plus 3 tablespoons for the whites
  • 2 egg whites
  • Neutral oil

Steps

  1. Whisk rice flour, tapioca starch, coconut milk, and salt into a thin batter. Rest 15 minutes.
  2. Cook the yolks with coconut cream and 2 tablespoons of sugar over low heat, stirring, until thick. Cool.
  3. Whip the egg whites with 3 tablespoons of sugar until they hold a soft shape.
  4. Oil a pan and cook small thin crepes until the rims are crisp and pale gold.
  5. Stripe each crepe with yolk paste and a little of the sweet white. Fold and serve.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A savory shrimp topping, which adds shellfish and changes the dish
  • A little salt in the yolk paste

Pairings

  • Hot jasmine tea
  • A second crepe
  • Fresh coconut water
