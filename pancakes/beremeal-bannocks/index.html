Beremeal Bannocks

Pronounced BARE-meal

Malty, earthy barley bread cooked on a girdle.

15 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 8 · one round, 8 pieces

History

Bere is an old barley still grown in Orkney, used for bannocks and bannock-like breads. Beremeal bannocks are a documented Orcadian bread, distinct from a generic wheat scone. Bere meal tastes earthier than modern pearl barley flour. Buttermilk and soda are a common way to raise them. This home version uses beremeal if you can get it, with a note that ordinary barley flour is a milder substitute, not the same grain.

Cultural background

They are eaten with butter, cheese, or soup. Orkney’s short season is why a hardy barley mattered.

Why it tastes this way

Bere tastes malty and slightly bitter because it is a different barley from the pale flour in a box of biscuits. Orkney’s wind and short summer favored it. Buttermilk softens the bitterness and soda lifts a dough that has little gluten. The girdle browns the malt. If you use ordinary barley flour, say so. The taste will be milder.

How it is made

Beremeal, a little wheat flour, baking soda, and buttermilk are patted into a round and cooked on a griddle.

Authentic touch

Do not expect wheat elasticity. Pat the dough. Do not knead it like bread. Cook it through, because barley tastes raw when it is underdone.

How it is used

Butter or cheese, warm.

Field notes

  • Bere is a specific old barley associated with Orkney, not a brand name for any brown flour.
  • A little wheat in this home version means it is not barley-only.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup beremeal or barley flour
  • 1/2 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 2/3 cup buttermilk

Steps

  1. Mix the meals, soda, and salt. Add buttermilk to a soft dough.
  2. Pat into a round.
  3. Cook on a medium-low girdle, turning once, until cooked through.
  4. Cool briefly and break apart.
  5. Serve with butter.
  6. The crumb should not look wet in the center.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • All beremeal, without wheat, is more traditional and more fragile. This version uses a little wheat so it holds.
  • Ordinary barley flour is a substitute and should be described as barley, not as bere.

Pairings

  • butter
  • Orkney cheese
  • soup
