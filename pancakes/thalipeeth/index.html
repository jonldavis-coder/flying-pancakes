History

Thalipeeth is a Maharashtrian savory flatbread made from bhajani, a roasted multigrain flour that often includes wheat, sorghum, chickpea, and other grains. It is mixed with onion, cilantro, and spices and roasted on a tawa. Because bhajani usually contains wheat, this version is not gluten-free. It is a breakfast with yogurt or pickle.

Cultural background

A hole poked in the center helps it cook. It is thicker than a cheela and more breadlike. House flours vary. This recipe uses a workable mix you can assemble.

Why it tastes this way

Roasted flours taste nutty, which is why thalipeeth is more aromatic than a single-flour pancake. Onion and cilantro keep it savory. The multigrain mix is the Maharashtrian pantry, a way to use several local grains in one bread. Yogurt on the side is cool and tangy. Wheat in the mix is why it holds together and why it is not a dosa.

How it is made

A soft dough of mixed flours, onion, and spice is patted into rounds and cooked on a tawa with oil.

Authentic touch

Pat the dough onto a wet cloth or parchment if it sticks to your hands, then invert it onto the tawa. Poke a hole in the middle and add a few drops of oil there.

How it is used

With yogurt or pickle. Yogurt adds dairy. The bread is vegan.

Field notes

  • Thalipeeth is a multigrain Maharashtrian bread, not a crepe.
  • The center hole and the oil are how cooks help it roast through.

Ingredients

  • 1/2 cup whole wheat flour
  • 1/4 cup besan
  • 1/4 cup sorghum flour or additional wheat flour
  • 1 small onion, finely chopped
  • 2 tablespoons chopped cilantro
  • 1 green chile, minced
  • 1/2 teaspoon cumin
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • Water, to bind
  • Oil

Steps

  1. Mix the flours, onion, cilantro, chile, cumin, and salt. Add water to a soft dough.
  2. Pat four rounds, each with a small hole in the center.
  3. Cook on a medium oiled tawa, adding a few drops of oil in the hole and around the edge.
  4. Turn and cook until both sides are spotted and the center is done.
  5. Serve with yogurt or pickle.
  6. Keep the dough soft, not stiff.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Using only sorghum and besan can reduce wheat. Read the flours. This written mix includes wheat.
  • A store-bought bhajani flour is the traditional shortcut if you can get it.

Pairings

  • yogurt
  • pickle
