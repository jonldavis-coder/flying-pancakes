Thalipeeth

You come in by Train. India · South Asia.

Pronounced TAH-lee-peeth

A hearty multigrain griddle bread, onion-flecked, with toasted millet and chickpea in the crumb.

25 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 4 · 6 flatbreads

History

Thalipeeth is a Maharashtrian griddle bread made from bhajani, a mix of roasted flours. Sorghum, millet, rice, chickpea, and wheat are a typical blend, though every household flour tin varies. Onion and cilantro are patted into the dough. It is a flatbread, not a poured dosa. This recipe includes wheat, so it is not gluten-free. Pune snacks and home breakfasts both rely on it.

Cultural background

Bhajani was a way to use several local grains in one dough. Roasting the flours before they are stored gives thalipeeth its toasted smell even before the pan. A hole poked in the center is a common cook's habit so the middle griddles through.

Why it tastes this way

Roasted sorghum and millet taste nutty and slightly sweet. Besan adds chickpea. Wheat makes the dough easier to pat. Onion and green chile steam inside the bread and keep the grains from tasting dry. Oil on the griddle, rather than a yeasted crumb, is the richness. There is no ferment tang.

How it is made

Mix a multigrain dough with onion and spices, pat it into thick rounds, and griddle both sides with oil.

Authentic touch

Pat the dough on a wet leaf, plastic sheet, or damp cloth. It cracks if you treat it like a smooth wheat chapati. Oil the cracks and keep going.

How it is used

Serve warm with a dry garlic chutney or a spoon of yogurt. Yogurt is a dairy side. The bread itself is cooked here in oil.

Field notes

  • Thalipeeth is a patted flatbread, not a ladle-poured pancake.
  • This blend includes wheat flour, so the bread is not gluten-free.

Ingredients

  • 1/2 cup sorghum flour
  • 1/4 cup rice flour
  • 1/4 cup besan
  • 1/2 cup whole wheat flour
  • 1 small onion, finely chopped
  • 1 green chile, minced
  • 2 tablespoons chopped cilantro
  • 1/2 teaspoon cumin seeds
  • 3/4 teaspoon salt
  • Warm water and neutral oil

Steps

  1. Stir the flours, salt, and cumin together. Add onion, chile, and cilantro.
  2. Add warm water a little at a time and mix to a stiff dough that can be patted. Rest 10 minutes.
  3. With wet hands, pat a ball into a thick round on a damp sheet. Poke a small hole in the center if you like.
  4. Transfer it to a hot oiled griddle. Cook on medium, oiling the surface, until both sides are spotted brown and the center is cooked.
  5. Repeat with the remaining dough. Serve warm.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Ghee in place of oil, which adds dairy
  • A little grated cucumber if the dough feels stiff

Pairings

  • Dry garlic chutney
  • Plain yogurt as a side
  • Sliced red onion
