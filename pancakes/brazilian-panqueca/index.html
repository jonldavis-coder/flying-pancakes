Brazilian Panqueca

Savory crepes rolled around a meat filling and baked with sauce.

30 min prep · 30 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 rolls

History

Panqueca in Brazil often means a savory crepe rolled around ground beef or chicken in a tomato sauce, then baked. It is weeknight family food, closer to a cannelloni than to a dessert crepe. This recipe uses a shredded chicken filling and says so. A cheese-and-spinach filling is the meatless variation.

Cultural background

The crepe is thin. The sauce is a simple tomato sauce. It is a baked dish, a supper.

Why it tastes this way

A mild crepe and a savory filling make a meal that can be assembled ahead, which is why it became weeknight food. Tomato sauce keeps it moist in the oven. The technique is the filled crepe of southern Europe, adapted in Brazilian home cooking with local cheese and a simple sauce. It is not a tapioca crepe.

How it is made

Thin crepes are filled with seasoned shredded chicken, rolled, and baked with tomato sauce and cheese.

Authentic touch

Do not overfill. A thin line of filling rolls. Bake covered for part of the time so the crepes do not dry.

How it is used

Hot, as supper.

Field notes

  • Brazilian panqueca often means a savory rolled crepe.
  • This filling is chicken. A spinach filling is the meatless variation.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup flour
  • 2 eggs
  • 1 1/4 cups milk
  • Pinch of salt
  • 1 tablespoon oil
  • 2 cups shredded cooked chicken
  • 1 cup tomato sauce
  • 1/2 cup grated cheese
  • Salt and pepper

Steps

  1. Cook thin crepes.
  2. Season the chicken and roll a little into each crepe.
  3. Lay the rolls in a dish, cover with tomato sauce and cheese.
  4. Bake at 375°F for 15 minutes.
  5. Serve hot.
  6. Do not overfill.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A spinach and cheese filling is meatless and still has dairy.
  • Tapioca crepes are the cassava breakfast.

Pairings

  • tomato sauce
  • rice
