Arriving in São Paulo

Brazilian panquecas

You come in by Plane. Brazil · South America.

Pronounced pahn-KEH-kahs

Thin wheat crepes rolled around seasoned ground beef and a simple tomato sauce.

20 min prep · 30 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 filled crepes

History

Panqueca in Brazil is a thin wheat crepe, a home and restaurant dish rather than a street tapioca. The usual savory version is rolled around ground beef and often finished with tomato sauce and cheese. This recipe uses ground beef, so it contains meat and is not vegetarian. The batter is wheat, egg, and milk, closer to a European crepe than to anything made from corn or cassava. São Paulo kitchens turn them out for lunch and dinner.

Cultural background

A pan of panquecas is family food: the crepes can be made ahead, filled, and warmed. The beef filling is seasoned with onion, garlic, tomato, and cumin, not with a sweet dessert sauce. Cheese on top is optional and is not required here because the batter already has dairy.

Why it tastes this way

The crepe tastes of milk and browned flour, flexible enough to roll. The beef is savory from onion and garlic, with tomato for acid and cumin for a warm note. There is no chile in the written filling, so the heat stays at none. Salt and black pepper do the rest. The contrast is a mild wheat pancake and a meat filling that has been cooked down until it is not watery.

How it is made

Make a thin wheat crepe batter and cook it in a lightly oiled pan. Simmer ground beef with onion, garlic, and tomato, season it, and roll it inside the crepes.

Authentic touch

Rest the batter so the flour hydrates, and cook the crepes thin. A thick pancake will not roll. Cook the beef until it is browned and cooked through, and let the tomato reduce so the filling does not soak the crepe into mush.

How it is used

Serve two or three rolls for lunch, with the tomato left in the filling rather than a heavy blanket of sauce. A salad on the side is enough.

Field notes

  • This panqueca is a wheat crepe with ground beef. It is not gluten-free and not vegetarian.
  • Tapioca crepes are a different Brazilian page, made from cassava starch.
  • Reduce the tomato so the filling stays thick enough to roll.

Ingredients

  • 120 g wheat flour
  • 1 egg
  • 250 ml milk
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt, plus more for the filling
  • 1 tablespoon oil, plus more for the pan
  • 250 g ground beef
  • 1 small onion, chopped
  • 1 garlic clove, minced
  • 1 tomato, chopped
  • 1/2 teaspoon ground cumin
  • Black pepper

Steps

  1. Whisk the flour, egg, milk, 1/4 teaspoon salt, and 1 tablespoon oil into a thin batter. Rest it for 15 minutes.
  2. Fry the onion and garlic in a spoon of oil. Add the beef and cook, breaking it up, until it is browned and cooked through.
  3. Add the tomato, cumin, salt, and pepper. Simmer until the filling is thick, not watery.
  4. Film a skillet with oil and cook thin crepes, flipping once, until spotted. Stack them.
  5. Roll a spoon of beef in each crepe and serve warm.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Shredded chicken is another meat filling and is still not vegetarian.
  • A cheese and tomato filling, with the beef left out, can be vegetarian. It is a different recipe from this one.
  • A sweet panqueca with dulce de leche exists. This page is the savory beef one.

Pairings

  • White rice
  • A simple green salad
  • Hot sauce at the table, which would add heat
