Pronounced nah-LIS-nih-kih

Thin pancakes rolled around sweet cheese and baked.

30 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 rolls

History

Nalysnyky are Ukrainian filled crepes. A sweet cheese filling is a common home and feast version, and they also appear in the buttery foods of Masnytsia, the Ukrainian pre-Lenten week related to Maslenitsa. Savory fillings exist too. This page is the cheese nalysnyky, baked so the rolls set.

Cultural background

They are a dish for guests and for the pre-Lenten table, not only a Tuesday night. Farmer cheese and a little sugar are the filling. Butter on top browns in the oven.

Why it tastes this way

A thin egg pancake is a way to turn tvorog into a feast dish that can be made ahead and baked. Butter on the pre-Lenten table is the same logic as Russian blini: use the dairy before the fast. The cheese tastes gently sour. The pancake tastes of butter. Together they are richer than either one.

How it is made

Thin pancakes are filled with sweetened farmer cheese, rolled, and baked with butter.

Authentic touch

Spread a thin layer of filling. A fat roll bursts. Bake covered for part of the time so the pancakes do not dry into chips.

How it is used

Warm, with sour cream.

Field notes

  • Nalysnyky are filled. Deruny are potato cakes. They are not the same dish.
  • They belong to Masnytsia foods as well as to ordinary entertaining.

Ingredients

  • 3/4 cup flour
  • 1 large egg
  • 1 cup milk
  • Pinch of salt
  • 1 tablespoon oil
  • 1 cup farmer cheese
  • 1 egg yolk
  • 2 tablespoons sugar
  • 2 tablespoons butter

Steps

  1. Make thin pancakes from flour, egg, milk, salt, and oil.
  2. Mix cheese, yolk, and sugar.
  3. Fill and roll. Place in a buttered dish and dot with butter.
  4. Cover and bake at 375°F for 15 minutes, then uncover for 5.
  5. Serve with sour cream.
  6. Do not overfill.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A savory meat filling is another nalysnyky and adds meat.
  • Blintzes are the related Jewish rolled pancake, often fried rather than baked.

Pairings
