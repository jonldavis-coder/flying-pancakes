History

Beiju is a cassava-starch bread of Brazil, with Indigenous origins, cooked from moistened tapioca starch on a hot surface. It is plainer than the filled tapioca crepe of a city stall. Some beiju are thick. Some are thin. This is a thin griddled round of commercial tapioca starch.

Cultural background

It is a bread, eaten with butter, coconut, or nothing. The stall crepe is the dressed-up descendant.

Why it tastes this way

Cassava starch toasts into a neutral, slightly sweet sheet. Indigenous cooking in Brazil used the root as a staple bread. Commercial starch is the safe home ingredient. Butter or coconut on top is flavor. The bread itself is almost plain, which is the point of a staple.

How it is made

Moistened tapioca starch is cooked on a dry pan into a round.

Authentic touch

Scatter an even layer and let it knit. Do not add a filling until it has become a sheet. Keep the heat moderate so it does not scorch before it binds.

How it is used

Warm, plain or with coconut.

Field notes

  • Beiju is the plainer cassava-starch bread.
  • The filled street tapioca is the dressed-up relative.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups tapioca starch
  • About 3/4 cup water
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt

Steps

  1. Moisten the starch and salt until it clumps.
  2. Scatter thin rounds in a dry medium pan and let them knit.
  3. Flip once.
  4. Serve plain or with coconut.
  5. Do not brown them black.
  6. Use commercial tapioca starch.

Use commercial cassava flour or yuca sold ready to cook. Do not process bitter cassava at home from these pages.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • The tapioca crepe page is the filled street version.
  • A thicker beiju needs a mold and more starch.

Pairings

  • coconut
  • butter
