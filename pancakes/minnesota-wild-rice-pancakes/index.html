Arriving in Bemidji

Minnesota wild rice pancakes

You come in by Plane. United States · North America.

A wheat pancake with chewy, toasted grains of wild rice scattered through it.

15 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 12 pancakes

History

Minnesota wild rice pancakes stir cooked wild rice into a wheat batter and cook it like any other breakfast cake. Manoomin is central to Ojibwe food and history in this region, and this pancake is a Minnesota cafe and home recipe, not an Ojibwe bread. Cooked lake rice and cooked cultivated wild rice both work in the batter, and they are not the same harvest. Bemidji is the map pin.

Cultural background

Wild rice is a grass seed, not a leftover of the Lowcountry rice pot. It stays chewy when it is folded into batter. The wheat, buttermilk, and egg make this a standard risen pancake with a grain added.

Why it tastes this way

Wild rice tastes toasted and slightly tea-like, and the grains pop between the teeth because they do not dissolve. Buttermilk is the soft background. If the rice is blended smooth, that texture disappears and the cake tastes muddy.

How it is made

Griddled wheat batter with cooked wild rice folded in.

Authentic touch

Fold cooked grains in at the end. Do not call this an Ojibwe recipe. If you use lake rice, know that it is a different harvest from paddy-grown wild rice.

How it is used

Hot, with butter and maple syrup.

Field notes

  • Manoomin is central to Ojibwe life, and this wheat pancake is a separate Minnesota breakfast.
  • Lake-harvested wild rice and cultivated paddy rice are not the same crop.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup cooked wild rice, cooled
  • 1 1/2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • 2 teaspoons baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 1/2 cups buttermilk
  • 1 large egg
  • 2 tablespoons unsalted butter, melted, plus more for the griddle

Steps

  1. Fluff the cooked wild rice so the grains are separate.
  2. Whisk the flour, sugar, baking powder, baking soda, and salt.
  3. Stir in the buttermilk, egg, and melted butter until just combined. Fold in the rice.
  4. Butter a griddle over medium heat and pour 1/4 cup cakes.
  5. Cook until the underside is brown and the grains on top look hot, then flip and finish.
  6. Serve with butter and maple syrup.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Leftover cooked wild rice from a supper is the practical start.
  • A savory cake leaves out the sugar and is eaten with eggs.

Pairings

  • Butter
  • Maple syrup
  • A few extra warm grains of wild rice on top
