Arriving in Lithuania

Farmer cheese latkes

You come in by Train. Lithuania · Eastern Europe.

Pronounced farmer cheese LAHT-kuhs

The older Ashkenazi Hanukkah pancake, made of farmer cheese rather than potato.

15 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 cakes

History

These are cheese latkes, the older Ashkenazi Hanukkah dairy pancake. The customary teaching connects Hanukkah dairy foods with Judith, who served cheese to an enemy general. Potato latkes became common later, after potatoes spread in Eastern Europe in the 19th century. This page is written from a Lithuanian Jewish table and does not claim that only Lithuania fried cheese. The potato page is pinned to Poland. Cheese, egg, and flour make a cake that is vegetarian and not vegan, not dairy-free, and not gluten-free.

Cultural background

They are Hanukkah food and a breakfast cake when cheese is in the house. Sweet and lightly salty versions both exist. This one is slightly sweet, with sour cream or a little jam.

Why it tastes this way

Farmer cheese is tangy and rich. Sugar is restrained so it still tastes like cheese. Egg sets the center. Flour makes a thin crust in the butter. They brown slowly. A high flame dries them out and loses the creamy middle that distinguishes them from a potato latke.

How it is made

Farmer cheese is mixed with egg, sugar, salt, and flour, shaped into small cakes, and fried in butter.

Authentic touch

Use a dry farmer cheese or well-drained tvorog. This is a dairy Hanukkah dish, so do not serve it as the potato latke and do not call it gluten-free.

How it is used

Warm at a dairy Hanukkah meal, with sour cream or jam. They are vegetarian.

Field notes

  • Cheese pancakes are the older Ashkenazi Hanukkah custom.
  • The Judith story is the customary explanation for dairy at that holiday.
  • Potato latkes came after potatoes were common, and they have their own page.

Ingredients

  • 400 g farmer cheese or drained tvorog
  • 2 large eggs
  • 3 tbsp plain wheat flour
  • 1 tbsp sugar
  • 1/4 tsp fine salt
  • 30 g unsalted butter, for frying
  • Sour cream, for serving

Steps

  1. Mash the cheese with the eggs, flour, sugar, and salt until it holds a shape.
  2. Form eight small cakes.
  3. Fry in butter over medium heat until both sides are golden and the center is hot, not dry.
  4. Serve with sour cream or jam.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A savory cheese latke with salt and dill and no sugar
  • Potato latkes, the later Hanukkah cake

Pairings

  • Sour cream
  • Jam
  • A dusting of sugar
