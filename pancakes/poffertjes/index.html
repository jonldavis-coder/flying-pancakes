This stop is on Poffertjes to the north

Pronounced POF-er-tyuhs

Tiny, puffy buckwheat-and-wheat cakes with butter and powdered sugar.

15 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · about 40 small cakes

History

Poffertjes are small Dutch pancakes cooked in a special pan with round wells. They are fair and street food as well as home food, served with butter and powdered sugar. A portion of buckwheat flour is traditional, which makes them taste a little earthier than a plain flour puff. The pan is the tool. A takoyaki or æbleskiver pan can stand in.

Cultural background

They are dessert, not dinner. Vendors turn them with a fork so both sides brown into a sphere.

Why it tastes this way

The wells hold a wet, yeasted or chemically leavened batter so it puffs instead of spreading into a crêpe. Buckwheat is the older grain in many recipes and gives a faint mineral note under the powdered sugar. Butter is the finish because the cakes are small and dry out without it. The sweet snow of sugar is how the Dutch sell them.

How it is made

A batter with wheat and buckwheat is cooked in a dimpled pan and finished with butter and powdered sugar.

Authentic touch

Fill the wells only halfway. Turn the cakes when the bottoms set so they become round. A crowded well makes a mushroom shape that is still edible and less traditional.

How it is used

Immediately, with more butter and a heavy dust of powdered sugar.

Field notes

  • The dimpled pan is the defining tool. A flat skillet makes a different cake.
  • Buckwheat in the batter is traditional, even though the topping is pure sugar.

Ingredients

  • 1/2 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1/2 cup buckwheat flour
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 2 tablespoons sugar
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1 large egg
  • 1 cup milk
  • 2 tablespoons melted butter
  • Powdered sugar and butter, to serve

Steps

  1. Whisk the batter until smooth.
  2. Butter a poffertjes pan, an æbleskiver pan, or a takoyaki pan and heat it over medium.
  3. Fill the wells halfway.
  4. Turn the cakes with a skewer when the bottoms set, and cook until round and cooked through.
  5. Toss with butter.
  6. Dust with powdered sugar and serve at once.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • All wheat flour works if you cannot find buckwheat, and the taste is milder.
  • A yeast batter rested an hour is more traditional. Baking powder is the quicker home method written here.

Pairings

  • powdered sugar
  • butter
