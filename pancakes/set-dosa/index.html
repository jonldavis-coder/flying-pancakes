Arriving in Bengaluru, Karnataka

Set dosa

You come in by Train. India · South Asia.

Pronounced set DOH-sah

Thick, soft, and mildly sour, with a spongy face and a pale golden underside.

40 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 3 · 8 thick dosas, about 3 sets

History

Set dosa is a Karnataka breakfast dosa made from fermented rice and urad, cooked thick and served in a set, usually two or three cakes on one plate. The name comes from that serving, not from a new grain. A little flattened rice in the grind is a common way to keep the crumb soft. Hotel kitchens in Bengaluru and Mysuru plate it with a potato palya and chutney, and homes do the same with a smaller pan.

Cultural background

The batter is close to an idli batter, sometimes a touch looser. Cooks cover the pan so steam finishes the top. Leaving it uncovered and thin would turn it into an ordinary crisp dosa, which misses the set.

Why it tastes this way

A thicker pool of fermented batter keeps moisture in the center, so the sourness reads as soft and bready instead of as a toasted edge. Flattened rice lightens the crumb. Oil on the underside adds a little browning, but the cake is not chased to a cracker. The mild ferment is the main flavor.

How it is made

Ferment a soft rice and urad batter, pour small thick rounds, cover them, and serve two or three together.

Authentic touch

Cook on medium-low and cover the pan. If you spread the batter thin, you have left set dosa behind.

How it is used

Bring the set to the table stacked, with potato palya and coconut chutney. They are eaten with the hands, torn, not cut like a cake.

Field notes

  • A set is the portion: two or three thick dosas, not a different cereal.
  • Covering the pan is what keeps the center spongy.

Ingredients

  • 3/4 cup idli rice
  • 1/4 cup skinned urad dal
  • 2 tablespoons flattened rice (poha)
  • 1/8 teaspoon fenugreek seeds
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • Water for soaking and grinding
  • Neutral oil for the pan

Steps

  1. Rinse and soak the rice in one bowl. Soak the urad, fenugreek, and flattened rice in another for 4 to 5 hours.
  2. Grind the urad mixture to a fluffy paste and the rice to a fine batter. Combine with salt. The batter should be thick, like a soft idli batter.
  3. Ferment covered until risen and mildly sour, often 8 to 12 hours. Do not count the ferment as active prep. Discard it if it spoils.
  4. Stir gently. Heat a pan on medium-low, grease it, and pour a small ladle without spreading it to the rim. The round should stay about a finger thick.
  5. Cover and cook until the top is set and the underside is pale gold. Repeat. Serve three cakes as one set.

Discard fermented batter that smells rotten or shows mold.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A pinch of sugar in the batter, common in some Karnataka homes
  • Ghee on the griddle, which adds dairy

Pairings

  • Potato palya
  • Coconut chutney
  • A thin vegetable sagu
