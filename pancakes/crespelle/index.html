Arriving in Florence

Crespelle

You come in by Train. Italy · Southern Europe.

Pronounced cres-PEL-leh

Italian crepes, plain or baked. Crespelle alla fiorentina is one variation, not the only form.

15 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 crepes

History

Crespelle are Italian crepes, thin egg pancakes used both sweet and savory. Crespelle alla fiorentina, filled with spinach and ricotta and baked in béchamel, are a famous Florentine way to serve them. They are one variation, not the only form. This page cooks the plain crepe and writes a simple butter and cheese finish, with the Florentine bake described as a variation you can build. The pin is Florence because of that famous dish, and the crepe itself is cooked across Italy.

Cultural background

Crespelle stand in for pasta in baked dishes and also appear with sugar as a dessert. The batter is the same idea as other European crepes. The Italian part is how they are filled and sauced.

Why it tastes this way

A thin crepe tastes of milk, egg, and browned butter. Parmesan on a hot crepe melts and salts it. That is the simple plate. The Florentine version adds the green bitterness of spinach, the mildness of ricotta, and the nutmeg of béchamel. Both are crespelle. The plain one should still be good with nothing but butter.

How it is made

A thin batter is cooked as crepes and served with butter and grated cheese, or finished as the Florentine variation.

Authentic touch

Cook the plain crepe first and treat alla fiorentina as a variation, not as the definition. If you bake the spinach version, use a béchamel and ricotta, and do not claim it is the only Italian crepe.

How it is used

Hot, as a savory course with cheese, or as the base for a baked filling. Sugar turns the same crepe into a dessert.

Field notes

  • Crespelle alla fiorentina are one baked form, not the only crespella.
  • The same thin crepe is used sweet and savory.
  • This recipe serves the plain crepe with butter and cheese.

Ingredients

  • 125 g plain wheat flour
  • 2 large eggs
  • 300 ml whole milk
  • 1 pinch fine salt
  • 30 g unsalted butter, plus more for the pan
  • 40 g grated Parmesan

Steps

  1. Whisk the flour, eggs, milk, and salt into a thin batter. Rest 15 minutes.
  2. Cook eight thin crepes in a lightly buttered pan.
  3. Brush each with butter and sprinkle with Parmesan while hot.
  4. Fold and serve. For the Florentine variation, fill the crepes with seasoned spinach and ricotta, cover with béchamel, and bake until bubbling.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Crespelle alla fiorentina: fill with cooked spinach and ricotta, cover with béchamel and cheese, and bake
  • A sweet crepe with jam

Pairings

  • Butter and grated Parmesan
  • A spinach and ricotta filling, the Florentine variation
  • Sugar and lemon, for a dessert crepe
