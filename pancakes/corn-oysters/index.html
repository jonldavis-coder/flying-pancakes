Arriving in Urbana

Corn oysters

You come in by Train. United States · North America.

Sweet corn, egg, and pepper, fried in small irregular cakes.

20 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · About 16 fritters

History

Corn oysters are small fried cakes of grated sweet corn, egg, and a little flour. American cookbooks knew them in the nineteenth century. The name comes from their shape, which reminded cooks of fried oysters, and the fritter does not contain shellfish. Urbana, in the Illinois sweet-corn country, is the map pin.

Cultural background

Fresh summer corn is the point. Grating or chopping the kernels releases starch that helps the fritter bind. Canned creamed corn is a duller substitute and is not what the old recipes meant.

Why it tastes this way

Corn juice caramelizes against the pan and tastes sweeter than boiled corn. Egg sets the cake. A small amount of flour keeps it together without making it bready. Black pepper is the seasoning. They taste of corn, not of oyster.

How it is made

Small spoonfuls of grated corn fried in butter.

Authentic touch

Fry spoonfuls, not large pancakes. The irregular oval shape is why they were called oysters. Do not add shellfish.

How it is used

Hot, as a side dish beside ham or tomatoes, or as a snack.

Field notes

  • The name comes from the shape, which looked like a fried oyster to nineteenth-century cooks.
  • There is no shellfish in the fritter.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups fresh corn kernels, from about 4 ears
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1/4 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1/4 teaspoon black pepper
  • 2 tablespoons unsalted butter

Steps

  1. Grate the corn on the large holes of a box grater, or chop it finely, and catch the juice.
  2. Stir in the eggs, flour, salt, and pepper. The batter should be thick but spoonable. If it is watery, add another teaspoon of flour.
  3. Melt the butter in a skillet over medium heat.
  4. Drop heaping tablespoons into the pan, leaving space between them.
  5. Fry until the underside is brown and the fritter holds together, then flip and cook until set, about 2 minutes per side.
  6. Serve hot. They soften as they cool.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Oil in place of butter makes them dairy-free, if the rest of the recipe stays the same.
  • A sliced scallion makes them more savory.

Pairings

  • Sliced tomatoes
  • Salt
  • Ham, if you eat meat
