Coconut rice cakes, sweet and cardamom-scented, crisp outside.

History

Vitumbua are Swahili coast rice-and-coconut cakes, cooked in a special pan with round wells, related in shape to appam pans and to æbleskiver pans. They are a Zanzibar and coastal Tanzanian breakfast and snack. Rice, coconut milk, sugar, and cardamom are the flavor. Yeast lifts them.

Cultural background

The coconut is the coast. Cardamom is the Indian Ocean trade. They are sweet, not savory, and they are eaten with tea.

Why it tastes this way

Coconut milk makes the crumb rich without dairy, and rice keeps it soft. Cardamom is a traded spice that became local in coastal sweets. The well pan fries the sides so the cake is crisp outside and tender inside, a shape that suits street selling. Sugar is moderate. The coconut is the sweetness people remember.

How it is made

A yeasted batter of rice flour and coconut milk is cooked in round wells.

Authentic touch

Use full-fat coconut milk. Light coconut milk makes them dry. Turn the cakes in the wells if your pan is deep, so both ends brown. An æbleskiver or appe pan is a fair tool.

How it is used

Warm, with tea. They are already sweet.

Field notes

  • Vitumbua are a Swahili coast coconut rice cake.
  • Cardamom points at Indian Ocean trade, not at a random spice.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup rice flour
  • 1 cup coconut milk
  • 2 tablespoons sugar
  • 1/4 teaspoon instant yeast
  • 1/2 teaspoon cardamom
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • Oil for the pan

Steps

  1. Stir the batter and rest 1 hour until slightly risen.
  2. Oil an æbleskiver, appe, or similar well pan over medium.
  3. Cook the batter in the wells, turning, until golden and set.
  4. Serve warm.
  5. Do not use low-fat coconut milk if you can help it.
  6. They are best the day they are cooked.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A little mashed banana in the batter is a home variation.
  • Plain rice flour pancakes in a flat pan are not quite vitumbua. The wells matter.
