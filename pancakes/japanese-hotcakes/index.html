Thick, fluffy buttermilk-style pancakes, a kissaten breakfast.

10 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 3 · about 6 thick pancakes

History

Hotcakes, hotto keki, are Japan’s thick diner pancakes, served in kissaten coffee shops with butter and syrup. They are an American-style pancake adapted in twentieth-century cafés, distinct from the later soufflé trend and from okonomiyaki. A little baking powder and a thick batter make the height.

Cultural background

They are a sweet breakfast or a café snack. Syrup and butter are the usual. Whipped cream is a dessert turn.

Why it tastes this way

A thick batter and a covered pan steam the center so the cake rises tall and tastes mild and milky. The style came with Western-style cafés and stayed because it photographs and eats like a treat. It is not a peasant grain cake. It is a café food, and the mild sweetness is the point.

How it is made

A thick milk batter is cooked covered in deep rounds.

Authentic touch

Cook covered on low heat. Flip once. A high griddle burns the outside of a thick cake and leaves a raw ring.

How it is used

With butter and syrup.

Field notes

  • Japanese hotcakes are the thick café pancakes, older in Japan than the soufflé fad.
  • Low heat and a cover are how the center cooks.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup flour
  • 2 teaspoons baking powder
  • 2 tablespoons sugar
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1 egg
  • 3/4 cup milk
  • 2 tablespoons melted butter

Steps

  1. Stir into a thick batter. Do not overmix.
  2. Cook on a buttered medium-low griddle, covered, in deep rounds.
  3. Flip when the tops are full of bubbles and the edges look set.
  4. Cook covered until the center springs back.
  5. Serve with butter and syrup.
  6. They should be tall, not crisp crackers.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Soufflé pancakes are the whipped-white café cousin.
  • A banana on top is a kissaten habit.

Pairings

  • syrup
