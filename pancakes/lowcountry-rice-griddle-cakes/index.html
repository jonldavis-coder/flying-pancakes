Arriving in Charleston

Lowcountry rice griddle cakes

You come in by Train. United States · North America.

Soft, lightly sweet rice cakes with a crisp buttered edge and separate grains in the crumb.

15 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 10 griddle cakes

History

Lowcountry rice griddle cakes use the long-grain rice of the South Carolina and Georgia coast. Enslaved West Africans brought rice-growing knowledge, and planters built a brutal rice economy on their labor and on the labor of their descendants. The cake is a later griddle use of that grain. It is not a rename of one village dish.

Cultural background

Charleston and the surrounding Lowcountry were the center of that rice system. Carolina-grown long-grain rice is the grain to use when you can get it. Any cooked long-grain white rice will hold the cake.

Why it tastes this way

Cooked rice stays in distinct grains, so the cake tastes steamy and mild instead of like a smooth wheat crumb. Rice flour binds the cakes without gluten. Butter browns the outside and keeps the rice from tasting flat.

How it is made

Cooked rice and rice flour griddled in butter.

Authentic touch

Use leftover cooked rice, not raw rice thrown into a wheat pancake. Name the labor when you tell the story of the grain.

How it is used

Warm, with butter. A little sorghum or cane syrup fits the coast.

Field notes

  • Rice in the Lowcountry is tied to West African knowledge and to enslaved labor, not to a single exported village recipe.
  • Leftover cooked rice is the usual base, which is why these cakes are a breakfast after a rice supper.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups cooked long-grain white rice, cooled
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1/2 cup milk
  • 1/3 cup rice flour
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 2 tablespoons unsalted butter, melted, plus more for the griddle

Steps

  1. Mash the rice with a fork so some grains break and some stay whole.
  2. Stir in the eggs, milk, rice flour, baking powder, sugar, salt, and melted butter.
  3. Heat a buttered griddle over medium heat.
  4. Drop 1/4 cup mounds and flatten them slightly. They will not look like smooth wheat batter.
  5. Cook until the underside is golden and the cake holds together, then flip and finish.
  6. Serve warm with butter.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A savory version leaves out the sugar and adds a sliced scallion.
  • Wheat flour in place of rice flour means the cakes are no longer gluten-free.

Pairings

  • Butter
  • Cane syrup
  • Shrimp gravy, for a savory plate, if you eat shellfish
