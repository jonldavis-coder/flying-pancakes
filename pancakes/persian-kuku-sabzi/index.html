Arriving in Tehran

Kuku sabzi

You come in by Plane. Iran · Middle East.

Pronounced koo-KOO sab-zee

A dense herb frittata, bright green inside, with walnut and a sour note from barberries.

25 min prep · 35 min cook · Serves 6 · 1 round cake, about 8 squares

History

Kuku sabzi is an Iranian herb and egg cake served at Nowruz, the new year, and at other tables through the year. It is a frittata, not a pancake. Eggs bind a large quantity of parsley, cilantro, and dill, with a spoon of flour for structure, and the cake is cooked slowly until the center sets. Walnuts and dried barberries, zereshk, are the usual scatter inside. It is not one of the symbolic haft-sin items. It is a dish on the Nowruz table.

Cultural background

The herbs are the body of the dish. A kuku that is mostly egg and a pinch of green is a different, thinner omelette. In Tehran and in other Iranian cities it is cut into squares for guests. The same cake appears at breakfast the next day, eaten cold or warm.

Why it tastes this way

Parsley, cilantro, and dill in this amount taste grassy, citrusy, and a little bitter, and egg makes that mass rich instead of like a salad. Turmeric colors the egg gold at the edges. Walnuts add fat and a tannic bite. Barberries are sour and small, so they pop against the herbs. Because it is a frittata, the texture is set and sliceable, not folded like a crepe.

How it is made

Chop a large bunch of herbs finely, squeeze them dry, and stir them into beaten eggs with turmeric, salt, a spoon of flour, chopped walnuts, and barberries. Cook the cake slowly in an oiled skillet, covered, until the center is set. Flip it or finish it under heat if the top is still wet.

Authentic touch

Call it a frittata, not a pancake. Use enough herbs that the batter looks green before it cooks. Walnuts mean this cake is not nut-free. If you cannot find barberries, leave them out rather than substituting a sweet dried fruit.

How it is used

Kuku sabzi is a Nowruz dish, a breakfast, and a savory plate with flatbread, yogurt, and herbs. The yogurt is extra dairy. The cake itself already has egg, wheat, and tree nut. It is feast food when the new year is the occasion, and ordinary food the rest of the year.

Field notes

  • Kuku sabzi is a frittata, an egg cake, not a pancake.
  • It is served at Nowruz, the Iranian new year, and it is not one of the haft-sin symbols.
  • Walnuts in this recipe are a tree nut, so the cake is not nut-free.

Ingredients

  • 4 eggs
  • 2 cups finely chopped parsley
  • 1 1/2 cups finely chopped cilantro
  • 1 cup finely chopped dill
  • 4 scallions, finely chopped
  • 1 tablespoon wheat flour
  • 1/2 teaspoon ground turmeric
  • 3/4 teaspoon fine salt
  • 1/3 cup chopped walnuts
  • 2 tablespoons dried barberries, rinsed
  • 2 tablespoons oil

Steps

  1. Chop the herbs finely and squeeze out excess moisture. Beat the eggs in a bowl.
  2. Stir the herbs, scallions, flour, turmeric, salt, walnuts, and barberries into the eggs. The mixture should look green, not pale. The walnuts are a tree nut.
  3. Heat the oil in a skillet over medium-low heat. Pour in the herb mixture and smooth the top. This is a frittata, not a pancake batter.
  4. Cover and cook slowly until the sides are set and the center no longer jiggles like liquid, about 20 minutes.
  5. Flip the cake onto a plate and slide it back to cook the second side for a few minutes, or set the top under a hot broiler. Cool slightly, then cut.

Cook the egg until the center is set. Walnuts are a tree nut. The spoon of flour is wheat. Barberries are sour berries, not a nut.

Refrigerate kuku the same day and eat it within a couple of days, cold or reheated. Do not leave the egg cake out. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Without walnuts the cake drops the tree-nut allergen. This written recipe includes them.
  • A pinch of fenugreek leaf is common and makes the aroma stronger.
  • Baking the pan in a moderate oven, after a start on the stove, sets the center evenly.

Pairings

  • Flatbread
  • Plain yogurt, which adds dairy
  • More fresh herbs
