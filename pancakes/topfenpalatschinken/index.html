Arriving in Vienna

Topfenpalatschinken

You come in by Train. Austria · Western Europe.

Pronounced TOP-fen pah-lah-CHIN-ken

Austrian crepes filled with sweetened quark and baked until the cheese is warm.

25 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 filled crepes

History

Topfenpalatschinken are thin Austrian pancakes filled with topfen, a fresh quark cheese, then rolled and warmed. Palatschinke belongs to the same name family as Hungarian palacsinta and Slavic palačinka. This page is the sweet cheese filling, which is an Austrian home and coffeehouse dessert. It is not Kaiserschmarrn, which is torn in the pan, and it is not a savory Hortobágy pancake.

Cultural background

Topfen is everyday cheese in Austrian cooking, used in dumplings and strudel as well as here. The crepes are thin. The filling is lemon, sugar, egg, and raisins. A dusting of powdered sugar finishes the plate.

Why it tastes this way

Quark is tangy and soft, not salty like a hard cheese. Sugar and lemon make it a dessert. The crepe tastes of butter and egg. Warming the rolls melts the filling without browning it into a crust, unless you bake them longer. Raisins add a dark chew. Vanilla is enough perfume.

How it is made

Thin crepes are filled with a mixture of quark, egg yolk, sugar, lemon, and raisins, rolled, and warmed in a buttered dish.

Authentic touch

Use quark, farmer cheese, or a dry ricotta if topfen is not sold where you are. Drain it so the filling does not weep. The crepe is a palatschinke, thin and flexible, not a thick stack.

How it is used

Warm, dusted with powdered sugar, as dessert.

Field notes

  • Topfen is quark, a fresh curd cheese.
  • Palatschinke is the Austrian word in the same family as palacsinta and palačinka.
  • The filling is sweet cheese, not the torn Kaiserschmarrn.

Ingredients

  • 125 g plain wheat flour
  • 2 large eggs, plus 1 yolk for the filling
  • 300 ml whole milk
  • 1 pinch fine salt
  • 30 g unsalted butter, for the pan and the dish
  • 300 g quark or farmer cheese, drained
  • 40 g sugar
  • 1 tsp lemon zest
  • 40 g raisins

Steps

  1. Whisk the flour, 2 eggs, milk, and salt. Rest 10 minutes and cook eight thin crepes in butter.
  2. Mix the quark, egg yolk, sugar, lemon zest, and raisins.
  3. Spread filling on each crepe, roll them, and lay them in a buttered dish.
  4. Warm in a 180°C oven for 10 minutes, until the filling is hot. Dust with extra sugar if you want.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Apricot jam instead of the cheese, a simpler palatschinke
  • A savory ham filling, which is a different meal and adds meat

Pairings

  • Powdered sugar
  • A spoon of plum jam
  • Coffee
