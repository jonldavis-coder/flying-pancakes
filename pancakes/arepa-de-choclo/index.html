Arriving in Bogotá

Arepa de choclo

You come in by Plane. Colombia · South America.

Pronounced ah-REH-pah deh CHOH-kloh

A thick sweet-corn cake with pockets of fresh cheese, griddled until the edges brown.

15 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 6 · 6 cakes

History

Choclo means fresh field corn. An arepa de choclo is a thick sweet-corn cake, often with cheese, and the batter is ground fresh kernels. It is not a dough of precooked dried cornmeal, and it is not nixtamalized masa. It belongs to Andean Colombian cooking, from Bogotá bakeries to home skillets when corn is in season. Corn cakes here are older than wheat hotcakes. This one simply keeps the corn in its fresh form. It is also not a Venezuelan cachapa, though both start from fresh kernels and both carry cheese.

Cultural background

Street and bakery versions in Bogotá are often larger and sweeter than a plain Antioquia arepa, with cheese pressed into the top or mixed through. People eat them at breakfast or in the middle of the afternoon. The fresh corn is the ingredient you cannot skip.

Why it tastes this way

Fresh corn is juicy and sweet, so the cake tastes like corn milk even before any sugar is added. A pinch of sugar only underlines what is already there. Cheese stays in soft salty pieces and keeps the cake from eating like dessert. Browning on the griddle adds a toasted edge. A spoon of fine untreated cornmeal, if the blend is watery, thickens it without turning it into masa.

How it is made

Pulse fresh corn with salt and a little sugar. Fold in crumbled white cheese and a spoon of fine cornmeal if the batter will not hold. Spoon thick cakes onto a buttered griddle and cook them through.

Authentic touch

Grind the kernels until thick and nubby, not into a juice. Fold in the cheese at the end. Cook over moderate heat so the center sets before the bottom scorches. If you only have precooked cornmeal, make a different arepa.

How it is used

Eat them warm, as breakfast or a snack, with more cheese or a pat of butter. They do not need a split or a stew.

Field notes

  • Choclo is fresh corn. This batter is not precooked arepa flour and not Mexican masa.
  • Cheese in the cake is what keeps an arepa de choclo from tasting only like a sweet pancake.
  • A smooth puree will run. Leave some of the kernel in pieces.

Ingredients

  • 3 cups fresh corn kernels
  • 100 g fresh white cheese, crumbled
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1 teaspoon sugar
  • 1 tablespoon fine cornmeal, not masa harina
  • Butter for the griddle

Steps

  1. Pulse the corn, salt, and sugar until thick and coarse. Stir in the cornmeal if the blend will not hold a shape.
  2. Fold in the crumbled cheese.
  3. Butter a griddle over medium heat.
  4. Spoon thick rounds and cook until the underside is browned and the top sets, then flip and cook through.
  5. Serve warm.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A Venezuelan cachapa is the other fresh-corn pancake in this journal, usually folded, and it is not called an arepa.
  • More sugar pushes the cake toward dessert. This one stays only lightly sweet.
  • Leave the cheese out and use oil instead of butter if you need a dairy-free cake. The corn still has to be fresh.

Pairings

  • Hot chocolate, a Bogotá breakfast drink
  • Butter
  • A mild coffee
