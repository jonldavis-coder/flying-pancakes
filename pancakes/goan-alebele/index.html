Pronounced ah-leh-BEH-leh

A thin coconut crepe with a fresh coconut filling.

20 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 8 · 8 rolls

History

Alebele, or alle belle, are Goan coconut crepes, a sweet with a clear Portuguese-Goan life: a thin pancake wrapped around a coconut filling. Goa’s long Portuguese period is why crepes sit in the local sweets beside coconut, which was already there. They are a home and bakery sweet.

Cultural background

The filling is fresh coconut, sugar, and cardamom. The crepe uses egg and coconut milk. They are rolled and eaten as a snack or dessert.

Why it tastes this way

Coconut milk in the batter and coconut in the filling make the sweet taste of the coast rather than of dairy cream. Cardamom is the perfume. The crepe technique is the European layer. The filling is the local one. That combination is the honest Goan story, without pretending the crepe was always a Konkan dosa.

How it is made

Egg crepes made with coconut milk are filled with sweetened coconut and rolled.

Authentic touch

Cook the filling until the sugar dissolves and the coconut looks moist, not wet. A thin crepe rolls. A thick one cracks around the coconut.

How it is used

Warm or at room temperature.

Field notes

  • Alebele are a Goan coconut crepe with a Portuguese-era crepe habit.
  • Coconut milk keeps the written batter free of dairy. It still contains egg and wheat.

Ingredients

  • 1/2 cup flour
  • 1 large egg
  • 3/4 cup coconut milk
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • Pinch of salt
  • 1 cup grated coconut
  • 1/4 cup sugar, for the filling
  • 1/4 teaspoon cardamom

Steps

  1. Cook grated coconut, 1/4 cup sugar, and cardamom over low heat until the sugar melts and the filling is fragrant. Cool.
  2. Whisk flour, egg, coconut milk, 1 tablespoon sugar, and salt.
  3. Cook thin crepes.
  4. Fill and roll.
  5. Serve warm.
  6. The filling should not be watery.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A chocolate filling is a modern bakery turn.
  • Patishapta is the Bengali coconut crepe, a different batter.

Pairings
