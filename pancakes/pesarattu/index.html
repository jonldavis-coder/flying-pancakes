Pesarattu

You come in by Train. India · South Asia.

Pronounced peh-sah-RAH-too

A green-gold moong dal pancake, herbal from ginger and green chile, with a soft crisp edge.

25 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 pesarattu

History

Pesarattu is the moong-dal pancake of Andhra cooking. Whole green gram is soaked and ground. Many kitchens cook it after that soak, with no long ferment, or after only a short rest. It is often eaten with ginger chutney, and a famous plate pairs it with upma. The batter is lentil, not the rice-and-urad ferment of a classic dosa, even though both are griddled breakfasts in South India.

Cultural background

Andhra tables treat pesarattu as its own order, especially when upma is tucked inside or served beside it. This recipe is the plain moong pancake. Upma is a pairing, and it is usually wheat semolina, so adding it would change the gluten-free tag.

Why it tastes this way

Green moong tastes grassy and slightly sweet, different from the sour rice dosa beside it on many menus. Ginger and green chile are ground in, so the heat is aromatic and mild. Because the batter is often unfermented, there is little tang. The griddle dries the surface and leaves a legume toast.

How it is made

Soak whole green moong, grind it with ginger and chile, and cook small pancakes on an oiled griddle without waiting for a long ferment.

Authentic touch

Grind the soaked moong until it is smooth but not watery. A batter that is too thin tears. A batter that is pasty never crisps at the rim.

How it is used

Serve with ginger chutney. If you add semolina upma, say so at the table, because that side contains wheat.

Field notes

  • Pesarattu is a moong pancake. It is not a rice-and-urad dosa renamed.
  • Many Andhra cooks grind it and cook it the same morning.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup whole green moong dal
  • 1 inch ginger
  • 1 or 2 green chiles
  • 1/2 teaspoon cumin seeds
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • Water for soaking and grinding
  • Neutral oil for the griddle

Steps

  1. Rinse the moong and soak it in water for 3 to 4 hours. Drain.
  2. Grind with ginger, chile, cumin, salt, and just enough fresh water to make a thick, pourable batter. Cook it the same day, or rest it up to an hour. No overnight ferment is required.
  3. Heat a griddle and grease it. Pour a ladle and spread it into a small round, thicker than a paper dosa.
  4. Oil the edges. Cook until the underside is spotted brown and the top looks set. Flip if you want both sides dry.
  5. Repeat with the rest of the batter, stirring between pancakes so the cumin does not sink.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A handful of chopped onion pressed on top
  • A short one-hour rest if you like a little tang

Pairings

  • Ginger chutney
  • Chopped onion
  • A rice upma made without wheat, if you want the stuffed plate and still need it gluten-free
