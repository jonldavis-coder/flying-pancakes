Arriving in Stowe

Vermont maple pancakes

You come in by Hot air balloon. United States · North America.

A mild wheat cake with real maple in the batter and a stronger maple pour on top.

15 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 12 pancakes

History

Vermont maple pancakes are wheat cakes served with maple syrup from the state's sugaring season. Indigenous nations of the Northeast, including the Abenaki, made maple sugar long before a buttermilk pancake existed. The stack on the plate is the later New England breakfast, with maple as the sweetener. Stowe is the map pin for a sugaring landscape that covers the state.

Cultural background

Maple syrup is graded by color and flavor, and a darker syrup tastes stronger in a batter. The pancake does not replace the older practice of making maple sugar.

Why it tastes this way

A spoon of syrup in the batter perfumes the crumb, but most of the maple taste has to come from syrup on the hot cake, where it stays fluid. Butter carries that flavor across the surface. Boiling the syrup into the batter would dull it.

How it is made

Griddled wheat cakes with maple syrup in the batter and on the plate.

Authentic touch

Use maple syrup, not a flavored table syrup. Darker syrup is easier to taste in the crumb.

How it is used

Hot, with butter and a generous pour of maple syrup.

Field notes

  • Maple sugaring in this region is older than the buttermilk pancake.
  • Darker maple syrup shows up in the batter more clearly than a very light syrup.

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 2 teaspoons baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 1/2 cups buttermilk
  • 2 tablespoons maple syrup, plus more to serve
  • 1 large egg
  • 2 tablespoons unsalted butter, melted, plus more for the griddle

Steps

  1. Whisk the flour, baking powder, baking soda, and salt.
  2. Whisk the buttermilk, maple syrup, egg, and melted butter. Stir into the flour until just combined.
  3. Butter a griddle over medium heat.
  4. Pour 1/4 cup cakes and cook until the bubbles set, then flip.
  5. Cook until the center springs back.
  6. Serve with butter and more maple syrup.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Skip the syrup in the batter and use it only as a topping.
  • A side of warm apples is a common Vermont plate and is not part of the batter.

Pairings

  • Maple syrup
  • Butter
  • Crisp bacon, if you eat pork
