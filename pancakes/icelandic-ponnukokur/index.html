Pronounced PUHN-noo-kuh-kur

Very thin pancakes, rolled with sugar or jam.

History

Pönnukökur are Iceland’s thin pancakes, cooked in a special shallow pan and rolled with sugar, jam, or whipped cream. They are a familiar home dessert. Skyr can appear alongside, though the cake itself is a wheat batter. They are thinner than American pancakes and close to other Nordic crêpes, with their own name and pan.

Cultural background

A stack of rolled pancakes disappears at birthdays and ordinary evenings. Cinnamon sugar is a common filling. Jam is the other.

Why it tastes this way

A thin batter in a hot pan tastes mostly of butter and of whatever you roll inside. Sugar and cinnamon were pantry flavors in a country that imported spices and grew little fruit. The cake had to be fast and filling. Rolling, rather than stacking with syrup, is the Icelandic way of eating them.

How it is made

A thin batter is cooked in a hot buttered pan, then rolled with sugar or jam.

Authentic touch

Use less batter than you think and tilt the pan at once. Icelandic pönnukökur should be thin enough to see a shadow through the edge.

How it is used

Rolled, warm, with sugar, jam, or cream.

Field notes

  • Pönnukökur are the thin ones. Lummur are thicker and oaty.
  • They are rolled, not served as a tall syrup stack.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup flour
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 1/2 cups milk
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1 tablespoon melted butter
  • Sugar or jam, to fill

Steps

  1. Whisk a thin batter.
  2. Cook in a hot buttered pan, swirling thin.
  3. Flip briefly.
  4. Add sugar or jam and roll.
  5. Serve warm.
  6. The edges should be spotted, not stiff.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Lummur are the thicker oatmeal pancakes of Iceland.
  • A spoon of cocoa in the sugar makes a chocolate roll and is a home variation.

Pairings

  • sugar
  • jam
  • whipped cream
