History

Lokše are Slovak potato flatbreads made from mashed potato and flour, cooked on a dry pan. They are traditional at goose feasts and harvest meals in some regions, filled with duck fat or poppy seed and sugar, or simply buttered. This recipe is the bread. The goose is a separate roast.

Cultural background

They should be flexible. A stack wrapped in a towel stays soft enough to fill. Poppy seed and sugar is a sweet filling. Savory fat is the feast filling.

Why it tastes this way

Mashed potato and flour, cooked dry, taste mild and slightly sweet, a wrap rather than a crisp latke. Goose feasts used the bread to catch fat and meat juices. Poppy seed, a central European sweet filling, is the other old partner. The dough is unleavened, so the flavor is potato, not yeast.

How it is made

Mashed potato and flour are rolled into rounds and cooked on a dry griddle.

Authentic touch

Roll thin and cook on a dry pan until spotted, not oily. If they crack, the dough needs a spoon more potato or a gentler roll.

How it is used

Buttered, or filled with poppy seed and sugar. Goose is the festive meal beside them, not part of the dough.

Field notes

  • Lokše are flatbreads, often served at goose dinners.
  • The bread itself is potato and flour.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups plain mashed potato
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 3/4 cup flour

Steps

  1. Mix potato, salt, and flour into a soft dough.
  2. Roll thin rounds.
  3. Cook on a dry medium griddle until spotted on both sides.
  4. Stack in a towel.
  5. Butter or fill.
  6. Serve warm.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A savory filling of cooked goose or duck is a feast use and adds meat.
  • They are closer to lefse than to bramboráky.

Pairings

  • butter
  • poppy seed
  • sugar
