Thin dessert crepes, often with manjar blanco.

15 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 crepes

History

Peruvian panqueques are thin crepes served with manjar blanco, the local dulce de leche. They are a dessert-case and home sweet, not a highland potato dish and not picarones, which are squash-and-sweet-potato fritters in syrup and a different recipe. The crepe is the page.

Cultural background

They are rolled. Manjar blanco is the filling. Fruit is a lighter finish.

Why it tastes this way

Manjar blanco is thick and milky, so the crepe is a mild wrapper, the same idea as in Chile and Argentina with a Peruvian name. The dessert belongs to café cooking. Picarones are the older-feeling street fritter and are not rewritten here as if they were crepes.

How it is made

Thin crepes are filled with manjar blanco.

Authentic touch

Warm the manjar blanco. Roll loosely. A tight roll squeezes the filling out.

How it is used

Warm, as dessert.

Field notes

  • Manjar blanco is the usual filling.
  • Picarones are a different fried sweet and are not rewritten here.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup flour
  • 2 eggs
  • 1 1/4 cups milk
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • Pinch of salt
  • 1 tablespoon melted butter
  • 3/4 cup manjar blanco

Steps

  1. Cook thin crepes.
  2. Warm the manjar blanco and spread a ribbon on each.
  3. Roll loosely.
  4. Serve warm.
  5. Do not overfill.
  6. Picarones are a different sweet and are not this recipe.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A passion-fruit sauce is a brighter, more tart finish.
  • Picarones are a different, fried street sweet.

Pairings

  • manjar blanco
