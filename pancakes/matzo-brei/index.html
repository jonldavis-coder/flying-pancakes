Matzo brei

You come in by Train. United States · North America.

Pronounced MAHT-suh bry

Soft scrambled egg bound around soaked matzo, salty and toasted in butter.

10 min prep · 8 min cook · Serves 2 · 2 skilletfuls

History

Matzo brei is matzo soaked, mixed with egg, and fried, a Passover breakfast in Ashkenazi homes and a year-round habit in many Jewish American kitchens. Matzo is unleavened wheat, so the dish is not gluten-free. Both a salted version and a cinnamon-sugar version are established. This written recipe is the salted one, pinned in Brooklyn.

Cultural background

Plain wheat matzo, broken and briefly soaked, is the base. Egg matzo is a different cracker. The dish is an egg skillet cake more than a poured pancake.

Why it tastes this way

A short soak softens the cracker without dissolving it, so you still taste shards of toasted matzo. Egg makes a soft scramble around those shards. Butter browns at the edge of the pan. Too much water turns it into mush, which is why the soak is brief.

How it is made

Soaked matzo scrambled with egg in butter.

Authentic touch

Soak the sheets just until they bend, then squeeze them. The sweet table version gets cinnamon sugar at the end. This batch is salted.

How it is used

Hot from the skillet, salted. A sweet serving gets sugar and cinnamon instead.

Field notes

  • Matzo brei is a Passover breakfast in many Ashkenazi homes and is also eaten the rest of the year.
  • Matzo is wheat, so the dish is not gluten-free.

Ingredients

  • 4 sheets plain wheat matzo
  • 4 large eggs
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 2 tablespoons unsalted butter
  • 1/4 teaspoon black pepper

Steps

  1. Break the matzo into small pieces and cover them with cold water for about 1 minute, until they bend. Drain and squeeze gently.
  2. Beat the eggs with the salt and black pepper. Stir in the matzo.
  3. Melt the butter in a skillet over medium heat.
  4. Add the egg mixture and cook, turning with a spatula, until the egg is just set and some edges are brown.
  5. Serve at once, salty, or follow the sweet variation if that is your table.
  6. Eat it hot. It toughens as it sits.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • For the sweet version, reduce the salt and dust the finished brei with sugar and cinnamon.
  • Some kitchens fry it as a single pancake instead of a scramble.

Pairings

  • Salt and black pepper
  • Applesauce
  • Cinnamon sugar, for the sweet version
