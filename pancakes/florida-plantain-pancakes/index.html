Arriving in Miami

Florida plantain pancakes

You come in by Plane. United States · North America.

Sweet ripe plantain, a little cinnamon, and a fried edge.

15 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 small cakes

History

These plantain pancakes are Cuban-American home cooking in Miami, where ripe plantains are mashed, bound with egg and a little flour, and fried in small cakes. The dish belongs to twentieth-century Cuban kitchens in the city. It is not a colonial-era recipe.

Cultural background

A plantain for this cake has a heavily spotted or black peel and tastes sweet. Green plantains are starchy and belong in savory fried chips, not in this batter. The small amount of wheat flour helps the cakes hold.

Why it tastes this way

Ripe plantain caramelizes in the pan and tastes like banana with a firmer, drier sweetness. Egg sets the mash. Cinnamon is a light perfume. Oil, not butter, is the frying fat in this recipe, so the edge tastes clean rather than milky.

How it is made

Mashed ripe plantain fried as small cakes in oil.

Authentic touch

Wait until the plantains are very ripe. A firm yellow plantain will taste starchy. Keep the cakes small so they fry through.

How it is used

Warm, as breakfast or a snack, with coffee.

Field notes

  • This is Cuban-American home cooking in Miami, not a colonial-era recipe.
  • The plantains need to be ripe, with black-spotted peels, or the cakes taste starchy.

Ingredients

  • 2 very ripe plantains
  • 2 large eggs
  • 3 tablespoons all-purpose flour
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • 1/4 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 3 tablespoons neutral oil

Steps

  1. Peel the plantains and mash them with a fork until mostly smooth. A few lumps are fine.
  2. Stir in the eggs, flour, sugar, cinnamon, and salt.
  3. Heat the oil in a skillet over medium heat.
  4. Drop about 1/4 cup of the mixture for each cake and flatten it slightly.
  5. Fry until the underside is brown, about 2 minutes, then flip and cook until the center is set.
  6. Drain briefly and serve warm.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Skip the cinnamon for a plainer cake.
  • More flour makes them cakier and less like plantain.

Pairings

  • Coffee
  • A squeeze of lime
  • Salt, for a less sweet bite
