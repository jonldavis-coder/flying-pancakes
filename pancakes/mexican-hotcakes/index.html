History

Hotcakes are a standard Mexican breakfast, the American-style pancake under a Spanish name, served in fondas and at home with butter, honey, cajeta, or fruit. They are not a pre-Hispanic cake. Corn tortillas are the older griddle bread. Hotcakes are the twentieth-century breakfast beside eggs and coffee.

Cultural background

Cajeta, goat’s-milk caramel, is a beloved pour and is dairy. Honey is the other. The batter is a simple baking-powder batter.

Why it tastes this way

The cake tastes like a mild wheat pancake because that is the café style that spread. Cajeta makes the plate Mexican: goat’s milk cooked with sugar until it is caramel, a colonial-era dairy sweet. Condensed milk is the humbler pour. The hotcake itself is not an ancient recipe, and the page does not pretend it is.

How it is made

A milk batter is cooked as pancakes and served with cajeta or honey.

Authentic touch

Cook them as ordinary pancakes. Warm the cajeta so it pours. Cold cajeta sits in a lump on a hot cake.

How it is used

With butter and cajeta, honey, or fruit.

Field notes

  • Hotcakes are a Mexican breakfast. They are not a pre-Hispanic corn cake.
  • Cajeta, goat’s-milk caramel, is a classic pour.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup flour
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1 cup milk
  • 1 egg
  • 2 tablespoons melted butter
  • Cajeta or honey, to serve

Steps

  1. Whisk a batter and rest 5 minutes.
  2. Cook on a buttered medium griddle until both sides are brown.
  3. Warm the cajeta.
  4. Serve with butter and cajeta or honey.
  5. Do not sweeten the batter like cake.
  6. Eat them hot.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Hotcakes de elote add corn.
  • Crepas de cajeta are the thin version.

Pairings

  • honey
  • butter
