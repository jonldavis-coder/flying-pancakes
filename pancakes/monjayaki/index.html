Arriving in Tsukishima, Tokyo

Monjayaki

You come in by Plane. Japan · East Asia.

Pronounced mon-jah-YAH-kee

A loose, runny savory batter cooked on a griddle and eaten while it is still half set.

15 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 2 · 1 shared pan

History

Monjayaki is Tokyo's runny savory pancake, especially the food of Tsukishima's monja street. The batter is much looser than Osaka okonomiyaki. Cabbage and other bits are fried first, then a wheat batter made with dashi is poured around them and cooked until it is partly set. People eat it from the griddle with small spatulas before it firms into a cake. This recipe uses bonito dashi, so it includes fish. It is a different dish from both Osaka and Hiroshima okonomiyaki.

Cultural background

The pleasure is the texture: a savory pool that stretches and then sets at the edges. Waiting for a firm pancake misses the point. Tsukishima shops expect you to cook it at the table. A home griddle or a wide pan is enough if you keep the batch small.

Why it tastes this way

Dashi is the flavor, a light fish stock that wheat and cabbage would not have on their own. The batter stays wet, so it tastes more like a savory custard than a browned pancake. Cabbage and a little onion sweetens it. The set edge is saltier and toastier. Eating it half-cooked on purpose is why the center tastes soft and marine rather than fried through.

How it is made

Fry cabbage, pour a very loose dashi batter over it, cook until the rim sets, and eat it from the pan half-set.

Authentic touch

Spread the batter thin and start eating from the firmed edge while the middle is still soft. If you flip it like a finished okonomiyaki, you have overcooked it.

How it is used

Serve it on the griddle with small spatulas. It is a shared savory snack, not a slice you pack for later.

Field notes

  • Monjayaki is meant to be eaten while the center is still soft.
  • Tsukishima in Tokyo is the neighborhood most tied to monja restaurants.

Ingredients

  • 1/2 cup all-purpose wheat flour
  • 1 cup bonito and kombu dashi
  • 1 egg
  • 200 grams finely chopped cabbage
  • 1/4 onion, sliced
  • 1 tablespoon neutral oil
  • A pinch of salt
  • Aonori, optional

Steps

  1. Whisk flour, dashi, egg, and salt into a thin batter, looser than crepe batter.
  2. Oil a griddle or wide pan on medium. Fry the cabbage and onion until they brighten.
  3. Push the vegetables into a ring. Pour the batter into the middle and across them.
  4. Cook without flipping until the edge looks set and the center is still soft and stretchy.
  5. Sprinkle aonori if you like and eat it from the pan with small spatulas, starting at the edge.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A few shrimp, which add shellfish
  • Cheese at the end, which adds dairy

Pairings

  • A cold barley tea
  • Pickled ginger
  • Aonori sprinkled at the table
