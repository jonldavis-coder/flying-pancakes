Bahamian johnnycake

You come in by Boat. Bahamas · Caribbean.

Pronounced JON-ee-kayk

A mildly sweet wheat cake, buttery, cooked covered in a skillet so the center steams.

15 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 8 · 1 skillet cake

History

Bahamian johnnycake is a wheat bread, mildly sweet, enriched with butter and milk. Baked versions, closer to a low loaf, are common in home ovens. This page is the pan version: one cake cooked in a covered skillet and turned once. It is not the thin white-flint-corn johnnycake of Rhode Island, and it is not Jamaican festival, which is a fried cornmeal log. The shared word hides three different doughs.

Cultural background

In Nassau and the Family Islands, johnnycake shows up at breakfast and beside stews. The baked loaf is what many people mean by the name. The skillet cake here is the same dough cooked on the stove when the oven is not the point.

Why it tastes this way

Sugar stays in the background, so the cake tastes of wheat and butter rather than of a doughnut. Milk softens the crumb. A lid traps steam and helps the center cook before the bottom scorches. The crust is browned only where it touches the pan. There is no cornmeal in this recipe, which is why it does not taste like a hoecake.

How it is made

Cut butter into flour, sugar, baking powder, and salt. Mix in milk, pat the dough into a buttered skillet, cover, and cook over low heat, turning once.

Authentic touch

Keep the heat low and the lid on. High heat burns the bottom while the center stays raw. If the cake is thick, give it time after the flip. Baked johnnycake uses the same dough in a tin, in an oven, until a skewer comes out clean.

How it is used

Slice it warm for breakfast or set it next to a stew. Say clearly, if you bake a loaf instead, that you have made the oven version of the same bread.

Field notes

  • This page is a skillet johnnycake. Baked loaves are also common in the Bahamas.
  • Rhode Island johnnycakes are a thin cornmeal cake. The Bahamian one is wheat.
  • The dough is slightly sweet and enriched, closer to a quick bread than to a fritter.

Ingredients

  • 250 g wheat flour
  • 2 tablespoons sugar
  • 2 teaspoons baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 40 g cold butter, plus more for the skillet
  • 120 ml milk

Steps

  1. Stir the flour, sugar, baking powder, and salt. Rub in the cold butter until the mixture is mealy.
  2. Stir in the milk until a soft dough forms. Do not knead it hard.
  3. Butter a skillet, pat the dough in, and cover the pan.
  4. Cook over low heat for about 12 minutes, until the bottom is browned and the top has set.
  5. Turn the cake, cover again, and cook until the center is done, about 10 minutes more. Slice and serve.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Bake the dough in a loaf tin if you want the oven johnnycake. The flavor is the same family.
  • Coconut milk can replace part of the dairy milk in some Family Island recipes.
  • A spoon of cornmeal is a different bread. This Nassau-style cake is wheat.

Pairings

  • Stewed fish, a plate that adds fish
  • Butter and a little guava jam
  • Coffee or cocoa
