Round filled cakes cooked in iron molds, usually with red bean paste.

History

Imagawayaki are round cakes cooked in hinged molds, filled with anko. They are festival and street food. Regional names include obanyaki and kaitenyaki. The batter is a sweet pancake batter. The mold, not a free griddle, makes the shape. Taiyaki, the fish-shaped cousin, uses a similar batter and a different iron.

Cultural background

They are eaten hot. The paste should be warm but not molten enough to burn. Shops flip the irons. At home, a takoyaki or æbleskiver style approach is a poor copy. A round muffin pan covered, or a dedicated iron, is more honest. This recipe uses a small covered skillet to make filled rounds and says the iron is the ideal.

Why it tastes this way

The mold fries both faces and seals the paste inside, so the cake tastes of browned honey batter and sweet bean. Street irons run hot and fast. Red bean is the expected filling because it is the standard wagashi paste. The many regional names are real. The cake is one family.

How it is made

Sweet batter and a spoon of red bean paste are cooked as sealed rounds.

Authentic touch

Put batter, then paste, then batter, so the paste is enclosed. If paste hits the pan it burns. Cook them through. A wet center is raw batter.

How it is used

Warm, in a paper if you like the stall feeling.

Field notes

  • Imagawayaki, obanyaki, and similar names are regional words for filled mold cakes.
  • A proper iron is ideal. This home method still encloses the paste.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup flour
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 2 tablespoons honey
  • 1 egg
  • 2/3 cup water
  • 1/2 cup smooth red bean paste
  • Oil

Steps

  1. Whisk flour, baking powder, honey, egg, and water into a thick batter.
  2. Oil a small skillet over medium-low. Pour a spoon of batter, add a spoon of bean paste, and cover with more batter.
  3. Cook covered until the bottom is brown, then flip and cook through.
  4. Repeat.
  5. The paste should be inside, not leaking.
  6. Serve warm.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A taiyaki iron makes the fish shape with the same idea.
  • Custard filling is a modern option.

Pairings

  • red bean paste
