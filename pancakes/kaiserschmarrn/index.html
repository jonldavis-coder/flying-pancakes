Kaiserschmarrn

You come in by Train. Austria · Western Europe.

Pronounced KYE-zer-shmarn

A torn, caramelized Austrian pancake with raisins and plum compote.

20 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 1 pan, for 4

History

Kaiserschmarrn is an Austrian pancake torn in the pan, caramelized in butter and sugar, and served with plum compote. The name means the emperor's shredded pancake. Tales that put the dish on one particular emperor's table are popular and not something this page can verify. What is stable is the method: a thick soufflé-like batter, raisins, a tear with two forks, and powdered sugar. It is eaten in Austria and also in Bavaria. The pin is Austria. The region in this journal is Western Europe.

Cultural background

It is a dessert and a mountain-hut dish, sweet enough to be the meal. Zwetschkenröster, a plum compote, is the classic side. The shreds should be uneven. A neat stack is the wrong shape.

Why it tastes this way

Separated eggs make it light, then the tear and the sugar make crisp caramel edges. Raisins, soaked so they are not hard, add dark sweetness. Butter is the cooking fat and the flavor. Plum compote is tart and deep. Powdered sugar is the last layer. If you do not tear it, you have baked a thick pancake and missed the dish.

How it is made

A batter of flour, milk, egg yolks, sugar, and rum-soaked raisins is lightened with whites, cooked in butter, torn, caramelized, and dusted with sugar.

Authentic touch

Tear the pancake in the pan while it cooks, using two forks, and dust with sugar so the new surfaces caramelize. Serve plum compote. Do not present it as a tidy stack.

How it is used

In a heap, with plum compote, as dessert. It is sweet enough to be the whole course.

Field notes

  • Schmarrn means the pancake is torn. The shreds are the point.
  • Stories about a specific emperor's plate are not verified here.
  • Plum compote is the usual side.

Ingredients

  • 40 g raisins
  • 2 tbsp rum, or hot water
  • 120 g plain wheat flour
  • 2 tbsp sugar, plus more for the pan
  • 1 pinch fine salt
  • 3 large eggs, separated
  • 250 ml whole milk
  • 40 g unsalted butter
  • Powdered sugar and plum compote, for serving

Steps

  1. Soak the raisins in the rum or hot water for 15 minutes. Whisk the flour, sugar, salt, yolks, and milk. Stir in the drained raisins.
  2. Beat the whites to soft peaks and fold them in.
  3. Melt the butter in a wide pan, pour in the batter, and cook over medium heat until the bottom is golden and the top is setting.
  4. Tear the pancake into pieces with two forks. Sprinkle with sugar, turn the pieces, and cook until the edges caramelize. Dust with powdered sugar and serve with plum compote.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Skip the rum and soak the raisins in hot water
  • A savory Schmarrn without sugar, which is a different dish

Pairings

  • Plum compote
  • Powdered sugar
  • A spoon of the raisin syrup
