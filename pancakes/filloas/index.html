Filloas

You come in by Plane. Spain · Southern Europe.

Pronounced fee-YOH-ahs

Thin Galician Carnival pancakes, scented with anise, rolled with sugar.

15 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 10 filloas

History

Filloas are the thin pancakes of Galicia, eaten at Entroido, the Galician Carnival. They are a Carnival food, not the French Chandeleur crepe and not the English Shrove pancake, though all three sit in the late-winter calendar. Some Galician recipes use pork blood. This page writes the milk, egg, and anise filloa, which is the home sweet pancake, and names the blood version only as a variation it does not cook. Frixuelos, from Asturias, are the neighboring Carnival pancake and a separate page.

Cultural background

Entroido is a public festival with its own pastries and costumes. Filloas are the home griddle part of that season. Anise is the scent many sweet filloas carry. Sugar is the filling.

Why it tastes this way

They taste like a thin crepe with a perfumed anise note, browned in butter. Sugar on the hot cake melts in patches. The anise should be a scent, not a mouthful of seeds. Milk and egg make the sheet tender. A blood filloa would taste minerally and savory. That is a different cake.

How it is made

A thin batter of flour, egg, milk, sugar, and a pinch of anise is cooked as crepes and rolled with sugar.

Authentic touch

Make them for Carnival and call the season Entroido. Keep this recipe as the sweet milk filloa. Do not confuse them with Asturian frixuelos, which have their own page.

How it is used

Rolled with sugar, during Carnival, as a sweet plate.

Field notes

  • Filloas belong to Galician Entroido, Carnival.
  • This recipe is the sweet milk filloa. Blood filloas exist and are not written here.
  • Asturian frixuelos are a related Carnival pancake with a separate page.

Ingredients

  • 150 g plain wheat flour
  • 1 tbsp sugar, plus more for serving
  • 1 pinch fine salt
  • 1/4 tsp ground anise
  • 2 large eggs
  • 350 ml whole milk
  • 30 g unsalted butter, for the pan

Steps

  1. Whisk the flour, sugar, salt, anise, eggs, and milk into a thin batter. Rest 15 minutes.
  2. Cook thin pancakes in a buttered pan until spotted on both sides.
  3. Sprinkle with sugar.
  4. Roll and serve during Carnival, or whenever you want the same cake.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Filloas de sangue, made with pork blood, a different and non-vegetarian recipe this page does not write
  • Frixuelos, the Asturian Carnival pancake

Pairings

  • Sugar
  • Honey, which adds a honey allergen
  • A lemon squeeze
