Pronounced pah-tee-SHAP-tah

A thin rice-and-wheat crepe filled with coconut and jaggery.

20 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 8 · 8 rolls

History

Patishapta is a Bengali crepe of the winter harvest festivals, filled with grated coconut cooked with jaggery or with a milk-kheer filling. It is made in both West Bengal and Bangladesh. This page uses Bangladesh as the home and lists India as well. The coconut-jaggery filling is the one written, a winter sweet.

Cultural background

The crepe is thin and pale. The filling is moist but not runny. They are eaten as a seasonal sweet, not as a savory breakfast.

Why it tastes this way

New rice, coconut, and date or cane jaggery are winter flavors in Bengal, which is why the filling tastes caramelized and not like white sugar. The crepe uses rice flour and a little wheat so it bends around the filling. Coconut fat keeps the filling rich without nuts of the tree-nut type. The festival calendar is the reason to cook a stack.

How it is made

Thin rice-and-wheat crepes are filled with coconut cooked in jaggery and rolled.

Authentic touch

Cook the coconut and jaggery until the mixture holds together and is no longer watery. A wet filling bursts the crepe. Spread it thin and roll while the crepe is flexible.

How it is used

Warm or at room temperature, as a sweet.

Field notes

  • Patishapta is a festive Bengali filled crepe.
  • Coconut and jaggery are the filling written here.

Ingredients

  • 1/2 cup rice flour
  • 1/4 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1 cup milk
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • Pinch of salt
  • 1 cup grated coconut
  • 1/3 cup jaggery, chopped
  • 2 tablespoons milk, for the filling

Steps

  1. Cook coconut, jaggery, and 2 tablespoons milk until the filling is thick. Cool slightly.
  2. Whisk the flours, 1 cup milk, sugar, and salt into a thin batter.
  3. Cook thin crepes in a lightly oiled pan.
  4. Spread filling and roll.
  5. Serve warm.
  6. The crepe should bend, not crack.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A kheer filling of thickened milk is the other classic and is richer in dairy.
  • Chitoi pitha is the holey rice cake, not this crepe.

Pairings
