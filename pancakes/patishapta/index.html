Patishapta

You come in by Train. India · South Asia.

Pronounced pah-tee-SHAHP-tah

A soft rice-and-wheat crepe rolled around a coconut and jaggery filling, fragrant with cardamom.

30 min prep · 30 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 filled crepes

History

Patishapta is a Bengali winter crepe filled with coconut or with kheer, cooked for Poush Sankranti, the harvest festival that falls in mid-January. West Bengal and Bangladesh share the pitha season. This page places the cake in Kolkata and lists Bangladesh beside it. The crepe often uses rice flour with some wheat flour so it bends without cracking. The written filling is coconut and jaggery, which keeps the recipe free of dairy. A milk kheer filling is the other classic and would add dairy.

Cultural background

Poush Sankranti tables fill with pithas. Patishapta is the rolled one, made after the rice of the new season is in the house. It is a feast sweet, not a weekday hotel dosa, and it is eaten through the winter weeks that follow the festival too.

Why it tastes this way

Jaggery and fresh coconut are the winter sweet of Bengal: molasses, perfume, and fat. Cardamom lifts both. The crepe tastes of rice first and of wheat second, soft rather than crisp, so it wraps the filling without shattering. Because the filling is cooked until the coconut looks dry and glossy, the roll slices instead of leaking syrup.

How it is made

Cook a coconut-jaggery filling, make thin rice-and-wheat crepes, and roll them while warm.

Authentic touch

Cook the coconut filling until it leaves the sides of the pan. A wet filling splits the crepe. Roll patishapta while the crepe is still flexible.

How it is used

Serve warm or at room temperature on a Poush Sankranti plate, with other pithas if you have them. They keep for a day, covered.

Field notes

  • Patishapta is a Poush Sankranti pitha, shared by West Bengal and Bangladesh.
  • Coconut filling and kheer filling are both traditional. This recipe writes the coconut one.

Ingredients

  • 1/2 cup rice flour
  • 1/4 cup whole wheat flour
  • 1 cup water, plus more if needed
  • A pinch of salt
  • 1 cup freshly grated coconut
  • 1/2 cup jaggery, chopped
  • 1/4 teaspoon cardamom powder
  • Neutral oil for the pan

Steps

  1. Warm the jaggery in a pan until it loosens. Add the coconut and cardamom and cook, stirring, until the filling is glossy and holds together. Cool it.
  2. Whisk rice flour, wheat flour, salt, and water into a thin crepe batter. Rest 10 minutes.
  3. Oil a nonstick pan lightly over medium heat. Pour a small ladle and swirl a thin crepe.
  4. Cook until the top is set and the underside is pale gold. Do not brown it hard or it will crack.
  5. Spread a spoon of filling along one side and roll. Continue with the rest. Serve the same day.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A kheer filling of thickened milk and rice, which adds dairy
  • All rice flour if you can roll it without cracking, which would drop the wheat

Pairings

  • A cup of milk tea
  • Nolen gur when date-palm jaggery is in season
  • A second pitha such as puli, on a feast table
