Chickpea, olive oil, and salt, a little thicker than Nice socca.

History

Farinata is Liguria’s chickpea pancake, especially associated with Genoa, baked in a wide pan and eaten by the slice. It is the Italian cousin of socca. The batter is the same family: chickpea flour, water, olive oil, salt. Farinata is often slightly thicker and is a bakery and street food rather than a French crêpe.

Cultural background

It is cut into wedges and eaten hot, sometimes with onion or rosemary in local variations. The plain cake is the base.

Why it tastes this way

Chickpeas store well and grind into a flour that sets without egg or gluten, which is why the cake is a port food that sailors and bakers could make from a sack and water. Olive oil is both fat and flavor. Liguria’s olive and chickpea trade is in the taste. A slightly thicker pour than socca gives a creamier center and a crisper rim.

How it is made

A rested chickpea batter is baked in a hot oiled pan until the center sets and the top browns.

Authentic touch

Skim any foam after the batter rests, and stir before you pour. Bake in a pan that is already hot and oily, or the bottom steams.

How it is used

Warm wedges, with pepper. It is a snack or the start of a meal.

Field notes

  • Farinata and socca should be told as cousins, not as one city inventing the other in a single year.
  • The cake is naturally free of gluten, egg, and dairy.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup chickpea flour
  • 1 1/2 cups water
  • 3 tablespoons olive oil, plus more for the pan
  • 3/4 teaspoon salt
  • Black pepper

Steps

  1. Whisk flour, water, oil, and salt. Rest at least 1 hour. Skim foam.
  2. Heat the oven to 450°F. Heat an oiled metal pan.
  3. Pour the batter about 1/3 inch deep.
  4. Bake until set and browned in spots, about 20 minutes.
  5. Pepper and rest 2 minutes.
  6. Cut into wedges.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Socca is the Niçois cousin, usually thinner.
  • Sliced onion on top is a common variation. Salt it or it will taste raw.

Pairings

  • black pepper
  • olive oil
