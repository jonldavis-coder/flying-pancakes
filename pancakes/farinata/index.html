Farinata

You come in by Boat. Italy · Southern Europe.

Pronounced fah-ree-NAH-tah

Ligurian chickpea flatbread, baked thick enough to stay creamy, with oil and pepper.

1 hr 10 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 8 · 1 round farinata, about 8 slices

History

Farinata is the chickpea flatbread of Liguria, especially Genoa, baked in a wide pan in a very hot oven. Socca, from Nice, is the close cousin and has its own page. Farinata is usually a little thicker, with rosemary in many Ligurian pans, and it is also cooked along the neighboring French coast. That is why France is listed. The batter is chickpea flour, water, olive oil, and salt. There is no wheat, egg, or dairy. It is gluten-free and vegan if the flour was milled apart from wheat.

Cultural background

It is street food and bakery food, cut into slices and eaten hot with pepper. A copper pan is traditional. A hot oven pan is the home tool. It is an accompaniment to a meal or a snack, not a dessert.

Why it tastes this way

The center sets like a savory custard and the top spots brown. Olive oil keeps it rich. Chickpea flour tastes sweet and roasted. Rosemary perfumes it. Black pepper at the end is sharp. If the oven is cool, the cake dries out before it browns and tastes of raw flour. Resting the batter improves the texture.

How it is made

Chickpea flour and water rest, then the batter is poured into a hot oiled pan, topped with rosemary and oil, and baked until set and spotted.

Authentic touch

Bake it hotter than a cake, in a pan you have heated with oil. Do not cook the Nice socca recipe and rename it. Socca is thinner and has its own page. Rosemary is the Ligurian note here.

How it is used

Hot, in slices, with pepper, as a snack or the start of a meal. It is vegan as written.

Field notes

  • Farinata is Ligurian. Socca is the Nice cousin and a separate page.
  • The batter is chickpea flour, water, olive oil, and salt.
  • It is also cooked on the neighboring French coast, which is why France is listed.

Ingredients

  • 150 g chickpea flour
  • 450 ml cold water
  • 1 tsp fine salt
  • 3 tbsp olive oil, plus more for the pan
  • 1 tsp fresh rosemary leaves
  • Black pepper, for serving

Steps

  1. Whisk the chickpea flour, water, and salt. Rest 1 hour. Skim the foam.
  2. Heat the oven to 230°C. Oil a wide oven pan and heat it.
  3. Stir the olive oil into the batter. Pour it into the hot pan. It should be deeper than a sheet of socca. Scatter the rosemary.
  4. Bake until set and browned in spots, about 20 to 25 minutes. Pepper it and cut while hot.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Socca, the thinner Nice cake
  • No rosemary, only oil, salt, and pepper

Pairings

  • Black pepper
  • A green salad
  • Olives
