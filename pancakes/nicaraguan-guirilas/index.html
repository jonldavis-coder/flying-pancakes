Arriving in Matagalpa

Nicaraguan güirilas

You come in by Plane. Nicaragua · Mexico & Central America.

Pronounced gwee-REE-lahs

Thick young-corn pancakes with cuajada folded in, savory and a little milky, barely sweet.

20 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 8 güirilas

History

Güirilas belong to Nicaragua, especially the northern highlands, where young corn comes in and cuajada is on the table. The cake is a fresh-corn pancake with curd cheese in the batter. A chorreada across the border in Costa Rica is a different breakfast, usually sweeter and finished with sour cream. Neither cake is masa. Both sit with the older corn foods of the isthmus, which predate wheat hotcakes. This recipe keeps the cheese inside the güirila and leaves the sugar out.

Cultural background

In Matagalpa and nearby highland towns, güirilas are a seasonal breakfast and a snack when maíz tierno, young corn, is being cut. Cuajada is a fresh curd, mild and a bit crumbly, not a long-aged cheese. The cake is thicker than a crepe and smaller than a stuffed arepa.

Why it tastes this way

Young corn is juicier and less starchy than a dried kernel, so the pancake tastes green and sweet even with no sugar added. Cuajada melts only partly and leaves salty, milky curds in the crumb. Egg sets the cake. A spoon of untreated fine cornmeal, if the blend is thin, disappears into that texture. You should taste corn and cheese, not a wheat griddle and not nixtamal.

How it is made

Pulse young corn with egg, a little milk, and salt. Fold in crumbled cuajada and a spoon of fine cornmeal if needed. Cook thick rounds on a buttered skillet.

Authentic touch

Grind the corn coarse and fold the cuajada in at the end so it stays in pieces. Cook the pancakes thicker than a chorreada and more slowly, until the center loses its raw shine. Do not sweeten the bowl.

How it is used

Serve them warm, with more cuajada on the side if you have it, and coffee. They are breakfast, not a dessert crepe.

Field notes

  • Güirilas are Nicaraguan. Chorreadas are Costa Rican. Both use fresh corn, and they are not the same cake.
  • Cuajada is a fresh curd cheese. It stays in soft pieces inside the pancake.
  • This batter is not nixtamalized masa and not a wheat hotcake.

Ingredients

  • 4 cups young corn kernels
  • 100 g cuajada or other fresh unsalted curd cheese, crumbled
  • 1 egg
  • 2 tablespoons milk
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1 tablespoon fine cornmeal, not masa harina
  • Butter for the skillet

Steps

  1. Pulse the corn, egg, milk, and salt until coarse and thick, not smooth.
  2. Stir in the crumbled cuajada and the cornmeal. The batter should hold a spoon.
  3. Heat a skillet over medium-low heat and film it with butter.
  4. Spoon thick rounds. Cook slowly until the underside browns and the top loses its wet shine, then flip and cook through.
  5. Serve warm, with more cuajada if you want it.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A pinch of sugar is a home choice. The highland version here stays unsweetened.
  • Costa Rican chorreadas are the fresh-corn pancake without cuajada in the batter, usually served with natilla.
  • Masa harina is the wrong sack. The corn has to be fresh and young.

Pairings

  • Extra cuajada
  • Black coffee
  • A spoon of sour cream, which is closer to the Costa Rican plate
