Crisp shell, eggy steam, and a hollow center.

10 min prep · 35 min cook · Serves 6 · 6 popovers

History

Popovers are an American baked batter related to English Yorkshire pudding and to earlier popped breads. New England cookbooks have carried them since the nineteenth century as a breakfast bread and a partner for roast beef. They are not flipped. High egg and a very hot oven puff the batter into a hollow roll.

Cultural background

They come to the table in a basket, buttered, beside eggs at breakfast or a roast at dinner. The drama is the puff, which collapses if you open the oven early.

Why it tastes this way

Steam, not baking powder, lifts a popover. A hot oven turns the water in milk and egg into steam before the gluten and egg set, so the shell inflates. That is the same principle as Yorkshire pudding. New England’s version is usually a breakfast bread, served with butter and jam rather than roast drippings, because the region’s famous use of it moved from the meat course to the morning table.

How it is made

A thin batter of egg, milk, and flour is baked in a preheated hot pan until puffed and browned.

Authentic touch

Do not open the oven for the first 20 minutes. Fill the cups no more than half. A crowded cup cannot rise.

How it is used

Butter and jam at breakfast, or with a roast as you would Yorkshire pudding.

Field notes

  • Popover batter is close to Yorkshire pudding batter. The New England habit is often breakfast.
  • They are leavened by steam. Baking powder is not the engine.

Ingredients

  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 cup whole milk
  • 1 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 tablespoon melted butter, plus more for the pan

Steps

  1. Heat the oven to 425°F. Butter a popover or muffin pan and put it in the oven for 5 minutes.
  2. Whisk eggs, milk, flour, salt, and butter into a smooth thin batter.
  3. Pour the batter into the hot cups, filling them halfway.
  4. Bake 20 minutes without opening the door.
  5. Lower the heat to 375°F and bake about 10 minutes more, until deep brown.
  6. Serve at once. They settle as they cool.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A little grated cheese makes a savory popover.
  • Whole wheat flour works but rises less.

Pairings

  • butter
  • jam
  • roast beef
