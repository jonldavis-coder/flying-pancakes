History

Chitoi pitha is a Bengali rice cake cooked in a special pan that encourages holes, or in a covered pan that steams the top. It is a winter pitha, often eaten with molasses or with a savory filling in some households. The batter is rice, not wheat. It is part of the pitha season that also produces patishapta.

Cultural background

The holes soak up molasses. A savory chitoi exists. This is the plain rice cake with molasses.

Why it tastes this way

Rice batter on a moderate covered pan forms holes the way baghrir does, from steam escaping. The cake tastes of rice and, when molasses is poured on, of the jaggery that defines Bengali winter sweets. No ferment is required for this home version, so it is milder than baghrir. The pan and the cover do the work.

How it is made

A thin rice-flour batter is cooked covered on one side until holey, then finished with molasses.

Authentic touch

Cook covered and do not flip. Pour molasses while the cake is hot so it runs into the holes. A thin batter makes more holes than a thick one.

How it is used

Hot, with molasses or jaggery syrup.

Field notes

  • Chitoi is a holey rice cake. The holes are for the molasses.
  • It is a winter pitha, not a fermented dosa.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup rice flour
  • 1 1/4 cups water
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • Molasses or melted jaggery, to serve

Steps

  1. Whisk rice flour, water, and salt into a thin batter. Rest 10 minutes.
  2. Oil a nonstick pan lightly and heat it to medium.
  3. Pour a small round, cover, and cook until the top is set and holey. Do not flip.
  4. Serve with molasses.
  5. Keep the batter thin.
  6. Eat them hot.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A browned garlic and chile topping makes a savory chitoi.
  • Patishapta is the filled crepe of the same season.

Pairings
