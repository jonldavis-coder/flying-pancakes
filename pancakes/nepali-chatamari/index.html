Chatamari

You come in by Plane. Nepal · South Asia.

Pronounced chah-tah-MAH-ree

A thin Newar rice crepe topped with egg, onion, and a little chile.

15 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 4 topped crepes

History

Chatamari is a Newar rice crepe from the Kathmandu Valley. English speakers sometimes call it a Nepali pizza. That is a comparison. The Newar name is chatamari. Rice flour batter is cooked like a crepe and topped with egg, minced meat, sugar, or a simpler savory mix, depending on the kitchen and the day. This recipe writes an egg, onion, and chile topping and leaves minced meat as a variation that would add a meat allergen.

Cultural background

Newar feast food and everyday snacks overlap on this pan. A plain sweet chatamari, dusted with sugar, is one old home habit. The topped savory one is what invited the pizza comparison. The comparison is not the recipe.

Why it tastes this way

Rice flour makes a mild, slightly sweet sheet with a chew that wheat crepes do not have. Egg on top adds richness and a soft set. Onion and chile keep it savory. Because the batter is rice and not a sourdough, the tang is missing on purpose. The flavor is the grain, the egg, and the topping, eaten while the edge is still a little crisp.

How it is made

Pour a thin rice-flour crepe, crack an egg onto it with onion and chile, and cover until the white sets.

Authentic touch

Cook the crepe only until it can hold the egg. A brittle cracker cannot carry a soft topping. Medium heat is enough.

How it is used

Cut into wedges if you are sharing. Eat it hot, with a spoon of achar if you want more heat. The cake itself is mild.

Field notes

  • Chatamari is the Newar name. Nepali pizza is only an English comparison.
  • The base is rice flour, cooked as a crepe in the Kathmandu Valley.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup rice flour, with no wheat added
  • 1 1/4 cups water
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 4 eggs
  • 1 small onion, finely chopped
  • 1 green chile, minced
  • Neutral oil for the pan

Steps

  1. Whisk rice flour, salt, and water into a thin batter. Rest 10 minutes.
  2. Oil a pan over medium heat and pour a crepe-sized round. Cook until the top is just set.
  3. Crack an egg on the crepe. Scatter onion and chile over it. Salt lightly.
  4. Cover and cook until the egg white sets and the edge of the rice crepe is dry. The yolk may stay soft.
  5. Slide it out and repeat with the remaining batter and eggs.

Set the egg white fully if you need the egg well cooked.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Minced meat under the egg, which adds a meat allergen
  • A plain crepe sprinkled with sugar and ghee

Pairings

  • Tomato achar
  • A cup of black tea
  • A fresh cucumber salad
