Arriving in Bandung, Java

Serabi

You come in by Train. Indonesia · Southeast Asia.

Pronounced seh-RAH-bee

A small, thick coconut-rice pancake with a porous face, served with palm-sugar syrup.

15 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 10 small pancakes

History

Serabi are Indonesian coconut rice pancakes, especially at home on Java. Bandung and other Javanese towns sell them from small charcoal stoves and clay molds. Rice flour and coconut milk make a batter that cooks into a plump round, often finished with kinca, a palm-sugar and coconut syrup. Savory serabi with shrimp exist. This page is the sweet one, with no shrimp and no wheat. Kue ape, the dimpled cake with a sunken center, is a different Jakarta sweet and has its own page.

Cultural background

The pancake is thicker than a crepe and is not folded. The syrup is poured on or used for dipping. Some batters include a little yeast or fermented rice for a tang. This one is mixed with coconut milk and cooked the same day, so the flavor stays sweet rather than sour.

Why it tastes this way

Coconut milk makes the crumb rich and slightly sweet before any syrup. Rice flour tastes clean and a little chewy at the edge. Palm sugar in the kinca adds a smoky, dark sweetness that white sugar does not have. The porous top catches the syrup. Because there is no egg, the cake tastes of grain and coconut rather than of a rich omelet.

How it is made

Cook small coconut-rice pancakes and serve them with a palm-sugar coconut syrup.

Authentic touch

Cook the rounds small and leave them until the top is full of holes and dry. Covering the pan helps. Pour the syrup at the table so the pancake does not sog before anyone eats.

How it is used

Serve warm, two or three to a plate, with the syrup beside. They are a snack or a dessert, not a stuffed crepe.

Field notes

  • Serabi are Javanese coconut rice pancakes, often served with palm-sugar syrup.
  • Kue ape is a different cake, with a crater in the middle.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup rice flour, with no wheat added
  • 1 cup coconut milk
  • 2 tablespoons sugar
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 cup palm sugar, chopped
  • 1/4 cup water
  • 1/4 cup more coconut milk for the syrup
  • Neutral oil

Steps

  1. Whisk rice flour, 1 cup coconut milk, sugar, and salt into a batter about as thick as cream. Rest 10 minutes.
  2. Simmer palm sugar, water, and the extra coconut milk until the sugar dissolves and the syrup thickens slightly.
  3. Oil a pan or small molds over medium-low heat. Pour small rounds.
  4. Cover and cook until the tops are set and bubbly and the undersides are golden. Flip only if the center is still wet.
  5. Serve with the palm-sugar syrup.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A pinch of yeast and a short rise for a tangier crumb
  • A shrimp topping, which would add shellfish and make a savory serabi

Pairings

  • Palm-sugar syrup
  • Grated coconut
  • Hot tea
