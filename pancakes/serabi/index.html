Pronounced seh-RAH-bee

Small coconut-rice pancakes, sometimes sweet and sometimes savory.

10 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · about 10 cakes

History

Serabi, or surabi, are Javanese coconut-rice pancakes cooked in small molds. Sweet serabi are finished with coconut sugar syrup. Savory ones can be plainer. They are a snack food. This recipe is the sweet one, with palm sugar or brown sugar.

Cultural background

A small pan or a well pan works. The center stays soft. The edge browns. Coconut milk is the liquid.

Why it tastes this way

Rice flour and coconut milk taste of the Indonesian snack kitchen: mild, a little sweet, and rich without dairy. Palm sugar is the deeper sweetness, with a molasses note white sugar lacks. The small mold browns the rim. It is a cousin of khanom krok and of other coconut-rice cakes of the region, with a Javanese name.

How it is made

A rice and coconut batter is cooked as small pancakes and finished with palm-sugar syrup.

Authentic touch

Cook covered on medium until the center sets. A high flame burns the coconut sugar if you have already poured it on. Sauce them after, or the sugar burns.

How it is used

Warm, with the syrup.

Field notes

  • Serabi are Javanese coconut-rice pancakes.
  • Palm sugar syrup is the sweet finish written here.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup rice flour
  • 1 cup coconut milk
  • 2 tablespoons sugar
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1/4 cup palm sugar or brown sugar
  • 2 tablespoons water

Steps

  1. Whisk rice flour, coconut milk, sugar, and salt.
  2. Cook small cakes covered on a medium oiled pan until set.
  3. Simmer palm sugar and water into a syrup.
  4. Spoon the syrup over the warm cakes.
  5. Serve immediately.
  6. Do not pour the syrup into the pan or it will burn.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A savory serabi skips the syrup and may include scallion.
  • Khanom krok is the Thai cousin with two batters.

Pairings
