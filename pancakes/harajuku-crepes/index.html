A thin sweet crêpe wrapped around whipped cream and fruit, a street snack.

15 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 6 · 6 crepes

History

Sweet crêpes became a Harajuku street food in the late twentieth century, wrapped in a cone with whipped cream, fruit, and often ice cream. They are a Tokyo youth-street sweet, not a farmhouse pancake. The crêpe is French technique. The pile of cream is the local serving.

Cultural background

Vendors cook the crêpe, add cream and strawberry or banana, and fold it in paper. This recipe stops at cream and fruit so it stays a clear home version.

Why it tastes this way

A thin, slightly sweet batter is a wrapper for cold cream and fruit, which is why the crêpe itself is mild. The pleasure is the contrast of a warm wrapper and a cold filling. Harajuku’s takeout culture made the cone practical. It is a modern import, openly, and a real street food.

How it is made

A sweet crêpe batter is cooked thin and filled with whipped cream and fruit.

Authentic touch

Cook the crêpe thin and let it cool for half a minute before the cream, or the cream melts into a puddle. Wrap it in paper so it holds.

How it is used

Immediately, in a cone.

Field notes

  • Harajuku crêpes are a modern Tokyo street sweet.
  • The wrapper should be thin. The filling is the point.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup flour
  • 2 eggs
  • 1 cup milk
  • 2 tablespoons sugar
  • 1 tablespoon melted butter
  • Pinch of salt
  • 1 cup whipped cream
  • 1 cup sliced strawberries

Steps

  1. Whisk a thin batter and rest 10 minutes.
  2. Cook thin crepes in a buttered pan.
  3. Add cream and strawberries and fold into a cone.
  4. Serve at once.
  5. Do not overfill or the cone collapses.
  6. The crêpe should still be flexible.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Chocolate spread and banana are the other stall order and add no new allergen if the spread is dairy.
  • A savory crêpe is not the Harajuku style.

Pairings

  • whipped cream
  • strawberries
