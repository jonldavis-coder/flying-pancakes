Small sour-milk pancakes, simpler than svele.

10 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · about 12 small cakes

History

Lapper are Norwegian pancakes, often smaller and a bit tangier than svele, made with sour milk or buttermilk. The name covers a homey pancake rather than the ferry cake. They are breakfast and coffee food, served with butter and sugar or jam.

Cultural background

A sour milk batter was a way to use milk that had turned, which is how many northern pancakes began. Buttermilk is the reliable modern stand-in.

Why it tastes this way

Acid in the milk reacts with a pinch of soda and tastes clean and slightly tart, so the cakes want jam rather than a salty cheese. They are thinner than svele and closer to a small buttermilk pancake. The flavor is the sour milk, not a spice blend.

How it is made

Buttermilk batter is cooked as small cakes.

Authentic touch

Do not sweeten them like cake. Sugar on top is the sweet. The batter should stay mildly tangy.

How it is used

Butter and jam or sugar.

Field notes

  • Lapper and svele are both Norwegian and are not identical.
  • Buttermilk stands in for the older sour milk.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup flour
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/4 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1 large egg
  • 1 cup buttermilk
  • 1 tablespoon melted butter

Steps

  1. Stir into a smooth batter and rest 5 minutes.
  2. Cook small rounds on a buttered griddle.
  3. Flip when the bubbles hold.
  4. Serve with butter and jam.
  5. Keep the heat at medium.
  6. They are best warm.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Svele are thicker and often cardamom-scented.
  • Sour cream thinned with milk can stand in for buttermilk.

Pairings

  • jam
  • butter
  • sugar
