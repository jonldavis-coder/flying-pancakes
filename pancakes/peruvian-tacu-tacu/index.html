Arriving in Lima

Peruvian tacu-tacu

You come in by Plane. Peru · South America.

Pronounced TAH-koo TAH-koo

A pan-fried cake of leftover rice and beans, garlicky, with a crisp face and mild ají amarillo.

15 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 2 · 2 cakes

History

Tacu-tacu is a cake of leftover rice and beans, fried in a skillet until the outside is crisp. It is Lima criollo cooking. Afro-Peruvian cooks helped build that coastal cuisine in the colonial city and afterward, working with Indigenous ingredients and Spanish techniques in kitchens shaped by slavery and, later, by free Black communities. The dish itself is practical: the previous day's rice and beans, garlic, onion, and ají amarillo, pressed into a hot pan. A fried egg or a breaded steak is a common addition. Those are not in this recipe.

Cultural background

Criollo cooking is the coastal home cooking of Lima and other Peruvian port cities. Tacu-tacu is what a cook does with rice and beans that are already seasoned and a little dry. It is lunch or a heavy breakfast. This version stays with the rice and beans so the plate can be vegan.

Why it tastes this way

Day-old rice fries harder and crisper than fresh rice, which is why leftovers are the right start. Beans break into the grains and salt the cake. Garlic and onion, cooked first, perfume the oil. Ají amarillo is fruity and mildly hot, enough to set the heat at mild without burning. There is no cheese and no egg in the cake, so the flavor stays with the beans, the rice, and the chile.

How it is made

Fry onion and garlic, add ají amarillo, cooked beans, and cooked rice. Mash part of the mixture, press it into a cake in the skillet, and fry until a crust forms on both sides.

Authentic touch

Use rice and beans that are dry enough to mash and hold a shape. Wet rice steams and never crusts. Press the cake into the pan and leave it alone until the bottom is brown. Then flip once. Stirring makes fried rice, not tacu-tacu.

How it is used

Slide the cake onto a plate for lunch or breakfast. A fried egg on top is a different plate and adds egg. Breaded steak adds meat. The cake here is the rice and beans alone.

Field notes

  • Tacu-tacu is leftover rice and beans fried into a cake, a Lima criollo dish.
  • Afro-Peruvian cooks were part of building that coastal kitchen. The page stays with the dish and that history.
  • Let the cake sit in the pan long enough to crust. Stirring it turns it into fried rice.

Ingredients

  • 3 cups cooked white rice, cold
  • 2 cups cooked canary or black beans, drained
  • 1 small onion, chopped
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 tablespoon ají amarillo paste
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 3 tablespoons neutral oil

Steps

  1. Heat 1 tablespoon oil and cook the onion and garlic until soft. Stir in the ají amarillo paste.
  2. Add the beans and mash about half of them into the onion. Fold in the rice and salt. The mixture should hold together.
  3. Heat the remaining oil in a skillet over medium heat. Add half the mixture and press it into a thick cake.
  4. Cook without stirring until the bottom is brown, then flip and brown the other side. Repeat with the rest.
  5. Serve hot.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Canary beans, black beans, or lentils all appear in Lima kitchens. Keep them cooked and dry.
  • A breaded steak beside the cake is a restaurant plate and adds meat.
  • Leave out the ají and the heat drops to none. The written recipe includes a tablespoon.

Pairings

  • A fried egg, which adds egg and means the plate is no longer vegan
  • Salsa criolla of onion and lime
  • Avocado
