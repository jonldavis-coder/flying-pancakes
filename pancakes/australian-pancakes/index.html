A larger, softer breakfast pancake than a pikelet, served with lemon and sugar or syrup.

10 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · about 8 pancakes

History

Australian pancakes, as distinct from pikelets, are the larger breakfast cakes, closer to a thin-to-medium crepe or a fluffy stack depending on the house. Lemon and sugar are a common finish, inherited from British Shrove pancakes. Weekend breakfast is the setting. They are not the same thickness as pikelets, which is why both pages exist.

Cultural background

A thinner batter makes them more crepe-like. This one is in between: pourable, not a drop scone, not paper-thin.

Why it tastes this way

Lemon and sugar are the British finish that stayed, sharp against butter. A breakfast pancake is larger than a tea pikelet because it is the meal, not the accompaniment to a cup of tea. Golden syrup is the other pour. The wheat-and-egg batter is the settler breakfast, stated as that.

How it is made

A pourable batter is cooked as medium pancakes and finished with lemon and sugar.

Authentic touch

Pour a larger round than a pikelet and cook it through. Lemon should go on while the cake is hot.

How it is used

Hot, with lemon and sugar or golden syrup.

Field notes

  • Lemon and sugar are the British finish that stayed.
  • Pikelets are the smaller tea cakes.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup flour
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1 egg
  • 1 cup milk
  • 1 tablespoon melted butter
  • Lemon and sugar, to serve

Steps

  1. Whisk a pourable batter.
  2. Cook medium rounds on a buttered griddle.
  3. Serve with lemon and sugar or golden syrup.
  4. They are larger than pikelets.
  5. Lemon goes on while they are hot.
  6. Eat them the morning they are made.

This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Pikelets are smaller and thicker.
  • Banana in the batter is a home weekend habit.

Pairings

  • lemon
  • sugar
  • golden syrup
