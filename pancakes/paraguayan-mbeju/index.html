Arriving in Asunción

Paraguayan mbeju

You come in by Plane. Paraguay · South America.

Pronounced m-beh-YOO

A crisp griddled cake of manioc starch, cornmeal, and cheese, salty at the edge and tender in the middle.

15 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 1 large cake

History

Mbeju is a Paraguayan breakfast cake made from manioc starch, corn flour, and cheese, cooked on a griddle until the edge crisps. Manioc, cassava, is an old staple along the Paraguay River. The starch in a modern kitchen is commercial, already processed. This recipe uses that starch and does not teach you to grind raw cassava. Cheese and butter make it a dairy dish. It is often eaten with cocido, a hot yerba mate infusion, which is a drink beside the plate and not an ingredient in the dough.

Cultural background

In Asunción and in the countryside, mbeju is breakfast and an afternoon bite, sometimes cooked in a broad pan so several people can break pieces off. The dough is crumbly when it goes into the pan and sets as the starch heats. Corn flour keeps it from being only elastic tapioca.

Why it tastes this way

Manioc starch goes chewy and then crisp as it dries on the griddle. Cornmeal adds a toasted grain note. Fresh cheese salts the cake and browns in spots. Butter helps the edge fry. There is no sugar and no chile, so the flavor stays savory and mild. Cocido beside it is smoky and bitter, which is why the two share a table.

How it is made

Rub commercial manioc starch with cornmeal, grated fresh cheese, butter, salt, and a splash of milk. Press the crumb into a skillet and cook slowly until the bottom crisps and the center sets. Flip once if it will hold.

Authentic touch

Mix the starch, cornmeal, cheese, and butter to a damp crumb, not a pourable batter. Press it into a hot pan and let it set before you touch it. Turning too soon breaks the cake. The cheese should be fresh and salty, not a hard aged cheese that stays in pebbles.

How it is used

Break it into wedges at breakfast, with cocido or coffee. It is a bread and a cheese dish at the same time.

Field notes

  • Use commercial manioc starch. Raw bitter cassava can be poisonous, and this recipe does not teach home processing.
  • Mbeju is starch, corn, and cheese on a griddle, a Paraguayan breakfast.
  • Cocido is the drink beside it, not a liquid in the dough.

Ingredients

  • 150 g commercial manioc starch (cassava starch)
  • 80 g fine cornmeal
  • 100 g grated fresh cheese
  • 30 g butter
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 2 tablespoons milk

Steps

  1. Rub the manioc starch, cornmeal, cheese, butter, and salt together until the mixture looks like damp crumbs. Sprinkle on the milk and mix. It should clump, not pour.
  2. Heat a skillet over medium-low heat and press the crumb in an even layer.
  3. Cook without stirring until the bottom is crisp and the center has set, about 10 minutes.
  4. Flip in one piece if you can, or cut it in the pan and turn the pieces. Cook until the second side spots.
  5. Serve warm, in wedges.

Use commercial manioc (cassava) starch. Raw bitter cassava can be poisonous. This recipe does not teach home processing of raw cassava.

Refrigerate leftovers and reheat on a dry skillet. Egg or dairy batter should not sit at room temperature. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Pork fat instead of butter is used in some kitchens. It adds meat and is not vegetarian.
  • More cornmeal makes a mealier cake. More starch makes it chewier.
  • An egg in the dough adds the egg allergen and is not required here.

Pairings

  • Cocido, hot yerba mate
  • Coffee with milk
  • A slice of tomato
